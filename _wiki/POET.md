## Snapshot
POET Technologies develops an Electrical-Optical Interposer (EOI) platform — wafer-scale optical integration targeting co-packaged optics (CPO) and near-package optics (NPO) for 800G/1.6T datacenter transceivers. The company is pre-revenue at meaningful scale (~$1M annual run-rate per FinTwit, May 2026) but secured a landmark $50M initial order from Lumilens in May 2026 (scalable to $500M over five years), framed as a ~50x run-rate de-risking event. POET sits in the photonics/CPO basket alongside [AAOI](AAOI.md), SIVE, ALMU, and LWLG, with persistent short pressure and a live valuation debate vs. Taiwan-listed FOCI (3363).

## Debate / thesis
- **Bull — CPO optionality + Lumilens de-risks the story:** The **$50M Lumilens order (scalable to $500M / 5 yrs)** for EOI wafer-scale integration — engineering samples late 2026, production targeted 2027 — gives POET its first material revenue anchor (2026-05-15). Stock rose +22–27% on the news. The deal implicates the broader chain: SIVE (lasers), Semtech (receivers), Foxconn/Liteon (module assembly). The $400M raise at a premium gives a long runway. POET's InP exposure is directly relevant to datacenter laser demand (SemiAnalysis InP thread, 2026-05-05).
- **Bear — valuation vs. FOCI; revenue still negligible:** @aleabitoreddit (213k–372k views) argues FOCI (3363, ~$3.1B MC) is "literally the bottleneck for CPO volume ramp" and NVDA/TSM's primary CPO supplier — yet POET traded at a comparable/higher MC (~$3.14B at the $50M announcement) with a fraction of FOCI's revenue and ramp visibility (2026-05-18). @crux_capital_ trimmed his position on the Lumilens pop. The $50M's ~50x rev-rate nature underscores how tiny current revenues are.
- **Structural — CPO timeline risk:** Jefferies (Beavington, 2026-06-12) flagged a 2–3 year CPO OIO pushout to 2030–31 due to interposer overheating. If CPO slips, POET's NPO/CPO addressable market is delayed; near-term pluggable 800G/1.6T is the bridging opportunity.
- **Where the sell-side stands:** No formal sell-side PT/rating found in the ported corpus. Coverage is FinTwit-driven, with persistent short pressure on the small-cap optics basket (@crux_capital_, June 2026).

  - **What POET actually builds, for the record, since the page describes the platform but not the mechanism: the Optical Interposer forms optical and electrical layers simultaneously on a standard silicon wafer using CMOS-compatible processes, then attaches InP lasers, detectors and modulators by **flip-chip bonding**. Two differentiators are named: **PASSIVE ALIGNMENT** (alignment features built into the interposer so pick-and-place achieves optical-axis registration, replacing real-time active alignment) and the **ELIMINATION OF WIRE BONDS** (*"at 800G and above, wire bonds are a primary source of RF crosstalk and signal degradation"*). Product lines: optical engines (**800G Wavelight/Infinity, 1.6T Teralight** — a Lightwave Innovation Reviews 2026 winner) and ELS modules (**Blazar, Starlight**), with a **3.2T TFLN engine co-developed with QCi**. Partner network: **Foxconn, Luxshare, LITEON (prototype late 2026, mass production 2027), Lessengers (1.6T 2xDR4, samples Q2 2026), Sivers (DFB laser-integrated light engine), Mitsubishi Electric** (**DAMNANG (Substack)** — "The War of Light, A Laser Shortage", **2026-03-31**; "CPO, Fully Dissected (CPO Special Part 2)", **2026-03-18**). ➤ **Two things this page should take from it. (1) The bottleneck is named and it is not the design: *"the biggest unknown is **PRODUCTION YIELD**… there is a world of difference between passive alignment working in a lab and sustaining consistent yield at tens to hundreds of thousands of units per month… there is no publicly available data confirming that wafer-level processes have achieved this at scale."* (2) The supply-chain exposure the platform-integrator model creates: POET *"is closer to a **platform integrator**, assembling optical engines on its Interposer using components sourced from EXTERNAL suppliers… this structure offers flexibility and customization advantages, but it also introduces supply chain exposure **if InP laser availability becomes constrained**"* — i.e. POET is short the exact commodity [[LITE]] and [[COHR]] are long.**
  - ℹ️ **ONE MANAGEMENT DATAPOINT WORTH KEEPING, on the modulator fork: POET’s CEO is quoted saying that *"among high-speed modulator materials currently in development, including silicon, InP, and advanced polymers, **TFLN is the most promising for early commercialization**"* — the author’s gloss being that *"a company that builds optical engine platforms chose TFLN over EO polymer"* (**DAMNANG (Substack)** — "LWLG Structural Technology Risk Analysis", **2026-04-12**). ➤ **Relevant because the QCi/TFLN 3.2T engine is POET’s furthest-out product bet and this is the only first-party rationale for it in the archive. ⚠️ A quote relayed by a third party; no date or venue given for the original.**
