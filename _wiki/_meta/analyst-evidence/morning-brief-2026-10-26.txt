FIVE PRIORITIES

1. ORCL — Make October 28 about funded execution, not just demand.
Broker estimates: JPM / Samik Chatterjee, October 20, has May-FY28/FY29 capex of approximately $120bn/$133bn (chart reads). GS / Gabriela Borges, October 22, has $112.2bn/$95.4bn; Bloomberg’s October 23 evening fiscal-year check has $103.8bn/$95.4bn. The disagreement is whether investment peaks, not merely its next-quarter level. Barclays / Raimo Lenschow and Sheldon McMeans, October 26, also highlight FY28 funding.
Inference/action: prepare a funding bridge separating cash capex, leases, customer prepayments and new financing; demand a dated route from contracted capacity to revenue and cash. An enlarged demand forecast alone does not settle this.
Falsifier: binding funding and a credible cash-generation bridge covering the higher-capex path would weaken the financing-risk case.
Signals: cata-9f2e588078f2; supp-20261026-orcl. Evidence: R; E, idea_evidence.orcl. Barclays evidence is an email excerpt, not a full-report review.

2. AMZN — A Street beat may still miss the real expectations bar.
Broker estimates: BofA / Justin Post, report dated October 25, forecasts Q3 AWS growth of 40% versus Street 38%, but says investor expectations may be 42%+. He models AWS margin at 39%; his Q4 operating-profit guide expectation is $29–34bn against Street $32.9bn, with retail fuel costs a possible offset.
Inference/action: prepare separate consensus and investor-expectation scorecards for October 29. Require evidence that utilization, Trainium and Bedrock economics offset depreciation; do not score a 40% growth print as an unequivocal positive surprise.
Falsifier: stronger AWS growth accompanied by worsening margins or weak retail guidance would defeat the uncomplicated beat-and-raise thesis.
Signal: supp-20261026-amzn. Evidence: E, idea_evidence.amzn.

3. GOOG — Quarantine the apparent house revenue edge until definitions match.
Recorded inputs: Capstone’s June 5 model extraction has 2026/2027 revenue of $505bn/$641bn; Bloomberg’s October 23 calendar-year series has $427.9bn/$553.5bn. But the wiki’s JPM / Doug Anmuth et al. October 15 entry explicitly distinguishes JPM gross revenue from its Bloomberg ex-TAC comparison.
Inference: the engine’s advertised 18%/16% house upside is not yet an investable same-basis disagreement. The field definition and house workbook still require direct confirmation; the entire discrepancy has NOT been conclusively reconciled.
Action: normalize gross versus ex-TAC, then split TPU hardware from recurring Cloud services and bridge gross profit/FCF ahead of October 28.
Falsifier: a verified same-period, same-definition comparison retaining a material gap would restore the fundamental disagreement.
Signals: esti-d75e5d0173ba; esti-d11f3446a5ba. Evidence: _wiki/GOOG.md, October 15 entry; _wiki/_data/house.json; _wiki/_data/estimates.json; E, idea_evidence.goog.

4. NVDA — Demand indicators are resilient, but orders are not powered usage.
Observed broker samples: JPM / Joseph Cardoso, Manmohanpreet Singh and Marc Vitenzon, October 26, report OpenRouter spending growth accelerating to 40% m/m from 28%, while token-volume growth slows to 25% from 71%. Mix and repricing matter. Their non-hyperscaler rental indices show H100/B200 rates rising 6.5%/4.7% m/m, versus A100 falling 3.5%.
Broker model: MS / Stephen Byrd, Joseph Moore et al., October 26, estimates a 2026–28 power shortfall of 70GW before mitigation and 38GW after mitigation/APAC spillover. The upward revision partly reflects added rack loads and revised load-factor treatment—not solely fresh demand.
Inference/action: test deployment dates and realized utilization against chip deliveries. OpenRouter is a one-week monthly sample excluding direct-provider traffic; rental indices are not customer-level realized economics. Keep grid power, IT load and utilized power separate.
Falsifier: sustained newer-GPU rental deterioration and customer deployment deferrals would undermine the resilient-demand interpretation.
Signal: supp-20261026-nvda. Evidence: E, idea_evidence.nvda; _wiki/_meta/assumptions.md.

5. MU — Contract protection is real; immunity from the cycle is not established.
Filed evidence: Micron’s October 9 10-K describes enforceable take-or-pay volumes, deposits and letters of credit, but also periodic pricing negotiations, price bands and enforcement risks.
Management relayed by BofA / Vivek Arya, October 25: approximately 35% of sales are expected under strategic customer agreements, targeting approximately 50% by CY30. Counterview: former Micron CSO Michael Sadler, Barclays / Simon Coles call, October 23, doubts full through-cycle economics will survive renegotiation; he explicitly has not seen the agreements.
Inference/action: distinguish protected volumes, pricing floors, unsecured exposure and product mix before lifting trough earnings. Neither management optimism nor a former executive’s skepticism proves the outcome.
Falsifier: material contract reopening, unenforceable floors or delivery deferrals would weaken the downside-protection thesis.
Signal: supp-20261026-mu. Evidence: E, idea_evidence.mu; MU/MU_10-K_2026-10-09_0000723125-26-000023.html; _equity_calls/Semis/2026-10-23_Barclays_Memory-outlook-strategic-priorities-ex-Micron-CSO-Michael-Sadler-Simon-Coles.md.
