FIVE PRIORITIES

1. ORCL — Underwrite economic cash flow, not just the capital-markets cap.
Company evidence, October 28 Investor Day: additional capital-market funding through FY30 is capped at $20bn, split $10bn equity/$10bn gross debt, excluding refinancing and on top of the $20bn ATM already completed. Oracle targets positive FCF in 2H FY28 and $30bn+ in FY29, using vendor financing, customer hardware and prepayments. These are targets, not completed funding. [Oracle release](https://investor.oracle.com/investor-news/news-details/2026/Oracle-Investor-Day-2026-Accelerating-Value-Creation/default.aspx)
Broker conflict: UBS / Karl Keirstead and Radi Sultan, October 28, welcomes the funding relief even while raising FY28 capex. MS credit / Lindsay Tyler and Benjamin Guerrero, October 29, remains cautious: vendor principal might sit outside reported FCF, but that accounting treatment is explicitly unconfirmed; future lease liabilities have no disclosed ceiling.
Inference/action: the financing bear case weakened, not vanished. Reconcile FCF after vendor-principal payments, leases and customer concessions, then match obligations to contracted receipts and site delivery. “No more capital-market issuance” is not “no more debt-like financing.”
Falsifier of the residual bear case: binding funding and matched payment schedules demonstrate sustainable cash generation after all financing obligations.
Signal: supp-20261029-orcl. Evidence: E.orcl; ORCL/ORCL_8-K_ex991_pressrelease_2026-10-28_0001193125-26-435853.html.

2. GOOG — Search monetization is now the critical test.
Company evidence, October 28 release/call: Cloud grew 103%, Search 12%; capex was $51.4bn and FCF negative $9.1bn. The cash result included TPU inventory investment and a $5.2bn EC fine: do not annualize it mechanically. Management expects third-party bridge capacity to pressure Cloud margins as usage rises in Q4.
New revision: JPM / Doug Anmuth, Dae K Lee and Margaret Hoffman, October 29, keeps Overweight but cuts PT $425→$375 and 2027/2028 GAAP EPS 5%/2%. It now models Search growth of 9% reported in Q4 and 2027. JPM’s approximately $3bn Q3 TPU sales and approximately 80% ex-TPU Cloud growth are estimates, not company-disclosed segment figures.
Inference/action: separate query growth from monetization and Cloud consumption from TPU hardware. Build the Search revenue-per-query and bridge-capacity margin sensitivities before treating the Cloud beat as an EPS upgrade.
Falsifier of the cautious view: AI Mode monetization improves sufficiently to restore Search growth while Cloud profitability absorbs the bridge-capacity ramp.
Signal: supp-20261029-goog. Evidence: E.goog; GOOG/transcripts/GOOG_Q3-2026-earnings_2026-10-28.md.

3. MSFT — Better Azure delivery, with a concentration question.
Company evidence, October 28 release/call: Azure grew 48% both reported and constant currency; CFO Amy Hood guided 49–50% constant currency next quarter. Capex of $45.2bn was held back by third-party capacity delayed into 2H FY27—not a cancellation.
The October 28 10-Q discloses $10bn quarterly revenue from commercial arrangements with OpenAI, including revenue-sharing payments. It is not all Azure infrastructure rental.
Broker revision: UBS / Karl Keirstead, Taylor McGinnis and Jack Fyda, October 29, raises its FY27 exit-quarter Azure growth forecast 43%→47% and PT $675→$700. Its low-equity-raise assessment after the callback is broker judgment, not a company commitment.
Inference/action: prioritize Microsoft for a positive estimate review, but isolate OpenAI-linked revenue from broader enterprise consumption. Obtain the delayed-capacity schedule; do not assign an unnamed supplier delay to a particular neocloud.
Falsifier: the growth guide fails despite capacity catch-up, or concentration rises without corresponding cash/margin improvement.
Signal: supp-20261029-msft. Evidence: E.msft; MSFT/MSFT_10-Q_2026-10-28_0001193125-26-436133.html; MSFT/transcripts/MSFT_Q1-FY27-earnings_2026-10-28.md.

4. AMZN — Tonight must pass a joint growth-and-returns test.
BofA / Justin Post, October 25: AWS growth model 40% versus quoted Street 38%, with investor expectations potentially 42%+; AWS operating-margin forecast 39%. Its anticipated Q4 operating-income guidance of $29–34bn versus Street $32.9bn is a broker forecast, not issued company guidance.
Inference/action: freeze AWS growth, margin, capex and retail-profit expectations together. Ask how capacity utilization, Trainium and depreciation affect returns; a growth beat purchased with weaker economics would not settle the thesis.
Falsifier: AWS acceleration requires margin sacrifice, or investment and retail costs overwhelm cash/profit upside.
Catalyst: today, October 29, call at 5pm ET, confirmed by Amazon’s October 15 announcement. [Amazon calendar](https://press.aboutamazon.com/2026/10/amazon-com-to-webcast-third-quarter-2026-financial-results-conference-call)
