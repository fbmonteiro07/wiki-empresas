## Scope and bases — read first

- **Universe:** the ~101 ticker pages of this wiki (roster in [00_INDEX](../00_INDEX.md)), including the private names (OpenAI, Anthropic, Nscale, Cerebras, SpaceX) and the hyperscalers' data-centre SPV/JV financings, which are **keyed by the tenant's ticker** (the legal issuer is named in the row). Adjacent issuers that sit in the official credit workbook but have no page (SoftBank, IREN, Cipher, Zenith Arc/Jane Street) are listed as *adjacent* and never summed with the roster.
- **Event date** = pricing date where there is one, otherwise closing or announcement. **Bases are explicit per row** (face, gross, net, facility, commitment, reported) and the totals only add **priced/closed/funded rows on a face or gross basis in USD or at a sourced USD equivalent** (Barclays at-issue equivalents for Alphabet and the Amazon CHF/CAD deals; Amazon's sterling deal at the 10-Q carrying value, flagged). Announced, committed, planned, reported-talks and undrawn facilities are tabulated separately and are **never** added to the priced total. Non-USD deals without a sourced USD equivalent (Uber €4.5bn at mid-swaps +45 to +170bp; CoreWeave's €2.0bn leg) are listed, not summed.
- **Credit marks** quoted in the notes are the official workbook's bases — price pts, yield YTM %, OAS bp (Bloomberg, 2026-11-03 09:34); never mixed with Barclays' 15-Sep YTW or the issuer-board CDS series.
- **Boundary rows** are shown with a ⚠ flag: Meta's $25bn (launched 04-30, priced 05-04) and Core Scientific's $3.3bn (announced 04-21, issued 05-06) count; Tract/Fleet's $4.585bn (issued 05-01) is listed and excluded.
- Out of scope by design: routine bank-holding-company and payment-network funding (JPM, GS, V, MA benchmark issuance and commercial-paper programmes), convertible redemptions/conversions (Lumentum's $757.8m early conversions, Western Digital's 2028 call), cash-funded M&A (Dynatrace/Arize, Vertiv/UIG $1.45bn) and non-roster names (Celestica's 08-05 follow-on, SB Energy's delayed IPO, Oura). Positioning and trade ideas are not on this page.

## What it tells us

### 1. Who is funding AI capex, with what

- **The hyperscalers have moved from cash flow to every public channel at once.** In six months the roster priced or closed **$684.6bn** across 58 rows; IG bonds are the largest bucket ($195.5bn, 18 rows: Meta $25bn in May, NVIDIA $25bn and SpaceX $25bn in June, Amazon $25bn in July, Alphabet $25bn in August, Oracle $10bn in November, plus ~$43bn of non-dollar paper from Alphabet and Amazon). Barclays' count: hyperscalers were 14% of all USD IG corporate supply YTD by 08-20 (~17% with data-centre bonds), 21% of IG non-financial supply; Tech 37%, "a record for a single sector" if held; $247.7bn across the Big-6 plus SpaceX and a ~$285bn year-end path, with its investor survey at $275–325bn (Barclays FICC/Credit · Keches et al., 2026-08-20). Goldman's ceiling: ~$510bn of further hyperscaler IG capacity before issuer-concentration conventions bind, with 32% of the $194bn issued by 07-09 placed outside USD (GS Credit Strategy · Lynam, 2026-07-09). Morgan Stanley's across-channel tracker: ~$466bn of AI-related credit through 09-30 vs $216bn in all of FY25, but the monthly run slowed — $54bn in July, $41bn in August (partial), $23bn in September (MS · Lima/Campbell/Tirupattur et al., 2026-10-01; 2026-08-20).
- **Equity is now a primary channel, not a last resort.** Equity follow-ons, preferreds, private placements and IPOs are **$263.4bn** of the priced total (~38%): Alphabet's $84.75bn package (first equity since the IPO), SpaceX's $75bn IPO, SK hynix's $26.5bn ADR, Intel's $23bn (first raise since 1971), Meta's $37.5bn underwritten (plus a ~$60bn ATM planned), Oracle's $19.9bn ATM and $10bn follow-on, Super Micro's $5.6bn, Cerebras' $5.55bn IPO, Nebius's $2.8bn ATM. The sell-side framing has flipped from "why would a $127bn-cash company raise?" (Vital Knowledge, 2026-06-01) to "equity first, then credit" (Wells Fargo, 2026-10-22). The bear read is unchanged: "Mag7 morphing from capital-light cash machines into capital-intensive giants — will weigh on multiples regardless of top-line growth" (Vital Knowledge, 2026-06-01, via briefing 2026-06-02).
- **The labs fund compute through their suppliers' balance sheets.** Anthropic's $65.2bn Series H was part-funded by Google ($10bn) and Amazon ($5bn) under note-purchase agreements that are themselves tied to compute milestones; its chip leases are financed by SPVs that lean on Broadcom's residual-value support ($35bn Big Sky closed in June; $63bn North Star committed in October, with ~$21bn of IG bonds mooted for 1Q27 — Bloomberg · Barbuscia, 2026-10-27); OpenAI's March round was completed in the window by NVIDIA, SoftBank (via an $11.1bn HY bond) and Amazon ($21.3bn), while its next round shrank from "$30bn at $1.4tn" to a first close below $20bn at $1.25tn pre (FT via CNBC, 2026-10-31). NVIDIA's $105bn of conditional SB Energy guarantees and Amazon's $80bn supplier facility show the same pattern from the vendor side. Air Street's RVG tally and MS's ">$3tn off-balance-sheet" count (State of AI Report 2026, 2026-10-08, on the compute-deal timeline) are the aggregate frame; this register keeps the contingent amounts out of every total.
- **Neoclouds and their landlords are the marginal borrowers, and the market now prices them by offtaker.** CoreWeave priced $20.4bn of its own paper (two DDTLs, a USD/EUR bond pair, a convertible) and its landlords $9.3bn of project bonds (Core Scientific, Applied Digital, Prime, Galaxy); Nebius $9.3bn plus a $3.5bn Citi facility; Nscale $3.1bn of converts and $3.05bn of GPU facilities. The same physical asset prices 300–600bp wider with CoreWeave as tenant than with Amazon (MS Global Credit Strategy, 2026-08-20: APLD 8.2% / CORZ 11.5% / ELKGVP 12.0% / EDGCOM 14.2% vs STNGRY 7.9–8.8%); Microsoft's lease covenant prices ~173–184bp inside its landlords' paper (Jefferies FI trading desk · Haber, 2026-09-14). Nebius's counter is customer prepayments: >$9bn of 2026 prepayments covering 50–60% of the associated capex (Q2 letter, 2026-08-12); UBS's version is that 50–60% of Nebius capex is self-funded by the customer vs ~22–26% at Oracle (UBS · Keirstead/Giuditta, 2026-09-22).
- **Data-centre project bonds keyed to roster tenants: $31.6bn in the window** (nine rows) against Barclays' ABS/CMBS universe of >$66bn outstanding at 7/31 ($18bn YTD) and a $180bn-by-YE2028 forecast with QTS at 26% of the market (Barclays FICC/Credit, 2026-08-20). The Google backstop is a complex, not a one-off: five Fluidstack-tenant bonds totalling $15.18bn (WULF, CIFR, FLASHC, MERIDI, HUTRBA — all pre-window), and Google's own 10-Q now carries $48.0bn of executed third-party backstops (2026-10-29).

### 2. Pricing across the six months

- **IG: concessions up, coupons up for the weakest credit, secondary mixed.** Alphabet's new-issue concession went from −3/+3bp (February) to +13/+15bp on every tranche (August) and Amazon paid double-digit concessions on both its USD deals (Barclays, 2026-08-20) — yet Alphabet's August bonds tightened 5bp after pricing while the hyperscaler complex was ~30bp wider YTD (MS Credit, 2026-08-20). NVIDIA's 424B5 (2026-06-15) states prices to public of 99.63–99.957 and no reoffer spreads; the press marks of T+35/50/65 on the 2031/2036/2056 are single-source and unconfirmed and SpaceX a 5.855% weighted coupon with a ~$85–90bn book (SpaceX 8-K 2026-06-23). Oracle's November coupons (7.00%–8.65%) are the window's clearest sign that the market now differentiates by issuer, not by sector: its February 2031s moved from T+115 at issue to 7.34% / OAS 263bp (official workbook, 2026-11-03), and S&P cut it to BBB- on 2026-07-09 (workbook dossier). Index levels: US IG OAS 84bp (10-16) → 92bp (11-02); US HY OAS 324bp (10-14) → 340bp (10-30) (credit-alerts lane, Bloomberg LUACOAS/LF98OAS).
- **Project bonds: priced at 6–7% in May–July for IG-tenant paper, 9.875% for CoreWeave-tenant paper by late July, and most of it is now 100–400bp wider.** Issue coupon → 2026-11-03 workbook YTM/OAS: Stingray/Amazon 6.00% → 7.24%/248bp; Beacon Point/NVIDIA 6.129% (T+165) → 7.09%/235bp; QTS Odyssey/Microsoft 7.228% yield → 7.46%/255bp; Sopaipilla/Meta 7.534% → 7.64%/255bp; Yondr/Oracle 6.875% → 9.50%/503bp; APLD ComputeCo 3/CoreWeave 7.00% → 9.02%/446bp; Core Scientific/CoreWeave 7.75% → 9.73%/515bp; Elk Grove/CoreWeave 7.50% → 9.12%/460bp; Galaxy/CoreWeave 9.875% → 12.23%/752bp; Zenith Arc/Jane Street 8.875% → 13.27%/857bp. MS's rule of thumb — HY data-centre deals get pulled near ~10% — is the workbook's amber line (workbook notes). Google's own bonds are +10–20bp since June while HY project bonds leased to Google-backed tenants are +40–200bp (Richardson @ UBS 'AI's Credit Stress Test' call, 2026-10-26 — spoken, basis unstated).
- **Loans: the wrap decides the spread.** Amazon's $17.5bn term loan at SOFR+62.5–87.5bp (10-Q); CoreWeave DDTL 4.0 (Meta-backed) S+225 → DDTL 5.0 S+450 → DDTL 5.5 S+550; Nebius's MUFG asset-backed loan S+250 (07-10) and Citi facility S+275 rising to +350 (10-22); Nscale GPU facilities S+237.5 (08-27); IREN's Microsoft-offtake DDTL S+213/225 (MS/Barclays trackers, Aug); North Star's initial senior talk S+150/187.5 with Broadcom support (IFR, 2026-10-10 via relays). The practitioner read is that chipmaker/hyperscaler credit wrappers "have fallen out of favor" and IG parties now sign the lease and assign it (John Day, CleanArc @ UBS expert call, 2026-10-23 — private developer, not research), while Google moved to limited guarantees of ~80% of the construction loan about a year ago (Jeff Moerdler, Haynes Boone @ Bernstein webinar, 2026-10-20 — practitioner, not research).
- **Converts and equity-linked paper stayed cheap in coupon, expensive in structure:** Robinhood 0% (June, 60–65% premium), Nebius 0.50%/4.50% accreting to 110%/125% (effective ≈3.3%/≈7.5% — Redburn, 2026-09-21), CoreWeave 2.875% (September), Snowflake 0% (refinancing), Cipher 0% (October); Alphabet's and Meta's mandatory preferreds printed at 6.25% and 6.50%, Super Micro's at 7.00%. Equity discounts widened as the window went on: Intel −6.5% (August), Oracle −11.2% (November).

### 3. Pulled, delayed, reshaped, upsized

- **Upsized:** SpaceX notes $20bn→$25bn and IPO over-allotment exercised; Alphabet $80bn→$84.75bn (common $15bn→$18bn, preferred $15bn→$16.75bn); Intel $15bn→$23.0bn gross; Nebius converts $4.5bn→$5.0bn plus $0.75bn options; CoreWeave convert $3.0bn proposed → $3.7bn + $0.5bn option; Snowflake $3.5bn→$3.75bn; Meta common $22.5bn→$25bn; Amazon's sterling debut enlarged even as orders fell off (Bloomberg, 2026-09-09); Dell's refinancing bond launched at ~$4bn on 09-09 and completed at $5.0bn (four tranches 5.10%–5.90%, 2029–2037; Dell 8-K and counsel announcement 2026-09-21).
- **Reshaped or cut:** QTS Odyssey marketed to HY as a $5.4bn package ($4.9bn bond + $500m TL, five facilities), cut to three facilities and relaunched IG at $3.9bn (Barclays Credit Digest, 2026-09-16); OpenAI's round from ≥$30bn at $1.4tn to a sub-$20bn first close at $1.25tn (FT, 2026-10-31); Oracle's preliminary 424B2 carried a 2029 floating-rate tranche that was dropped at pricing (SEC filing vs the 11-02 release).
- **Delayed or stuck:** Nscale's roadshow "could slip" from October (WSJ via TMT Breakout, 2026-09-30) and had not priced by 11-03; Anthropic's S-1/A (11-02) still carries a blank price range after "chatter" of a listing before the midterms (Jefferies · Favuzza, 2026-09-07); SB Energy delayed its IPO on valuation pushback and Oura postponed (same WSJ relay); OpenAI deferred its autumn IPO to 2027 (FT, 2026-09-18); Meta's Class C ATM waits for the 2027-01-19 effective date.
- **Marked down after pricing:** Zenith Arc (99.5 at issue → ~91.5 by 30-Sep on fund marks → 84.60 / 13.27% on 11-03) after the county denied the floodplain permit without prejudice; Oracle's Jupiter bank loan sold below 90c by a lender (WSJ, 2026-09-25); CoreWeave's unsecureds to 80–87c and CDS above 1,000bp on 14–16 Oct, dragging Galaxy and Core Scientific paper with them; SpaceX's 2056s at 82.43 (official workbook). CoreWeave's DDTL 5.5 priced into "one of the most dislocated weeks for credit this year" and was completed at full size (CFO, Q2 call 2026-08-11); the debt markets are "softening a bit on leverage" (Lipschultz, Blue Owl Q3 call, 2026-10-29).
