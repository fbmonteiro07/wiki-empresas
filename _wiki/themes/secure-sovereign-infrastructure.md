**① 🔴 THE DEFINITION, IN MS's OWN WORDS — AND IT IS DELIBERATELY *NOT* AUTARKY, WHICH IS THE PART MOST READINGS GET WRONG.** *"AI sovereignty reflects a country's ambition to maintain meaningful control over the AI stack, albeit through **A COMBINATION OF DOMESTIC CAPACITY AND TRUSTED PARTNERS RATHER THAN FULL SELF-SUFFICIENCY**. That includes reliable access to **compute, energy, data, talent, models, and critical supply chains**, while reducing exposure to technologies, providers, or legal jurisdictions viewed as strategically vulnerable."* AI now *"classifies as **strategic infrastructure where dependence on foreign or adversarial providers can itself be a vulnerability**"* — with countries *"prioritizing national security (and AI-related national security) over economic efficiencies."*
- ⚠️🔴 **HOLD THIS AGAINST THE [[PLTR]] DEFINITION AT THE HEAD OF THIS PAGE (Aug 19), BECAUSE THE TWO ARE MEASURING DIFFERENT THINGS AND THE PAGE NOW CARRIES BOTH. Palantir's sovereignty is CUSTOMER-level and portability-based — control of the decision system, with freedom to swap the model or the infrastructure. MS's sovereignty is NATION-level and jurisdiction-based — domestic and trusted-partner capacity in compute, energy, data, talent, models and supply chains. Neither is wrong; they are the enterprise and the state versions of the same anxiety, and they imply different buy lists. Palantir's version sells a control layer that makes the underlying stack fungible; MS's version sells more stacks. ➤ THE TESTABLE DIFFERENCE: if MS is right that "compute becomes LESS globally fungible" (see ④), the value of Palantir-style portability rises precisely because the substrate is being fragmented — the two theses are complements, not substitutes.**

- **The definition, from Palantir's second Sovereignty Bootcamp in New York (Deutsche Bank · Brad Zelnick, Buy, TP $200, 2026-08-19): sovereignty = the customer retains control of the broader DECISION SYSTEM — its data, business logic, permissions, workflows and ultimately outcomes — WHILE REMAINING FREE TO CHANGE the underlying models or infrastructure.** The **Ontology** is the unified control layer that binds those elements so institutional knowledge stays protected and compounds inside the customer's environment. 🔴 **Note how much narrower this is than "sovereign AI" as normally used on this page: it is not about national data residency or domestic compute — it is about MODEL AND INFRASTRUCTURE PORTABILITY as the definition of control.**
- 🔴 **The product that operationalises it, and the mechanism is specific: AIP EVOLVE dynamically optimises AI workflows across QUALITY, COST, LATENCY AND CONTROL — testing frontier, open-weight ([[NVDA]] Nemotron highlighted multiple times) and proprietary models against a customer's hierarchical needs, then rerouting workloads, rewriting prompts, or REPLACING UNNECESSARY MODEL CALLS WITH DETERMINISTIC CODE. DB's term: "MODEL LIQUIDITY." AutoTune, inside Evolve, fine-tunes models using the governed feedback the workflow itself generates — so proprietary knowledge stays in the customer's environment and sensitive context is not repeatedly exposed to an outside provider.**
- 🔴 **The cost datapoint is the one that gives the whole sovereignty argument commercial teeth: in one customer demo an OPEN-WEIGHT model operated at "1/70th the cost and SIX TIMES the speed" of frontier models, with the customer continuing to fine-tune it toward its quality target.** ⚠️ **A vendor-selected demo at a company-run event, not an independent benchmark — but it is the sharpest number this wiki carries on open-weight substitution, and it makes "sovereignty" a cost argument rather than only a governance one.**

## What it is / why it matters
AI spend is broadening from hyperscaler accelerators into cybersecurity, hardware-backed identity, private/hybrid infrastructure and sovereign capacity. The core read-through is not “public cloud is dead”; it is that predictable AI workloads and high-value proprietary data can justify private or on-prem deployments for cost, control, latency and sovereignty, while elastic workloads remain in public cloud (Citrini Semis, 2026-07-20).

## Key debates
- **Bull / where the money is:** the marginal buyer of merchant AI infrastructure broadens to enterprises, sovereigns, governments, neoclouds and regulated industries; security and physical trust layers become more valuable as AI lowers the cost of attacking systems (Citrini Semis, 2026-07-20).
- **Bear / what could break it:** repatriation can remain a partial optimization rather than a volume shift; public cloud retains the advantage for variable workloads; OEM server economics may remain thin; and weak vendor execution can overwhelm a sound theme (Citrini Semis, 2026-07-20).
- **🆕★ NEW AXIS (2026-09-01) — DOES SOVEREIGN AI PAY THE SHAREHOLDERS OF THE COMPANIES THAT BUILD IT? THE FIRST BEAR CASE ON THIS PAGE THAT IS NOT ABOUT DEMAND, BUT ABOUT WHO CAPTURES IT.** (SemiAnalysis · Max Kan / Ray Wang / Dylan Patel, 2026-09-01 — **independent research house, no rating or PT**.) Every prior framing on this page treats sovereignty as *"a larger and longer-lasting global AI capex cycle"* (MS, 08-19) — i.e. unambiguously good for the suppliers. SemiAnalysis argues the opposite can hold at the LISTED-ENTITY level: **the strategic benefit accrues to the GROUP while the cost lands on the SUBSIDIARY whose shares you own.** The stated mechanism: **SK Group needs [[NVDA]] GPU allocation to deliver its 5GW + 10GW obligation, so *"there is an implicit understanding whereby SK Hynix offers a sweetheart deal on HBM to Nvidia, and Nvidia then gives SK Group priority GPU allocation"*** — in a market where *"it is the SUPPLIERS who hold the power"* and *"if Nvidia doesn't want to pay up, there are plenty of other customers who would absorb any available supply."* SemiAnalysis's conclusion is blunt: ***"we believe SK Hynix has left money on the table… While the strategic benefits flow to SK Group, this nevertheless comes at the expense of SK Hynix's profitability,"*** constraining earnings, FCF and therefore the capital available for core memory investment, dividends and buybacks — so *"some shareholders are likely to scrutinize the costs and benefits of the broader SK–Nvidia partnership."* ➤ **THE TESTABLE, GENERALISABLE CLAIM: wherever a sovereign programme is executed through a chaebol/conglomerate structure, the listed supplier inside it can be used as the currency for the group's infrastructure ambitions. That is a governance risk, not a demand risk, and it does not show up in any TAM. ⚠️ SCOPE: the per-Gb HBM4 pricing estimates and the SK hynix-vs-Samsung margin-gap work that underpin this argument belong to the memory company pages ([[SKHYNIX]], [[SAMSUNG]], [[MU]]) and are deliberately NOT reproduced here — this page carries the STRUCTURAL argument only.**
- **Portfolio framing:** favor vendors that combine an installed hardware position with recurring software/services, or that make workloads portable across private, public and on-prem environments (Citrini Semis, 2026-07-20).
