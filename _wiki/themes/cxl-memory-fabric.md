## What it is / why it matters

**Compute Express Link (CXL)** is a cache-coherent interconnect standard (PCIe physical layer) that lets CPUs, GPUs, and accelerators share a *pooled* memory fabric — DRAM or flash — outside the GPU package. For AI workloads the payoff is large: KV-cache, embeddings, and model weights can be offloaded to cheaper DRAM tiers while HBM stays dedicated to active compute, breaking the per-chip memory wall. The AI memory bottleneck is shifting from HBM allocation to *fabric architecture*, which makes CXL a front-burner theme for whoever owns the silicon/controller, switch/retimer, and module/integration layers.

## State of play (latest)

**🆕 2026-08-09 — HBF finally gets a SPEC, and the tiering thesis gets its clearest sell-side statement yet (J.P. Morgan · Jay Kwon / Sangsik Lee / Neelay Kamath / Harlan Sur / Mio Shikanai, "Global Memory TAM and S&D update", 2026-08-09, from Flash Memory Summit 2026).** This page has carried HBF since June as an architectural direction sourced largely from X; JPM puts numbers on it and places it in the hierarchy.
- **The gating diagnosis:** the AI *"memory wall"* is *"still a primary bottleneck"* — driven by **longer context length, KV cache, agentic workloads** and the **memory cost burden (both DRAM and HBM)** — producing *"accelerating interest in lower cost memory/storage tiers."* **Cost, not just bandwidth, is now named as the driver of tiering.**
- **★ HBF sized: a new tier BETWEEN HBM and SSD — stacked NAND (8-hi / 16-hi) targeting up to 512GB capacity at ~0.4–3TB/s bandwidth.** For calibration against the tiers this page tracks: that is **roughly an order of magnitude more capacity than an HBM stack at roughly an order of magnitude less bandwidth**, sitting exactly in the gap the KV-cache offload argument has been pointing at. **The gating factor is explicitly NOT silicon — it is "ecosystem/software enablement."**
- **Disaggregation gets named as an architecture, not a workaround:** *"system-level disaggregation of compute-intensive **prefill (HBM XPU)** vs. memory-intensive **decode (HBM and/or HBF)**"* — the memory-side statement of the prefill/decode split this wiki tracks on [tokenmaxxing](tokenmaxxing.md) and in the AMD↔Cerebras / NVDA+Groq alliances on [custom-asic-tpu](custom-asic-tpu.md).
- **Two NVIDIA storage paths named:** **SCADA (GPU-direct-to-eSSD)** and **CMX (GPU→DPU→eSSD)**, *"aim[ing] to optimize latency/bandwidth by workload."* Read-through to [../STX.md](../STX.md), [../SNDK.md](../SNDK.md), [../KIOXIA.md](../KIOXIA.md) — and note this is a second, independent sighting of the NVDA KV-cache-to-SSD offload already on this page via Seagate.
- **JPM's net, which is the two-sided version:** *"inference memory constraints should lift demand for DRAM (**via CXL**) and potentially more NAND for near-GPU memory and high-speed storage"* — **a clear positive for NAND-based solutions (eSSD and HBF), a MODEST positive for conventional DRAM (DDRx/LPDDRx), specifically via CXL memory pooling/offload.** So the tiering trade is asymmetric: NAND is the primary beneficiary, CXL-attached DRAM the secondary one.
- **⚠️ The competitive subtext, which JPM states plainly elsewhere in the note and which reframes HBF's purpose:** with **YMTC's bit/wafer "largely on par with the leading memory makers"**, JPM expects the leading NAND makers *"to focus on manufacturing high-end NAND (SLC-based NAND or HBF) to **widen the gap**."* **HBF is therefore partly a defensive product against Chinese NAND parity, not purely an AI-demand product** — a motive this page had not previously recorded. See [china-export](china-export.md).
- **Supply-side caveat JPM attaches:** its NAND bit/incremental-capex productivity trend *"does not reflect the potential supply crunch from rising SLC/HBF (based on SLC-stacked memory) and there is a chance for the number to decline eventually"* — i.e. **an HBF ramp is bit-dilutive to NAND supply**, because SLC stacking spends far more wafer per usable bit. Directionally supportive of NAND pricing, and not in most NAND supply models.

Three datapoints converged in May–June 2026 to push CXL to the front burner:

- **Google is reportedly designing an MPU (Memory Processing Unit) with Marvell** specifically to access CXL-pooled DRAM, alongside a High-Bandwidth Fabric (HBF) — the first confirmed hyperscaler CXL co-design at scale (@zephyr_z9, 2026-06-02; @ParadisLabs, 2026-06-03). The co-design was corroborated via multiple Computex sources — "memory architecture is bifurcating" — with read-through to SNDK (NAND bull), MU (HBM + NAND), Samsung/SKH (HBM), MRVL (CXL-pooled DRAM architecture) (@ParadisLabs, 2026-06-03), and again by @zephyr_z9 ("structural AI memory architecture bet," 2026-06-03).
- **NVDA is already offloading KV-cache to SSDs via Seagate (STX)**, with weights staying in HBM — the same architectural instinct that tiered memory reduces cost without sacrificing active bandwidth (@zephyr_z9, 2026-06-02).
- **Marvell's FY29 revenue guide explicitly includes a CXL/NIC bucket of ~$1B** as part of the XPU attach pool. Full custom breakdown: **MSFT ~$4–5B (Maia300), Trainium ~$3B, XPU attach ~$3B (CXL $1B + NIC $1B + other $1B)**; interconnect growing faster than cloud capex in FY28 (Barclays / Tom O'Malley, OW, PT $275, 2026-05-28).
- Google's **CXL/DPU attach content/rate per TPU higher than expected** — a positive revision to MRVL attach revenue assumptions (@sssjeffpu / Jeff Pu, 2026-06-16). MRVL v10ax custom TPU bidding still ongoing; MAIA300 on track ~300K units 2027; AWS Trainium 4 bidding in play — CXL attach incremental on top (@sssjeffpu, 2026-06-16).
- **Astera Labs (ALAB):** "PCIe switch = NVSwitch for the open ecosystem," content per XPU growing from ~$1,000, UAL first deployments 2027 / ramp 2028, **CXL revenue starting 2027** (MS / Joseph Moore, bus tour, 2026-06-15).
- **Penguin Solutions (PENG):** +19% on AI memory infra demand; CEO "demand for memory and AI infrastructure continues to grow"; **CXL as moonshot bet** on future memory architecture; described as "most compelling high-beta bet on memory systems"; DDR5 + CXL positioning (@ParadisLabs, 2026-06-03).
- **Innodisk (Taiwan-listed CXL module maker):** Q1 rev **$420M (+403% YoY)**, GM 59%, NM 41%, EPS $1.84 vs $0.69 FY25; also NVDA Jetson Thor distributor; market cap <$5B (@ParadisLabs, 2026-05-11).
- **NAND-SSD-for-KV$ extension: Nvidia's ICMS + Bluefield-4 "Context Memory Storage Platform" names the concrete hardware behind the NVDA→SSD KV-cache offload instinct already tracked above (Vikram Sekar/Substack, 2026-01-18 — free-preview only, paywall-limited; SemiAnalysis "CPUs are Back," 2026-02-09).** Sekar's CES 2026 read: Nvidia's **ICMS (Inference Context Memory Storage)** platform lets NAND SSDs expand KV-cache storage well beyond HBM/DRAM limits — framed explicitly as *not* a novel technology, since **WEKA (Val Bercovici, Chief AI Officer, the piece's technical reviewer), VAST Data, and Dell already sell external KV-cache storage systems**; the point is that the tokenomics impact (agentic workloads run ~100x more tokens than human use; a KV-cache miss costs ~10x a hit) is under-covered relative to those vendors' existing footprint. SemiAnalysis's harder technical detail (pp.69-70) names the shipping silicon: **Bluefield-4**, a DPU co-packaging a full Grace CPU (64 enabled cores, 128GB LPDDR5) with a ConnectX-9 chip, deployed as the frontend NIC on every **Rubin** compute tray — four BF4s populate Nvidia's own-branded **"Context Memory Storage Platform"** for KV-cache offload to high-speed NAND (CES 2026 claim: "5X Higher Tokens per Second, 5X Higher Power Efficiency"). The partner ecosystem Nvidia lists is materially wider than the CXL dossier's current module/integration-layer coverage: **AIC, Cloudian, DDN, Dell, HPE, Hitachi Vantara, IBM, Nutanix, Pure Storage, Supermicro, VAST, WEKA** — i.e. Nvidia's platform now competes with *and* incorporates the same WEKA/VAST/Dell vendors Sekar cites as pre-existing KV-cache storage players. SemiAnalysis frames this NAND-based KV-cache tier as a **"third network"** being added to AI topology (alongside East-West GPU scale-out and North-South storage/internet), with Bluefield-4 blurring "the line between a NIC with CPU compute and a CPU with networking acceleration... even more." Also flagged: continued LPDDR adoption in the datacenter (LPDDR6 SOCAMM modules for higher bandwidth density at higher capacity than on-package memory) and the possible return of HBM-on-CPU (last seen in Sapphire Rapids Xeon Max, AMD MI300C) — both adjacent to this dossier's HBM/DRAM-tiering thesis (SemiAnalysis "CPUs are Back: The Datacenter CPU Landscape in 2026," p.70, 2026-02-09). ## Key debates

- **Bull / where the money is:** CXL becomes the default memory fabric for the next generation of hyperscaler AI clusters. HBM stays tightly allocated to active compute; CXL-attached DRAM expands *effective* memory capacity 4–8× per node at a fraction of HBM cost. Marvell wins the silicon/controller layer; Astera Labs the switch/retimer layer; Penguin Solutions and Innodisk the module/integration layer. TAM is proportional to hyperscaler capex expansion — and with memory reaching 36.2% of hyperscaler capex by 2027E, CXL is a structural beneficiary, not a cyclical bet (SemiAnalysis via @ParadisLabs, 2026-05-11 / 2026-06-03).
- **Bear / what could break it:** CXL adoption at hyperscale is materially slower than the hype — Google's MPU co-design with MRVL is early-stage and could be cancelled or substituted. The standard is not yet stable enough for production AI clusters (CXL 3.0 still maturing). Proprietary fabrics (NVLink, UAL) dominate at the GPU-to-GPU layer and may crowd out CXL before it achieves critical mass. ALAB's CXL revenue is not expected until 2027 and could slip further (MS / Joseph Moore, bus tour, 2026-06-15 — "UAL first deployments 2027, ramp 2028").
- **Timeline / inflection points:** Watch when Google's MRVL MPU co-design reaches production (MRVL FY27 vs FY28 CXL revenue), whether CXL 3.0 gets broad enough ISV/hyperscaler adoption to become the default vs UAL/NVLink crowd-out at the cluster layer, and ALAB content-per-XPU verification against future earnings (no Street-wide consensus model yet beyond MS's ~$1,000-and-growing).
