## What it is / why it matters

The AI build-out has turned electricity into the binding constraint on compute. Hyperscaler capex is running toward a **~$1.2T buy-side bogey for 2027** ("could be 1.3, could be 1.4," Jefferies power/hyperscalers, 2026-05-08) — and a data center is useless without firm, 24/7 power to plug into. Cantor frames the supply imbalance bluntly: NVIDIA can ship accelerators faster than anyone can build the buildings to house them — **~40 GW of chips into the US between 2025–2030 vs. ~21 GW of data center capacity built over the prior 25 years** (Cantor/CoreWeave, 2026-06-18). For a buy-sider, this is the rare theme where the bottleneck is physical — turbines, transformers, interconnects, baseload electrons, and the labor to assemble them — so the money concentrates in whoever owns scarce, contractable supply: nuclear/merchant generation (CEG, VST, TLN), gas-turbine OEMs (GEV), EPC labor (PWR, AGX), and gas midstream (WMB). The single biggest risk to the bull case is not demand — it is the grid, the labor pool, and timing.

- **NEW (2026-07-29) — THE CONSTRUCTION LAYER GETS ITS OWN BOTTOM-UP MODEL: 61GW+ OF MODULAR CAPACITY TRACKED, THE LABOR BOTTLENECK QUANTIFIED TRADE BY TRADE, AND A HARD FEDERAL CEILING ON HOW BIG A MODULE CAN LEGALLY BE (SemiAnalysis "The Wild Wild West Of LEGO Datacenters", 2026-07-29).** A 75-page teardown of how AI datacenters are actually built, and the first work on this page that treats *construction method* — not generation, not the grid — as a driver of GW delivered.   - **(1) LABOR IS THE BOTTLENECK "CAPITALIST INCENTIVES ALONE CANNOT BUILD PAST," AND IT IS NOW SIZED.** Electricians are **30-40% of total construction man-hours** on a datacenter project, with an **electrician shortage emerging in 2027**, most acute where the buildout concentrates — **Texas and Ohio**. Per-MW intensity: an AI data hall absorbs **~12,000 field man-hours per MW** during MEP fit-out, so a **50 MW hall concentrates ~600,000 field hours and stacks ~300 craft workers at peak**. Relocating that MEP scope to a factory cuts **on-site hours ~63% to ~4,500/MW and licensed-electrician hours ~85%**. The wage gap is *not* the lever — BLS puts **field construction at $34/hr vs $33/hr in the factory**, but overtime and site premiums lift the **effective field electrician to ~$63/hr**; the real saving is factory throughput. Field evidence already on the tape: **Crusoe pumped wages 30% to pull talent to the Abilene site, which needed 9,000+ workers at peak.**
  - **(2) THE SCHEDULE, BROKEN INTO ITS FOUR BLOCKS — AND PERMITTING SITS OUTSIDE THE CLOCK EVERYONE QUOTES.** A conventional 50 MW building runs **~18-24 months groundbreaking → IT-ready**: groundworks **4-6 months (~5)**, structure and shell **2.5-4.5 (~3.5)**, mechanical-and-electrical fit-out **7-11**, commissioning to IT-ready **3-8 (single-hall midpoint ~4.5)**. **Permitting adds another 12-13 months that cannot overlap construction**, taking the all-in to **~30-35+ months for a stick build vs ~24-30 for modular**. Full modular compresses the construction window to **~12-18 months — ~36% faster than a pure stick build and ~30% faster than today's partly-modular baseline**; MEP-skids-only lands **~17 months**; all-in-one prefab blocks or containerized DCs can get to **~12 months**. SemiAnalysis flags that aggressive "6-9 month" claims are partly a **counting convention** (starting the clock at electrical fit-out or vertical works rather than groundworks) and partly **compressed commissioning**, which "significantly shifts risk into operations."
  - **(3) SPEED IS WORTH MORE THAN THE CAPEX SAVING, AND THE $/MW/MONTH NUMBERS ARE THE USEFUL PART.** Capex savings are real but small: full modular on a 50 MW liquid-cooled US hall costs **~$1.1mn/MW less all-in — ~$13.5mn vs ~$14.6mn/MW, just under ~8%** (moving MEP to the factory saves ~$0.6mn/MW of construction services and ~$0.5mn/MW of installation). On an ~8-month lead that is **~$200mn undiscounted across a 50 MW hall, ~$4mn/MW**. **The governing caveat, which belongs on this page more than anywhere: "go-live is the latest of three dates, building ready, power available, and GPUs delivered, and accelerating the building earns nothing if it was not the date that bound."** Against the savings, the largest structural penalty is a **double margin** — a module-vendor margin stacked on the site integrator's — which compresses only where the OEM also integrates on site (Vertiv, Schneider on turnkey programs); Schneider's own WP163 puts **module hardware ~40% above traditional, netting to 13% lower first cost** after design and install-labor savings.
  - **(4) ⚠️ THE UNDERRATED STRUCTURAL CONSTRAINT: US FEDERAL LAW CAPS THE MODULE. "Federal law fixes the no-permit envelope at 102 inches wide and 80,000 pounds gross, leaving about 24 tons for the module on a standard deck."** Designs that ride inside a **40-foot ISO container at 96 inches wide** — Microsoft's Azure Modular Datacenter and Schneider's Easy Modular are the two examples given — **cross any state line or fly in a C-17 permit-free**. Favors electrical names (Eaton), Delta (in-rack power), and the AMD/MediaTek/Marvell ASIC leg. A useful low-end bookend to the Mizuho ~$50B/GW and MS ~$11-19mn/MW marks already on this page. [Source](../../relat%C3%B3rios%20bons/Bernstein_AI_DC_value_chain_2026.html)
- **Power demand inflecting after two flat decades.** Bernstein models **+3% US power-demand CAGR to 2030** (range heard 2.5–4.5%), with data-center electricity roughly doubling from **~250 TWh to ~500 TWh** by 2030 on ~40 GW of incremental DC capacity (Bernstein power names, 2026-06-18). VST sees ERCOT load +5–6% and PJM +2–3% to 2030 (VST Q1 FY26, 2026-05-07).
- **Gas is the bridge; nuclear/geothermal the only long-run baseload displacement.** Survey of 50+ DC operators: **98% prefer grid (or grid + on-site backup)**, with reliability/uptime and speed-to-power the deciding factors — and "three years out is effectively unacceptable" (Bernstein, 2026-06-18). Behind-the-meter stays a **5–10% niche** (more expensive than grid), not the 50% some bulls model (Bernstein, 2026-06-18).
- **Nuclear is the scarce premium product.** CEG runs the largest US nuclear fleet (~22 GW, 96.8% capacity factor) with ~900–1,000 MW of uprates identified and the Crane (ex-Three Mile Island) restart progressing (CEG Q1 FY26, 2026-05-11). Industry-wide turbine capacity ramps 55 GW (2025) → 87 GW (2030), yet the US is still "~10 GW of gas short every year" (GEVernova preview, 2026-04-17).
- **Labor is now the #1 constraint.** Both Jefferies desks: labor — not turbines — is the gating factor; projects booked "three years in the future"; construction EPC names (Quanta, MasTec) seeing "shocking positive revisions," with Quanta's near-term growth talked up toward ~30% (Jefferies, 2026-05-08).

No new company-page action — this is confirmatory of the existing capex-wall framing, and the META/AMZN/GOOGL capacity figures are already logged on those tickers' pages.
- **Satellite build-tracking: ~14–16 GW (2026), ~20 GW (2027) — far below the announced pipeline.** SinMax/Industrial Info's "Vulcan" platform (weekly satellite imagery + 500-analyst phone verification) tracks **11 GW built in 2025 → ~14–16 GW in 2026 → ~20 GW in 2027** (vs. ~40–45 GW *proposed* for 2027 — a **~50% haircut**, ~the same ~60% realization rate seen in renewables). - **The behind-the-meter power-architecture expert view (Aman Chakri / ex-GE Vernova).** Gas "will be king" five years out; BTM is a **permanent** solution (not just a bridge) because connecting hyperscalers to the grid raises rates on the public and ISOs "pick their battles." Cost is ~the same BTM vs front-of-meter — **the variance is *time*, not cost** (interconnection takes 4–6 yrs). Installed all-in cost **~$2,900–3,200/kW (BTM), ~$3,000–4,000/kW** for aero/recip; technology cost alone >$2,000/kW (aero ~$2,200/kW). AI loads need **~10-millisecond response** that **no turbine/engine can meet** — so every architecture pairs gas with **UPS + battery storage** as a buffer (the developer claim of "no batteries needed" is a "flawed argument"). DCs spec **simple-cycle (not combined-cycle)** for fast start; **N+5 / ~40% overbuild** (install ~140 MW for 100 MW of demand) for 99.999% uptime; one large frame turbine beats 10 small aeros on $/kW except at high elevation (Wells Fargo "Aman Chakri BTM expert", 2026-05-27).
- **DC broker on the ground (Chris, DC broker/advisor).** ~**16 GW** of tracked demand, of which **~40% is firm for 2026–28** (rest 2029–31); **>50% of new AI-DC development goes international past 2028** (US lacks power/capacity); SE Asia/Canada/South America the beneficiaries. **~30% of his pipeline is true grid-islanded BTM**, never intending to connect. ## Key debates

- **Bull / where the money is:** Demand is real, supply is static, and the scarce assets re-rate. CEG: ~$0.75T of data-center capital projected — "twice the size of the 3 largest listed US utilities" — and CEG sells "the product no one else has ready: firm, clean, 24/7 power, available now" (CEG Q3 FY25 / Q1 FY26). GEV is "an earnings-revisions machine," up off pace of EBITDA growth, with Electrification flipping from "peaking" to accelerating — TAM doubled $150B→$300B in a month and the $200B total-backlog target pulled forward to '27 (GEVernova callback, 2026-04-22). WMB: pipelines lag gas-gen, and "Williams is the ultimate manifestation of this thesis," compounding via 5x-build-multiple behind-the-meter gas for data centers (JSD via WMB page, 2026-05-06). PWR is the "pick-and-shovel" labor layer targeting >2x earnings power by 2030 (15–20% EPS CAGR) (PWR Q4 FY25, 2026-02-19).
- **Bear / what could break it:** Timing and slippage. Data-center delays are real — EIA, ERCOT and PJM all cut 2026 demand forecasts; "~50% of data centers delayed / canceled" headlines, though Jefferies calls this "normal course" (Jefferies, 2026-05-08). The big hyperscaler nuclear deals keep slipping (CEG promised announcements that haven't landed; some conversations "paused" on PJM regulatory clarity) (CEG Q1 FY26). Management teams are more cautious than the tape: VST says meaningful DC tightening doesn't begin until "late 2027 or early 2028" and load growth is "not at the extremely elevated levels… forecast by many third parties" (VST Q4 FY25). GEV order-peak risk is live — Q4'25's 24 GW / $22.2B order quarter is the comp to beat (GEVernova callback). Bernstein/Cantor procurement expert thinks the build pace is "plateauing" — "I think last year was the peak," with power and people the two gates (Bernstein DC buildout, 2026-06-11). And policy/rate-payer politics bite in an election year: PJM bills rose ~$20/mo, IPPs that signed rate-payer-protection pledges can't contract existing resources, and IPPs "are not participating in the rally" (Jefferies, 2026-05-08).
- **Timeline / inflection points:** Behind-the-meter and gas-gen scramble *now* (band-aid, 5–10% of market); large H-class turbines stay under-supplied through 2030 (relative positive), while diesel gensets and small on-site gen move to over-supply by 2027–2029 (Bernstein power names, 2026-06-18). Nuclear restarts/uprates land 2027–2034. Grid/electrification is the multi-decade compounder beyond 2030 (Bernstein, 2026-06-18). The cleanest near-term tell on supply: track the chip ramp and the order/backlog cadence at GEV (Q2 FY26 print, ~late July).

**Read-through — orbital data centers (not a near-term escape valve):** with terrestrial DCs power/water/permitting-constrained, "space compute" has moved from sci-fi into US-equity discussion (cheaper launch via Starship/reusables). FundaAI's expert work argues the binding constraint is *not* launch cost but **heat rejection**: in vacuum there's no convection, so cooling is radiation-only — a 1 GW orbital DC implies ~0.5–1.5 km² of radiator + ~2.9 km² of solar + ~2,000–3,000 kg/s coolant flow, dozens of launches, in-orbit assembly, and finite (~7–10 yr) asset life. Verdict: a long-dated *option* (1 MW demo plausible soon; GW-class far off), to be de-risked by engineering milestones, not modeled as near-term capacity.
