## What it is / why it matters
Meta's Muse (launched September 2026), Microsoft's new Copilot super-app with Autopilot, and the consumer agent the Street expects from OpenAI at DevDay are the first agent products with mass distribution. Morgan Stanley calls Muse "the most credible agentic threat we have seen to date" because "the product capability is paired with Meta's consumer data advantage" — prior agents "have had little measurable impact on commerce or advertising" (Morgan Stanley · Simeon Gutman / Brian Nowak et al. "Muse: Rewriting the Retail/Consumer Playbook", 2026-09-28, PAGE 1). MS distils the impact into two vectors: **"The transaction is defensible; discovery is not"** — retailers with price, speed and assortment keep the checkout, but advertising dollars "will need to follow the consumer to these new interfaces", so **retail media is the more meaningful source of disruption** (MS "Muse", 2026-09-28, PAGE 1). Stratechery's framing is structural: an agent "is an AI that has access to a computer", Meta is provisioning every US user a virtual machine, and once doing things becomes abundant "what is scarce is volition" — the companies that solve inspiration "will gain power over every entity that has things that need to be done", with apps reduced to "mere suppliers facing the fate of publications under Aggregators" (Stratechery · Ben Thompson "Apps, Agents, and Aggregation", 2026-09-28, PAGE 3-6). For the capex chain, Jefferies' summit conclusion is that personal agents "could accelerate AI adoption in both consumer AND business users, and extend capex spend for many years" (Jefferies · Brent Thill et al. "AI Summit Recap: Still Early with Demand Outstripping Supply", 2026-09-24, PAGE 1), and Goldman's Internet team reads Muse as "a potential paradigm shift from conversational AI canvases to more agentic-driven action ones", monetised later through subscription, advertising and commerce (Goldman Sachs · Eric Sheridan / Gabriela Borges / Jim Schneider "Sizing the AI Economy Needed to Justify ROIC on Hyperscaler AI Capex", 2026-09-24, PAGE 3).

## State of play (latest)
- 🔴🔴 **THE TWO VECTORS, THE "HANDSHAKE MODEL" AND THE NEAR-ZERO TAKE RATE** (Morgan Stanley · Simeon Gutman / Brian Nowak et al. "Muse: Rewriting the Retail/Consumer Playbook", 2026-09-28, PAGE 1-2, 7-8): on commerce, "relatively low disruption risk" — transactions "will ultimately be won on price, speed, and assortment"; on advertising, "a portion of the advertising dollars currently captured by retailers could migrate toward the agents influencing what consumers discover and ultimately buy", so retailers "may increasingly pay partners or agents directly to influence traffic and conversion". The emerging architecture is a **"handshake"**: retailers deploy their own agents that handshake with the LLM agent, so "the retailer retains the customer data, completes the transaction and preserves the opportunity to deepen the customer relationship" — which MS believes "is taking hold" in Muse's early partnerships. Two moats inside loyalty — **data trust and fulfillment trust** — mean "agents can disrupt discovery more easily than they can disrupt trust", and "owning discovery does not necessarily mean owning the customer". The watch item: **"We understand Muse's initial take rates are near zero, which could accelerate adoption"**; if Muse changes where the shopping journey begins, "it could ultimately challenge our 'commerce' thesis". Risk grid (Exhibit 4): **Commerce — Low; Retail Media — Moderate** ("Retailer may need to cede part of the economics to Muse to capture traffic"). The retail-media mechanism, in MS's words: today a retailer says to a brand "pay me to reach the consumer on my platform"; in an agent-mediated world the agent says to the retailer "pay me to influence or acquire that consumer" — implying margin leakage, more valuable measurement, and scale advantage for the largest retail-media networks (PAGE 8). **For WMT, MS estimates retail media income is over 15% of enterprise-wide earnings** (PAGE 11).

GOOGL could also see some pressure to its effective take rates; **our sensitivity shows a 500bp shift from unpaid to paid traffic can offset a 1,400bp decline in effective take rates, while GOOGL would need ~3,800bp of traffic shift to break even at a 5% take rate**. ## Key debates
- **🆕 Free vs paid — the first competitor test (2026-09-29/30):** OpenAI shipped a paid, work-first agent (Dots) and a new $500/month tier while Muse stays free with usage limits; Barclays' own framing is that the winner is whoever can *"afford to pay for the free compute"* (Barclays desk · Justin Kantrowitz, 2026-09-30, on Sandler's note) and the desks read DevDay as ceding the free consumer lane to META (see [[META]] 09-29/30 log row). Open: whether Google's Gemini 4 line (Argon shipped 09-30 to cyber partners and the US government only — [[GOOG]]) produces a free consumer agent before MS's Nov-Dec test of the multiple.
- **Bull / where the money is:** the transaction stays with whoever has price, speed, assortment and fulfilment — the "handshake" plus data trust and fulfillment trust keep the retailer at the checkout, and Muse becomes an incremental acquisition channel at near-zero cost while take rates are ~0 (Morgan Stanley · Simeon Gutman / Brian Nowak et al. "Muse: Rewriting the Retail/Consumer Playbook", 2026-09-28, PAGE 2, 7-8). The platform owners win either way: MS sees "trillions of spend" enabled long term even if "monetization will be low for quite some time" (PAGE 2); Stratechery says the two companies with the distribution already have the two agents that fit the use case (Stratechery · Ben Thompson "Apps, Agents, and Aggregation", 2026-09-28, PAGE 8); Jefferies ties it to a longer compute cycle (Jefferies · Brent Thill et al. "AI Summit Recap", 2026-09-24, PAGE 1).
- **Bear / what could break it:** discovery and advertising move upstream to the agent, retail media leaks margin and the agent becomes "a new gateway (and potentially a referral fee collector)" (MS "Muse", 2026-09-28, PAGE 8); GOOGL's search take rate is the biggest single pool exposed — needing ~3,800bp of traffic shift to break even at a 5% take rate (PAGE 38); Stratechery's stronger version is that apps and services become "implementation detail[s] … scrapping for crumbs from the Agent, the ultimate gatekeeper of not just user demand, but desire" (Stratechery 2026-09-28, PAGE 6). The counter-bears: trust — BKNG's argument that travellers still book where reliability and service live (Jefferies · John Colantuoni "Takeaways from Skift's 2026 Global Travel Forum", 2026-09-27, PAGE 1) and MacCartney's view that META's trust issues could keep it from being the primary agent (Jefferies AI Summit, 2026-09-24, PAGE 23); and reach — ChatGPT's US audience is smaller than Google, Meta and even Reddit, which caps the ad leg today (Jefferies expert call · Horizon Media, 2026-09-28, PAGE 4). 🆕 Barclays rejects the travel version of the trust defence — Instinct's GMV is 50% travel "despite OTA commentary to the contrary" — and files BKNG / EXPE / TRIP in its higher-risk quadrant, while AMZN, with the most ad income at stake (8.9% of GMV), is the most defended because it is heavily managed and can reprice fees (Barclays · Sandler et al., 2026-09-30, PAGE 8-10).
- **Who becomes THE agent:** Stratechery — one agent per person / company, decided by context and access; Meta (every person) vs Microsoft (every employee) (Stratechery 2026-09-28, PAGE 8). Jefferies' expert — winner-take-one on trust and data access, favouring GOOGL (7 apps, 2B users) and AAPL (Jefferies AI Summit, 2026-09-24, PAGE 23). MS — Meta's first-mover advantage and surging usage are notable, but watch the private labs, GOOGL's Gemini 4 agent in 4Q and an AAPL agent powered by GOOGL (MS "Muse", 2026-09-28, PAGE 2). The Street expects OpenAI's own consumer agent at DevDay (Capstone US Equities "OpenAI DevDay 2026: where the tape is exposed", 2026-09-28 — derived from this wiki, link only, no numbers cited from it). 🆕 Barclays (09-30) — trust, low-cost compute and connectors decide it; GOOGL and META lead on the funding model (ad stacks, $300+ US ARPU), Apple and Google lead on trust, Muse and ChatGPT lead on connectors, and the swing question is whether Apple and OpenAI will spend "$40B+ in extra annual compute" to win at the free tier (Barclays · Sandler et al. "The Battle for the Persistent Consumer AI Agent Begins", 2026-09-30, PAGE 1-2).
- **Timeline / inflection points — catalysts:** **OpenAI DevDay, Tue 29-Sep-2026, keynote 1pm ET** — the consumer-agent and cyber-product expectations are compiled in Capstone's own preview ([OpenAI DevDay 2026: where the tape is exposed](../../relat%C3%B3rios%20bons/OpenAI_DevDay_2026_preview_2026-09-28.html), Capstone US Equities, 2026-09-28; derived artifact, link only). Then: Shop Pay integration into Muse "coming" and AMZN-Muse terms (MS "Muse", 2026-09-28, PAGE 2, 7); GOOGL's "better agent around Gemini 4 in 4Q" (PAGE 2); ABNB's AI search launch "very soon" as the gate to sponsored listings (Jefferies · Colantuoni, 2026-09-27, PAGE 1); the first non-zero Muse take rate (MS, PAGE 2).
