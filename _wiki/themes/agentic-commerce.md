# Theme — Agentic commerce & personal agents (Muse, Copilot, ChatGPT agent)

_Wiki · generated 2026-09-28 · cross-company theme · sources: sell-side research (`relatórios bons`: Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, Jefferies), Stratechery, Capstone call notes (Jefferies expert call), the Capstone DevDay preview (derived artifact, link only). Company pages: [../00_INDEX.md](../00_INDEX.md) · themes index: [00_THEMES.md](00_THEMES.md)._

> **Boundary vs adjacent dossiers.** [tokenmaxxing](tokenmaxxing.md) carries the compute-cost and token-price side of consumer agents (Muse token tiers, 2026-09-25; the GS META model, 2026-09-16); [hyperscaler-capex](hyperscaler-capex.md) carries the capex they justify; [ai-cybersecurity](ai-cybersecurity.md) carries agent identity and attack surface. **This page is the demand-side question:** who owns the consumer's (and the employee's) interface once an agent sits between them and every app, retailer, OTA and search box — and where the transaction, discovery and advertising dollars move.

## What it is / why it matters
Meta's Muse (launched September 2026), Microsoft's new Copilot super-app with Autopilot, and the consumer agent the Street expects from OpenAI at DevDay are the first agent products with mass distribution. Morgan Stanley calls Muse "the most credible agentic threat we have seen to date" because "the product capability is paired with Meta's consumer data advantage" — prior agents "have had little measurable impact on commerce or advertising" (Morgan Stanley · Simeon Gutman / Brian Nowak et al. "Muse: Rewriting the Retail/Consumer Playbook", 2026-09-28, PAGE 1). MS distils the impact into two vectors: **"The transaction is defensible; discovery is not"** — retailers with price, speed and assortment keep the checkout, but advertising dollars "will need to follow the consumer to these new interfaces", so **retail media is the more meaningful source of disruption** (MS "Muse", 2026-09-28, PAGE 1). Stratechery's framing is structural: an agent "is an AI that has access to a computer", Meta is provisioning every US user a virtual machine, and once doing things becomes abundant "what is scarce is volition" — the companies that solve inspiration "will gain power over every entity that has things that need to be done", with apps reduced to "mere suppliers facing the fate of publications under Aggregators" (Stratechery · Ben Thompson "Apps, Agents, and Aggregation", 2026-09-28, PAGE 3-6). For the capex chain, Jefferies' summit conclusion is that personal agents "could accelerate AI adoption in both consumer AND business users, and extend capex spend for many years" (Jefferies · Brent Thill et al. "AI Summit Recap: Still Early with Demand Outstripping Supply", 2026-09-24, PAGE 1), and Goldman's Internet team reads Muse as "a potential paradigm shift from conversational AI canvases to more agentic-driven action ones", monetised later through subscription, advertising and commerce (Goldman Sachs · Eric Sheridan / Gabriela Borges / Jim Schneider "Sizing the AI Economy Needed to Justify ROIC on Hyperscaler AI Capex", 2026-09-24, PAGE 3).

## State of play (latest)
- 🔴🔴 **THE TWO VECTORS, THE "HANDSHAKE MODEL" AND THE NEAR-ZERO TAKE RATE** (Morgan Stanley · Simeon Gutman / Brian Nowak et al. "Muse: Rewriting the Retail/Consumer Playbook", 2026-09-28, PAGE 1-2, 7-8): on commerce, "relatively low disruption risk" — transactions "will ultimately be won on price, speed, and assortment"; on advertising, "a portion of the advertising dollars currently captured by retailers could migrate toward the agents influencing what consumers discover and ultimately buy", so retailers "may increasingly pay partners or agents directly to influence traffic and conversion". The emerging architecture is a **"handshake"**: retailers deploy their own agents that handshake with the LLM agent, so "the retailer retains the customer data, completes the transaction and preserves the opportunity to deepen the customer relationship" — which MS believes "is taking hold" in Muse's early partnerships. Two moats inside loyalty — **data trust and fulfillment trust** — mean "agents can disrupt discovery more easily than they can disrupt trust", and "owning discovery does not necessarily mean owning the customer". The watch item: **"We understand Muse's initial take rates are near zero, which could accelerate adoption"**; if Muse changes where the shopping journey begins, "it could ultimately challenge our 'commerce' thesis". Risk grid (Exhibit 4): **Commerce — Low; Retail Media — Moderate** ("Retailer may need to cede part of the economics to Muse to capture traffic"). The retail-media mechanism, in MS's words: today a retailer says to a brand "pay me to reach the consumer on my platform"; in an agent-mediated world the agent says to the retailer "pay me to influence or acquire that consumer" — implying margin leakage, more valuable measurement, and scale advantage for the largest retail-media networks (PAGE 8). **For WMT, MS estimates retail media income is over 15% of enterprise-wide earnings** (PAGE 11).
- 🔴 **THE PLUMBING: STRIPE LINK, SHOP PAY, SHOPIFY, INSTACART — AND AMAZON'S BLOCK** (MS "Muse", 2026-09-28, PAGE 6-7): "Meta has already built payment capabilities into Muse through **Stripe's Link, with Shop Pay integration coming**, while **Shopify and Instacart are actively integrating** their commerce infrastructure with the agent." Announced retailer partnerships (Exhibit 3): **Walmart, Best Buy, American Eagle Outfitters, DICK'S Sporting Goods, Fanatics, Gap, Michael Kors, Sephora, Ulta Beauty, Wayfair (direct retail connectors), Instacart (grocery-shopping integration)**. "Shopify's willingness to integrate Muse (and **Amazon's decision to block it**) illustrate that retailers retain meaningful control over whether and how third-party agents interact with their ecosystems"; Amazon's block "underscores precisely this tension around control of the shopping experience and associated data". MS nonetheless "expect[s] an eventual agreement with Muse" for AMZN and will focus on "who controls the shopper traffic flow/experience, data sharing, and retail media integration" (PAGE 2). Stratechery has covered the same episode in a member update titled "Amazon Blocks Muse, Amazon's Moat, Aggregator v Aggregator" (2026-09-22) and "More on Muse, Amazon, and Walmart; Muse and Expedia; Whither Google?" (2026-09-23) — titles only in the article on disk (Stratechery · Ben Thompson "Apps, Agents, and Aggregation", 2026-09-28, PAGE 1 sidebar). Elsewhere in the note: **Carrefour is "the first European food retailer to join Google's Universal Commerce Protocol"**; Muse "is not yet available in LatAm, nor is Amazon's Alexa for Shopping (formerly Rufus)", which MS's checks show offered in Europe and India (PAGE 37, 42).
- 🔴🔴 **THE GOOGL TAKE-RATE MATH — QUOTED EXACTLY** (MS "Muse", 2026-09-28, Internet section by Brian Nowak / Matt Cost / Nathan Feather, PAGE 38-40): "We estimate current search 'effective take rates' are **5-10x higher than early agentic offerings**, but acknowledge Muse commission rates could be higher. Note however, that **80%+ of retailer online traffic remains unpaid/direct**. This is a large base of traffic agents could capture and monetize, and we therefore expect unpaid/direct traffic to fall from current levels over time. GOOGL could also see some pressure to its effective take rates; **our sensitivity shows a 500bp shift from unpaid to paid traffic can offset a 1,400bp decline in effective take rates, while GOOGL would need ~3,800bp of traffic shift to break even at a 5% take rate**. For retailers, the agentic take rate transition highlights direct traffic and retail media risks. As agents monetize previously direct traffic, **retailers will need an average of 50% of agentic transactions to be incremental for EBIT breakeven at a ~5% agent fee**." Example from Exhibit 21: "At a ~2% agent fee, EBAY needs ~15% of its agentic purchases to be incremental for EBIT breakeven" (platform agents such as GOOGL, not owned agents such as Sparky; WMT US-only, AMZN NA-only). Near-term unit economics for partners are attractive ("below current marketing expense % of GMV"), with early transaction commissions expected "de minimis or close to zero"; long term MS expects agents "to monetize like scaled search players" — real-time bidding on conversion, FCF per conversion, ROAS targets (PAGE 38). Muse itself: "consistently been among the top 5 downloaded apps during its ~3 weeks post-launch", leverages Facebook / Instagram 1P data, integrates mobile web browsing "from initial search all the way to checkout", and "its free tier likely allows for 100+ queries per day" (PAGE 38). MS also expects "a better agent around Gemini 4 in 4Q" from GOOGL and an AAPL agent "powered by GOOGL", and watches "private labs' entrance into the agentic space" (PAGE 2).
- 🔴 **THE "5 I's" AND MS'S WINNERS / AT-RISK LISTS, AS STATED** (MS "Muse", 2026-09-28, PAGE 1, 4-5, 9): the framework, developed with MS's Internet team — **Inventory** (price and SKU availability → more price transparency and competition; differentiated / private-label assortment more insulated), **Infrastructure** (supply chain, speed of delivery → "travel and services appear more exposed than goods retail"), **Innovation** (larger retailers better able to fund integrations), **Incrementality** (is agent traffic additive or cannibalistic; subscription models could move where loyalty resides), **Income Statement** (exposure to retail media — "the key risk"). Three matter most for Muse: Inventory, Infrastructure and Income Statement. **Clearest winners: Cruise Lines, WMT, COST, W, NKE, ONON, META, AMZN, EBAY, SBUX, MCD, CVNA, ABI BB, CPR IM, MELI.** **More at risk: franchise auto dealers (PAG, GPI, SAH), Food / Home Care, EXPE, CHWY, PLNT** (cover), with the sector table adding WOOF, BBY, AEO, BURL, ROST, VSXY, CAKE, EAT, TXRH, HEN3 GY, RKT LN, BEI GY, CASH BZ, NATU BZ. In e-commerce "AMZN and EBAY screen among the best positioned while others like PTON, CHWY, and FIGS screen as being most at risk"; in online travel "BKNG/ABNB/EXPE have access to unique, fragmented, scaled, and growing real-time inventory which is a key differentiator… but direct traffic trends and these players' ability to capture users and transactions through their own on-platform agentic offerings will be important to the long-term multiples"; in the gig economy "horizontal agents will need UBER/LYFT/DASH's 'infrastructure' to build out consistent agentic delivery services" (PAGE 40). Key debates MS lists (Exhibit 6): convenience moat, data moat (Meta and Google "may have a broader view of user behavior across activities"), retail-media exposure, and "to partner or not to partner" — bears say retailers "have no choice but to accept 'Instant Checkout' under Agent economics (i.e., referral fee) or lose market share" (PAGE 10).
- 🔴🔴 **STRATECHERY: MUSE vs THE NEW COPILOT — TWO BIDS TO BE PLATFORM AND AGGREGATOR AT ONCE, AND THE REACH COMPARISON** (Stratechery · Ben Thompson "Apps, Agents, and Aggregation", 2026-09-28, PAGE 3-8): the Muse launch detail Thompson latched onto is not the Muse Spark model but that "Meta was provisioning every user in the U.S. … with a virtual machine with a **2-core processor, 8GB of RAM, and 8GB of storage**" — "the only way to make agents work for most people". His own test: a personal recipe-box app built from Instagram saves in 5 minutes while walking the dog — "the infinite app", disposable generative UI; "the number of apps I look at are plummeting" (689 on his phone). The mechanism: "starting with GPT-5.6 Sol, every app with a user interface was usable [by agents] too, albeit slowly. Astra made it fast" — "AI can basically use any app or any website that I don't want to." Meta Connect's glasses are "Muse delivery mechanisms". On Microsoft (quoting The Verge and Satya Nadella on X, 2026-09-25): the redesigned **Copilot app bundles chat, coding and agents; Autopilot (formerly Scout) is a "digital teammate" with "its own cloud computer instance"** that "lives in your tenant with its own identity, memory, computer, and workspace", built on Microsoft IQ; Nadella: "We're building Copilot as a new OS for work". Thompson: the new Copilot "isn't dissimilar to Muse — note that bit about Autopilot including its own cloud computer instance — just with all of the enterprise controls". The prize: "not just a platform like Windows, or an Aggregator like Facebook. What Muse and Copilot are making a bid to be is both". Stickiness: "most people and companies will only have one agent, not multiple"; and the distribution point — "**Meta reaches nearly every person on earth; Microsoft reaches nearly every employee**". The enterprise-software aside: Salesforce "is going to try and charge a three-digit premium to make their flagship product a Claude plugin" — "the audacity of the cash grab in the face of computer use that will ensure the Salesforce UI is never interacted with by a human again" (PAGE 8).

Meta is "the platform where brands are most-willing to tolerate mediocre performance because it has unmatched scale"; budgets are shifting from the open web and DSPs toward walled gardens and social. ➤ Read for this page: the retail-media pool MS says agents will tap is already decelerating before Muse's take rate turns positive, and the ChatGPT-reach point is the near-term cap on the ad leg of any OpenAI agent.

## Key debates
- **Bull / where the money is:** the transaction stays with whoever has price, speed, assortment and fulfilment — the "handshake" plus data trust and fulfillment trust keep the retailer at the checkout, and Muse becomes an incremental acquisition channel at near-zero cost while take rates are ~0 (Morgan Stanley · Simeon Gutman / Brian Nowak et al. "Muse: Rewriting the Retail/Consumer Playbook", 2026-09-28, PAGE 2, 7-8). The platform owners win either way: MS sees "trillions of spend" enabled long term even if "monetization will be low for quite some time" (PAGE 2); Stratechery says the two companies with the distribution already have the two agents that fit the use case (Stratechery · Ben Thompson "Apps, Agents, and Aggregation", 2026-09-28, PAGE 8); Jefferies ties it to a longer compute cycle (Jefferies · Brent Thill et al. "AI Summit Recap", 2026-09-24, PAGE 1).
- **Bear / what could break it:** discovery and advertising move upstream to the agent, retail media leaks margin and the agent becomes "a new gateway (and potentially a referral fee collector)" (MS "Muse", 2026-09-28, PAGE 8); GOOGL's search take rate is the biggest single pool exposed — needing ~3,800bp of traffic shift to break even at a 5% take rate (PAGE 38); Stratechery's stronger version is that apps and services become "implementation detail[s] … scrapping for crumbs from the Agent, the ultimate gatekeeper of not just user demand, but desire" (Stratechery 2026-09-28, PAGE 6). The counter-bears: trust — BKNG's argument that travellers still book where reliability and service live (Jefferies · John Colantuoni "Takeaways from Skift's 2026 Global Travel Forum", 2026-09-27, PAGE 1) and MacCartney's view that META's trust issues could keep it from being the primary agent (Jefferies AI Summit, 2026-09-24, PAGE 23); and reach — ChatGPT's US audience is smaller than Google, Meta and even Reddit, which caps the ad leg today (Jefferies expert call · Horizon Media, 2026-09-28, PAGE 4).
- **Who becomes THE agent:** Stratechery — one agent per person / company, decided by context and access; Meta (every person) vs Microsoft (every employee) (Stratechery 2026-09-28, PAGE 8). Jefferies' expert — winner-take-one on trust and data access, favouring GOOGL (7 apps, 2B users) and AAPL (Jefferies AI Summit, 2026-09-24, PAGE 23). MS — Meta's first-mover advantage and surging usage are notable, but watch the private labs, GOOGL's Gemini 4 agent in 4Q and an AAPL agent powered by GOOGL (MS "Muse", 2026-09-28, PAGE 2). The Street expects OpenAI's own consumer agent at DevDay (Capstone US Equities "OpenAI DevDay 2026: where the tape is exposed", 2026-09-28 — derived from this wiki, link only, no numbers cited from it).
- **Timeline / inflection points — catalysts:** **OpenAI DevDay, Tue 29-Sep-2026, keynote 1pm ET** — the consumer-agent and cyber-product expectations are compiled in Capstone's own preview ([OpenAI DevDay 2026: where the tape is exposed](../../relat%C3%B3rios%20bons/OpenAI_DevDay_2026_preview_2026-09-28.html), Capstone US Equities, 2026-09-28; derived artifact, link only). Then: Shop Pay integration into Muse "coming" and AMZN-Muse terms (MS "Muse", 2026-09-28, PAGE 2, 7); GOOGL's "better agent around Gemini 4 in 4Q" (PAGE 2); ABNB's AI search launch "very soon" as the gate to sponsored listings (Jefferies · Colantuoni, 2026-09-27, PAGE 1); the first non-zero Muse take rate (MS, PAGE 2).
