## What it is / why it matters
"Tokenmaxxing" was the 2025 reflex of enterprises (and their employees) maxing out token consumption to look AI-forward; the 2026 sequel is the pullback — firms now demanding **control** over token spend, routing cheaper tasks to cheaper models, and testing open-source/DeepSeek substitutes. The buy-side question this resolves: **does economic value keep accruing to frontier models (Anthropic/OpenAI pricing power, and the GPU capex that underwrites it), or does model substitution and cost discipline cap the monetization curve?** The on-disk evidence (Jun 2026) is two-sided: spend is still climbing fast and the frontier still captures ~90% of revenue, but a cost-control backlash is real on the margin and is starting to move enterprise behavior, vendor pricing (MSFT Copilot → usage), and the read-through to hyperscaler demand.

  - **MS's supporting mechanism — orchestration layers are the institutionalisation of token-optimising, and MS treats them as bullish, not bearish:** "we expect to continue to see a range of LLM advances that result in better efficiencies (an example being **the rise in the usage of 'orchestration layers' that allocate work to the most cost-effective LLM**)… **Importantly, this relentless pursuit of efficiency (by both Chinese and American LLM developers) reinforces our fundamental view that demand for compute is likely to vastly exceed supply.**" ⚠️ **This is precisely what [[MSFT]] is now doing first-party with MAI as the cheap tier (see [../MSFT.md](../MSFT.md): "90% of the tasks are done by the Cyber Flash One model and 10% of the tasks you still go to the frontier"), and what [[AMZN]] said on 07-30 it intends to do with its own frontier model in Bedrock. The number they optimise is power efficiency per bit, and the route to it is higher power lasers. The more bits moved per square area of indium phosphide, the more value delivered. That is a cleaner statement of the physical-layer's relationship to token economics than any of the vendor-side Jevons claims already on this page (MU 07-27, Hock Tan 07-07, SK's Chey Tae-won 07-10), because it does not invoke Jevons at all — it just denies the transmission channel exists at its level and then optimises the one variable that matters.**
  - **AURELION'S "THOUGHTS" — WHERE THE INCENTIVE ALIGNMENT ARGUMENT LIVES (this is the authors' analysis, not management):** "**Efficiency as the optimisation target is the right thing to be chasing. Hyperscalers are working on cost per token and that pressure runs all the way down to the 'plumbing'. The useful part is incentive alignment. Higher power and higher speed make the link more efficient and carry higher ASP at the same time. Lumentum gets paid to build what its customers actually need.**" ⚠️ **That is a genuinely different claim from the deflation-narrative on the rest of this page: at the plumbing layer, the cost-per-token squeeze is not a margin threat but an ASP driver, because the thing that lowers joules per bit is the higher-priced product. Lumentum is racing to relieve the bottleneck that gives it pricing power. We read that as confidence.**"

- **NEW (2026-07-27) — THE JEVONS ARGUMENT STATED EXPLICITLY BY A MEMORY VENDOR, PLUS A SUPPLY-SIDE CAVEAT THAT CUTS AGAINST A POPULAR BEAR DATAPOINT (Micron IR call · Satish, 2026-07-27).** Asked directly whether new architectures could reduce memory per chip, Satish gives the full chain: **"they could do various techniques you might have seen in the press — sparse attention or compression or mixture of experts. These are popular techniques that are used to reduce the amount of hardware need[ed], it could reduce the amount of memory you need, it could reduce the amount of compute you need in order to produce the output tokens. And that is a very important part of improving the economics of the tokens to increase the adoption of tokens. And it drives two things: it drives more users to use it if the prices of the token go down, which increases demand. Another factor… is to expand the context, so you can use agentic workflows more aggressively, for example, to do more useful work. And so these are things that collectively are called Jevons paradox and we believe in that as well — that reduction or improvement in economics of tokens leads to a net positive in terms of overall demand."** **Note the second mechanism, which is the less-cited one and the more interesting: cheaper tokens do not only add users, they let EXISTING users expand context and run agentic workflows harder. That is intensity growth on a fixed user base — a different and more durable demand vector than adoption growth.**

But it's, we think, more a supply related issue as opposed to an optimization that truly reduces the amount of memory you need per unit of hardware."** **The customers-cutting-memory-content datapoint is real, but it is rationing, not efficiency — and it reverses when supply arrives.** ⚠️ Vendor-sourced and self-serving; logged as Micron's argument, not as a finding. ## Key debates
- **Bull / where the money is:** Frontier captures the value and long-running agents expand token demand structurally — BG2Pod (2026-06-12): "two things can be true… majority of economic value accrues to the frontier, majority of tokens are open source." Bullish for the frontier labs *and* — counterintuitively — for compute: "the better open source does, the better it is for compute providers" because frontier labs capturing less margin means more spend on raw compute (Baker, 2026-06-12). NVDA bull "tokenmaxx" thesis cited by MS Sales (NVDA.md 2026-06-11). The unit-economics that *underwrite* the whole buildout: Dylan Patel (Sequoia, 2026-06) puts **Anthropic's Opus 4.8 API-token margin >80% and Anthropic net-income-positive ex-SBC in Q2** — so a lab "can pay above-market for every GPU" (double compute cost, still ~50% GM, NOI still rises), which is why per-GW rental rates diverge so widely (Trainium sub-$10B/GW vs SpaceX/Google ~$25B/GW-yr).
- **Bear / what could break it:** (1) Capital-intensive price war. Vital Knowledge (NVDA.md 2026-06-11): "OpenAI considering token price war while being capital-intensive — a price war in a capital-intensive industry is historically toxic"; Oracle F27 capex/sales >100% is "why NVDA and MU trade at such low PEs." (2) Substitution + routing caps frontier ARR if open-source closes the long-running-task gap (Jensen's model-routing argument, BG2Pod 2026-06-12). The gap is now visibly narrow: **GLM-5.2 (open-weight, MIT) trails Opus 4.8 by just 0.7pt on FrontierSWE at ~1/6 GPT-5.5's per-token cost** (TheValueist / Devansh, 2026-06-21/26) — but Opus keeps a **material lead on the hardest long-horizon reliability (SWE-Marathon 26.0 vs 13.0)** and Zhipu's US Entity-List status gates enterprise adoption, so the frontier premium compresses in mid-tier work while surviving at the top. (3) AWS read-through: Bernstein flagged a "tokenmaxxing hangover" — non-AI cloud growth flattening as compute is reallocated to AI labs, a 4Q risk (AMZN.md). (4) Chanos (NVDA.md 2026-06-14): if GPU data-center economics are as good as claimed, why not raise prices 3-4x — implying either the premium is overstated or rent is being left on the table. (5) **Demand-vs-cost ambiguity (audit add):** H100 compute spot fell to **$2.42/hr, −40% from the May peak** — SemiAnalysis flags the open read: structural cost improvement, or an early sign compute demand is waning (briefing 2026-06-27).
- **Inference efficiency is the governor on both monetization and capability.** Two SemiAnalysis pieces point the same way: serving cost per token is collapsing (GB300 at $0.156/Mtok; B200 +1.7x tokens/MW from software alone), which *expands* monetizable demand (Jevons) but compresses per-token pricing power; and RL post-training — the thing that creates the agentic capability worth paying for — is itself generation-bound, so the same inference advances cap how much capability frontier labs can afford to train. Bull read: cheaper inference → more agentic adoption → more tokens. Bear read: if open-source closes the Day-0 gap (DeepSeek V4 now Day-0 on CUDA *and* Ascend) the frontier's pricing premium erodes from both sides (SemiAnalysis "DeepSeek V4 Day 0→43", 2026-06-09; "RL Systems", 2026-06-16).
- **Timeline / inflection:** Next 12 months is "the most indicative" of whether closed-source keeps its stranglehold or open-source grabs the long tail (Clark Tang, BG2Pod 2026-06-12). Watch: MSFT Copilot usage-pricing rollout + hosted-DeepSeek decision; whether OpenAI's mooted price cuts dislodge stickiness; Ramp's next data prints on DeepSeek/open-source share.
- **Returns-on-capex is the central macro debate, and the funding gap makes it urgent.** Bernstein: the "biggest debate" is *how long can the build-out continue and when do we get proof of ROI?* AI revenue growth has accelerated but is "still early to justify the ROI on all these investments" — revenue/backlog/CFO send a *mixed* message. With 2026 capex (~$623B) now running roughly level with CFO (~$635B) and capex set to outgrow CFO in 2027, the marginal dollar of AI infra increasingly comes from capital markets — i.e. monetization has to inflect or the funding math breaks (Bernstein "Cloud in the Quarter", 2026-06-22).
- **Model wars as the winner/loser determinant.** Bernstein flags "do the model wars determine winners and losers?" as an open question, and observes that "who is an AI winner and who is a loser is changing quite frequently" even across the hyperscalers — the laggard (Google, vertically integrated) now "appears the leader in AI cloud." Read-through to monetization: the model-economics fight (frontier vs open-source, inference vs training) maps directly onto who captures token revenue (Bernstein "Cloud in the Quarter", 2026-06-22).
