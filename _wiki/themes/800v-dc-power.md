- 🔴 **THE DIRECTION OF TRAVEL, STATED AS A DESIGN PRINCIPLE:** *"Delivering power at a higher voltage reduces the current needed to transmit the same amount of power, which can reduce interconnect losses"* — while **the current drawn by compute dies keeps rising**, which is why the team works on *"stepping down the voltage close to the die, together with devices suited to that task"* and on *"bringing the power source closer to the die."* **That is the same physics this page has been trading as 800VDC, but stated one layer further in than the rack: the last conversion stage migrates onto/under the package.**
- 🔭 **THE VARIANT BIT — GaN AS A *PROCESS* OPTION, NOT A PART.** This page has carried GaN as merchant power-semi content (AIXA TAM, 08-25; FLEX/EPC, 09-03/07). The expert describes something different: *"Intel is researching **monolithic integration**, in which GaN and silicon circuits are fabricated together… This goes beyond placing separate components and aims to implement both types of devices together"*, with configurations where *"power could be converted underneath, with a CPU or GPU die above it."* Asked whether that makes it a foundry offering, he answered: *"Yes, that is the direction."* ➜ **If it works, the value of the last conversion stage moves from the power-semi BOM into the foundry/packaging bill — a share-shift risk this page does not currently price anywhere.** ⚠️ **Research-stage: a 3-15yr-horizon research organisation, no product, no date, no customer; there are already merchant GaN power devices on sale and this is explicitly framed against them.**
- **Why the power leg and the memory leg are the same conversation for this team:** the STCO scope is *a single server in a rack* — CPU/GPU boards, compute dies, HBM and conventional DRAM together — and the stated reason memory is *"the main bottleneck"* is partly a POWER argument: *"moving data between different memories also consumes a lot of power."* Cross-ref the [[hbm-memory]] block of the same date.

## What it is / why it matters

The jump from 48V/415V AC distribution to **800V DC** inside the data center is the single biggest power-architecture change in 20+ years, and it is being forced by NVIDIA's roadmap rather than chosen. As GPU racks climb from ~130kW (Blackwell) toward 450kW (Rubin Ultra) and 600kW+ (next-gen "Feynman"/Kyber), the only way to push that power without drowning the rack in copper is to raise the voltage and drop the current (Citi 800V expert, 2026-06-16; UBS 800V expert, 2026-05-14). The transition reshuffles a multi-decade-stable supply chain: it kills low-frequency transformers and legacy UPS, mints a new market for solid-state transformers (SSTs), and re-rates the power-semi and connector content per megawatt. For a buy-sider this is both a content-growth story (analog/power semis, connectors, electrical OEMs) and a disruption story (who adapts vs. who gets disintermediated). Trigger to watch is simple: **track the chip ramp and you track the 800V ramp** (Citi, 2026-06-16).

## Key debates

- **Bull / where the money is:** Content per MW rises across the stack and re-rates the analog/power-semi group; new SST + DC-protection markets open from zero. TI is Citi's top idea (PT $345, best-positioned on capacity + GaN/WBG), with a positive catalyst watch on ON Semi (vertical GaN, Sept analyst day) (Citi, 2026-06-16). Connectors (Amphenol, TE) win on the copper math — at current architecture a 1MW rack needs ~200kg of busbar copper, "astronomical" and physically displacing compute space, so higher voltage is unavoidable (Citi, 2026-06-16). SST optionality is a free call on GEV/electrical OEMs (Bernstein power names, 2026-06-18).
- **Bear / what could break it:** It's slower than the hype. A "very well-known enterprise/hyperscaler crossover" has hard-committed to **no DC until 2032**; sidecar is a floor-space eater feeding a 36-month chip on 15-year power amortization; SST players "don't even have proof of concept yet" with no field hours (AlphaSense expert, 2026-06-03). Real gating items are DC breakers/protection (DC arc doesn't self-extinguish — only 2–3 makers), insulation/clearance, EMI, and an OCP/UL safety spec not even published yet (UBS, 2026-05-14; AlphaSense, 2026-06-03). Incumbents (Eaton/Schneider) have little financial incentive to disrupt overflowing AC order books (AlphaSense, 2026-06-03).
- **Copper "tax" nuance:** absolute copper content actually *decreases* per rack at 800V vs. 48V (current falls, busbar cross-section shrinks); the offset is the Rubin Ultra TDP step-up, leaving content roughly flat-to-modestly-up gen/gen — not the runaway copper bull some assume (UBS expert Q&A, 2026-05-14).
- **Timeline / inflection points:** sidecar production must be ready 2H26; SST proof-of-concept this year → scale manufacturing mid-2027; GEV piloting an SST with a hyperscaler in fall 2026; full SST mainstream 2028–2029; long tail to 1,500V thereafter (UBS, 2026-05-14; Bernstein, 2026-06-18; Citi, 2026-06-16).
- 🆕 **The debate this page has been missing — STRUCTURAL vs CYCLICAL, and it is where the next twelve months of revisions live (Arete "Datacentre Power Semis", 2026-08-20).** Every TAM on this page argues the structural case (content per kW, per rack, per GW). Arete's contribution is to separate that from a **cyclical overlay that is already printing: lead times extending (IFX SPS 52 → 89 weeks in one quarter, broad MOSFET 20 → 40 weeks against a 55-week Covid peak), capacity tightening (analog foundry utilisation 85%+ → ~95% by 1H27; total analog supply +12% through '29 against demand +49%), and pricing up everywhere (broad analog ~+10% in '27, power semis +10-20%, AI power semis 20%+, SPS >30% already accepted).** ➤ **The distinction is investable: the structural case is a 2028 story that this page has repeatedly noted needs a 2027 down-payment; the cyclical case is a 2026-27 gross-margin story that does not depend on 800V adoption happening on schedule. If 800V slips, the shortage does not.** ⚠️ **The corollary is the bear case for the cyclical leg — it is a CYCLE. Arete's own pricing table is a list of price rises taken into a shortage; the same table read backwards is what unwinds when the 4% cumulative foundry capacity growth finally arrives, or when demand growth flattens (Arete's own model has analog demand +0% in '29 after +18/+15/+10% in '26-28).**
- 🆕 **And a NEW competitive axis: CAPACITY beats PRODUCT for the next two years (Arete, 2026-08-20).** Arete's ranking is explicitly built on portfolio **and** capacity grade, and the sharpest call in the deck is that **[NVDA](../NVDA.md) will likely design [TXN](../TXN.md) into Rubin Ultra despite Arete believing TI's VRM is inferior to IFX's, Renesas' and MPS' — "given how tight capacity is, this likely won't matter"** (TI lead times 16 weeks vs IFX 90). ➤ **This inverts the framing on this page's management table, which reads the semis cohort as a share-not-TAM fight decided on product. Arete says the share is decided at the fab: capacity STRONG for IFX/TXN/STM/WOLF, AVERAGE for MPWR/Renesas/Vicor/MCHP/Rohm/NVTS, WEAK for [ADI](../ADI.md)/[ON](../ON.md).** ⚠️ **Unresolved, and the thing to test on the next set of prints: whether a capacity-driven design-in is durable. A socket won because the preferred vendor was allocated is a socket that can be lost when the allocation clears — which is the opposite of how design wins normally behave.**
