## Snapshot
Cloud-native observability platform — infrastructure monitoring (>$1.6B ARR), log management (>$1B ARR, Flex Logs nearing $100M) and APM/digital experience (>$1B ARR) as the three pillars, plus 20+ newer products across security, data observability, software delivery and AI (Q4 2025 call, 2026-02-10). Total ARR crossed $4B and quarterly revenue crossed $1B in Q1 2026; of 26 products, five exceed $100M ARR and three sit at $50–100M (Q1 2026 call, 2026-05-07). Usage-based, annual-plus commit model; 48% of the Fortune 500 are customers with median F500 ARR still under $500K (Q4 2025 call). The corpus treats DDOG as the cleanest public proxy for AI-native cloud consumption: it monitors the model labs' production and, since Q1 2026, their training fleets (theme page; Q1 2026 call).

## Current state (Q2 2026, call 2026-08-06)
**The print.** Revenue $1.12B, +36% y/y, above the high end of guidance; +11% q/q — the best sequential growth since Q2 2022 — on a record $115M sequential dollar add (Obstler). Non-AI customer revenue growth accelerated again to the **high-20s%** (mid-20s in Q1, 18% a year ago); the AI cohort "continued to grow rapidly and diversify" — >750 customers including hyperscalers' in-house AI labs, 31 spending >$1M and 8 >$10M annually. Customers ~33,400 (from ~31,400); $100K+ ARR customers ~4,720 (from ~3,850), 91% of ARR. Multi-product: 58% on 4+ products (52% a year ago), 37% on 6+ (29%), 13% on 10+ (7%). RUM passed $200M ARR growing >50%. New logos contributed ~30% of y/y growth (25% in Q1); enterprise new-logo bookings more than doubled. NRR low-120s, GRR mid-to-high 90s. Billings $1.18B +38%; **RPO $3.47B +43%, cRPO ~+40%**. Gross margin 79.6% (80.2% Q1, 80.9% a year ago); operating margin 23% ($257M) after ~$15M of DASH; **FCF $279M, 25% margin**; cash $5.0B.

**The one caveat — the largest customer.** A nine-figure renewal was signed with "a leading AI company" (17 products) "albeit with a user reduction starting in Q3, which we considered in our guidance"; management chose to "fully de-risk" the guide for this account. Pomel: "if you backed out our largest customer from our growth, you get pretty much the same growth rate… five quarters of continuous acceleration from the rest of the business." Nothing on duration or pricing beyond "a lot of continuity in that renewal" (Q&A with Singh/MS, Zukin/Wolfe, Reback/Stifel).

**Guidance.** Q3 2026 revenue $1.135–1.145B (+28–29%), non-GAAP operating income $260–270M (23–24%), EPS $0.63–0.65. **FY26 revenue $4.45–4.47B (+30%)**, operating income $1.01–1.03B (23%), EPS $2.50–2.54; capex + capitalized software 4–5% of revenue. Guide trajectory: $4.06–4.10B (+18–20%) at Q4 2025 → $4.30–4.34B (+25–27%) at Q1 2026 → $4.45–4.47B now.

**Product.** DASH (June) brought >100 launches: Bits AI across the DevOps loop (monitor creation, root cause "within minutes", guard-railed remediation) and the development loop (Bits Release / Code / Testing); Datadog for AI (Data Observability, Agent Console, Bits Evals); Bring Your Own Cloud for logs (largest BYOC win to date, petabyte scale), coming to metrics and traces; federated log queries into Databricks and ClickHouse; Infinite Cardinality Metrics at no extra cost; AI Guard for custom and coding agents; Bits Security Analyst decoupled from Datadog's SIEM to run on third-party SIEMs — explicitly a move toward an AI SOC (Pomel to Kidron/Oppenheimer). MCP tool calls 4x q/q, 22x vs Q4 2025; a new **AI-credits packaging** for Bits AI is rolling out. Deals: Fortune 10 land, two seven-figure lands at "neuro labs" for **training** observability, a $30M+ TCV standardization at a large online media company.

## Debate / thesis
- **Bull — the AI stack's cleanest consumption toll.** Morgan Stanley's reframe — OW, PT $225 (from $180), "From Observability Leader to AI Winner" (Singh/Weiss, 2026-05-08) — rests on two engines visible in the transcripts: AI-natives compounding in number and size (>$1M spenders 15 → 19 → 22 → 31, Q3 2025 → Q2 2026) and a non-AI base that has accelerated five straight quarters (18% → 20% → 23% → mid-20s → high-20s). JPM's Murphy (OW, Street-high PT, "Hyperscalers' AI Labs Fetching New Workloads", 2026-05-08) and UBS's Keirstead (Buy, 2026 growth 27%→33%, 2026-05-08; hyperscaler core demand a "positive read-through" to DDOG, 2026-07-30) key on what management flagged in Q1 2026: "last year… training is not really a market for us yet. Now we actually see training becoming a market" (Pomel). Jefferies' Thill puts DDOG in the "token path" (2026-06-01); Jefferies' client survey ranks Datadog among the four software categories least threatened by AI (Beavington, 2026-05-20); Barclays desk: "DDOG clear favorite in infra" (Rand, 2026-05-22). Security is the second leg — ARR >$100M growing mid-40s (Q2 2025) → mid-50s (Q3 2025); the WFC cloud expert calls observability vendors stretching into security "the bigger threat to security vendors" (2026-04-14). On automation cannibalising consumption (Ikeda/BofA, Q2 2026), Pomel: "when they use Bits AI, they use more of our product… It's not a zero sum game."
- **Bear 1 — concentration is no longer theoretical.** The largest customer (an AI lab, nine-figure annualized, 17 products) is reducing usage from Q3 2026 (Q2 2026 call). Management has flagged AI-cohort volatility on every call since Q4 2024 ("optimization and volume discounts related to contract renewals", 2025-02-13) and stopped disclosing the cohort's revenue share after Q3 2025 (12%). The Q3 guide (+28–29%) sits ~7pts below Q2's print; Reback/Stifel's "300–400bps" ex-customer question went unanswered. Barclays' investor read post-DASH: DDOG "solid but step-down on valuation/concentration", ranking slipped to SNOW > DDOG > MDB from DDOG > MDB > SNOW (2026-06-09); "pushback continues on customer diversification" (2026-06-05). Eight >$10M AI customers is diversification inside a cohort still built on a handful of labs whose own economics are unproven.
- **Bear 2 — valuation and crowding.** DDOG replaced MongoDB as JPM's "Most Crowded Name in Software" (Schilsky, 2026-05-26) — the prior holder fell 22% on its next print. Market cap +37% above SNOW at 13.5x vs 7.25x CY27 EV/S (Schilsky, 2026-05-19); R&Co specialist "AI Winner" at 13x '27 EV/S (2026-05-26); UBS's Sultan files it as a "high flyer at 14 times sales with the hope of more estimate revisions", and Keirstead warns bogeys "have inched up… set-up problems, just given richer multiples, more crowded positioning" (both 2026-08-31). BofA's Ikeda: "I don't hear a lot of bears on Datadog. That is something to think about" (2026-06-08).
- **Bear 3 — log-cost deflation.** Palo Alto's Arora justified Chronosphere as observability "not designed for the AI era… a third of the cost" (PANW Q1 FY26 call, 2025-11-19); Octahedron says Databricks' LakeWatch "completely takes out the economics from the old providers like Splunk" (2026-07-15). Datadog's counter is on the calls — Flex Logs ($50M ARR in six quarters, Q1 2025; nearing $100M, Q4 2025), BYOC on customers' own storage, federated queries into Databricks/ClickHouse, Infinite Cardinality Metrics (Q2 2026) — but each is a price-per-GB concession by another name, and gross margin has drifted 81.7% → 79.6% across the six quarters. Pomel's dismissal of the acquired competitors as "not particularly winning companies" (Q4 2025) is assertion, not evidence.
- **Bear 4 — AI on the P&L, and LLMs as the observer.** The Archera expert used Datadog to explain why vendors run model routers: "Datadog on Bits AI does care if it has negative margins" — hence a frontier router plus an owned fine-tuned open model (2026-07-13); the AI-credits packaging (Q2 2026) is the monetisation response. Borges/GS asking whether frontier LLMs become the anomaly-detection layer drew Pomel's most substantive defence: the moat is being "embedded into the data plane", running detection in-stream on flows "many orders of magnitude larger… than what you typically feed into an LLM" (Q4 2025 call). Whether Bits AI automates away the workflows that drove consumption (Ikeda, Q2 2026) remains open.

## Risks
- **Customer concentration:** one AI lab is a nine-figure annualized account with a usage reduction underway; 31 AI customers >$1M and 8 >$10M concentrate the fastest-growing cohort in few hands (Q2 2026 call).
- **Consumption volatility at renewals** — higher commits bought with lower unit prices produce "a flat or down month or quarter" (Q4 2024 call); the 2022–23 cloud-native optimisation cycle is management's own analogue (Q1 2025 call).
- **Gross-margin pressure** from usage spikes at the largest customers (Q1 2025: "cloud hosting costs rose more quickly than we expected") and unoptimised new products; GM 79.6% in Q2 2026.
- **Log economics / competition:** Chronosphere (PANW), LakeWatch (Databricks), Grafana and open source at AI-native accounts; Datadog's Flex/BYOC/federation responses lower its own price per GB (theme page §3.1; Q2 2026 call).
- **LLM disintermediation** of the analysis layer; Bits AI automating the workflows that drive consumption (Borges Q4 2025; Ikeda Q2 2026).
- **Valuation / positioning:** 13–14x sales, "Most Crowded Name in Software" — MDB's set-up before a −22% print (Schilsky 2026-05-26; UBS 2026-08-31).
