## Snapshot
Consumer-hardware franchise (iPhone ~50%+ of revenue) plus a high-margin Services annuity layered on an installed base of **over 2.5bn active devices** (Q2 FY26, 2026-04-30). Revenue mix: iPhone, Mac, iPad, Wearables/Home/Accessories, and Services (App Store, advertising, iCloud, payments, Apple TV+, music). In the semis/AI chain, Apple is the largest TSMC customer (~17% of TSMC sales per BofA, 2026-05-11) — though Bernstein estimates NVIDIA rose to ~20% of TSMC revenue in 2025, "about as big a customer as Apple" (Bernstein Semis deck, 2025-05-05) — a leading-edge-node and DRAM/NAND buyer, and a hyperscaler-divergent AI player: capex/revenue has fallen ~3%→2% over Q1'20→Q1'26 (@TheTranscript_, briefing 2026-05-11) vs. 40–60% for peers (Vital Knowledge, 2026-06-09). AI strategy is capex-light and partner-led — a multi-year Google/Gemini collaboration powers next-gen Apple Foundation Models behind a privacy-first, on-device + Private Cloud Compute architecture; Bernstein frames the resolved Google DOJ remedies as not only clearing a downside risk but opening the path for Apple to leverage Gemini's AI (Bernstein initiation/Newman, 2025-09-15). CEO transition: Tim Cook → John Ternus effective 2026-09-01.

## Current state (latest quarter)

**🆕 2026-08-10 — the constraint is TSMC logic, not memory: Stratechery's read on the FQ3'26 print (Ben Thompson, Stratechery Update, 2026-08-10).**
- **The print and the guide:** June-quarter iPhone and Mac sales were strong — **iPhone +22% (and +22% YTD), Mac +29%** — but the **September-quarter revenue guide of +9-11% came in below the ~12% Street estimate**, with gross margin also just short, and **shares fell 7% after hours** (WSJ via Stratechery).
- **Cook, on the call, is explicit that this is not the memory story:** *"we did experience supply constraints primarily on the Mac and to a lesser extent on iPhone and iPad… primarily driven by **the availability of the advanced nodes that our SOCs are produced on**… we expect the impact from the supply constraints to **increase significantly sequentially** [in the September quarter]… affecting iPhone, Mac, and iPad."* And on root cause: *"it's not a regular supply issue. It's a **demand forecast issue**, to be candid, where the iPhone and the Mac are both doing remarkably better than we thought they would do… we've been pulling supply ahead, and at some point there's a limit to that."*
- **Thompson's mechanism:** Apple had N3 capacity spaced out over time and **pulled wafer starts forward** with TSMC — taken from other customers and from its own future contracted capacity — so **"the bill is coming due"** exactly as Apple was already planning to ramp N3 down ahead of the **N2-based iPhone 18 Pro**. Expected consequence: **Apple sells out of iPhone 17 Pro and runs significant shortages across the entire M5 chip family.** His investment conclusion: Apple has been the obvious safe harbour from the AI trade, *"but just because the company is relatively insulated from major AI spending doesn't mean the company is guaranteed to actually grow"* — and the chip shortage works directly against a price that had started to require sustained higher growth.

- **🆕 Apple Silicon, sized as a cost centre — and the Full-COT premium nobody prices (SemiAnalysis "EDA Market Primer", 2026-05-21):** the page tracks Apple Silicon as a product/moat story and (via Baltra) as an AI-server risk, but has never carried the *cost structure* of the design organisation. ## Risks
- **Memory (DRAM/NAND) cost inflation** — "unlike anything in 40+ years" (Cook via Vital Knowledge, 2026-06-17); largest near-term GM and pricing risk; NAND going "parabolic" (MS, 2026-06-16). Note relative mitigant: lowest memory BOM exposure + supply-chain priority in JPM/Citi coverage (JPM, 2025-12-15; Citi, 2026-01-20). A TSM/Asia-supply-chain expert added color on Apple's supply-chain leverage: Apple reportedly **traded a wide-angle camera module to Samsung in exchange for stable iPhone-18 DRAM allocation**, and TSMC raised Apple's advanced-node wafer pricing only modestly (Apple is TSMC's #2 customer at ~17% of sales — note OCR in the source garbled this as "70%") — i.e. Apple is among the least memory-impacted OEMs (BofA best-ideas expert, 2026-04-09). [Source](../relat%C3%B3rios%20bons/2026_04_09_baml_best_ideas_9_apr_2026.html)
- **Forecast that played out — SemiAnalysis's Feb estimate vs. the June price hikes:** SemiAnalysis's "Memory Mania" report estimated Apple faces **~15% memory-driven price increases vs. ~20% for the broader industry** — structurally better positioned than peers on procurement leverage and strong iPhone 17 demand — but flagged Apple as **"unlikely to be immune,"** with higher memory costs weighing on margins into 2026 and "limited evidence" ASPs would fully offset the pressure; Apple's own earnings commentary (per SemiAnalysis) confirmed this and pointed to the larger memory-cost impact landing in **2H26**, despite relatively favorable 1H26 positioning (SemiAnalysis "Memory Mania," 2026-02-06). That forecast has now played out in real time: Apple hiked prices on **computers and iPads "by hundreds of dollars,"** saying it "can no longer absorb" AI-driven memory-cost increases — and the stock fell **6.1% on the day**, "the most in more than a year" (Barron's/Salzman, 2026-06-29). The weekly tape: AAPL closed **$283.78**, down **$14.23 (-4.8%)** on the week (Barron's, 2026-06-29). Sanctuary Wealth's Mary Ann Bartels frames Apple's pricing action as a read-through for the broader tape: "I think Apple's going to be a very important barometer, to see if raising your prices can actually stick... If they do, it means you're still in this inflation-boost phase, which is actually good for corporate profits" (Barron's, 2026-06-29). Read-through: the Feb estimate (~15% Apple-specific vs. ~20% industry) predicted both the magnitude and the 2H26 timing of the June event 4+ months ahead — a genuine forecasting hit worth flagging for the memory thesis generally, even as it validates the bear case that Apple is "not immune" on margins.
- **Unit-demand elasticity** if price hikes pass through into a soft global smartphone market (−5% to −10%+; Citi sees global smartphone units −5% in 2026E, IDC −10%+).
- **AI execution / monetization** — repeated timeline slips (Apple Intelligence China, global personalized Siri), AI-team talent exodus (JPM, 2025-12-15), narrow device eligibility, no third-party agent integration yet, China/EU regulatory (DMA) overhang; "funding short vs. semis."

RF supply highly concentrated to AAPL (QRVO/SWKS content volatile gen/gen) and Apple is in-housing the modem (~$5bn QCOM revenue at risk) (UBS 2026 overview). [Citi](../relat%C3%B3rios%20bons/2026_04_30_msft_wfc_c1q26_recap_30_apr_26.html) · [SiTime](../relat%C3%B3rios%20bons/2026_05_07_sitm_arm_7_apr_2026.html)

Apple has announced a **$600bn US investment over four years**, with Corning (3x KY glass), Coherent (TX VCSELs) and Lumentum scaling US supply for Apple (JPM, 2025-12-15).

- **"Chipflation" — memory becoming Apple's single-largest cost item:** Culpium (Tim Culpan, Substack) reports Apple facing "unprecedented cost expansion" as memory turns into the single-largest cost line — a read-through from the AI-driven DRAM/NAND price surge and Samsung/SK Hynix pricing power (Jefferies/KB forecast DRAM +308%/NAND +256% in 2026) (Culpium, 2026-07-01). Corroborating signals from the sell/trading desk: Morgan Stanley's Amir Amerian (camera/Canon short-thesis sales note) flags that **Apple's recent price-hike announcement raises the risk of memory-driven price hikes triggering demand-destruction concerns among consumers** (MS/Amerian, 2026-06-30); separately, MS's Skyler Risom sales note (citing the FT) reports **Apple is lobbying for access to CXMT (Chinese memory maker) chips** as memory supply stays constrained — read as a sign of Apple's weakening negotiating leverage in the AI-driven memory boom (MS/Risom via FT, 2026-06-30) — consistent with the 06-25 CXMT-lobbying story already logged above, now with a second independent sourcing line (MS sales desk vs. the original compiled-briefing/press report).
- **🆕 The downside leg of the March-2026 Bernstein scenario grid — dated, but it is the cleanest statement of what "pass-through fails" is worth (Bernstein · Mark C. Newman "Apple Deep Dive: Risks and Opportunities in a year of transition", 2026-03-03; ~5 months stale, does not supersede any Q2/Q3-2026 estimate on this page).** Two downside cases are worth carrying against the current memory×price argument. **(1) Partial pass-through plus demand destruction:** ASP +17% against a **24.5% device-cost increase** with **units -10%** → iPhone GM **39%** and FY27 EPS **$9.54** — still **+2.6% versus consensus**, which was Newman's point: consensus was already conservative enough that even a bad outcome cleared it. **(2) Apple holds ASP flat** and mitigates by halving BOM inflation to **+14.5%** — explicitly contemplating **"turning to Chinese suppliers to offset cost inflation"**, which is the same lever the page now tracks live as the CXMT/YMTC lobbying fight — with units +5.5% on share gains → iPhone GM **34%** and FY27 EPS **$8.81**, **-5.3% versus consensus**; Newman notes the real impact would likely be smaller given Services pricing and other component cost-downs. The structural risk underneath both: **premium-tier price increases above 10% have historically required a step-function design change** (iPhone X +29.4%, 2014's larger display +17.0%, 2010's flat-edge redesign +22.2%), so a purely cost-driven hike is the case where elasticity finally bites. Bernstein's own stated downside risks to the $340 PT: **(a) Apple falls too far behind in Gen AI and sees its ecosystem disrupted by AI-native competitors** (the standing bear hole on this page); **(b) new form factors erode the smartphone's relevance and with it Apple's moat** — now literal, given the Apple↔OpenAI hardware suit above; **(c) US-China escalation disrupting both supply and demand**; and **(d) regulatory limits on ecosystem monetisation, which Bernstein flagged as "significantly mitigated" by the Google DOJ case having largely passed** — note that this last mitigant has since been partly re-opened by the **DMA gatekeeper loss (07-08)** and the **Epic case reaching the Supreme Court (07-02)**, both of which post-date the note. [Source](../relat%C3%B3rios%20bons/BERN_252051.html)

- **🆕 App Store / developer-ecosystem erosion — now a *disclosed* risk, and no longer a regulation-only story (Morgan Stanley · Erik Woodring, "What's New in the 10-Q, Including 2 Post-Earnings Thoughts", 2026-08-03).** For **the first time since December 2023, Apple did not highlight the App Store as a top driver of Services revenue growth** in its 10-Q. The filing states customer purchasing decisions depend in part on the availability of third-party applications and services — if developers cease to support the platform, **"customers may choose not to buy the Company's products"** — and it concedes the economics: if developers pursue **"alternative methods of distribution and payment,"** Apple **"may earn a lower commission"** or none at all (MS cites the **Epic Games Store in Brazil** as the live example). Add management's own call-out of **mobile-gaming weakness** last quarter plus the flag that consumers may **shift time to other AI-powered apps (social media, productivity) that may not be as monetizable as gaming apps**. MS's conclusion is the load-bearing one: **a growing Services risk beyond regulation itself** — Apple must retain developers *and* keep the App Store their **preferred distribution and monetization channel**. Why it outranks a routine risk-factor tweak: the bull case on this page is an installed-base Services annuity valued at 30-35x, and the same analyst's 07-31 callback found long-onlys break that framing below ~10% Services growth. Stack it with the standing regulatory items — **DMA gatekeeper loss (07-08)**, **Epic at the Supreme Court (07-02)**, **DOJ settlement talks (07-20)** — and with Jefferies' flag of a **lower revenue share with [GOOG](GOOG.md)** (07-31). [Source](../relat%C3%B3rios%20bons/APPLE_20260803_1311.html)
- **🆕 AI infrastructure cost + compute-access risk, in Apple's own 10-Q language (Morgan Stanley · Erik Woodring, 2026-08-03).** The 10-Q now says Apple's AI/ML offerings depend on access to sufficient computing resources and that industry-wide demand has produced **"constrained supply, extended lead times, and increasing costs"**; it discloses reliance on **third-party cloud providers** and warns Apple may be unable to secure adequate capacity **"on commercially reasonable terms, or at all."** R&D is already elevated on **"higher infrastructure-related costs, including investments in artificial intelligence"** — **R&D reached 10.7% of revenue in F3Q26, an all-time June-quarter high**. The risk is genuinely two-tailed and both tails cost money: **too little compute delays new AI features (product/execution risk), too much drives higher-than-expected infrastructure spend (margin risk)** — so AI becomes **an incremental source of earnings risk if monetization lags investment**, on top of being the replacement-cycle opportunity. This is the filing-level version of the mechanism Woodring described on 07-31: capex "comically low" at ~$2.5bn/qtr against R&D +35% y/y means Apple leans harder on **Google Cloud**, an unmonetized Services gross-margin headwind. Note the direction of travel versus the page's older framing — Apple's *capex-light* AI strategy was logged as a valuation advantage (BofA/Wigg's "FCF advantage vs the capex-heavy rest of Mag7"); the 10-Q reframes the same choice as a **cost line Apple does not control the price of**. [Source](../relat%C3%B3rios%20bons/APPLE_20260803_1311.html)
- **🆕 Component-cost inflation is now a *forward-looking* disclosure, and pricing is explicitly not presented as a full offset (MS · Woodring, 2026-08-03).** The 10-Q escalates from the earlier "supply chase mode" language to stating Apple is experiencing **"supply constraints and increasing costs for components"** — advanced semiconductors (TSMC 3nm wafers) and NAND/DRAM — and that it **"expects these trends to intensify."** The offset is hedged in Apple's own words: actions **"such as price increases… may not effectively mitigate these negative impacts"** and could reduce demand. Two reads for this page: (i) it removes the ambiguity the memory-pass-through bull case relied on — Apple is on record that pricing may not fully cover it, which is the elasticity risk already logged above, now self-disclosed; and (ii) the door stays open to **further price increases, iPhone 18 Pro / Pro Max the most imminent**. Separately, new language about products using **"new technologies"** facing **"initial capacity constraints"** reads onto the **foldable iPhone** (display/hinge yields) — though MS has seen **no meaningful foldable build cuts and still embeds 6.5M units in the December quarter**. [Source](../relat%C3%B3rios%20bons/APPLE_20260803_1311.html)
