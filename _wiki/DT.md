## Snapshot
Dynatrace sells an enterprise observability platform (APM, infrastructure, logs, real-user monitoring, application security, business observability) built on three architectural pieces management repeats every call: **Grail** (indexless MPP data lakehouse), **Smartscape** (real-time topology graph) and **Davis / Dynatrace Intelligence** (causal + predictive + agentic AI) (Q3 FY26 call, 2026-02-09). Revenue is ~95% subscription, recognized ratably, but the underlying contract is now consumption-shaped: the **Dynatrace Platform Subscription (DPS)** — full platform access with a committed drawdown — covers >75% of ARR and >60% of customers (Q4 FY26 call, 2026-05-13), up from >60% / >40% five quarters earlier (Q4 FY25 call, 2025-05-14). The target customer is the Global 500 / "global 15,000" (McConnell, Q3 FY26); average ARR per customer is "well over $500,000" with a stated long-term opportunity of $1M+ (Q1 FY27 call, 2026-08-05). Management sizes observability + application security at $65B (Q4 FY25) and a new **AI-observability TAM at >$10B by 2030, growing >50%/yr** (Q1 FY27). In the AI stack it sits in the observability column next to DDOG, Splunk/Cisco, New Relic and Grafana (see [theme §3](themes/ai-data-infra-observability.md)).

## Current state (Q1 FY27, call 2026-08-05)
Best quarter of the six on disk. **ARR $2.14B, +17% cc** (first move off the 16% plateau held for four quarters); **net new ARR $85M, +66% y/y — $73M / +41% organic** excluding the $13M ARR from BindPlane; trailing-12-month organic net new ARR growth 17%, up from 12% at Q4 and the fourth consecutive quarter of acceleration (Benson). **Revenue $555M (+15%), subscription $530M (+15%)**, 100bp above the high end of guidance. **NRR 110%** (TTM), gross retention mid-90s. 122 new logos at an **average land of ~$285K** (fifth straight quarter of larger lands; TTM average was $130K at Q4 FY25), new-logo ARR **+160%+**, including an **eight-figure ACV land** at one of Latin America's largest financial institutions. **Logs ~$200M annualized consumption, growing well above 100%** — "nearly doubling since surpassing the $100 million milestone just two quarters ago" (McConnell); BindPlane is inside that figure and "already performing ahead of plan". Non-GAAP **operating margin 29%** (100bp above guide, partly expense timing), EPS $0.48; **adjusted FCF** (new definition, ex-restructuring/acquisition cash) $309M in the quarter, $579M / 28% of revenue TTM, 32% pre-tax. Buyback stepped up again to **$275M / 7.1M shares** (vs $224M in Q4).

AI is now framed as three discrete monetization levers: (1) AI workloads generate far more telemetry → consumption; (2) AI observability ("is it accurate?") as a new capability; (3) direct monetization of Dynatrace's own agents via DPS usage. AI-cohort customers consume 1.5x faster than non-AI cohorts (McConnell). **FX:** with ~40% of business in foreign currency, USD strength is now a **$14M headwind to ARR and $4M to revenue** (an incremental $23M / $19M swing vs the May guide).

**FY27 guidance (as of 2026-08-05):** cc ARR growth **15.5–16.5% maintained** (revisited at mid-year, per practice); revenue and subscription growth raised 25bp to **+14.5–15%**; operating-margin high end raised 25bp to **29.75%**; **EPS $1.97–1.99** (+$0.04 at midpoint, 295–297M shares); adjusted FCF margin 26.5%. **Q2 FY27 guide:** revenue +15–16%, OM 29.5–30%, EPS $0.48–0.49. Benson's own framing: at the high end, Q2–Q4 organic net new ARR needs high-teens growth; "you're not going to see [41%] every quarter". 70% of DPS renewals / annual resets fall in H2, and management expects an **NRR inflection in the back half** if consumption holds (Benson to Bachman/BMO and Singh/MS). **CFO Jim Benson will retire by fiscal year-end**; successor search under way. No decision on premium pricing for on-demand consumption; guide assumes none (Benson to Boolani/Citi).

## Debate / thesis
- **Bull — the cheaper way into observability.** UBS's Roddy Sultan named Dynatrace the best risk/reward in his coverage: *"Still a reasonable multiple, cheaper than Elastic, for in my view, better growth and a higher quality business"* (UBS · Sultan, "State of AI & Software" call, 2026-08-31), six weeks after UBS **assumed coverage at Buy from Neutral** (stock +2.5% pre-market; morning briefing / UBS Tech note, 2026-06-16). Jefferies' Thill puts it in the token path with a lag: *"if you look at Snowflake, Datadog, even Dynatrace at some point when AI comes to the enterprise that will help"* (Jefferies · Thill / Samana, 2026-06-01). The mechanism: platform consumption has run >20% against 16–17% ARR growth since Q2 FY26; the bull case is that the DPS lag converts in FY27, when the first full three-year DPS cohort renews (Benson, Q3 FY26) and 70% of resets land in H2 (Q1 FY27).
- **Bull — logs as the growth engine.** Consumption went from a "$100 million ambition" (Q4 FY25, 2025-05-14) to >$100M (Q3 FY26, 2026-02-09) to ~$200M (Q1 FY27, 2026-08-05), growing >100% every quarter, on a pitch of lower cost (no rehydration, unlimited querying in a fixed window) plus better outcomes from logs-in-context with traces and metrics (Q1 FY26). Customers >$1M in logs doubled y/y and >$500K nearly quadrupled (Benson, Q2 FY26); Vodafone migrated 8 TB/day of log ingest from its legacy provider in two months (Q3 FY26). Benson rejects the "what if logs slows" framing: infrastructure and full-stack consumption are "growing at a very healthy rate" too (Q1 FY27, to Ikeda/BofA).
- **Bull — AI observability / evals as a new category.** Management now sizes it at >$10B by 2030 (Q1 FY27) and the sell-side treats the category as real but unowned (theme §3.1). UBS's checks *"assess there's a real moat around the harness and workflow that Arize has doing eval"* and refer to *"the Arize acquisition in AI observability"* (UBS · Sultan, 2026-08-31). ⚠️ **Arize is not mentioned on any of the six calls on disk, including the 2026-08-05 call; deal terms, ARR and close date are not in the corpus.** Do not quote terms.
- **Bull — security convergence.** WFC's cloud expert: *"Datadog's stretched into security. Dynatrace has stretched into security. Splunk and Cisco are still trying to figure out what they're doing"* — and Palo Alto buying Chronosphere shows the category matters (WFC expert call, 2026-04-14). Management's own stance is narrower: AppSec is "growing nicely" but will take longer than logs to reach $100M, sold to the observability buyer rather than the CISO, with a cloud SIEM "over the course of time" (McConnell, Q3 FY26 and Q4 FY26). M&A optionality: one of two public infra names Jefferies thinks IBM "could really buy" (with Elastic) (Jefferies · Thill, 2026-06-01).
- **Bear — the ARR-growth ceiling and FX.** Five quarters of 16–17% ARR growth and 110–111% NRR despite >20% consumption growth; the FY27 cc ARR guide of 15.5–16.5% was not raised after a 41% organic net-new quarter, and USD strength took $14M off reported ARR (Q1 FY27). Q4 FY26 showed the downside: $81M net new ARR (~9% cc) vs a buy-side bogey in the high-$80s sent the stock -6.7% pre-market (morning briefing, 2026-05-13). Goldman's Martino asked the question directly — *"why aren't you growing faster?"* — and NRR "was maybe a little bit lower than what people are thinking" (Q4 FY26 call). Gross margin absorbs a 100bp hosting-cost headwind in FY27 because consumption hits COGS immediately while revenue is ratable (Benson, Q4 FY26). CFO transition adds execution risk into the year that is supposed to prove acceleration (Q1 FY27).
- **Bear — log deflation and disintermediation.** The cost attack DT uses on legacy log vendors can be turned on it: Palo Alto's Arora justified Chronosphere as observability *"not designed for the AI era… a third of the cost"* (PANW Q1 FY26 call, 2025-11-19) and Octahedron says Databricks' LakeWatch *"completely takes out the economics from the old providers like Splunk"* (Octahedron, 2026-07-15) — both via the theme page. McConnell says Chronosphere-type point solutions are rarely seen in the global-15,000 segment and rebuts "LLMs replicate observability" on architecture (Grail/Smartscape), control-plane role and deterministic-vs-probabilistic grounds (Q3 FY26); Datadog's broader portfolio (Bachman/BMO, Q4 FY25) and third-party agents disintermediating Dynatrace Intelligence (Kidron/Oppenheimer, Q3 FY26) remain the open analyst challenges.
- **Where the sell-side stands (corpus only).** **UBS Buy** (assumed 2026-06-16; Sultan's preferred risk/reward, 2026-08-31, Arize checks pending). **Rothschild & Co Redburn Neutral** (relayed in the 2026-05-13 briefing). BofA hosted DT's CPO and CFO for an observability webinar into the quiet period (BofA · Koji Ikeda, conference wrap, 2026-06-08) — no rating on disk. Bernstein's comps chart had DT at 0.51x EV/revenue/growth on CY25E, in line with SNOW/WDAY (Octahedron deck comps, 2024-11-25 — stale). **No PTs and no post-2026-08-05 sell-side reactions are in the corpus.** Regular call participants: Jefferies (Thill), MS (Singh), GS (Martino), JPM (Murphy), BofA (Ikeda), Barclays (Lenschow), Citi (Boolani), Scotiabank (Colville), RBC (Hedberg), BMO (Bachman), UBS (Sultan). An activist was referenced by BMO's Bachman on the Q4 FY26 call ("now with an activist involved"); management did not address it and nothing else in the corpus corroborates it.

## Risks
- Ratable model lags consumption: the 20%+ consumption vs 16–17% ARR gap has persisted for four quarters; if DPS customers keep going on-demand rather than expanding early, NRR stays at 110% (Q2 FY26 → Q1 FY27 calls).
- Large-deal timing: pipeline deliberately weighted to $500K–$1M+ consolidation deals, so net new ARR "is going to see timing" quarter to quarter (Benson, every call since Q1 FY26); one eight-figure land flattered Q1 FY27.
- FX and margins: ~40% of business in foreign currency ($14M ARR headwind, Q1 FY27); euro-weighted cost base (Q1 FY26); hosting COGS rise with consumption before ratable revenue catches up (Q4 FY26).
- Competitive/structural: Datadog breadth; hyperscaler first-party monitoring and open-source (Grafana) at cloud/AI-native accounts; log-price deflation from lake-based entrants (theme §3.1, §3.6); agents disintermediating the UI layer (Kidron/Oppenheimer, Q3 FY26).
- Execution: CFO retirement in the acceleration year; new go-to-market motion (~150 more strategic accounts, developer personas); EMEA softness in Q4 FY26; three acquisitions (DevCycle, BindPlane, and per UBS — unconfirmed on calls — Arize) plus a $28M restructuring inside one fiscal year.
