## Snapshot
Fabless designer of x86 CPUs, GPUs and adaptive/embedded silicon, fabbed at TSMC (2nm/5nm) with some GF, UMC, Samsung capacity. Four segments: **Data Center** (EPYC server CPUs + Instinct AI GPUs — ~56% of revenue and the entire growth story), **Client & Gaming** (Ryzen PCs + Radeon + semi-custom consoles), and **Embedded** (Xilinx FPGA + adaptive SoC, highest-margin business at ~38-40% segment OM). Competes head-to-head with Intel (x86), Nvidia (AI GPU), Arm-based merchant/custom CPUs, and the custom-ASIC complex (Broadcom/Marvell). The thesis is fully an AI-infrastructure story: AMD is the credible #2 merchant GPU and the share-gaining merchant server CPU into an agentic-AI demand pull (10-K FY25, 2026-02-04).

- **The independent engineering layer — SemiAnalysis "Can AMD break the CUDA Moat? AMD Advancing AI 2026" (2026-07-24). Read this as the counterweight to the sell-side fold above: same event, but built from supply-chain checks, a BOM/TCO model and daily first-hand use of ROCm rather than from AMD's slides. The headline is that the harshest ROCm critic on this page has upgraded AMD — conditionally.** SemiAnalysis's own rating arc, stated explicitly: **"we gave AMD a 0% chance of closing the gap with Nvidia in AI accelerators" → (the "AMD 2.0" piece) "a much more meaningful chance at success" → now, "a great chance of success as long as AMD solves the 2 major risks"** (the two risks are Helios rack ramp and internal cluster/CI capacity — both folded into Bear/Risks below). It also stresses this is not zero-sum: *"just because AMD gains market share, that doesn't mean NVIDIA will do poorly. The pie is getting massive."*
  - **Silicon — the most aggressive package shipping, and it has to be.** MI455X is the **first 2nm datacenter silicon** (both the MI455 compute tiles and the Venice CPU are early N2 adopters; **all competing accelerator platforms are on N3**), the **largest CoWoS-L module shipping at 5.5x reticle**, and AMD is **still the only company adopting TSMC SoIC-X hybrid bonding** — together **3,470mm² of logic silicon per package, "by far the most amount of silicon being shipped in a single package."** Layout: **8 N2 XCDs hybrid-bonded atop 2 reticle-sized base dies** (SRAM + HBM controllers + compute fabric) plus **2 new N3P I/O dies**, identical to each other, each carrying **72 flexible lanes** that support 212G UALoE / 64G Infinity Fabric / PCIe Gen6 / 128G UALink / xGMI4. Believed to be the **first chip shipping TSMC active LSI** (active, circuitry-bearing CoWoS-L bridges with mid-channel repeaters that let the top-die PHYs shrink) — the tell being that TSMC's ISSCC-2026 aLSI "test vehicle" *is* the MI455 interposer (2 base dies, 12 HBM4 stacks, 2 IO dies). **The honest scorecard on all that silicon: 20 PF dense FP8 vs Rubin's 17.5 PF — "these performance advantages are relatively mild compared to Rubin given how much more silicon content there is,"** because AMD trails Nvidia on GPU microarchitecture; specifically **MI455X lacks the 3-bit LUT tensor cores Rubin (SM107) has** (which cut relative HBM-bandwidth need). This is the sharpest available framing of the "AMD buys parity with area" concern and sits underneath the sell-side's FP4/FP8 headline specs.
  - **HBM4 — AMD wins capacity, Nvidia bought back the bandwidth, and the second memory tier has been deleted.** MI455X carries **12 HBM4 stacks = 432GB vs 8 stacks = 288GB on Nvidia and Google** (the base dies grew to a ~32mm HBM-facing edge to fit 3 cubes per side, vs 29mm on the MI300X AID) and takes the bandwidth crown at **23.3 TB/s vs Rubin's 22 TB/s** — up from the 19.6 TB/s advertised at Advancing AI 2025. But the margin is thin *despite a 50% wider bus*, because **AMD's HBM4 pin speed is 7.6 Gbps vs Rubin's 10.7 Gbps (40% faster)**: SemiAnalysis's read is that **Nvidia deliberately raised its HBM4 pin-speed target well above the original JEDEC spec precisely to close the bandwidth deficit to MI455X**, which forced memory suppliers to rework HBM4, pushed out Rubin upstream output (now resolved), and means **Nvidia must buy a materially higher-quality bin than AMD**. AMD and Nvidia are the only two shipping HBM4 this year. **Two margin/BOM consequences the sell-side fold does not carry:** (1) **the LPDDR5X second memory tier is gone** — earlier roadmaps had up to **1TB of LPDDR per MI455X attached on the EAM module**; it has "**disappeared**," together with a **significant DDR5 de-spec** (original 2025 BOM estimate: a "staggering 138TB in DRAM across LPDDR5X and DDR5 MRDIMM," now far lower DDR5 RDIMM content), attributed to tight memory supply — i.e. much of the DRAM price increase is being absorbed by de-spec rather than passed through (this **supersedes** the page's prior "off-package LPDDR as a second memory tier" statement — see Bear and Changelog); (2) SemiAnalysis expects a **significant HBM price increase in 2027** as memory suppliers move to match the margins they now earn on commodity DRAM, and AMD **cannot pass the full increase through** ("most of AMD's volume goes to large buyers with real negotiating leverage") — it marks HBM up at **a lower incremental margin than corporate**, **dragging gross margin on the MI455X package**. That is a concrete mechanism behind the Q4-FY26 GM step-down already flagged in Bear.
  - **Helios networking — merchant switching is the design constraint, and Broadcom is the only door.** 72 MI455X across **18 compute trays (4 GPUs + 1 Venice CPU each) and 6 scale-up switch trays** placed mid-rack, each tray holding **2 Broadcom Tomahawk 6 102.4T ASICs = 12 switch ASICs**, flat one-tier all-to-all; each GPU runs **72 lanes of 200G UALoE = 1.8 TB/s (14.4 Tbit/s) uni-di scale-up**, 6 lanes to each of the 12 switches. **Only 432 of each switch's 512 200G lanes are used** — pure over-provisioning, because AMD buys merchant TH6 instead of a co-designed switch; Nvidia's **28.8T NVSwitch divides evenly across 72 GPUs with no wastage**. Scale-out: **400G Pollara → 800G Vulcano**, two NICs = 1.6 Tbit/s/GPU (up to a third NIC for 2.4 Tbit/s, each on an x8 UALink128 at 256 GB/s bidirectional), plus a **Pensando Salina 400 DPU** on the front end. The strategic point: AMD ships GPU, CPU, NIC and DPU but **no scale-up switch**, and **"because Broadcom is the only merchant provider shipping a 100T switch with 200G SerDes, Broadcom is AMD's only choice"** → **"AMD cannot reach extreme codesign for the complete rack solution."** Read across to [[AVGO]]. (Also: for Meta's MI455X variant there are **six 51.2T Minipack Ethernet switches in-rack** and the power shelf sits in a separate IT rack.)
  - **Power is the outlier, and it directly contradicts a claim already on this page.** SemiAnalysis models MI455X at **~240kW of server power and ~257kW all-in per rack, versus GB300 NVL72 at ~142kW server / ~159kW all-in** — "MI455X lands even beyond VR NVL72 in power draw." **This contradicts BofA's read-through** (in the Current-state "Watts to Tokens" note above) that **AMD's MI350X/MI400X platforms are "less power-hungry than Nvidia's"** on its rack-power budget analysis. Both are kept on the page deliberately: BofA is a top-down power-budget model, SemiAnalysis a bottoms-up rack build, and **the disagreement is the alpha** — it decides whether AMD wins or loses the "watt-constrained world deprioritizes the watt-loser" argument already in Bear (BG2 panel). Resolvable against real Helios deployment data from 2H26. (BofA "Watts to Tokens," 2026-05-25 vs SemiAnalysis, 2026-07-24)
  - **TCO — competitive on paper, with a stated haircut.** On capital cost and on total cost of ownership **MI455X lands between GB300 NVL72 and Vera Rubin NVL72**. On **marketed dense FP8**, it looks competitive with even VR NVL72 on **TCO per PFLOP and on TFLOPs/W**, and because its TCO per marketed PFLOP is lower it **can be rented cheaper than VR NVL72 at the same ~15.6% minimum project IRR hurdle**. The caveat is explicit and material: **"AMD has historically overstated TFLOPs in their chip specifications relative to what is achievable, and we suspect that AMD's model FLOPs utilization (MFU) is quite low, which likely leads to a lower effective training throughput."** So treat the TCO/PFLOP win as a marketed-spec result, not a delivered-throughput one — the same caution that applies to the "30% more tokens/$" headline above.

Mitigant worth keeping: with only **6 switch trays vs 9 on GB300 NVL72**, UALoE signal distance is shorter, and the 9-up/9-down compute-tray split equalises path length. Net for the thesis: this is the most specific, most falsifiable version of the Helios-ramp risk on the page — and it is **cost, yield and reliability**, not silicon.

## Risks
- **Intense competition** vs Intel (improving x86 supply / Diamond Rapids, Coral Rapids pulled to mid-2027) and Nvidia (Vera Rubin), plus merchant/custom Arm and ASIC scale-up (10-K FY25).
- **Foundry concentration on TSMC** for leading-edge HPC/AI wafers + advanced packaging (CoWoS-L) — supply tightness in HBM/wafers a gating risk to the ramp; AMD raised 2027 CoWoS bookings to ~120K wafers but realization is Helios-execution dependent (Jefferies / Q425 Techknowledge, 2025-12-17).
- **Customer concentration** in Data Center AI (OpenAI/Meta) and uncertain hyperscaler allocation of 2027-28 GPU share.
- **Export-control / China** revenue volatility (MI308/MI325 licensing "dynamic").
- **Memory/component cost inflation** dragging 2H Client + Gaming demand; console cycle in year 7 (semi-custom down significant double-digits in 2026).
- Warrant-related share dilution; GPU gross margin dilutive during ramp.
- **Rack power is an unresolved contradiction in the file — and it is the axis the bear case runs on.** SemiAnalysis's bottoms-up build puts **MI455X Helios at ~240kW server / ~257kW all-in per rack vs GB300 NVL72 at ~142kW / ~159kW**, i.e. "even beyond VR NVL72 in power draw" (SemiAnalysis "Can AMD break the CUDA Moat?", 2026-07-24) — the opposite sign to **BofA's power-budget read that AMD's MI350X/MI400X platforms are "less power-hungry than Nvidia's"** (BofA "Watts to Tokens," 2026-05-25, already on this page). Both views are retained; neither is discarded. If SemiAnalysis is right, the **BG2 "in a watt-constrained world the watt-loser silicon gets deprioritized"** objection in Bear gets materially stronger and the ~6-of-27-GW share ambition looks harder; if BofA is right, that objection largely dissolves. Note SemiAnalysis's own offsetting point — on *marketed* dense FP8, MI455X still screens competitive on **TFLOPs/W** — so the disagreement may partly be absolute rack power vs power per unit of (marketed) work. **Resolvable with real Helios deployment data from 2H26 and with any customer disclosure of racks-per-MW.**
- **Scale-up switch single-sourcing on Broadcom.** AMD ships the GPU, CPU, NIC and DPU but **no scale-up switch of its own**; because **Broadcom is the only merchant vendor shipping a 100T switch with 200G SerDes, it is AMD's only option**, which puts the Helios roadmap on a partner's execution and, per SemiAnalysis, means **"AMD cannot reach extreme codesign for the complete rack solution."** The over-provisioning cost is visible today (**only 432 of each TH6's 512 200G lanes used**, vs Nvidia's 28.8T NVSwitch dividing evenly across 72 GPUs with zero waste). UALink switches at 115.2T and 57.6T are "on the horizon, but not close enough to anchor this architecture." (SemiAnalysis, 2026-07-24; see [[AVGO]] / [[ALAB]])
- **HBM cost pass-through is a 2027 gross-margin risk, not just a supply risk.** SemiAnalysis expects a **significant HBM price increase in 2027** as memory suppliers move to match commodity-DRAM margins; AMD passes it through **at a lower incremental margin than corporate**, because most of its volume goes to large buyers with real negotiating leverage and credible alternatives → **gross-margin drag on the MI455X package**. This is the mechanism behind the existing "Q4 FY26 GM steps down ~100-150bps on MI450 mix" bullet, extended into 2027, and it is partly being pre-absorbed by de-spec (LPDDR5X removed entirely, DDR5 de-specced) rather than by price. (SemiAnalysis, 2026-07-24)
- **Internal-CI capacity as an execution risk, and the key-person/geography concentration behind it.** AMD's software quality gates are bottlenecked on GPU clusters it keeps reallocating (see Bear, Risk 2); separately, **"most of AMD's best 10x engineers are in Shanghai"** — the MoRI collective, the UMBP KV-cache offloading team and the disaggregated forward-deployed engineering team are mostly China-based, meaning a large share of the most differentiated parts of the ROCm stack sits in one geography with obvious export-control/geopolitical tail risk. (SemiAnalysis, 2026-07-24)
- **Memory-supply lock-up dynamic — flagged as a reader/community comment, NOT SemiAnalysis analyst body content:** a comment from Drew Van Pelt on SemiAnalysis's "Memory Mania" piece (comment section, p.35, 2026-02-06) speculates that AMD (and Meta) could be incentivized to lock up disproportionate long-term memory supply if they perceive a risk of being squeezed out as laggards in the memory-shortage environment — i.e. a fear-driven over-ordering dynamic that could tighten HBM/DRAM allocation further or strand AMD with excess commitments if demand undershoots. This is explicitly a third-party comment, not part of SemiAnalysis's own analysis, and should be weighted accordingly.

The Citi "AMD #1 on memory supply" read moves from ✓ to ⚠. Net for the quarter: **the technology and the customer roster are no longer the debate; the debate is (a) price, (b) the warrant economics, and now (c) two named, dated execution gates on rack manufacturing and internal CI** — with the notable and bullish asymmetry that the most credible bear on AMD software has just moved to the other side of the trade. **08-03 (run-inbox) adds one datapoint to each of those three legs and changes none of them. Net: the execution gates are unchanged, the evidence on the software gate has begun to run in AMD's favour, and the binding capacity question has migrated from packaging to N2 wafers.
