## Snapshot
Cloud data platform (the "AI Data Cloud") sold on **consumption**, not seats: customers buy capacity, and Snowflake recognizes revenue as compute/storage/AI tokens are used — AI is "not a SKU … it's on their existing spend" (Ramaswamy, Q1 FY26, 2025-05-21). Product revenue is ~95% of the total; the core is the analytics warehouse, with data engineering (Snowpark, Dynamic Tables, OpenFlow), collaboration/data sharing (43% of customers share with at least one "stable edge"), applications (Streamlit, Snowflake Postgres, Hybrid Tables) and — since FY26 — a first-party AI layer: Cortex AI → Cortex Analyst / Search → Snowflake Intelligence (business-user agent surface, now presented as **CoWork**) → **Cortex Code / "CoCo"** (agentic coding harness, GA 2026-02-05) → Cortex AI Gateway (model router) (Q2 FY27, 2026-09-02). Management's framing has moved in two years from "easiest, most cost-effective data platform" (Q3 FY25, 2024-11-20) to "the governed control plane for the agentic enterprise" (Q2 FY27). In the AI stack it is the **analytical-platform / AI-data-repository** layer — where enterprise context lands so agents can reason on it (theme page §2.1). 14,554 customers, 829 Forbes G2K (41% of the G2K), 828 customers >$1M TTM, 65 >$10M (Q2 FY27).

## Current state (Q2 FY27, call 2026-09-02)
**Third straight quarter of acceleration.** Product revenue **$1.49B, +37% y/y** (30% exit-FY26 → 34% → 37%; "7 points of acceleration in just two quarters"), second consecutive quarter of record sequential dollar growth. **NRR 126%** (flat q/q). **RPO $9B, +30%**, ~54% current (the current portion +42% y/y); bookings expected to skew increasingly to Q4 renewals. **692 net new customers (+32% y/y)**, 14 G2K adds; **48 customers crossed $1M TTM → 828**; 65 above $10M. Use cases deployed +89% y/y; use cases won per AE +43%. **Non-GAAP operating margin 15% (+400bp y/y)** on "disciplined headcount management" — 334 employees added YTD, 173 of them from Observe, vs 935 in the year-ago period. Cash + investments $4.3B (Ramaswamy / Robins, Q2 FY27).

**Guidance raised again.** FY27 product revenue **$6.07B, +36%** (from $5.84B/+31% in May and $5.66B/+27% in February), ~1pt from Observe; **Q3 $1.588–1.593B, +37–38%**; FY27 non-GAAP operating margin **14.5%** (from 13.5%), Q3 15.5%; FCF margin 23% reiterated. Methodology unchanged — "based on observed consumption patterns" (Robins). Half of the raise is AI, half is core: "the people who adopt [CoCo/CoWork] … are also increasing business within the core" (Robins to JPM).

**The AI cost of it — gross margin.** FY27 non-GAAP product gross margin guide cut to **74%** (from 75%): "a higher revenue mix from fast-growing AI workloads, which carry a lower contribution margin today" (Robins). Keirstead (UBS) asked whether a mix shift from frontier to open/cheaper models would help GM and whether model neutrality is a competitive weapon. Ramaswamy: "We are absolutely seeing a lot of interest in being able to switch between different models and also to optimize cost"; open-weight models run in-house carry "pretty different economics"; most CoCo requests arrive in "auto" mode where Snowflake pairs task to model. Kleinerman: neutrality is "absolutely" an advantage — customers who "made large commitments to one specific model company" regret it; frontier labs "have been revising prices down on a regular basis." Robins: product first, adoption second, margin third — "committed to driving operating leverage" even as GM dips. This is the tokenmaxxing routing debate arriving inside Snowflake's P&L.

**Product / adoption.** **CoCo 9,100+ accounts (+2,000 net in the quarter)**; **CoWork 5,800 accounts (+~11% q/q)**; gross retention on CoCo accounts "relatively flat" q/q; CoCo cost-management is a top-10 skill (a rebuttal to the "sticker-shock" bear). Cortex AI Gateway launched (policy-based model routing + Natoma actions: email, Slack, Jira); Cortex Sense (business semantics). 330+ GA capabilities in 1H (+35% y/y). New metric: time-to-80%-of-purchased-consumption for new cohorts has "very visibly improved." Migrations compressing — a network-equipment maker doing a Teradata migration in <3 quarters vs "two to three years." Frontier-engineering (FDE) program is outcome-paid: "we get paid only when we deliver outcomes" (Ramaswamy to Wolfe). Still on track for **GAAP profitability in Q4 FY28**. Not disclosed despite two asks: the AI-adopter consumption uplift and the AI revenue run-rate (last disclosed at $100M, Q3 FY26) — the only uplift figure in the corpus is second-hand: GS cites a Ramaswamy LinkedIn post that early CoCo adopters "consume 11% more of the platform" (Goldman Sachs · Gabriela Borges, 2026-06-07). **Sell-side reaction (2026-09-02/03):** unanimous on the print, split on the stock. ## Debate / thesis
- **Bull:** (1) **"The token path is data."** Jefferies' Thill: Databricks and Snowflake are "crushing it because they're in the token path" (Jefferies · Brent Thill / Samad Samana, 2026-06-01); Citrini's mechanism — agents generate machine work that consumption databases bill automatically while seat-based apps do not (Citrini, 2026-07-20). (2) **AI data repository, verified in the field.** UBS checks: Kraft Heinz declined Workday's HR AI and will "migrate that Workday-generated HR data into Snowflake and use AI tools on top"; a CFO is building a dedicated Snowflake instance to put finance agents on — "a real role as an AI data repository layer, driving usage" (UBS · Karl Keirstead, 2026-08-31). DB's Anthropic-partner check names Databricks and Snowflake as beneficiaries of the enterprise control-plane build-out (Deutsche Bank · Brad Zelnick, 2026-08-20). (3) **Numbers are doing it:** three quarters of acceleration to +37%, NRR back to 126% from 124% (Q1 FY26), net-new-customer growth +32–40% for four quarters, and the beat/raise cadence lifted the FY27 guide 27% → 36% in six months (calls, 2026-02-25 → 2026-09-02). UBS's pre-print bar — "36–37% revs growth in July, exiting the year at close to 40" — was met (UBS · Keirstead, 2026-08-31). (4) **Neutrality as moat:** Kleinerman on the regret of single-lab commitments; Snowflake's cloud-neutral history repeating at the model layer (Q2 FY27). (5) Jefferies' 200-client survey ranks Database among the categories *least* at threat from AI (Jefferies · William Beavington, 2026-05-20). (6) Buy-side: Octahedron holds SNOW top-3, sees growth moving "into the mid-high 30s" with Cortex Code layering on (Octahedron 2Q26 LP call, 2026-07-15); BofA's Fenske: SNOW/MDB are "along the right side of AI" (BofA · Brian Fenske, 2026-04-16).
- **Bear:** (1) **Databricks share loss.** Rothschild & Co Redburn (Alex Haissl) **maintained Sell and cut PT $170 → $110** on PyPI download data: SNOW's share of dbt-adapter downloads fell from c.30% (2022–mid-2024) to ~20%; Jan–Apr 2026 vs 4Q25 average daily downloads SNOW +8.4% vs Databricks +50.3% / hyperscalers +22.6% / other +27.8%; the SNOW:Databricks ratio went from 4–5x (2H22) to near parity; connector and Snowpark downloads plateauing since mid-2025 — "a debate about terminal value, not the next quarter" (Rothschild & Co Redburn · Alex Haissl, "First Cracks", 2026-05-14). The R&Co desk framed it as "terminal risk": c.10% share lost over two years, share falling since Iceberg tables were introduced, Databricks winning SQL transformation "which is not even its core focus"; investor pushback was on timing (downloads = installed base + new pipelines) and on Cortex/CoCo being invisible to PyPI (R&Co Redburn desk · Rowan Adley, 2026-05-26). Redburn concedes the blind spots (dbt inside Snowflake's managed runtime, Cortex REST API) and answers on timing — the share decline pre-dates dbt Projects GA (Nov-2025) and CoCo in Snowsight (Mar-2026). Octahedron (Databricks' largest holder) has it growing ~80% and shipping into warehouse, SIEM, CDP and database (2026-07-15). Counter: Thill — "when Databricks has a $140+ billion valuation, I still think Snowflake's undervalued" (2026-06-01); management says CoCo now targets Databricks migrations too (Q1 FY27). (2) **"The model eats the platform."** Frontier models get good enough at data tasks that customers bypass data software. UBS vetted it across **7 enterprise partners/customers** and "concluded that VERY FEW ENTERPRISES ARE DIRECTLY HARNESSING THE DATA CAPABILITIES OF LLMs IN A WAY THAT IS CURTAILING SPEND ON SNOWFLAKE, PALANTIR OR DATABRICKS" (UBS · Keirstead, SNOW preview, 2026-08-31) — a finding about today's spend, not 2028's architecture. Management's own version: "AI is going to disrupt potentially everybody, including us" (Ramaswamy, Q2 FY26, 2025-08-27). (3) **Growth quality / pass-through.** Part of infra-data growth is rev-rec of frontier-model access (UBS · Keirstead on Azure "as well as Databricks and others", 2026-07-30); at SNOW it shows up as product GM guided down to 74% and AI products carrying "a lower contribution margin today" (Robins, Q2 FY27). Singh asked directly about "irrational behavior or poor operational hygiene" in CoCo/CoWork consumption — the pandemic-cohort analogy (MS · Sanjit Singh, Q2 FY27). (4) **Crowding / valuation.** Infra-data was "a fan favorite all year … up 50 to 80% year to date"; "each of these stocks has set-up problems, just given richer multiples, more crowded positioning" (UBS · Keirstead, 2026-08-31). Sentiment ordering flipped from DDOG > MDB > SNOW pre-print to SNOW > DDOG > MDB after (JPM · Mark Schilsky, 2026-05-29; Barclays desk · Jeffrey Rand, 2026-06-09). Bernstein sits at **Market-Perform, PT $250** on 17.6x / 13.2x / 10.4x EV/Sales 2026–28 (Bernstein Gen-AI Handbook, prices 2026-06-16). (5) **Lumpiness/seasonality:** large migrations are "lumpy and not all that easy to predict" (Q3 FY26); bookings and FCF (>60% of collections) skew to Q4 (Q4 FY26, Q2 FY27); Observe is a ~150bp FY27 margin headwind (Q4 FY26).

## Risks
- **Consumption volatility**: quarterly beats are "less indicative … especially in a consumption model" (Robins, Q3 FY26); large migrations lumpy; a hyperscaler outage cost ~$1–2M in Q3 FY26.
- **AI gross-margin dilution**: AI products carry lower contribution margin; GM guide already cut 75% → 74% in one quarter (Q2 FY27); dependence on frontier-lab pricing and on bandwidth/compute terms with AWS.
- **Model-layer disintermediation** — the structural bear UBS vetted and found absent *today* (2026-08-31); management concedes AI "is going to disrupt potentially everybody, including us" (Q2 FY26).
- **Databricks / hyperscaler competition** (Fabric, OneLake; Redburn/R&Co share data) and Postgres taking new-app share (theme page §4).
- **Customer cost governance / sticker shock** as agents proliferate — per-user and per-agent caps being built (Q4 FY26, Q1 FY27); the 2022–23 optimization cycle is the precedent management itself cites.
- **Concentration in Q4 bookings / FCF** and in large FS customers (a $400M+ single deal, 7 nine-figure deals in Q4 FY26).
- **Execution / leadership churn**: CFO change (Scarpelli → Robins, Q3 FY26), two CROs in 18 months (Gannon → Beaulier), co-founder Dageville stepping back (Q1 FY27); ~200-person RIF in Q4 FY26.
- **Crowded positioning / multiple** after a 50–80% YTD move in the group (UBS, 2026-08-31).
