How does the HBM wafer share change the fab’s bit output?

01 · WHY AREA IS LOST

The HBM die is bigger for the same bits

Micron’s own decomposition: the 3:1 “is made up of primarily factors of the die size growth. HBM die size is larger than standard products in the same node in order to be able to provide the high bandwidth capability… and also based on the yields… in particular the assembly process.” 12-high carries “maybe a slightly higher trade ratio than 8-high”, and the ratio rises again with HBM4 as more of the die is dedicated to bandwidth. Sumit Sadana, Micron Q4 FY24 callback, 2024-09-25 HBM4 doubles the interface to 2048 bits (banks per die 128 → 256, ~2,000 I/O on the same shoreline). Micron Hot Chips via newsletters, 2026-08-23 Samsung: the ratio keeps rising each generation “driven by the increase in chip size”. GS/Samsung mgmt, 2026-09-18

02 · WHY YIELD COMPOUNDS

Three yield limits, only one of them elastic

HBM faces (1) packaging-stage stacking yield, (2) a larger die area leaving fewer known-good dies per wafer, (3) TSV wafer-process loss. “Only (1) is elastic”: stacking 4- or 8-high is “a far more mature process than stacking 12-high or 16-high, so simply lowering the stack height yields more HBM.” hbm-memory theme, 2026-08-31 ingest At 98% per core die, 12-Hi → 8-Hi lifts stack yield 1.084x; with stacking time it is up to ~1.63x effective output. hbm-memory, 2026-08-27 When one die in a 12-high fails, twelve good dies are lost together — that is why the penalty compounds.

03 · THE CONVERSION

From wafer to GPU, in units

Stack specs: HBM3E 12-high = 36 GB (12 × 24Gb dies). Micron, 2024-10-10 B300 and Rubin carry 8 stacks = 288 GB; HBM4 keeps 24Gb dies, HBM4E moves to 32Gb. JPM · Kwon, 2026-09-22; SemiAnalysis, 2026-08-24 Dies ~50 µm thick, “millions of TSVs” in a 12-layer stack. DAMNANG, 2026-03-20
