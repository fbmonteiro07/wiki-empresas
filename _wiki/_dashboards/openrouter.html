Month-only anchors (e.g. 2026-03) are dated to the 15th by convention — a ±10-day shift moves these rates materially. The 05→07 segment is marked semi-circular because its later endpoint is our own feed, not an independent press figure.

Methodology — what we do NOT publish here, and why. (1) No single-snapshot growth proxy. Comparing the trailing-week run-rate to the trailing-month-ex-week rate and compounding it read +13.7%/wk on the 07-21 snapshot while the next real snapshot printed negative — it compounds a level gap between overlapping windows, so one hot week becomes a "rate". Left un-compounded as a "momentum" level it swings +0.2% / +9.7% / +9.7% / +20.1% across two defensible choices (month window 28d vs 30d × two aggregation bases), so it carries no information and is gone. (2) The week-to-week segment is not compounded into a %/week rate: the series is step-driven (promos start and end), so a compound rate would describe no real process. Only multi-month anchor segments are normalised to %/week. (3) A fixed aggregation bug is now corrected: the feed buckets each model by its last active day in the window, and we previously kept only the final bucket — silently deleting dormant models' real volume (0.0% of the 07-21 week but 3.0% of the 07-27 week, an asymmetry that by itself manufactured ~3pp of a fake decline). All windows now sum every bucket; verified no model appears in two buckets. (4) N=2. This is one observed transition. Treat direction as informative and magnitude as provisional until several more Mondays land.

The tell: at today's blend the cost curve tracks volume (~5.4× since May), but reported spend is ~flat ($83M Mar → $76M Jun) — so the blended price per token is collapsing as free tiers and cheap open models eat the marginal token. The "cost @ blend" line is a ceiling scenario (list price, no caching); realized sits ~⅓ of it.

How the input/output math is decided. The mix is observed, not assumed: every model row in OpenRouter's feed reports total_prompt_tokens and total_completion_tokens; the ratio is their quotient (platform 38.4:1; Anthropic 65:1, DeepSeek 20:1, Xiaomi 113:1). It drives margin through two asymmetries — cost: serving input (prefill: parallel, compute-bound) runs ~5× cheaper per token than output (decode: sequential, HBM-bandwidth-bound), per DeepSeek's own disclosure (73.7k vs 14.8k tok/s/node); price: providers list input at ~20-35% of the output price. Input-heavy traffic therefore stacks cheap-to-serve tokens priced above cost → structurally fat blended margin. The serving-margin model blends at each lab's own observed mix — using a chat-like 3:1 where the traffic is really 20-65:1 understates margin by ~15-25 points.

The pricing-power read. Frontier first-party pricing is venue-independent — observed, not just asserted: the routing feed shows Claude Opus 4.8 at $5/$25 and Sonnet 5 at $2/$10 identically on Anthropic, Bedrock, Vertex and Azure (regional tiers +10%), and Sonnet 5's $2/$10 promo is printed on AWS's own page. (AWS's page also shows a $6/$30 Claude line — those rows are labeled "Claude 3.5 Sonnet (extended access)", a legacy-model keep-alive fee; the page is JS-rendered and machine-reads badly, so treat any scrape of it with care.) Where hyperscalers DO mark up is open-weight hosting: DeepSeek V3.2 costs ~2.3× more per input token on Bedrock ($0.62) than its cheapest route ($0.27), Qwen3-Next ~1.5×; Mistral Large 3 is the exception at exactly list. So the cloud premium sits on open models (managed-hosting convenience), while frontier-lab pricing is venue-independent — the lab, not the cloud, owns the token P&L.

Beyond the sticker: discount & surcharge mechanics (official pages, 2026-07-21)

Among the first-party frontier labs (Anthropic / OpenAI / Google), cache-read ≈ 10% of input and batch ≈ 50% off — commoditized. Open-weight makers run wider: DeepSeek prices cache-read at 20–52% of input depending on model, xAI ~15%. The real differentiation is long-context surcharges (Gemini Pro doubles input above 200K ctx — the lever that monetizes big-context agents) and regional premia.
