Month-only anchors (e.g. 2026-03) are dated to the 15th by convention — a ±10-day shift moves these rates materially. The 05→07 segment is marked semi-circular because its later endpoint is our own feed, not an independent press figure.

Methodology — what we do NOT publish here, and why. (1) No single-snapshot growth proxy. Comparing the trailing-week run-rate to the trailing-month-ex-week rate and compounding it read +13.7%/wk on the 07-21 snapshot while the next real snapshot printed negative — it compounds a level gap between overlapping windows, so one hot week becomes a "rate". Left un-compounded as a "momentum" level it swings +0.2% / +9.7% / +9.7% / +20.1% across two defensible choices (month window 28d vs 30d × two aggregation bases), so it carries no information and is gone. (2) The week-to-week segment is not compounded into a %/week rate: the series is step-driven (promos start and end), so a compound rate would describe no real process. Only multi-month anchor segments are normalised to %/week. (3) A fixed aggregation bug is now corrected: the feed buckets each model by its last active day in the window, and we previously kept only the final bucket — silently deleting dormant models' real volume (0.0% of the 07-21 week but 3.0% of the 07-27 week, an asymmetry that by itself manufactured ~3pp of a fake decline). All windows now sum every bucket; verified no model appears in two buckets. (4) N=2. This is one observed transition. Treat direction as informative and magnitude as provisional until several more Mondays land.

The tell: at today's blend the cost curve tracks volume (~5.5× since May), but reported spend is ~flat ($83M Mar → $76M Jun) — so the blended price per token is collapsing as free tiers and cheap open models eat the marginal token. The "cost @ blend" line is a ceiling scenario (list price, no caching); realized sits ~⅓ of it.

How the input/output math is decided. The mix is observed, not assumed: every model row in OpenRouter's feed reports total_prompt_tokens and total_completion_tokens; the ratio is their quotient (platform 39.8:1; Anthropic 65:1, DeepSeek 20:1, Xiaomi 113:1). It drives margin through two asymmetries — cost: serving input (prefill: parallel, compute-bound) runs ~5× cheaper per token than output (decode: sequential, HBM-bandwidth-bound), per DeepSeek's own disclosure (73.7k vs 14.8k tok/s/node); price: providers list input at ~20-35% of the output price. Input-heavy traffic therefore stacks cheap-to-serve tokens priced above cost → structurally fat blended margin. The serving-margin model blends at each lab's own observed mix — using a chat-like 3:1 where the traffic is really 20-65:1 understates margin by ~15-25 points.

The pricing-power read. Frontier first-party pricing is venue-independent — observed, not just asserted: the routing feed shows Claude Opus 4.8 at $5/$25 and Sonnet 5 at $2/$10 identically on Anthropic, Bedrock, Vertex and Azure (regional tiers +10%), and Sonnet 5's $2/$10 promo is printed on AWS's own page. (AWS's page also shows a $6/$30 Claude line — those rows are labeled "Claude 3.5 Sonnet (extended access)", a legacy-model keep-alive fee; the page is JS-rendered and machine-reads badly, so treat any scrape of it with care.) Where hyperscalers DO mark up is open-weight hosting: DeepSeek V3.2 costs ~2.3× more per input token on Bedrock ($0.62) than its cheapest route ($0.27), Qwen3-Next ~1.5×; Mistral Large 3 is the exception at exactly list. So the cloud premium sits on open models (managed-hosting convenience), while frontier-lab pricing is venue-independent — the lab, not the cloud, owns the token P&L.

Who subsidizes whom. The neocloud-vs-maker relationship flips by lab: DeepSeek's own API is the FLOOR — Together/Fireworks charge up to ~4× DeepSeek's price for V4 Pro (its $0.44/$0.87 looks subsidized; no third party can serve it at that price). Z.ai and Moonshot are the opposite — neoclouds undercut the maker's API by ~40% (GLM-5.2: StreamLake $0.82 vs Z.AI $1.40). Together prices AT maker list (GLM $1.40/$4.40, MiniMax $0.30/$1.20 — matches its own pricing page) — it competes on reliability/quality, not price. And Google shows up as a neocloud (Model Garden) serving DeepSeek/Qwen/Llama at mid-pack prices. Sources and methodology

Silicon Data indices are delivered through the local Bloomberg Terminal: SDH100RT, SDA100RT and SDB200RT, field PX_LAST. SemiAnalysis publishes its H100, A100 and B200 spot-contract composites and the public H100 one-year survey. SAH100SC is the H100 composite delivered through Bloomberg.

The public API supplies dated daily observations. We retain those values without filling missing days or smoothing. Bloomberg and public delivery times can differ. Contract period dates identify the reporting period, not a trading-day close. Older observations and publisher revisions remain in archived source snapshots. No access to subscriber-only GPU contract data is used.

Snapshots remain available offline. Collection failures retain the last good source and display a warning. Daily observations older than seven calendar days are marked as old when the page opens.
