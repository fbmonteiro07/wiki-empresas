Definitions, coverage & interpretation

How to read it. Same framing as NVIDIA's "Open Models Enable New Builders" slide (Source: OpenRouter, monthly), rebuilt weekly from the feed itself. Classification is source-side, per model: open-weight = the model's OpenRouter listing carries a Hugging Face link; proprietary = listed without one; stealth previews (stealth/*, openrouter/*-alpha) stay unattributed while their author is undisclosed. Two bases: from the week of Jul 13, 2026 the split uses our trailing-7-day capture of every model (12 weeks, one more each Monday); before that OpenRouter's public history names only the nine largest models per week, so the long tail (35–55% of tokens) is drawn as unattributed rather than allocated — on the weeks we can check, the tail is far more proprietary than the top 9. OpenRouter is a developer/API slice (no first-party enterprise API, no consumer subscriptions) and counts free-tier tokens; weekly totals are OpenRouter's own, complete weeks only. Reconciling to NVIDIA's ~75% (from ~40% a year ago): NVIDIA's cut is monthly and its rule is not published; ours gives 53% plus the 23% stealth band (76% if every stealth preview turns out open), and it counts the API-only variants of open families (Qwen Plus, GLM Turbo, MiMo Pro) as proprietary. Treat the level as definition-dependent; the direction and the traffic multiple are not.

How the study is calculated · inputs, checks & sensitivity
HARD · observed tokens and listed ratesPARTIAL · snapshot-price application and weight classificationESTIMATE · cache-hit scenarios
  1. Volume: sum prompt and completion tokens across every row, including models last active earlier in the window.
  2. Average price: calculate (input list price + output list price) ÷ 2 for each valid standard text model, then average across models. This 50:50 convention is not actual input/output mix.
  3. Estimated spend: sum input tokens × input price + output tokens × output price for exact, same-vintage price matches.
  4. Weighted price: estimated matched spend ÷ matched tokens × 1,000,000. Free-tagged tokens are included at zero price; missing prices remain unknown.

How the input/output math is decided. The mix is observed, not assumed: every model row in OpenRouter's feed reports total_prompt_tokens and total_completion_tokens; the ratio is their quotient (platform 36.4:1; Anthropic 65:1, DeepSeek 20:1, Xiaomi 113:1). It drives margin through two asymmetries — cost: serving input (prefill: parallel, compute-bound) runs ~5× cheaper per token than output (decode: sequential, HBM-bandwidth-bound), per DeepSeek's own disclosure (73.7k vs 14.8k tok/s/node); price: providers list input at ~20-35% of the output price. Input-heavy traffic therefore stacks cheap-to-serve tokens priced above cost → structurally fat blended margin. The serving-margin model blends at each lab's own observed mix — using a chat-like 3:1 where the traffic is really 20-65:1 understates margin by ~15-25 points.

So the cloud premium sits on open models (managed-hosting convenience), while frontier-lab pricing is venue-independent — the lab, not the cloud, owns the token P&L.

Sources and methodology

Silicon Data indices are delivered through the local Bloomberg Terminal: SDH100RT, SDA100RT and SDB200RT, field PX_LAST. SemiAnalysis publishes its H100, A100 and B200 spot-contract composites and the public H100 one-year survey. SAH100SC is the H100 composite delivered through Bloomberg.

The public API supplies dated daily observations. We retain those values without filling missing days or smoothing. Bloomberg and public delivery times can differ. Contract period dates identify the reporting period, not a trading-day close. Older observations and publisher revisions remain in archived source snapshots. No access to subscriber-only GPU contract data is used.

Snapshots remain available offline. Collection failures retain the last good source and display a warning. Daily observations older than seven calendar days are marked as old when the page opens.
