Ramp sees a paid AI transaction at 55.9% of the 70,000+ businesses on its platform in Sep-26, up from 43.8% a year earlier and 24.5% at the start of the series (Jul-23). Verdict for the sample: volume, not price.
Verdict for the sample: volume, not price. Since the ladder flip, token volume in the sample grew 9.99x while blended price per token fell to 0.57x of its Dec-25 level — so dollars grew less than tokens, and the spend acceleration understates volume growth rather than overstating it. The Sep-02 caution on this page (that the +12.44%/mo of the old vintage was partly price/mix) was right to be asked and is answered the other way for this sample: the mix moved up the ladder and the blended price still fell, because cached-input tokens and cheaper tiers dilute the average, and list prices themselves came down. The cleanest price read confirms the mix story on the way up: Anthropic’s output $/M in the sample went $11.39 (12 Jan 25) → $19.69 (Dec-25) → $16.35 (11 Oct 26) — up with the ladder, then easing — so the mix effect is real at the output-token level and is simply outweighed by cache share and volume in the blend. Limits: the token sample is the subset of Ramp customers using its TSM product (Ramp: “not representative of Ramp’s total AI spend volume, though results are directionally similar”); the spend-per-employee series is the whole panel; the two are not the same firms. Blended $/M mixes token types (cached input is a fraction of list), so a falling blend is partly cache share, not only price cuts — section 8 separates output and uncached input.

Analysis & interpretation
8 · The token sample: 72.5 tn tokens and $33.7M a weekToken Spend Management sample · weekly · 809 model series, 14 makers

Ramp’s TSM product imports usage and billing from connected providers, so this cut is the only one with tokens in it and the only one where Google, Meta, DeepSeek and the Chinese labs appear at all. In the week to 11 Oct 26 Anthropic carried 58.9% of tokens and 60.1% of dollars; OpenAI 38.2% / 36.4%; Google 1.2% of tokens (first seen 12 Jan 25); everything else 0.3%. The sample’s own blended price fell from $2.45 to $0.80 per million tokens for Anthropic and $1.06 to $0.48 for OpenAI between 12 Jan 25 and 11 Oct 26.

Sources & methodologyread this before quoting anything above

Four different denominators, one panel. (i) Adoption counts firms with any AI payment (section A); (ii) vendor penetration counts firms paying a vendor, overlapping (B: Anthropic 43.8% vs OpenAI 40.6% in Sep-26); (iii) model share is share of named-model usage and sums to 100 (1: 53.8% vs 43.7% in Sep-26); (iv) token share is the TSM sub-sample’s token volume (8: 58.9% vs 38.2%). Read together: Anthropic and OpenAI are close on customer count while Anthropic takes roughly 1.2x the usage — the lead is intensity per customer. “Anthropic has 53.8% of the enterprise LLM market” is not a sentence any of these supports.
  • COVERAGE: the panel names only Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI and Cursor. Google/Gemini, Meta, Mistral and DeepSeek appear in NO month. This is share of the covered set, not share of enterprise LLM usage, and it is not a market share.
  • BASIS: what the share is weighted by (spend, API calls, tokens, or firms) is not stated on the page. The overall breakdown carries only named models; the curated segment cuts add an 'other provider' bucket per vendor.
  • NOT the same cut as Ramp's vendor-adoption series (share of US firms paying each vendor, now primary in panel.json / dashboard section B): that counts FIRMS and overlaps across vendors; this sums to 100. The two are reconcilable only as penetration vs intensity -- they are not alternative estimates of one quantity.
  • VINTAGE IS LEFT-CENSORED: model age is measured from first appearance in the panel (Jan-2025), not from true release. Models live in the first month are older than they read, so the >12-month bucket is only meaningful from 2026-01.
  • Anthropic's Haiku<Sonnet<Opus<Fable ordering is the wiki's read (ANTHROPIC.md), not a Ramp field. Ramp supplies no tier, price or token weighting.
  • Latest month can restate, and a model's debut month is a partial month of availability -- a debut share understates a launch's run-rate.
  • Cohort definitions per Ramp's published note: 'Spend per Employee' = monthly observed AI spending / employee count for businesses in the reported cohort; 'top 10%' / 'top 1%' are the MEDIAN within that cohort.
  • RESTATED: Ramp re-cut the whole history in the Oct-2026 vintage (every month moved; see restatement.json). Quote the vintage with the number.
  • Cohorts are re-cut every month, so a cohort's MoM step is part re-ranking and part same-company growth. This is why top-1% monthly sigma is ~3x the median's.
  • Per-employee is a ratio: headcount moves the denominator.
  • Latest month can restate. 2026-07 top-10% prints exactly 650.00 -- a round number to the cent in a two-decimal series reads as rounded or preliminary.
  • Panel = Ramp's US corporate-card / bill-pay customer base, not total enterprise AI spend, and not a public-market revenue proxy.
  • TOKEN SAMPLE: Ramp Token Spend Management product sample: usage + billing imported from connected AI providers. Ramp: a SAMPLE of its businesses, 'not representative of Ramp's total AI spend volume, though results are directionally similar'.
What would change the picture. OpenAI gaining usage share for a third consecutive month; Google rows appearing in the model-share panel (they exist only in the token sample, at 1.2% of tokens); the adoption curve flattening further (+5.7 pp in the last six months); the ≤3-month vintage band falling below ~50%; or Ramp restating again — this vintage already moved every month of the spend series.
