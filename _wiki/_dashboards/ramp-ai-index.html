5 · The cross-read — and the caution it carriesladder shift vs spend per employee, same axis

Two separate panels, one shared time axis, dashed rule at Dec-25. Anthropic’s mix flips to Opus in the same month the spend series changes regime: median spend per employee compounded +3.96%/mo over the twelve months to Dec-25, then +12.44%/mo over the seven months since ($5.26 → $11.95, +127%).

The wiki currently reads the spend series as evidence of demand diffusion (themes/macro-cycle.md: “spend diffusion IS accelerating sharply”). This chart says that read is not clean. The same panel shows a simultaneous mix shift up the price ladder, and a flagship token costs a multiple of a mid-tier one. Some unknown part of the +12.44%/mo is therefore price and mix, not more AI being used. The two series cannot separate them: Ramp gives spend and model identity, not tokens. Neither the volume read nor the price read is provable from this file — what is provable is that whoever quotes the spend acceleration as diffusion owes a mix adjustment. Resolving it needs per-model token volume or list-price weights, neither of which is in the panel. Co-timing is not causation in either direction; both could follow a third driver (agentic coding adoption is the obvious candidate).

7 · Reconciliation and basis trapsread this before quoting anything above

The 71.4%-vs-28.1% trap. _wiki/ANTHROPIC.md carries a different Ramp cut: as of Jun-2026 Anthropic is the most-used AI vendor among US businesses at ~41% of firms vs OpenAI ~39.5% (Big Technology podcast w/ Ramp’s Ara Kharazian, 2026-06-18). This page has Anthropic at 71.4% and OpenAI at 28.1% for the same month. Both can be right — they are different denominators. The vendor-adoption cut counts firms and lets a firm be counted for both vendors (it need not sum to 100); this cut is a share of named-model usage and does sum to 100. Read together they say Anthropic and OpenAI are close to tied on enterprise penetration while Anthropic takes roughly 2.5x the model-level share — i.e. Anthropic’s lead here is intensity per customer, not customer count. That is a claim worth carrying; “Anthropic has 71% of the enterprise LLM market” is not.
  • COVERAGE: the panel names only Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI and Cursor. Google/Gemini, Meta, Mistral and DeepSeek appear in NO month. This is share of the covered set, not share of enterprise LLM usage, and it is not a market share.
  • BASIS: what the share is weighted by (spend, API calls, tokens, or firms) is not stated in the export. Every row is model_type='Named model', which implies Ramp also holds a non-named bucket that this export excludes.
  • NOT the same cut as Ramp's vendor-adoption number (~41% of US firms use Anthropic vs ~39.5% OpenAI, Jun-2026, on ANTHROPIC.md): that counts FIRMS and overlaps across vendors; this sums to 100. The two are reconcilable only as penetration vs intensity -- they are not alternative estimates of one quantity.
  • VINTAGE IS LEFT-CENSORED: model age is measured from first appearance in the panel (Jan-2025), not from true release. Models live in the first month are older than they read, so the >12-month bucket is only meaningful from 2026-01.
  • Anthropic's Haiku<Sonnet<Opus<Fable ordering is the wiki's read (ANTHROPIC.md), not a Ramp field. Ramp supplies no tier, price or token weighting.
  • Latest month can restate, and a model's debut month is a partial month of availability -- a debut share understates a launch's run-rate.
What would change the picture. A second consecutive month of OpenAI share gains (Jul-26’s +7.9pp was one launch); any appearance of Google/Gemini rows, which would restate every share on this page; a Ramp methodology note fixing the weighting basis; or the vintage ≤3m band falling below ~50%, which would be the first sign that model releases have stopped resetting the panel.
