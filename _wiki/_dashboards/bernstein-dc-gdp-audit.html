The arithmetic

State ratio = project value assigned to construction / nominal state GDP. Labor ratio = state ratio × assumed labor fraction. Wyoming's printed components imply 36 / (36 + 63) = 36.36% labor; 8% × 36.36% = 2.91%, which rounds to 3%. This is a PARTIAL calibration to rounded labels, not proof of Bernstein's exact assumption, nor an independent labor-cost check.

Why the GDP interpretation fails

  • Projects span multiple years. A freeze affects spending that would otherwise occur during the period, not the entire original budget.
  • State GDP measures local value added. Imported or out-of-state equipment cannot all be assigned to the host state's production. Land transactions are not new production.
  • Labor cost is not automatically local value added: distinguish compensation, contractor invoices, off-site fabrication and local work.
  • Deduplicate campus costs and separate active, construction and future phases. Missing costs cannot be treated as zero.
  • To calculate aggregate exposure use sum of eligible budgets / sum of GDP for the same geography, not an unweighted mean of state percentages. The exhibit alone does not identify how 8% was aggregated.

A proper annual impact estimate needs the baseline annual spending schedule, the spending actually displaced by a freeze, local value-added content, and any redeployment/offsets. No GDP-loss number is estimated here. Sensitivity is linear in budgets and labor share and inverse in GDP; doubling the spending horizon halves a simple annualized exposure proxy, but does not establish GDP impact.
