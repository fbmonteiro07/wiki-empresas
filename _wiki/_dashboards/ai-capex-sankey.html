Where the sources disagree

Variant perception first. The wiki corpus is ahead of the BNP exhibit on memory, on total IT share and on the WFE conversion; it is close on the non-IT half. Bars are $ per $100 of AI capex.

Source-by-source comparison

Same buckets, every source on disk. Blank cells mean the source does not split that far. Basis flags matter: JPM's memory share is of CSP hardware capex (no land, shell or most of the power stack), so it is structurally higher than a share of total capex.

Dollars per gigawatt — the anchors behind the shares

Build cost per GW, never revenue per GW. The standing wiki rule applies: facility GW, IT-load GW and 800V-subset GW are different bases and must not be mixed.

Method and tags

HARD — the source states the number in that form. DERIVED — arithmetic on two sourced numbers, shown in the evidence panel. EST — house judgement or a ratio borrowed from one source and applied to another's level. BNP — the pasted exhibit as read; the note date was not visible on the figure and is not confirmed.
  • House top level (chips · networking+CPU+memory/storage · shell/power/site) is the Capstone AI Model Resumo | Total split of all-player AI capex, CY26E–CY28E. Sub-splits inside each bucket are corpus ratios applied to those levels and are tagged DERIVED or EST.
  • The WFE leg uses Lam's August-2026 conversion ($9–10 of WFE per $100 of AI capex) and is drawn from the silicon nodes pro-rata; segment shares are industry-wide (litho, etch+dep, process control, WLP), not leading-edge-only.
  • The BNP exhibit does not label how the $8 of WFE splits across accelerator, memory and CPU; it is drawn pro-rata to those three nodes.
  • Memory is the fork: the AI Model carries HBM inside the chip bucket (~$8) while the house memory-capex model and the sell-side memory desks size total memory at 31–55% of the relevant denominator. Both are shown; neither is netted.
