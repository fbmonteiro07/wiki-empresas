Labs → System → Cloud — the gross-up

Direction: the two frontier labs we model (OpenAI + adjusted Anthropic, house AI Model) are the anchor; the system is grossed up off their market share; the cloud bill then follows from the system. Revenue grosses at 1/srev. Compute grosses on tokens, not revenue — because a served token costs the same whoever sells it — at 1+(1/stok−1)·rcost, where rcost is the rest of the system's cost per token relative to the big-2. Since frontier labs earn far more per token than they cost, srev ≫ stok and the cloud bill grosses up harder than revenue does.

The monetisation asymmetry

Big-2 share of system revenue vs share of system tokens. The gap is the yield premium — and it is what makes the compute gross-up bigger than the revenue gross-up.

System cloud bill — inference vs training

Inference grosses on tokens; training grosses on revenue share (it scales with lab ambition, not with served tokens).

Calibration — against two independent house builds

The share is the load-bearing input, so it is taken from house work, not guessed — but be clear what the check is. srev is the AI Labs Revenue MAP's share path (0.71 → 0.50 by 2035, a mid-year ARR share); the "LLM MAP" line is a second house construction that shares the same $140bn mid-26 anchor (0.71 = 100/140), so the CY26 ratio is a vintage check, not corroboration. The LLM MAP also holds the big-2 share flat at 72.6% where this path declines — the two house views disagree on the trend, so quote the range. rcost is back-solved on CY26 (that year is the fit); CY27–29 test it against OpenRouter's observed open price (weakly independent — the MAP's open price is a residual, but it takes IN_RATIO and K3 marks from OpenRouter); CY30 fails. The LLM MAP's own big-2 token line (162 → 1,201 Q) sits 6% above ours in CY26 and 22% above in CY30, because it embeds cost deflation we hold flat — the CY30 cloud bill differs ~10% between the two.

Labs Official Ref → how much of the labs' spend is the clouds' required revenue?

Coverage = the two labs' compute bill (inference + training — the reference tab's row 19, what OpenAI + Anthropic pay for compute) ÷ the cloud fleet's hurdle revenue (ROIC Cloud r22/r23: what the in-service AI fleet must earn for after-tax EBIT on average net assets to hit 15% / 20% ROIC). The remainder is the gap the rest of the market — enterprises, Google/Meta first-party, everyone else — must pay for the fleet to clear its hurdle. Caveat: CY26–27 coverage above 1x is inflated by the workbook's 2-year commissioning lag (the in-service fleet is still small while the labs already rent capacity) — the trend is the message, not the early level.
