The repeated process loop

Deposit → coat / expose / develop → etch → strip / clean → measure. Some layers follow other routes. CMP flattens selected levels; implantation and annealing establish electrical properties. Cleaning and inspection repeat throughout.

Lithography defines the stencil. Etching transfers it. Deposition supplies material. EUV does not drill the capacitor hole. More exposures do not mean more stacked memory tiers.

How to read this sketch

The wordline controls an access transistor. The bitline carries data. A separate storage contact connects to a capacitor with two electrodes separated by an insulator.

1a–1d are successive manufacturing generations, not stages of one wafer. Geometry changes are qualitative, not measured node comparisons. This is conventional DRAM rendered in 3D; HBM die stacking and monolithic 3D DRAM are different concepts.

EUV / DUV: where the intensity changes

Earlier-node DUV route

DUV prints a seed pattern → spacer deposition / etch or additional patterning → transfer the final pattern into the hard mask.

Selected critical layers at later nodes

EUV can print finer features with fewer pattern-splitting operations. More critical layers migrate to EUV; some may eventually require multiple EUV patterning cycles.

After the mask is made

Deep plasma etch makes the hole. Deposition coats it. These operations do not consume EUV photons. DUV remains on less critical layers.

Two different intensity measures: EUV exposure work can rise while DUV work on a converted layer falls. That does not establish a drop in total DUV fleet demand: wafer volume and the remaining layer mix matter. Nor does it guarantee more total etch/deposition steps—EUV can remove some DUV multipatterning operations.
