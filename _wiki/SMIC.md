## Snapshot
China's largest pure-play foundry (0981 HK / 688981 SH), the national champion for domestic semiconductor manufacturing. Predominantly a **mature/legacy-node** house — 12-inch is ~76-77% of revenue, 8-inch ~23-24% (Q1 2026, 2026-05-15) — serving consumer electronics (~43-46%), smartphone (~19-25%), computer/tablet (~14-15%), industrial/automotive (~11-14%) and connectivity/IoT (~7-8%). Revenue is overwhelmingly China-facing (~85-89% domestic), making SMIC the cleanest listed proxy for the **China semiconductor self-sufficiency / import-substitution** theme. It also runs the only credible advanced-node line inside China — pushing 7nm (N+1/N+2, reportedly 5nm-class) via **DUV multi-patterning** since it is cut off from EUV under US export controls — which makes it Huawei's de facto leading-edge fab and the production backbone for domestic AI silicon (Huawei Ascend 910B/910C are made on SMIC 7nm; MS China DataCenter, 2025-04-29). In the chain: TSMC owns the leading edge globally; SMIC is the China-localized substitute pulling mature-node demand onshore and the bottleneck for any domestic AI/advanced-logic ambition. The sell-side now bifurcates sharply: Bernstein rates SMIC **Outperform** as the chief beneficiary of Huawei's AI scale-up (Bernstein Huawei roadmap, 2025-09-22), while MS sits **Equal-weight** flagging the mature-node downcycle (MS AI supply chain, 2025-08-18) — see thesis.

## Advanced-node / Huawei supply line (the policy-driven optionality)
- **SMIC is Huawei's wafer provider for advanced logic.** Huawei Ascend 910B/910C run on SMIC 7nm (N+1/N+2-class); foreign fabs cannot manufacture Huawei advanced-node chips under US restrictions, so SMIC remains the sole supplier (MS China DataCenter, 2025-04-29). MS wafer math: 910B die ~666mm², ~70-75 good dies per 12-inch wafer → **10kwpm could yield ~200k 910B units/month**; the 910C die has roughly doubled, lowering yield, so MS estimates **53-106k 910C units/month** at 15-30% yield on the same 10wpm — the mid-to-high end of which it judges enough to meet Chinese hyperscaler demand if H20 stays unavailable.
- **Capacity expansion is the swing factor.** Jefferies channel checks (Q425 Techknowledge, 2025-12-17) put SMIC N7 capacity at **~15kwpm going to ~25kwpm**, allocated roughly: ~10k mobile (Huawei Qilin/Kirin, 110-120mm² die, 50-60% yield supports ~50m smartphone units), ~2k CPU (Kunpeng/Loongson/Feiteng/Hygon), and an AI ramp of Ascend ~5k + Cambricon ~0.5k + others ~3.5k wpm; at 20-30% yield on large dies that implies ~900k–2m AI chips/year. FT (via Bernstein Huawei roadmap, 2025-09-22) reported **local AI production capacity to triple in 2026**, corroborated by Bernstein's WFE-import tracker showing stronger-than-expected China capex.
- **Huawei roadmap is the demand pull.** Bernstein's Huawei roadmap (2025-09-22): Ascend 950DT/960/970 launching 4Q26/4Q27/4Q28; Huawei's single chip is only ~6% of Nvidia (VR200) performance, so it competes through scale — putting ~114x more chips into a SuperPoD via its UB networking protocol. That "more chips, less-advanced node" strategy requires **~16x more chips**, which Bernstein calls **inflationary for foundry/WFE** and the core of its Outperform — SMIC as the leading provider of advanced-logic capacity directly supporting Huawei accelerator production. Huawei publicly articulating the roadmap signals confidence its **local foundry supply is secured** (though Bernstein notes the chain is not yet fully localized).
- **Onshoring is real but costly.** Bernstein's foundry-decoupling work (Semis deck, 2025-05-05): Chinese IC local-sourcing rose from 30-35% (2017-20) to 40-46% (2021-24); Huawei and other Chinese customers shifted **~$1-3bn/yr more advanced-node production onshore to SMIC**. But SMIC spent **>$10bn of extra capex over 2020-24** to bring advanced-node production home — a heavy cost burden — and Bernstein stays conservative on Huawei smartphone units because SMIC is **struggling to improve advanced-node yield** without EUV. Decoupling is happening mostly in advanced nodes; in mature nodes localization barely moved (high-40s% → low-50s%).
- **STEEL teardown quantifies what “good enough” means — density caught up, PPA did not.** SemiAnalysis measured the Kirin 9030's SMIC N+3 at **32.5nm M0 pitch and ~113M transistors/mm²**, versus **40nm and ~108M/mm²** for the TSMC N6-based MediaTek Helio G99. The density result is real, but it is achieved through **self-aligned quadruple patterning (SAQP) on the tightest layer plus DTCO**, adding masks, overlay sensitivity, cost and yield risk. The end-product check is much less flattering: SemiAnalysis says Kirin 9030 performance is roughly at **three-year-old Android-flagship** level; Apple's small efficiency core beats Huawei's large prime core on integer performance at roughly **1W vs 4.5W**, and Apple's 2020 M1 remains ~35% faster per clock at similar power. Read-through: export controls have not frozen Chinese process learning, but N+3 is an economic/PPA workaround rather than leading-edge parity (SemiAnalysis / STEEL Team, 2026-06-14).
- **Density ≠ leading-edge parity — the headline resolved (SemiAnalysis email edition, 2026-06-14):** the report's own answer to its "is N+3's metal pitch smaller than Intel 18A's?" title is *tighter pitch, not denser chip* — on a normalized Bohr-density basis SMIC N+3 is **~114 MTr/mm², ~38% BELOW Intel 18A's HD library**. The 32.5nm M0 beats Panther Lake's 36nm M0 only because **Intel deliberately ships 18A at a looser 36nm** (18A *supports* 32nm M0; it runs the loosest M0 of the leading edge because **PowerVia backside power** pulls routing off the front side and relieves congestion). Huawei's answer to the EUV-less gap is **3D stacking**: its "τ (tau) scaling" / **LogicFolding** roadmap (system-technology co-optimization) targets ~215 MTr/mm² by 2030 and ~295 by 2031 on a *per-package* basis — claiming **foundry-A14-equivalent density by 2031** via stacked active logic (prime-core clocks 2.75GHz Kirin 9030 → ~5GHz target 2031), not comparable to true single-die foundry density. Theoretical planar next steps: N+4 ~137.8 MTr/mm² (≈TSMC N5 / Samsung SF4), N+5 ~163.6 (≈Intel 18A HP, needs backside contacts) — each plausible but cumulatively harder/costlier without EUV. **Diffusion risk:** SMIC is **licensing N+2/N+3 to HLMC/Hua Hong at the government's direction**, shifting the choke-point from one fab to an ecosystem (Alibaba T-Head, Cambricon also potential beneficiaries) — so sanctions aimed at SMIC alone become less effective once the process knowledge spreads. Also confirms Huawei still packages **Samsung 1a-node LPDDR5X (and CXMT G4) DRAM** in the Kirin 9030 (SemiAnalysis/STEEL Team full report, 2026-06-14). [Source](../relat%C3%B3rios%20bons/2026_06_14_ENC_Is_SMIC_N3s_Metal_Pitch_Smaller_than_Intel_18As_email.html)
- **Third-pass detail on the same teardown — the cell-level geometry, the SRAM caveat and the one process-control tell (SemiAnalysis / STEEL Team, 2026-06-14):** the density result on the page is built from measurements worth carrying explicitly, because they are what determines whether N+4/N+5 are reachable. **N+3 geometry:** fin pitch **30-32nm** (vs **34nm measured on TSMC N6** — note N7's HD library is generally listed at 33nm and N6 "did not shrink pitches directly; its density gains came from DTCO"), **CGP 57nm**, **cell height 228nm** (-5% vs N6's 240nm HD cell, -9.5% vs N+2's 252nm), **5 M0 tracks** between power rails, 2 fins per transistor (same as N6 HD), plus COAG and single-diffusion-break — i.e. SMIC is using **every available DTCO booster aggressively** rather than getting density from lithography. Only **one standard-cell library** was found in the Kirin 9030 (2 NMOS / 2 PMOS fins) vs N6's widely-used HD *and* HP libraries — which SemiAnalysis reads as a narrower library strategy reflecting "the smaller customer base and the more constrained domestic design and EDA ecosystem." **SRAM:** shrank **~19% from N+2 to N+3, close to the theoretical logic shrink — but "N+2's bitcells were unusually large, bigger than comparable 7nm-class nodes, so part of the gain is catch-up rather than true scaling"**; the measured 8T cell is 0.0463µm² (21.6 Mib/mm² peak), with an estimated 6T HDC at 0.0260µm² (38.5 Mib/mm²) landing near **Samsung 7LPP/5LPP and slightly below TSMC N7/N6**, and an estimated 6T HCC **~12% larger than on Intel 3/Intel 4**. **The process-control tell:** patterning is judged "robust and well controlled" on the XTEM evidence, *but* there is "a clear deviation in M0 integration compared with the big three foundries: **a blowout of the liner bottom at the alumina etch-stop layer**" (SemiAnalysis is careful that it is unclear whether this is a control limitation or a deliberate attempt to widen the bottom M0 CD given a sharp taper); the TaN barrier also appears oxidized. **Why the roadmap gets harder, in the report's own words:** *"Future nodes will be tougher. N+3 still had room to tighten local metals and reduce cell height and CGP. Further scaling without EUV leaves fewer levers. More aggressive multi-patterning adds masks and overlay error. SMIC can keep pushing DUV, but each step will get more expensive and less forgiving"* — and on N+5 specifically, *"this would not make N+5 cost-competitive with the leading edge. It would reach a similar density through a much more expensive route,"* requiring entirely new backside alignment, wafer-thinning, contact-reveal and backside-metallisation flows. **The balanced verdict, which is the right frame for the SMIC thesis:** *"China is not closing the gap with Intel, Samsung and TSMC. The teardown shows the opposite in several places: no EUV, no backside power, higher process complexity, and visible trade-offs. But China is still advancing. If domestic chips become good enough for phones, inference, networking and security-sensitive workloads, they can matter strategically without matching TSMC at the leading edge."* [Source](../relat%C3%B3rios%20bons/2026_06_14_ENC_Is_SMIC_N3s_Metal_Pitch_Smaller_than_Intel_18As_email.html)

- **🆕 SMIC is a named first-year recipient of China's domestic immersion DUV — the tooling constraint gets its first (small) domestic release valve (The Information · Staff "China Starts Mass-Producing Homegrown DUV Chipmaking Tools, An Advance for Local Chip Industry", 2026-07-27).** ⚠️ **Single-outlet exclusive sourced to "two people familiar" — not a company disclosure, no rating/PT/estimate; SMIC did not respond to requests for comment.** A **Shanghai-based state-backed company, deliberately unnamed in the article**, has begun **mass production of immersion DUV lithography machines** — a first for China — after pulling immersion-DUV development teams from other Chinese firms **including the state-backed equipment startup Shanghai Yuliangsheng Technology**. **Systems are slated for delivery this year to SMIC, Hua Hong Semiconductor and CXMT**, following prototypes sent to some chipmakers last year whose "encouraging test results paved the way for production to begin this year." **Size it honestly: ~5 machines in 2026 and ~20 in 2027, against the 131 immersion DUV systems [[ASML]] shipped last year** — this does not relieve SMIC's tooling constraint in 2026-27. Four gates in the article: the machines **"still trail ASML's products in performance and build quality"**; moving them onto mass-production lines **"could take many months or longer"** as chipmakers test accuracy, reliability and compatibility with other equipment; the build is mostly domestic but **some key parts still come from Japan**, with **local-supplier delays already holding back this year's output**; and China's domestic **EUV remains prototype-stage, "likely years away"** from producing working chips (Reuters first reported the prototype in December) — so the EUV ceiling on SMIC's roadmap is unchanged. **Where the tools land first is the mature-node line, not N+3:** the article says domestic DUV "could initially help expand production of mature chips, including power-management components essential to AI servers and data centers," where shortages hold up whole systems — i.e. it maps to SMIC's 12-inch mature/legacy base and the AI-power-management demand already flagged on the Q1'26 call, not to the advanced-node effort. **Independent corroboration of the multiple-patterning workaround this page has documented from the SemiAnalysis teardown:** the article states plainly that immersion DUV "can also be pushed to manufacture more-advanced semiconductors by printing some layers repeatedly, a costly and difficult process known as multiple patterning," and that **SMIC has used that approach to produce advanced chips for smartphones and AI systems despite lacking access to EUV**. **Policy read-through, and it cuts both ways for SMIC:** the bipartisan **Match Act would widen restrictions on immersion DUV *and* curb servicing of tools at some Chinese fabs** — SMIC is already explicitly named in the Match Act (AlphaSense "DR ASML primer", 2026-06-22, see Catalysts) — so the domestic tool is the hedge against exactly the scenario the same bill creates, but it arrives at ~5 units a year against a fleet need measured in the hundreds. Structural backdrop: lithography has been a Chinese national priority since **2002**, and the **2022 US controls pushed Beijing from state-led research toward commercial execution, with Huawei coordinating industry resources**. ## Debate / thesis
- **Bull:** The Q2 +14-16% guide blew past a low Street bar (JPM, briefing 2026-05-17), and the lever is **pricing power** — 5-10% hikes across BCD/analog/PMIC/storage plus ASP +2.5% q/q in Q1 — evidence the mature-node price war is easing as memory-driven supply tightness (NOR/NAND constraints, Q1 2026 call) lets SMIC reallocate capacity to higher-value parts. Domestic localization is secular: China stayed ~85-89% of revenue through 2025, with industrial/automotive +60% y/y and consumer +30% y/y in FY2025 (2026-02-11 call). Q3 2025 (2025-11-14) management was running 95.8% utilization and explicitly **supply-constrained** ("shipment volumes unable to fully satisfy customer demand"). As China's only advanced-node foundry, SMIC captures any onshore AI/logic build it can supply via DUV — and the Huawei AI roadmap is structurally inflationary for foundry demand: the "scale via more chips" approach needs ~16x more silicon, with local AI capacity set to triple in 2026 (Bernstein Huawei roadmap, 2025-09-22, **Outperform**). MS frames the prize as China local-GPU revenue growing to **Rmb287bn by 2027**, with domestic supply almost fully meeting China AI demand by 2027 (MS AI supply chain, 2025-08-18).
- **Bear:** Margins are structurally pressured — gross margin sits ~19-22% (well below TSMC's 50%+), and **depreciation +~30% y/y** from the new-fab ramp (FY2026 outlook, 2026-02-11) eats into incremental volume. The mature-node **capacity glut / price war** is the core risk: MS flags a **prolonged downcycle in mature-node foundry and niche memory** driven by the China fab supply ramp (MS AI supply chain, 2025-08-18); GM fell to 18-20% guides through 2H 2025 (Q3/Q4 guides) and ASP swung -6.4% q/q in Q2 2025 (2025-08-08) before the recent recovery — pricing is volatile and China is adding capacity industry-wide. Management itself flags **"2H consumer risk"** (JPM, briefing 2026-05-17). Net profit leans on government subsidies (Q1 2026 miss). No EUV access caps the advanced-node roadmap at DUV multi-patterning — higher cost, lower yield, hard to scale economically; Bernstein notes SMIC absorbed **>$10bn extra capex 2020-24** to onshore advanced node and is still struggling to lift advanced-node yield (Bernstein Semis deck, 2025-05-05). No dividend; capital sink.

- **Q2 2026 print** (next earnings; mid-Aug cadence) — does the +14-16% guide convert, and does GM hold the 20-22% guide as depreciation steps up. Watch ASP trajectory (pricing-power durability) vs shipment volume.

The proposed US **MATCH Act explicitly names SMIC** (with Hua Hong, Huawei, CXMT, YMTC) as firms ASML would be barred from selling/servicing advanced DUV immersion tools to — a direct hit to SMIC's leading-edge tooling path if enacted (AlphaSense "DR ASML primer", 2026-06-22).

What to track from here, in order: (1) whether the tools actually **move onto mass-production lines** — the article says that "could take many months or longer" while chipmakers test **accuracy, reliability and compatibility with other equipment**, so commercial-line insertion (versus trials) is the milestone that matters, not delivery; (2) whether **local-supplier delays** (which held back this year's output) and the **key parts still sourced from Japan** ease enough for the 2027 step-up to 20 units; (3) whether customer feedback closes the acknowledged gap in **performance and build quality vs ASML**; and (4) **Match Act progress** — the bill would widen immersion-DUV restrictions *and* **curb servicing of tools at some Chinese fabs**, which would hit SMIC's existing ASML fleet uptime, not just future purchases. - **China CapEx splurge — a demand tailwind read-through** — Jefferies' supply-chain checks (Daifuku/clean-room build-out) flag a "gigantic" China CapEx wave: the China client base has consolidated to ~10 real spenders — **SMIC, CXMT, YMTC, the Huawei complex** — spending heavily; Daifuku raised its China revenue forecast to ~¥110bn (from ¥98bn), and Tokyo Electron lifted its China-WFE view to +10% (potentially more on the CXMT/YMTC IPOs). ## Risks
- **Mature-node price war / capacity glut** — China-wide foundry capacity additions; MS flags a prolonged mature-node downcycle (MS AI supply chain, 2025-08-18); ASP volatility (-6.4% q/q in Q2 2025).
- **Margin compression** — GM structurally ~19-22%; depreciation +~30% y/y on new-fab ramp; subsidy-dependent net profit.
- **Advanced-node yield / cost burden** — no EUV access caps the roadmap at DUV multi-patterning; SMIC absorbed >$10bn extra capex 2020-24 to onshore advanced node and still struggles on yield (Bernstein Semis deck, 2025-05-05); risk of tighter DUV/SME or Entity List restrictions impairing the leading-edge effort and Huawei relationship.
- **China-concentration / demand** — ~85-89% domestic revenue; exposed to China consumer/smartphone cycle and any 2H 2026 consumer softness.
- **Capital intensity / no return of capital** — ~$8bn/yr capex, no dividend; FCF negative on the build-out (FY2025 investing -$6.5bn).
- **Geopolitical tail** — US/China tariffs and sanctions; secondary-customer / supply-chain disruption.
- **🆕 Tool-servicing risk, distinct from tool-purchase risk (The Information · Staff, 2026-07-27; single-outlet reporting).** The bipartisan **Match Act would not only widen restrictions on immersion DUV sales but also curb servicing of tools at some Chinese chip plants** — an under-modeled variant of the export-control risk already on this page, because it degrades the **installed ASML fleet's uptime and yield** rather than only capping future capacity adds. The domestic alternative does not cover it on any relevant horizon: China's first domestically mass-produced immersion DUV runs at **~5 units in 2026 / ~20 in 2027 (vs the 131 immersion systems ASML shipped last year)**, the machines **"still trail ASML's products in performance and build quality,"** insertion onto mass-production lines "could take many months or longer," **some key parts still come from Japan**, and **domestic EUV remains prototype-stage and "likely years away."**
