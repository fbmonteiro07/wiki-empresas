## Snapshot
Three reportable segments: **North America** (Q1'26 $104.1B rev, +12%, 7.9% OI margin), **International** ($39.8B, +11% ex-FX), and **AWS** ($37.6B, +28%, $14.2B OI). AWS is ~21% of revenue but the overwhelming majority of operating income and the entire valuation debate. On top of the retail flywheel (1P/3P marketplace, FBA, Prime, fast/quick commerce) sit three high-margin attach businesses — **Advertising** ($17.2B Q1'26, +22%), **Prime Video**, and now an AI-infrastructure complex (Trainium/Graviton custom silicon, Bedrock, Anthropic anchor). New optionality: **Amazon LEO** (satellite broadband, commercial launch Q3'26) and **Amazon Supply Chain Services** (3P logistics / LTL). The stock is positioned by the Street as the "infrastructure backbone of AI" — OpenAI + Anthropic both running on AWS (JPM AI Capex & Financing, 2025-11-10: "best positioned of the hyperscalers to generate tangible returns from AI investment").

- **⚠ QUALITY OF THE BEAT: ~$1.2bn of the $27.5bn operating income is DISCRETE, and it lands on both sides of the beat.** Olsavsky: OI "includes the benefit from two items that reduced expenses by approximately **$1.2 billion** during the quarter. Second, we recorded a separate benefit of approximately **$600 million related to the change in fair-value measurement of energy contracts subject to derivative accounting. This primarily impacts the AWS segment.**" These are electricity-supply contracts whose terms make them derivatives; the marks "have not been significant in prior quarters." **So against the $27.5bn reported vs Street $23.6bn, roughly a third of the beat is non-operating, and ~$600mn of it sits inside the AWS margin everyone is quoting.**
- **★ The clean AWS margin number, which no desk note on this page carries: AWS operating income was $16.6bn on $42.2bn of revenue, and margins were "up 650 basis points year-over-year, 520 basis points if you exclude the derivative accounting gain."** So the durable y/y expansion is **~520bps, not ~650bps**, and ~130bps of the reported expansion is a fair-value mark on energy contracts. Olsavsky's own framing of the rest: "the profitability you're seeing from AWS isn't random. It's a result of disciplined efficiency gains, **capacity optimization, which we benefited quite a bit from in Q2**, and always closely managing our fixed costs" — with the standing caveat that "these margins will fluctuate." Jassy adds the structural claim: "we see the margins and returns in AI tracking what we saw with core at the same point of evolution, **actually a little ahead**." **Reconciliation note: this page and the desk notes carry AWS margin at 39.4% and "~600bp above plan"; $16.6bn / $42.2bn = 39.3%, and the y/y expansion decomposes 650bps reported / 520bps clean. Both stay on the page with their bases named.**
- **★ COST PASS-THROUGH — Amazon's answer to the same question Microsoft's IR was asked the same day, and the answers rhyme.** Asked how AWS pricing incorporates future cost inflation given long-term contracts, Jassy: "most of the deals that you sign… tend to be deals and agreements that you've signed. And the deals that you've signed, those will be the prices… over the duration of that contract. **And new agreements that you sign, you always take into account what your costs are in how you ultimately build a price** that you agree to with your customers. And I think it's no secret right now to any company in the world that **there are inflated prices right now on some of the components like memory and hard drives and SSDs.**" **Read against [MSFT](MSFT.md): Microsoft's IR said pricing is NOT locked over a contract's life (frontier-lab pricing resets by capacity tranche; enterprise discount-off-list can move). Amazon's answer is the more conservative version — signed price holds for the signed term, cost inflation is recovered on NEW agreements. Same direction, weaker mechanism, and it implies AWS carries more of the memory-inflation risk inside the existing book than Azure claims to.** This is a genuine cross-company divergence worth tracking as the FY27 tranches/renewals land.

## Debate / thesis
- **Post-Q2'26: the ROI debate got an answer, the FCF debate got worse (2026-07-30).** The two halves of the print pull in opposite directions and both are now on the page permanently. **For the bulls:** AWS +36.7% is the fastest in 18 quarters and *accelerating* off a $150B base, the backlog went $364B → **$496B** in one quarter, AWS margin *expanded* to 39.4% while capex ripped, and management put hard numbers on payback for the first time — **<3 years to break even on servers/networking, ≥5-6 year useful life, ≥5-year contracted terms on most AI capacity, and 5-6 server generations off a single 30+ year shell**. That is the specific rebuttal to the "circular / unprovable ROI" bear case that drove the July de-rating, and it is why the AI-supply-chain complex (ALAB/MRVL/CRDO) traded up on someone else's print (MS Wigg, 2026-07-30). **For the bears:** **FCF was −$9B against consensus −$1B**, and FY26 capex went **~$200B → ~$220B with the increment attributed to memory-cost inflation** — Vital Knowledge's framing is the cleanest: "the only two numbers that anyone will talk about are 37% and 39.4% … but people should also talk about −$9B, as this speaks to the cash pressures facing hyperscalers" (Vital Knowledge, 2026-07-30).
