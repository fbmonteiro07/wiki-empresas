## Snapshot
Incorporated in New South Wales on 2018-11-06 as Iris Energy and closed its US IPO on 2021-11-19. It has done business as IREN since 2024-02-15 and has been legally named IREN Limited since 2024-11-27 (FY26 10-K). Run by co-founders and co-CEOs Daniel and Will Roberts. A US-listed **ex-bitcoin miner turned vertically integrated AI cloud**. IREN owns the land, the grid connections and the data centres, and rents NVIDIA GPU capacity on 3–5-year contracts (plus some short-term). **AI Cloud is 88% of Q1 FY27 revenue.** Management *aims to* substantially complete the bitcoin-mining exit by 2026-12-31 (CFO Anthony Lewis, Q1 FY27 call, 2026-11-05), but the 10-Q adds that *"immaterial"* mining continues through CY27. Mining was $28m of revenue in Q1.

Its edge over a typical neocloud is ownership: no 20-year colocation leases, ~5 GW of executed grid-connection agreements (10-Q MD&A), and $6bn of data-centre assets that management calls unencumbered (release and call). Its fragility is concentration and funding. Three customers were **70% of Q1 revenue** (10-Q Note 4), and Microsoft (200 MW IT, ~$9.7bn) plus NVIDIA are ~⅔ of a $20.3bn backlog (CFO). Growth is now **paced by capital markets, not demand** (management's own framing, Q1 FY27).

**The message behind the print: pace, don't chase** (Capstone summary of Roberts and Lewis, Q1 FY27 call, 2026-11-05)
1. **Capex cut on purpose, and blamed on markets, not demand.** About $5bn was spent in Q1 and ~$15bn is planned for the rest of FY27, "down from the prior estimate". That implies roughly $20bn for FY27 (Capstone sum of the two company figures). **In August the FY27 capex outlook was $25–30bn** ($27.5bn midpoint), split as $14bn funded, $8bn from expected GPU-linked financing and ~$5.5bn residual (GS / Michael Ng, Q4 FY26 note, 2026-08-27). So the cut is **~$5–10bn**. DAMNANG (2026-08-28) quotes the same $25–30bn, but his "leaving roughly $8B" counts only the GPU-linked leg; GS's split is the clean one. The Q4 FY26 release itself gives only the $14bn funding figure. Roberts agreed with BTIG's Greg Lewis that capex is "ticking down". His reason: *"purely and simply financial markets"*, not a lack of demand, customers or sites. The Childress air-cooled rollout is now spread across CY27 instead of front-loaded. GPUs for next year are deliberately not fully bought ("right-way risk": GPU prices may fall if markets stay weak). Development spending, which is cheap, continues across the 35 GW pipeline; construction, the expensive stage, is what gets paced. BBG's FY27 capex consensus was already **$18.0bn** (high $28.0bn), so the cut moves the plan *toward* the Street. It is still above it, not below.
2. **No 2027 ARR target.** Lewis said ARR will grow "meaningfully" beyond $4bn through CY27, but gave no target because it depends on contracting and acceptance timing.
3. **Not a "WeWork business model"** (Roberts). IREN owns the land, power and data centres outright, with no 20-year colocation leases and no "$100 billion TCV" lab contracts. That lets it flex spending up or down. The funding pitch: **$9bn of cash (incl. restricted), committed financing and contracted-but-unreceived prepayments, against $15bn of capex, leaves $6bn to raise.** That is as of 9/30, subject to conditions precedent, and before the ~$630m post-quarter ATM. Indicative sources are **$12–15bn** before any converts or equity:
   - data-centre financing on the $10bn of data-centre assets targeted by June 2027 (Horizons 1–4 first, backed by the Microsoft contract);
   - GPU financing on $6bn of unencumbered GPUs;
   - prepayments;
   - "other corporate-level initiatives". Portfolio target: financing plus prepayments covering **90% of GPU capex**.
4. **The delay is presented as customer-driven.** The >$4bn ARR date moves from **2026-12-31** (Q4 FY26 release, 2026-08-27) to **January 2027, possibly end-February**. The causes are a Microsoft-requested network redesign across the 200 MW plus supplier timing. **The 10-Q is more cautious than the call.** A Microsoft-requested change extends delivery dates *"without delay credits"*, but the change is being built *"in parallel with discussions regarding delivery dates and the application and payment of fees, credits and cost reimbursements"*. The 10-Q also cites *"third-party delays"*, and puts delivery *"during the following quarter"*, meaning by end-March 2027, with no assurance on the revised timeline. That is a wider window than the call's January/February. The FY26 10-K had already disclosed contractual grace periods for Horizons 2–4 *"extending from mid-Q4 2026 to the beginning of Q2 2027"*. Build status (call): Horizon 2 is built and Dell is commissioning it; Horizon 3 has its GPUs on site and is energised; Horizon 4's GPUs arrive "in the coming weeks".

**December quarter (Q2 FY27) — the Street looks too high.** BBG consensus for the Dec quarter is **revenue $344m (n=17), adj. EBITDA $153m**. Management's framing points lower:
- **Revenue:** about a quarter of the $1bn exit ARR, so **~$250m of AI Cloud revenue**. That $1bn was already reached on 2026-08-26, so it is flat run-rate, not a ramp. New Horizon and Mackenzie capacity adds nothing material because "most of" it arrives late Dec or early 2027. Mining revenue falls toward zero by quarter-end.
- **Costs:** cash opex rises **$50–60m q/q** from $118m.
- **Capstone arithmetic (rough):** revenue − cost of revenue − cash opex reproduces Q1 at $64m vs $66m reported. Applying the same identity to Q2 (AI Cloud ~$250m at Q1's 14% cost ratio, mining $5–20m, cash opex $168–178m) gives **adj. EBITDA of about $40–55m and revenue of about $255–270m**. An independent re-derivation, run on 2026-11-06 by a verifier agent, matched this. Small upside items (Mirantis software on top of GPU-hour ARR, some capacity landing before late December, lease vs services cost mix) do not close the gap to $153m. Expect Dec-quarter numbers to come down. The CY27 consensus (revenue $4.73bn, EBITDA $3.46bn) depends on the Jan–Feb ramp happening on time.
