## Snapshot
AXT manufactures compound-semiconductor substrates — principally indium phosphide (InP), gallium arsenide (GaAs), and germanium. InP exceeded 50% of quarterly revenue as of Q1 2026, making AXTI the most direct public-market expression of the InP supply constraint that has become the bottleneck for AI-datacenter photonics (CPO / optical transceivers). China controls 70%+ of global InP output; AXT + Sumitomo together hold ~80% of non-China InP capacity. The stock re-rated from a ~$500M market cap to a reported $7.24B on the optical-supply thesis, but is increasingly framed by FinTwit as a higher-beta "trading vehicle," with [LITE](LITE.md)/[COHR](COHR.md)/IQE preferred for lower China-regulatory-risk core exposure.

## Current state (latest quarter)

**🆕 SELL-SIDE CORROBORATION FROM THE CUSTOMER SIDE — and it reframes where the bottleneck now sits (Morgan Stanley · Meta Marshall, "[[LITE]]/[[COHR]] FQ4 Preview", 2026-08-03).** Writing about AXT's downstream customers rather than about AXT, MS states: **"AXTI suggests the InP SUBSTRATE BOTTLENECK IS BEGINNING TO EASE, leaving INTERNAL FAB CAPACITY AS THE MORE IMPORTANT CONSTRAINT… Improved substrate visibility should help both companies, but INTERNAL InP FAB CAPACITY increasingly appears to be the PRIMARY BOTTLENECK on incremental output."** ⚠️ **Read this carefully, because it is double-edged for the AXTI thesis. The BULL half: it independently confirms the capacity story on this page — "AXTI remains on track to DOUBLE InP substrate capacity in 2026 and expand slightly beyond that level in 2027," and "export permits out of China are also becoming MORE REGULAR, although applications still require a supporting customer order and take time to process." The BEAR half: if the substrate is no longer the scarce link, AXT loses pricing leverage over time even as volumes grow — scarcity rent moves downstream to whoever owns the laser fab. This page's "demand outpaces supply no matter how fast they add capacity" framing and MS's "bottleneck beginning to ease" are not the same claim, and the difference matters for margin durability rather than for revenue.** **Two supply-detail marks worth keeping: "supply remains TIGHT ENOUGH THAT CUSTOMERS ARE USING LEGACY 2-INCH WAFERS alongside mainstream 3-inch and 4-inch formats, while 6-INCH WAFERS ARE PROGRESSING BUT HAVE NOT REACHED VOLUME PRODUCTION" — so the higher-value 6-inch mix shift this page cites as a revenue-per-unit driver is real but not yet at volume. And the two LTAs are differentiated by purpose: "LITE's long-term supply agreement RESERVES SUBSTRATE CAPACITY THROUGH 2031, while COHR's agreement supports the DEVELOPMENT AND SUPPLY OF 6-INCH WAFERS."** Also relevant: MS expects management commentary industry-wide to shift emphasis toward **NPO over CPO** as the nearer-term architecture, which broadens the customer base beyond NVIDIA platforms — consistent with the "hyperscalers advancing toward NPO and CPO" demand mechanism below. See [themes/optical-cpo](themes/optical-cpo.md).

**Q2 2026 — PRINTED 2026-07-30 (AMC). A blowout, and the ramp has barely started.** ⚠️ **THESIS DRIFT: the "into the print" framing below is superseded and retained as the pre-print bar, which was cleared. Primary source: the Q2 2026 earnings call, notes taken live by @Gaetano ("Significant", 2026-07-30/31) — the same author who was a public AXTI bear until late July, which is why the reversal is worth weighting.**

- **"This is the highest quarterly revenue in AXT's history."** Segment detail: **InP $30.7m** (against management's pre-print statement that ~$34m of Q2 revenue was already realizable — see below), **gallium arsenide $6.6m**, **germanium $272k**, and **consolidated raw-material joint-venture companies $10.0m** — the last of which "doesn't get talked about much but is currently still a great rev contributor." **Top 5 customers ~30% of total revenue, with NO customer above the 10% level** — unusually low concentration for a name framed as a two-customer InP story. **"Crazy margin improvements."**
- ⚠️ **The LTA cash does NOT sit in revenue, and this is the single most misread item on the page: the $22.3m from Coherent and $25.4m from Casela are "significant upfront cash… posted as a LIABILITY and they will be converted to revenue as they ship against the agreements. So keep in mind these didn't show up in the rev numbers this Q."** Anyone bridging LTA announcements to reported revenue will double-count.
- **Capacity is running ahead of plan: AXT is AHEAD OF SCHEDULE on its commitment to double InP capacity in 2026, and the InP revenue opportunity is "on track to MORE THAN TRIPLE by the end of 2026, with continued significant expansion in 2027."** Three stated reasons, and the second is the interesting one: **(1) able to expand faster than expected; (2) "significant strides with NEW CRYSTAL GROWTH FURNACE DESIGNS and increased output"; (3) customers moving to LARGER-DIAMETER substrates and higher-value products (6-inch = more revenue per unit).**
- **Backlog >$100M.** **Demand outpaces supply "no matter how fast they add capacity"** — the author's framing is the right one to keep: *"This is what EVERY layer of the supply chain is saying. AXT is shipping record levels of InP wafers to companies like [[LITE]] and [[COHR]]. And they still want more. And LITE and COHR's customers want more."*
- **The demand mechanism, stated three ways: the 800G→1.6T transition (overlapping demand, not substitution); MORE LASERS PER TRANSCEIVER; and higher-spec/more valuable substrates. Plus "hyperscalers are advancing toward NPO and CPO which will continue increasing demand for AXT."**
- **Guidance/trajectory: ~$66M of revenue realizable in Q3 TODAY** — i.e. not requiring an export permit — and **~$130M/QUARTER exiting 2027, which annualises to ~$520M at the low end.** After the 2027 expansion AXT expects to be **"by far the largest indium phosphide producer in the world."**
- ⚠️ **THE LUMENTUM SIGNING IS THE STRONGEST SINGLE DATAPOINT AND IT INVALIDATES THE PRIOR BEAR CASE, in the author's own words: "just a few months ago Lumentum felt confident in their long term supply agreement with SUMITOMO, covering 7 years… This was ONE OF THE BIGGEST REASONS WHY I WAS BEARISH ON AXT. They didn't really feel necessary to me. So the fact that just a few months later Lumentum is signing LTAs with AXT really shows just how fast things progress and how significant the demand is."** The Lumentum CEO has been **"open recently about how they need to MULTI SOURCE AGGRESSIVELY and secure as much as possible."** The inference: **substrate COST is not the binding concern — AVAILABILITY is.**
- **China is the growth engine and the least-constrained one: China InP revenue DOUBLED q/q in Q2, "not gated by permits… just straight capacity ramp + demand," with China at roughly 40-60% of InP revenue.** Competition is coming online in China "but it's going to take a long time." ⚠️ **The corollary is the asymmetry: 40-60% China leaves substantial headroom in the rest of the world, and the US permit question is still unresolved — "this is still without permits to ship into the U.S. If that unlocks, you are looking at a major step up again that isn't being forecasted."** Some easing noted: **"more regularity in the process of permits in certain geographic regions"** (in a Coherent context, so likely Germany/Sweden/Switzerland).
- **Vertical integration flagged as underappreciated: AXT's subsidiaries give it direct control of critical raw-material supply for InP, and it is investing to expand that capability.**
- **Margin: management implied margins could go above 50% with more volume and larger wafers, but would NOT commit to it.** Logged as an unguided possibility, not a target.
- **On GaAs/VCSELs: customers buying gallium arsenide are "also very interested in InP"** — consistent with both [[COHR]] and [[LITE]] carrying VCSEL and InP product lines, and an argument that the two demand curves move together rather than competing.

## Debate / thesis
- **🆕 Two independent post-print reads, and both point at the same thing — the InP constraint has moved UPSTREAM of the substrate (2026-07-31 / 2026-08-01).** **① The tool layer, from the [[AIXA]] cross-read (@crux_capital_, 2026-07-31): "AIXA saying that their DEMAND OUTSTRIPS THEIR SUPPLY and that customers are RESERVING SLOTS INTO NEXT YEAR means that their customers are SHORT MOCVD MACHINES relative to what their demand is."** ⚠️ **This page's bull case has rested on substrate scarcity — AXT + Sumitomo controlling ~80% of non-China InP — and the 07-31 print (record revenue, capacity ahead of plan, and the [[LITE]] LTA signed DESPITE a ~7-year Sumitomo deal) established that AVAILABILITY, not cost, is the binding constraint. The AIXA datapoint says the same scarcity now shows up one layer further up: even customers holding substrate cannot necessarily deposit on it, because MOCVD tool slots are booked into 2027. That is additive to the AXTI thesis, not competing with it — it lengthens the window in which substrate supply commands price — but it also caps how fast AXTI's own customers can convert substrate into shipped lasers, which is a demand-timing risk the page should carry.** The author's downstream inference — that constrained laser makers outsource epitaxy to IQE, citing MTSI and [[TSEM]] as proof the model works — is his hypothesis, not a disclosed arrangement, and is logged to test rather than adopted. **② A structural explanation for the DRAWDOWN that is not fundamental at all (Irrational Analysis, 2026-08-01): "AXTI is a giga-long. if you look at the chart, IT STARTED TO COLLAPSE A MONTH OR TWO EARLIER THAN EVERYTHING ELSE. This is because CHINA BANNED TOTAL RETURN SWAPS and a bunch of Chinese and Hong-Kong institutions became mini-Leopolds"** — i.e. the author attributes AXTI's early de-rating to forced deleveraging by TRS-financed Asian holders ahead of, and by the same mechanism as, the [[ANTHROPIC]]-page Situational Awareness unwind. ⚠️ **Unverified and from a pseudonymous, self-declared long. Logged because if it holds it means the AXTI drawdown that preceded the sector's was a FLOW event that resolved before the 07-31 blowout print, not an early fundamental warning — which is a materially different read of the same chart. It needs an independent check on the China TRS rule change before it is used.**
- **Bull — InP supply oligopoly + demand surge:** AI inter-rack bandwidth is upgrading 400G→800G and CPO is going mainstream. AXT + Sumitomo control ~80% of non-China InP. China eased some InP export restrictions in June 2026 (first 2026 batch approved, relieving H2 CPO schedules), while CSPs (led by Google) are personally visiting non-China substrate makers to secure long-term supply — entrenching pricing power (@jukan05, @aleabitoreddit, 2026-06-15/16/17). Every major optical name ([LITE](LITE.md), [COHR](COHR.md), [AAOI](AAOI.md)) needs InP substrates it cannot quickly switch away from due to validation cycles.
- **Bear — China regulatory exposure + trading-vehicle discount:** AXTI is already obtaining permits to ship to US companies' ex-US sites (e.g., COHR Sweden), showing the permitting regime is a live gating factor (@crux_capital_, 2026-06-12). @ParadisLabs, after taking profits, explicitly demoted AXTI from core hold to trading vehicle, preferring [LITE](LITE.md) (lower China-supply beta), [COHR](COHR.md) (InP-permit torque), and IQE (asymmetry) (2026-06-12/13). The ~14x MC run sets a high bar.
- **Debate — 6-inch InP transition:** [COHR](COHR.md) is ~1 year early to 6-inch InP (4x capacity expansion underway), which could displace AXTI substrate demand if validated at scale. @insane_analyst raised yield concerns and noted "there is a reason Lumentum and AXTI both avoiding 6-inch" — implying AXTI's conservatism is either prudence or a capability gap (2026-05-07, 2026-06-12).
- **Where the sell-side stands:** No formal equity-research ratings or PTs for AXTI in the ported corpus. Jefferies Asia TMT Sales (Rankie Wong) mentions AXTI in roundup subjects (sales commentary, no rating/PT). Vital Knowledge covers AXTI episodically (see the Jan 8 preannouncement). FinTwit consensus: higher-beta InP play, trimmed after the run.
- **Jefferies optical expert call — AXTI named directly in the InP supply chain (Jefferies · expert "Rick Li," 2026-07-14).** A Jefferies-hosted optics-expert call lists AXTI explicitly among the related names in the InP/optical-transceiver bottleneck (alongside AVGO, Marvell, Sumitomo Electric, Innolight, DSBJ) — independent sell-side corroboration of the core thesis, not just FinTwit. The call reiterates that **InP substrate stays indispensable regardless of modulation path** (EML, SiPh, or the emerging TFLN at 3.2T all still need an external InP CW laser), supporting AXTI's structural relevance beyond any single technology transition. One nuance to flag: the call also notes a first Chinese **200G EML** entrant (**DSBJ, targeting MP 2H26**) and that **China controls ~70% of *global* InP supply** — since DSBJ is a downstream EML-chip maker likely sourced on domestic Chinese substrate, this doesn't directly erode AXTI/Sumitomo's ~80% *ex-China* share, but it is a watch-item for how fast China's own InP-substrate supply chain verticalizes behind its EML entrants (Jefferies optical expert call, 2026-07-14).

- **The cleanest framing yet of why InP is structurally tighter than DRAM (Irrational Analysis, "Stranded Indium-Phosphide," 2026-07-25).** An industry contact's analogy, relayed and endorsed by the author: *"Only 25% of the world's DRAM goes to AI. Can just take more from consumer. **80% of InP already going to AI.**"* The author explicitly says to ignore the precise percentages but insists the core argument holds — **InP has essentially zero consumer end-market, so unlike DRAM there is no non-AI allocation to raid**. In memory you can "metaphorically shoot the smartphone industry and annex their DRAM allocation"; in InP that release valve does not exist. This is the sharpest articulation on the page of why the AXTI/Sumitomo oligopoly should hold pricing longer than a normal materials squeeze — and it is an *engineering-source* opinion, not a broker model, so treat the percentages as directional. The same note names **AXTI as "more interesting" than both Aeva and its InP fab partner Seivers**, and the author discloses owning **Lumentum, AXTI and AAOI** (Irrational Analysis, 2026-07-25). Cross-ref [LITE.md](LITE.md), [AAOI.md](AAOI.md), [COHR.md](COHR.md), [themes/optical-cpo.md](themes/optical-cpo.md).
- **Demand-side watch — a new InP-consuming architecture (SOA-based ELSFP), and a new Chinese substrate project.** The same note models an **ELSFP module for NPO/CPO** (1310nm single-wavelength, 22 dBm coupled per fiber, 8 output fibers) two ways: the conventional **8× discrete DFB** design vs a **high-power SOA** design. A 650 mW SOA has **better InP area efficiency** (more photons per unit of InP area) at the device level, but at product level the SOA route still needs a **CW seed laser** and eats heavy **coupling losses** from extra lenses/isolators and beam-shape mismatch — netting **51% gross margin the normal way vs 17% the SOA way**. Relevant to AXTI two ways: (i) if SOA-based ELSFP is adopted for its InP efficiency, it is mildly *deflationary* for substrate demand per module; (ii) the author judges only two firms can build viable high-power SOA (one private IDM plus **$AEVA**, fabbing at Seivers), so adoption is supply-gated regardless. Separately on supply: a **GaAs and InP substrate project was approved in Zhejiang province, China** (TrendForce via Susquehanna · Mehdi Hosseini weekly, 2026-07-23/25) — a watch-item for the "how fast does China's InP substrate chain verticalize" question already flagged in the Jefferies expert-call bullet above.

## 🔴 Recent signals (Aug 4) — AXTI becomes the retaliation vector in the US-China optical fight, and the substrate bottleneck is reported to be EASING (two contradictory items, same day)

**1) The retaliation vector.** With the Trump administration reported to be drafting a **ban on US imports of new-model Chinese optical transceivers** (Reuters; per MS drafting at the FCC — see [themes/optical-cpo](themes/optical-cpo.md)), MS's desk names AXT explicitly as China's lever: **"China has an obvious retaliatory mechanism by possibly restricting bottlenecked InP (Indium Phosphide) supply via AXTI (Chinese)"** — and adds, in the same note, that **"[[LITE]] also just agreed to terms with AXTI re InP supply"** (MS TMT desk · Tom Wigg, 2026-08-04). ⚠️ **So the fresh LITE supply agreement is now the exposed link in a live trade dispute.** The bear case was stated flatly on FinTwit: **"Will China still allow InP exports to the U.S.? I'M VERY BEARISH ON AXTI. Now I'm starting to wonder whether U.S. companies will even be able to use CXMT DRAM"** (@jukan05, 2026-08-04). ⚠️ **Note the structural asymmetry this exposes: AXT is a US-listed company whose InP substrate production sits in China, so an export-control fight cuts BOTH ways through the same P&L — it is simultaneously the chokepoint the West needs and the asset China controls. That is the cleanest statement of the page's core risk yet, and it was made by the market rather than by the company.**

**2) …and the opposite datapoint, same day.** Per an MS note relayed in Chinese-language FinTwit, **"AXTI indicates that InP substrate bottlenecks are BEGINNING TO EASE, with INTERNAL FAB CAPACITY becoming the more important constraint"** (Morgan Stanley Research relayed via @QQ_Timmy, 2026-08-04). ⚠️ *Relay of an MS note via a third-party translation — treat as paraphrase, not verbatim, and let the primary note supersede it.* MS in the same note says its CPO concern is **adoption degree rather than timing "because NPO seems to be gaining more traction,"** with pluggables the near-term growth driver for [[LITE]]/[[COHR]].

⚠️ **These two items point in opposite directions and are deliberately left unreconciled: the physical bottleneck may be easing at exactly the moment the political one is being created. Both are logged; neither is netted. Cross-check against this page's own Jul-14 (Jefferies expert: AXTI named in the InP bottleneck) and Jul-25 ("Stranded Indium-Phosphide") threads, which argued InP is structurally tighter than DRAM — if the substrate bottleneck genuinely eases, that framing needs revisiting.**

## Catalysts / what to watch
- **🆕 Whether the drafted US ban on new Chinese optical transceivers advances — and whether China responds by restricting InP exports.** This is now the dominant swing factor on the page: MS names AXTI as the explicit retaliation mechanism, days after [[LITE]] agreed InP supply terms with AXT. Watch for scope definition (corporate-domicile vs manufactured-in-China), any MOFCOM export-licence action on InP, and whether a **reciprocal-component-purchase US-China deal** (MS's expected resolution path) materialises instead.
- **🆕 Reconcile "InP bottlenecks easing" against "InP is structurally tighter than DRAM."** An MS note relayed 08-04 has AXT saying substrate bottlenecks are easing with internal fab capacity now the binding constraint — the opposite of this page's Jul-14/Jul-25 framing. **Get the primary MS note and settle it**; if easing is real, the scarcity leg of the bull case weakens independently of the political risk.
- **China InP export-license cadence** — whether eased restrictions hold and ex-US shipment permits (e.g., COHR Sweden) clear.
- **6-inch InP validation** at [COHR](COHR.md) and whether AXTI follows or stays conservative.
- Next print: revenue/EPS/GM and the GaAs/Ge contribution beyond "InP >50%."
- Any US-government response to China InP export controls; CSP long-term supply agreements.

## Risks
- China MOFCOM permitting directly gates revenue (proven by the FQ4'25 miss).
- 6-inch InP displacement risk if [COHR](COHR.md) scales first.
- "Trading vehicle" beta / large MC run-up sets a high valuation bar.
- Customer/supply-chain concentration in a regulatory-sensitive material.
