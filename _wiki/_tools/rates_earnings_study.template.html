Research conclusion: the strongest bull argument is the direction of forward estimates, supported by sales and operating profit. The strongest historical warning is that stocks had already fallen sharply in the first half of 2022 while annual EPS forecasts were still rising. The decisive question is whether today’s stronger earnings can persist through tighter financial conditions.

1. Define the comparison before interpreting it

Three different earnings measures

Growth compares earnings with the prior year. Revisions compare today’s forecast with an earlier forecast for the same year or quarter. Surprises compare reported earnings with the estimate immediately before the release.

A company can grow quickly and still suffer forecast cuts. A rolling next-12-month EPS series also rises when a lower-earnings quarter rolls out and a higher-earnings quarter rolls in; that is not necessarily an upgrade.

Two time windows

Use the full 2022 episode to understand the sequence, then compare the recent June–September rate moves. For revisions, the cleanest matched window is June 30–August 31. A later snapshot establishes whether the direction continued.

Index membership and earnings weights differ between years. These are investable-index comparisons, not a constant-company panel. “Technology,” “Mag 7” and the broader AI supply chain are distinct universes.

Rate increase since June 30, basis points

The macro mechanism

In 2022 the Fed tightened to a 4.25–4.50% target range by December and continued reducing its securities holdings. The September 2026 decision was a 25 bp hike to 3.75–4.00%, with the Fed citing resilient spending, strong productivity and robust investment. [[cite:fed22]] [[cite:fed26]]

Inference: a growth-supported rate rise can coexist with earnings upgrades, but a higher required return still compresses what investors will pay. The observed nominal-minus-real spread is not pure expected inflation; liquidity and risk premia matter. These data alone do not identify the term-premium contribution.

3. 2022 began with strong growth. Deceleration came next.

The economy entered 2022 after an exceptional earnings rebound: FactSet expected 45.1% growth for 2021 and 9.2% for 2022 on 30 December 2021. The problem was the expected and then realized slowdown from that unusually strong base. [[cite:enter22]]

This rolling-EPS change includes calendar effects; it is not a fixed-period earnings revision. Multiplying the reported rounded ratios reproduces the price move only approximately. [[cite:sep26]]

The distinction that matters most

The current-quarter upgrade is modest; the full-year upgrade is much larger. Full-year forecasts incorporate results that have already been reported, including investment gains. They are not a clean measure of prospective operating momentum.

The next-year forecast helps address this: CY27 EPS increased approximately 4.9% over June 25–September 24, while CY23 EPS fell 3.5% over June 30–September 29 in 2022. The observation windows differ by five days. [[cite:jun26]] [[cite:sep26]] [[cite:q322]]

Breadth is the shared vulnerability. In July–August 2022, nine sectors cut Q3 EPS; IT fell 9.1% and Communication Services fell 12.8%, while Energy rose 9.6%. [[cite:rev22]] In the same 2026 window, only four sectors increased Q3 EPS and seven cut; Energy led at +11.8%. [[cite:rev26]] A rising aggregate still does not describe every company.

Year-over-year growth, not estimate revisions. NVIDIA fiscal Q2 ends in July/early August; Microsoft’s quarter ends in June. Each EPS growth rate uses the company’s contemporaneous basis. NVIDIA’s 2026 non-GAAP series includes stock compensation and restates its prior-year comparator, so cross-era non-GAAP definitions are not identical.

NVIDIA itself illustrates why starting growth is not sufficient: its summer 2021 quarter delivered 68% revenue growth and 89% non-GAAP EPS growth. That did not prevent the following year’s contraction in earnings. What supports the current comparison is continued operating momentum, not a claim that the earlier cycle lacked strong companies. [[cite:nv21]]

Investment gains distort the headline

FactSet’s 28 August analysis puts Q2 Mag 7 earnings growth at 118.5%; excluding Alphabet and Amazon, growth is 43.2%. The two companies’ reported other-income gains were $98.0bn and $53.4bn, respectively. Those gains entered FactSet’s earnings calculations. [[cite:mag26]]

The 7 August index snapshot still showed 32.0% earnings growth excluding those two companies, versus 50.4% including them. That supports underlying strength, but excluding two whole companies is not the same as normalizing every company’s operating earnings. [[cite:underlying26]]

What the 2022 analogy can still capture

Inference: high growth can attract excess capacity and become harder to sustain. Equipment suppliers are exposed to changes in customer investment plans; cloud operators also face depreciation and cash-flow demands as infrastructure expands. Higher financing costs can affect both.

Monitor revenue, operating income and cash conversion alongside EPS. Non-operating investment marks should not receive the same recurring multiple as durable operating earnings.

6. Test the Fidelity exhibit without double-counting earnings

The user confirmed Fidelity as the exhibit source; its visible date is 27 September 2026. The commentary proposes roughly 16x P/E if the 10-year approaches 6%, versus a starting 19–20x range, with about 30% earnings growth providing an offset. [[cite:fidelity]]

HARD / ANCHOR The independent FactSet observation is 19.2x forward P/E. Its September forecast has CY26/CY27 earnings growth of 32.0%/15.4%, and Q3/Q4 2026 growth of 29.1%/26.8%. Q3 EPS revisions remained positive at +1.3% through the September report. [[cite:sep26]]

PARTIAL 19.5x below is the midpoint of Fidelity’s range. SCENARIO 16x and the future EPS-denominator changes are assumptions. This is an accounting bridge with an observed starting valuation; it is not a fitted rule that 6% Treasury yields imply 16x P/E.

Price return = (1 + change in EPS denominator) × (ending P/E ÷ starting P/E) − 1

Worked example, using the exhibit’s midpoint

  1. Hold EPS constant: 16 ÷ 19.5 − 1 = −17.9%.
  2. Assume that same earnings denominator rises 30%: 1.30 × 16 ÷ 19.5 − 1 = +6.7%.
  3. Solve for the growth needed for a flat price: 19.5 ÷ 16 − 1 = 21.9%.
  4. For illustration, a 15.4% increase yields 1.154 × 16 ÷ 19.5 − 1 = −5.3%.
The horizon is the critical assumption. CY26 growth is measured against CY25; much of it is already realized or embedded in forecasts. It cannot simply be added to future returns. For an instantaneous re-pricing, hold the forecast period fixed and apply revisions only. Over a year, use the change between comparable forward earnings denominators, including the calendar roll. CY27 year-over-year growth is not automatically that number either.

The scatterplot in Fidelity’s exhibit is descriptive. Its horizontal axis is inverse bond yield; it is not equity duration or a directly comparable bond cash-flow multiple. Regime, inflation, expected growth and the equity risk premium all affect the relationship. A historical association cannot establish a stable causal valuation floor.

Investment interpretation: today’s earnings evidence supports a more resilient outcome than the later stages of 2022. It does not establish that the market can absorb any rate increase, nor that +30% earnings growth is still available from today’s valuation denominator. The strongest test is recurring, fixed-period forward EPS progression through the next reporting season.
