## Snapshot

CME Group runs the dominant US listed-derivatives franchise: futures and options on interest rates (Treasury, SOFR), equity indexes (E-mini S&P under licence from S&P Dow Jones Indices, in which CME owns 27%), energy (WTI, Henry Hub), agricultural commodities, metals and FX, all cleared in-house at CME Clearing, plus the BrokerTec (US Treasuries/repo) and EBS (FX) cash venues and a fast-growing market-data business. Revenue is average daily volume (ADV) × rate per contract (RPC) plus data; below the line, CME keeps a spread on clearing-member cash collateral, and it returns capital through a regular dividend, an annual variable dividend and buybacks. The thesis is that a "risk-always-on" macro (Fed policy shifts, Middle East conflict, AI-related equity volatility) keeps volume compounding while pricing and data add points of growth at ~70% adjusted operating margins. The open tests are whether perpetual futures and vertically integrated retail venues erode the institutional moat or only funnel retail flow, how much of 2026's volume is cyclical, and whether new ventures (Treasury clearing, FanDuel event contracts, Compute futures, tokenized collateral) add revenue without diluting the expense line through a CEO transition. [Company Q3 release, 2026-10-21](../CME/earnings/2026-10-21_exhibit9919302026.htm); [Duffy / Fitzpatrick, Q3 call, 2026-10-21](../CME/transcripts/CME_Q3-2026-earnings_2026-10-21.md); [FY2025 10-K, filed 2026-02-26](../CME/CME_10-K_2026-02-26_0001156375-26-000009.html); Capstone synthesis, 2026-11-02.

**Volume is broad and macro-driven; RPC is the swing factor.** Every asset class grew ADV by double digits. Equity index (+22.9%) led, and that also lifted license fees. The 10-Q attributes the growth to inflation pressure that it believes led the Fed to raise rates for the first time in over three years, to skepticism about AI spending that drove equity-index volatility, and to the Middle East conflict, which disrupted energy, agricultural and metals supply. Total RPC was flat sequentially but 2.3c below Q3 2025. In equities and metals the year-on-year rate decline offset much of the volume gain: metals fees were flat despite +13.3% ADV. Management put Q4-to-date volume 17% above last year's record Q4 and year-to-date volume 12% ahead; quarter-end open interest was 128m contracts and above 133m on the call date. [Q3 10-Q](../CME/CME_10-Q_2026-10-23_0001156375-26-000068.html); [Duffy / Fitzpatrick, Q3 call, 2026-10-21](../CME/transcripts/CME_Q3-2026-earnings_2026-10-21.md).

**Market data and pricing add growth on top of volume.** Market data has grown for 34 consecutive quarters. Julie Winkler (CCO) split Q3 growth roughly into thirds: professional subscribers; retail and simulation (non-professional) platforms; and feeds, software, pricing and policy changes. Audit and catch-up revenue was $4.7m, below Q2. On pricing, fee changes effective 2026-01-01 (market data) and 2026-04-01 (transactions) were guided to add ~1–1.5% to total revenue on 2025-like activity. The 2026-10-01 changes (crypto, grains, interest-rate tiers, micro equities) add ~0.5–1% of clearing and transaction fees annualized. CME is moving away from a once-a-year December repricing. [Winkler / Fitzpatrick, Q3 call, 2026-10-21](../CME/transcripts/CME_Q3-2026-earnings_2026-10-21.md); [Fitzpatrick, Q4 2025 call, 2026-02-04](../CME/transcripts/CME_Q4-2025-earnings_2026-02-04.md).

**Costs and capital return: low leverage, all excess returned.** Year-to-date adjusted expenses ex-license fees were $405m + $412m + $428m = $1,245m. That implies about $450m in Q4 to hit the ~$1.695bn guide, which compares with ~$1.625bn in FY2025 (Capstone arithmetic on CFO figures). In February the board declared a $6.15 annual variable dividend alongside the $1.30 regular quarterly dividend. Nine-month buybacks were $1.8bn at an average $272.89, and the CFO said redeployment of the OSTTRA sale proceeds is now complete. The regular-dividend policy targets 50–60% of the prior year's cash earnings. **Bull case — an all-weather toll with new layers.** CME owns the benchmark futures pools in US rates, equity index, WTI/Henry Hub, agriculture and precious metals. Clearing them in one house produced over $100bn a day of average margin savings in Q3, which is hard for a new venue to replicate. Volume rises with uncertainty, and the macro has supplied plenty: a Fed hike, a new Fed chair, war-driven commodity disruption and AI-driven equity volatility. Pricing power is now exercised several times a year, market data grew 15–20% in each 2026 quarter, and retail client acquisition has grown at double digits for ten straight quarters. All excess capital is returned, debt is low, and the dividend yield is >5% (Redburn). Redburn treats retail, prediction markets and blockchain as optionality "not in numbers"; Treasury clearing, Treasury Link, single-stock futures and Compute futures are further options. [Q3 release](../CME/earnings/2026-10-21_exhibit9919302026.htm); [Winkler / McCourt / Dennis, Q3 call](../CME/transcripts/CME_Q3-2026-earnings_2026-10-21.md); [Redburn 5, 2026-06-14 — Outlook](https://outlook.office365.com/owa/?ItemID=AAMkADllY2NkMmU1LTc3YTEtNGRkMC1hMjBkLWU1NjIzOTY5OTZhMQBGAAAAAAC2MTk9L2QgQLGbcPk%2FhCdiBwDBpLwblpT0TZ%2Bt%2Fvz7Ao6sAAAAAAEMAADBpLwblpT0TZ%2Bt%2Fvz7Ao6sAAVIC53oAAA%3D&exvsurl=1&viewmodel=ReadMessageItem); Capstone interpretation, 2026-11-02.

**Bear case — cyclical volume, eroding rate and a regulator that favors entrants.** 2026 growth leans on a volatility regime that can fade; Q2 already showed flat revenue on a tough comparison. Open interest is not growing with volume, and RPC is below last year; the company names product, venue and customer mix and tiered pricing as its drivers. The perpetuals fight is being waged in court rather than in products, and the withdrawal of 24/7 energy contracts shows that CME's commercial clients set the pace, while regulators may approve similar products for others. Net collateral income depends on balances and on the rate CME keeps: the 10-Q shows a lower average rate of return year on year. One clearing firm was 12% of 2025 clearing fees, and the largest clearing firms have lobbied for utility-style clearing. A CEO transition (Lynne Fitzpatrick, expected March 2027 per Redburn) coincides with these fights. [Q2 release](../CME/earnings/2026-07-22_exhibit9916302026.htm); [Q3 10-Q](../CME/CME_10-Q_2026-10-23_0001156375-26-000068.html); [FY2025 10-K](../CME/CME_10-K_2026-02-26_0001156375-26-000009.html); Capstone interpretation, 2026-11-02.

**What would change the view:** a court or CFTC outcome that treats perpetuals as swaps, or keeps branded-index lookalikes retail-only, together with Q4/2027 ADV holding up against 2026 comparisons and stable RPC, would support the bull case. The bear case would gain from a CFTC approval of equity-index perpetuals with real institutional uptake, a sustained RPC decline, or a 2027 expense guide that absorbs pricing gains. Compare rates/energy franchises and Treasury clearing with [ICE](ICE.md), equity-index options and the S&P license with [CBOE](CBOE.md), retail/event-contract economics with [HOOD](HOOD.md) and [COIN](COIN.md), and FICC-volatility read-across with [GS](GS.md), on matched definitions only.

## Risks / monitoring

Track volume cyclicality as volatility normalizes; RPC mix (tiering, member vs non-member, micro and retail products); net collateral interest, which depends on balances and on the rate of return CME keeps (lower year on year in Q3 2026); clearing-firm concentration (one firm was 12% of 2025 clearing fees) and clearing members' push for utility-style clearing; competition from ICE, Cboe, FMX, digital-asset platforms and prediction markets; regulatory treatment of perpetuals, 24/7 trading and sports event contracts (litigation is named as a JV risk); Treasury clearing execution against DTCC/FICC and ICE's Treasury clearing service; dependence on the S&P index licence through the JV; Google Cloud migration costs and resilience; transaction taxes or repeal of 60/40 tax treatment; cyber risk; and execution through the CEO transition.
