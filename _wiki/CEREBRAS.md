## Snapshot
Cerebras is a wafer-scale AI-compute company built around the **Wafer Scale Engine (WSE)** — it uses an entire silicon wafer as one compute engine rather than dicing it into chips, putting enough on-die SRAM next to compute to hold a model's weights without round-tripping to HBM (UBS/Tim Archer education call, 2026-05-06). The result is a **fast-inference** machine: the niche is the low-latency, lower-right corner of the throughput/latency curve where HBM-based GPUs are weak. It is the only player that spans **from chip to its own inference cloud** — "even Nvidia sells only hardware and software and does not sell tokens from its own cloud" (UBS, 2026-05-06). 2025 revenue $510.0M (+76% Y/Y), split **$358.4M hardware (70%) / $151.6M cloud & other (30%)** (S-1/A via Futurum, TechTimes, 2026-05). Mix is shifting hard toward cloud on the OpenAI ramp.

## Position in the value chain
Cerebras sits between a concentrated TSMC-based supply chain and a concentrated, largely UAE-/OpenAI-driven demand base. It competes against NVIDIA only in the **fast-inference** slice (decode), not broad multi-tenant training/throughput. The two structural tells: a defensible-but-narrow wafer-scale moat vs NVIDIA, and acute **customer concentration** (G42/MBZUAI ~86% of 2025 revenue, migrating to OpenAI ~70-80% by 2028 per UBS, 2026-05-06).

## Debate / thesis
**🗄️ ARCHIVE BACKFILL — DAMNANG (Substack), 2026-05-30 and 2026-05-13.** ⚠️ **INDEPENDENT KOREAN SEMIS SUBSTACK (damnang2.substack.com; also publishes as "The Optical Edge"), NOT A BROKER — no rating, no price target, no formal estimate.** Launched 2026-03-15, ~9k subscribers, paid. **Three months old; filed as historical context ONLY and superseding nothing — in particular, every figure below predates the 08-19 Supernova launch of CS-4 / Nexus / WSE-3T and describes WSE-3-era economics.**
- **🗄️ The most complete external teardown of the WSE-3 ceiling on this wiki, and it reaches the same conclusion SemiAnalysis reached three months later by a different route** (**DAMNANG (Substack)** — "Cerebras WSE-3: The Technical Achievement and the Physical Ceiling", **2026-05-30**). **The specs, as printed: 44GB of on-chip SRAM; ~900,000 operating cores of 970,000 physical laid down, each with a dedicated 48KB of SRAM; 21 PB/s aggregate on-chip bandwidth; 125 PFLOPS of SPARSE FP16 (⚠️ he flags that Cerebras has not published dense figures); a 462.25cm² / 46,225mm² die, ~21.5cm on a side, "57 times the H100" and ~54x a reticle field; CS-3 power cited at 23kW by Cerebras and ~27kW in Groq's comparison material, against a DGX B200 at 14.3kW, for a claimed 2.2x PFLOPS/watt on peak FP16; MemoryX expandable from 4TB to 2.4PB over 100GbE with CS-3 system I/O "around 1.2 Tb/s (about 150 GB/s)"; still on TSMC 5nm — "It launched in 2024 and remains the same in 2026."** 🔴 **THE CEILING ARGUMENT, WHICH IS THE PART WORTH KEEPING: 44GB caps servable model size at roughly 22B parameters at FP16, 44B at FP8, 88B at INT4 — while DeepSeek V3 (671B total MoE) is ~1.3TB at FP16 and Llama 4 Maverick (400B total) ~800GB, so "Neither fits in 44GB of SRAM." And the ceiling barely moves: TSMC HD SRAM cell size goes 0.021µm² at 5nm to 0.0199µm² at 3nm, "a shrink of only about 5%", against HBM projected "past 1TB by 2028" — "The capacity gap between SRAM and HBM is not closing, it is widening."** **Three further constraints he raises that the sell-side on this page does not: (1) fixed-precision risk — the cores compute in FP16 while NVIDIA adds a native low-precision path (FP8, then FP4) each generation, so a 2-3 year wafer-scale redesign cycle risks "chasing one generation behind"; (2) yield-as-variance rather than yield-as-scrap — at TSMC 5nm's ~0.09 defects/cm², a 462.25cm² wafer carries ~42 expected defects versus ~0.73 for an H100, and because bad die cannot simply be discarded the result is "performance variance across chips" under one product name, which undermines the unit-substitutability that makes GPU fleets fungible; (3) the MemoryX cliff — once a model exceeds 44GB and must stream weights, "the core advantage of 'zero off-chip access' breaks."** **Business marks as printed at that date (all pre-Supernova, pre-Q2): 2025 revenue $510M, +76% y/y; IPO priced $185, closed day one at $311.07, $236.99 on 2026-05-29 for ~$52B of market cap and "a PSR of roughly 100x"; the [[OPENAI]] compute purchase agreement at "more than $20B" of RPO recognised 2026-2029, plus a $1B working-capital loan at 6% and 33.4M warrants struck at $0.00001 (~$7.9B of value at the May-29 price); and revenue concentration of MBZUAI 62.0% + G42 24.0% = 86% of 2025 revenue.** ⚠️ **His conclusion — that Cerebras is "a vertical accelerator specialized in low-latency decode" rather than a general inference platform, and that at ~100x PSR "the risk-reward does not line up at the current price" — is one independent author's view from May, on WSE-3 economics, before CS-4. It is NOT a rating and it does not supersede any house mark on this page.
