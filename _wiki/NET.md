## Snapshot
Cloudflare is the "connectivity cloud" — a single global network (present in 120+ countries / 350+ cities) that sits in front of a large share of Internet traffic and sells application performance/security, network security, and a developer/compute platform on top of it. Management frames the business as four "Acts": **Act 1** = application services + the legacy CDN/security/performance layer (the largest revenue base); **Act 2** = Zero Trust / SASE (Access, Gateway, DLP, CASB, email security, browser isolation) displacing legacy hardware (Palo Alto, Zscaler-type incumbents); **Act 3** = the **Workers developer platform** (Workers, Workers AI, Durable Objects, Dynamic Workers, AI Gateway) — edge compute/inference; **Act 4** = the emerging AI-content layer (AI Crawl Control / "pay per crawl") to control and eventually monetize AI bot traffic (NET Q4 2025 call, 2026-02-10). FY2025 revenue $2,167.9M, +29.8% YoY (NET Q4 2025 PR, 2026-02-10). The core debate is whether Cloudflare is the structural winner of the "agentic Internet" re-platforming — owning both the traffic agents pass through (Act 1) and the platform they run on (Workers/Act 3) — at a premium multiple, against gross-margin dilution from the fast-growing-but-lower-margin developer platform.

Four reasons they think NET sets up well into 2Q26:
- **(1) The lag is the thesis — traffic has inflected, revenue has not (yet).** Traffic through NET's network has **continued to inflect upward over the past three quarters (4Q25, 1Q26)** but Barclays has **"not seen revenue inflect in the same fashion yet."** The mechanism is the analytically interesting part: **the majority of NET's contracts are cap-based, which makes requests/traffic a good leading indicator of revenue.** Customers set the initial cap by forecasting their own traffic growth; when **actual request volumes grow faster than forecast they do NOT upgrade on the inflection — they must hit the limit of the current cap first**, so monetization only shows up after request volume crosses that threshold. Timing differs per customer depending on headroom left in the cap and the rate of traffic growth. Barclays' framing: **"the key debate focuses on timing rather than underlying demand."** The demand driver: **non-human/agentic traffic has SURPASSED human traffic on the network** and is likely to keep growing (Barclays · Saket Kalia, 2026-08-04).
- **(2) Pool-of-funds (PoF) ramp should support both revenue and DBNR in 2Q and through 2H.** 4Q25 was a strong PoF quarter — **new ACV booked up almost 50% y/y with PoF ~20% of ACV booked in the quarter**. Because a PoF contract gives the customer **better unit pricing**, near-term revenue falls (same consumption, cheaper units) and the **DBNR numerator is artificially deflated** — which is why **DBNR ticked down to 118% in 1Q26 mechanically**, not as a demand signal. Barclays expects that large cohort to exit the initial dip and begin ramping in 2Q, taking **DBNR up to ~119%** and supporting the ~30% revenue growth (Barclays · Saket Kalia, 2026-08-04).
- **(3) Margins — gross margin stabilising, Rule of 50 by 2027.** Gross margin modelled to **stabilise at ~72% in 2Q**, inside the **new 70–77% long-term target range from Investor Day**; **~14% EBIT margin for 2Q**, on track to a **"Rule of 50" by 2027**. After the RIF and Investor Day the **long-term EBIT margin target went UP to 30%+ from 20%+**, as the company was already on pace to reach the prior target in FY27 on Barclays' numbers. Barclays models the Rule of 50 as **high-20s revenue growth + 20%+ EBIT margins**, with **management noting NET could exceed the Rule of 50 in FY28** (Barclays · Saket Kalia, 2026-08-04).
- **(4) Share gain is ACCELERATING, not merely continuing — and it is greenfield.** Per the **W3Tech Reverse Proxy Survey**, NET gained **>100bps of reverse-proxy share for the third quarter in a row** and now sits **in front of ~24% of the internet**. The rate is the news: historically share gain **fluctuated around 0–30bps per quarter**, versus **~110bps q/q in 4Q25, ~110bps in 1Q26 and ~150bps in 2Q26**. ⚠️ **Critically, NET has been gaining share "largely from websites that did not previously use a reverse proxy"** — i.e. **greenfield adoption, not seats taken from Akamai/Fastly**; do not read this as direct CDN share transfer. On IDC data, over **2024–2025 the CDN market grew ~6% while Cloudflare's revenue grew ~30%**; NET is the **3rd-largest vendor in global CDN and the fastest share gainer of the last 2 years, with the rate of share gain increasing y/y** (Barclays · Saket Kalia, 2026-08-04).
- **Act 4 / microtransaction optionality — first real sizing.** NET processes **~half a billion transactions per second** and **management estimates 1–10% of those are monetizable through some microtransaction**. Barclays calls Act 4 **"still early"** but sees a large monetization opportunity as internet business models evolve around agentic traffic — being **in front of ~24% of the internet** is the asset that makes the position "more valuable" if that shift happens (Barclays · Saket Kalia, 2026-08-04).

- **Intense and increasing competition** — across application performance/security, network security and the developer/compute platform (hyperscalers AWS/Azure/GCP, Akamai/Fastly in CDN, Zscaler/Palo Alto in SASE/Zero Trust).

- **Content / customer-activity liability** — the activities or content of paying/free customers (and Cloudflare's response) can create political, reputational and legal consequences.
- **Macro / geopolitical** — reduced enterprise security/performance spend, tariffs, conflicts and elections could pressure customers and demand.
- **Key-person / talent** — reliance on co-founders and key sales/technical/management personnel; the ~20% RIF adds execution and retention risk to this.
- **Indebtedness** — convertible senior notes (incl. 2030 Notes) and revolving credit facility — ability to repay when due.
