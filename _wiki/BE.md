## Snapshot
Bloom Energy manufactures solid-oxide fuel cells (SOFC) that generate electricity on-site from natural gas or hydrogen, sold primarily to commercial/industrial customers including data centers. The narrative centers on data-center power demand: hyperscalers and AI-infra builders (e.g., Nebius) are signing power-supply deals with BE as grid capacity constraints intensify, validating the "AI factory power" bottleneck thesis alongside [GEV](GEV.md), [VRT](VRT.md), [PWR](PWR.md), and [ETN](ETN.md).
_Note: many "BE" tags in sell-side digests are false positives for BE Semiconductor (BESI) — Bloom-specific coverage is thinner than headline counts suggest._

## Debate / thesis
- **🆕 THE BEST THING ON THIS PAGE IS NOW A CUSTOMER'S CANDID ASSESSMENT, NOT A SELL-SIDE MODEL — a data-centre developer's verbatim view of Bloom, relayed from a Jefferies energy/power roundtable (Jefferies Sales & Trading · James Snyder, "Energy/Power Rundown", 2026-08-02).** ⚠️ **This is the most useful single row on the name because it is a buyer explaining the trade-off rather than a bull explaining the TAM.**
  - **The praise is technical: the developer "LIKES THE BE SYSTEMS… THEY'RE CLEAN INSTALL," and cites BE solving two specific problems that the alternative does not — "BE GETS TAX CREDIT AND BE SOLVES AIR PERMIT CONCERNS." ⚠️ THE COST GAP HAS CLOSED FROM THE OTHER SIDE: "Value for BE? Recips are BUMPING AGAINST THE $3000/kW so CLOSE TO BE COST." That is a material change to the competitive frame — Bloom's historical objection was capex per kW versus reciprocating engines, and engine pricing has inflated into Bloom's range. Add the tax credit and the permitting advantage and the developer's economics now favour BE at the margin.**
  - ⚠️ **THE CRITICISM IS OPERATIONAL AND IT IS THE REAL LIMIT: "The downside is that THEY DON'T RAMP FAST OR REACT FAST. NEED COMPLIMENTARY ASSETS AROUND THEM like solar, batteries, etc. THEY ARE NOT AT SCALE and HIGHER CAPEX but like the technology, availability, etc." So fuel cells are being designed in as BASELOAD with reciprocating engines or storage doing the load-following — which caps Bloom's share of any given site rather than its presence on it. The concrete instance: "PLAN TO USE BE ON 40 GW PROJECT (20 GW OF BE FUEL CELLS) AND LOAD FOLLOW WITH SMALLER RECIPS." A 20GW fuel-cell allocation inside one project is an enormous number against Bloom's installed base; treat it as a developer's plan, not an order.**
  - **Speed to power confirmed by the customer: "BE TALKING ABOUT MEETING DEMAND IN ~9 MONTHS."** ⚠️ **But the same developer punctures the industry timeline generally: "Delays? Got to have right offtake, right equipment at the right time, E&C contract, gas contracts, gas pipes, permitting… 2027 TIMEFRAME FOR ANYTHING DOESN'T SEEM REAL… 2028 SEEMS LIKE A MORE LEGITIMATE TIMEFRAME for a lot of these sites." And on counterparty behaviour: "HYPERSCALERS HAVE BEEN FLAKY, WISHY WASHY, AND HAVE GHOSTED THEM A COUPLE OF TIMES. 60 days go by and then they come back." That is the honest counterweight to every 2027-dated behind-the-meter forecast on this wiki, including Bloom's own.**
  - **On the overcapacity question, the developer is structurally constructive on BTM: "Potential overcapacity on BTM side? Think it is FIRST IN, LAST OUT. Once the generation is on site, DON'T SEE THAT EQUIPMENT MOVING… don't think grid connection for 4-5 years so see MODULAR WORKING WELL FOR A WHILE before large frames come on." And the framing of the whole bottleneck: "LAND IS NOT THE ISSUE… IT IS HOW DO YOU POWER THAT LAND."**
- **🆕 Management's own intra-quarter messaging, from the same Jefferies roundup — the demand claims, with the capacity answer notably withheld (2026-08-02).** **Bloom's positioning as relayed: "SPEED TO POWER: meets the time-critical needs of customers and delivers power IN MONTHS… customers now come to them LATE IN THEIR DEVELOPMENT PROCESS and ask Bloom to step in as the PRIMARY ON-SITE POWER SOLUTION"; "ability to provide 800-VOLT DC POWER"; "NOT DEPENDENT ON ONE CUSTOMER OR ONE PROJECT… multiple customers and multiple projects across every stage of development"; "WORKING WITH ALL THE MAJOR US HYPERSCALERS AND OVER A DOZEN OF THE NEOCLOUDS"; and "engagement with customers suggests that THE PACE OF INVESTMENT WILL NOT JUST CONTINUE BUT ACCELERATE."** **The market-sizing claim: "somewhere between 30 GIGAWATTS AND 40 GIGAWATTS OF NEW AI DATA CENTER CAPACITY IS GOING TO BE TURNED ON IN 2027."** ⚠️ **Two deflections to note. (1) On capacity: "confident of keeping that promise for everything we have in the order book and everything that we see coming forward. NO OTHER COMMENT ON CAPACITY ADDITIONS" — management will not size the manufacturing ramp, which is the binding constraint the developer above identified ("they are not at scale"). (2) On the financing structure of a specific project: "won't comment specifically on individual projects… will say THE FINANCIER IS ON THE HOOK TO TAKE DELIVERY OF THE EQUIPMENT FROM BLOOM" — i.e. Bloom's receivable sits with a financier rather than the end user, which de-risks Bloom and shifts the credit question one step down the chain. ## Risks
- **🆕 Regulatory charges on C&I customers using distributed generation** — MS's stated #1 downside risk for BE; attacks behind-the-meter economics directly (MS "Power Struggle", p.53, 2026-07-14). Alongside it, MS lists **stricter emissions regulation**, **new entrants/technologies with cost-competitive products**, and **cost reductions failing to materialise**.
- **🆕 The power shortfall BE is valued off may be closing.** MS cut its US DC power shortfall to **38 GW (2026-28) from 47 GW (2025-28)**, and at MS's own "most likely" midpoint the probability-weighted solutions leave a **net shortfall of only ~1 GW through 2028 (2% of deployments)** (MS "Power Struggle", Exhibit 49, 2026-07-14). ⚠️ **Bloom is also only the third-largest bucket in MS's solution stack (5-8 GW) behind nat-gas turbines (15-20 GW) and bitcoin-site conversion (10-19 GW, MS's "highest conviction" solution).**
- **🆕 AI-capex shortfall risk, quantified for the first time on this page:** ~**$156bn of projects cancelled or delayed in 2025 and $130bn already in 1Q26**, against MS's **$877bn 2026 global AI-capex estimate**, with MS explicitly flagging *"a greater risk of actual AI capex falling short"* if local opposition keeps growing (MS "Power Struggle", 2026-07-14).
