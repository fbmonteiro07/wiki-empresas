## Snapshot
Vertiv is the leading pure-play in **critical digital infrastructure** for data centers, communication networks, and commercial/industrial environments — it powers, cools, deploys and services the electronics that process and store data (10-K, 2026-02-13). Portfolio spans **power** (AC/DC power management, single- and three-phase UPS, low/medium-voltage switchgear, busbar/busway, rack PDU), **thermal management** (air- and liquid-cooled — chillers, CDUs, rear-door, direct-to-chip), **integrated modular/prefab** (SmartRun overhead busway, OneCore converged white-space), **racks/enclosures** (Great Lakes), and a large **service & spares** annuity. Reports three geographic segments: **Americas** (~68% of Q1'26 sales), **APAC** (~19%), **EMEA** (~12%). End markets: data centers (hyperscale/cloud, colo, neocloud, enterprise), comms networks, C&I — with hyperscale/cloud + neocloud (CoreWeave, Nebius) the AI growth engine. The directest listed AI-datacenter power/cooling pure-play, and NVIDIA's named **800 VDC** ecosystem partner.

## Position in the value chain
Vertiv sits in the middle of the AI-datacenter physical-infrastructure stack: it buys power and thermal components (power semis/SiC, transformers, compressors, copper busbar, controls) and integrates them into the **gray-space-to-white-space** systems — power train, thermal chain, racks, prefab modules, plus lifecycle services — that hyperscalers, colos and enterprises plug compute into. It is a direct **AI-datacenter pure-play** and, via the **NVIDIA 800 VDC collaboration**, co-designs the next-gen power architecture (centralized rectifiers, high-efficiency DC busways, rack-level DC-DC) for Rubin Ultra-era AI factories (Vertiv–NVIDIA PR, 2025-10; Q3 FY25, 2025-10-22).

**Market sizing / share.** BofA sizes the global DC equipment market at $400bn+ in 2024 → >$500bn 2025E, +23% CAGR to >$900bn by 2028, of which **Electrical equipment ~$18bn and Thermal ~$10bn in 2024**; within those, **Schneider leads electrical (~21%), Vertiv is #2 electrical (~17%) and the thermal share leader (~20%), with Eaton ~16% and ABB ~9% of electrical** (BofA DC primer, 2025-09-15, Exhibits 6-7). _[Corrected 2026-07-14: prior text read "Schneider 22% / Vertiv thermal 17%" — a mis-transcription that placed Vertiv's electrical share (17%) into the thermal slot (true thermal 20%) and omitted Eaton's 16%; caught during the Semis MAP P&C recalibration.]_ BofA's content/MW work shows the architecture shift is content-accretive: NVL72 raises infrastructure content/MW to ~$3.1mn from ~$2.8mn for DGX SuperPOD (electrical +9%, thermal +18%, driven by CDUs/power-shelves), with Kyber/800V roughly in line with NVL72 (BofA, 2025-09-15). JPM frames the addressable pools as growing faster than the installed base: **server PSU TAM 3-4x to ~$38bn by 2028 (~64% CAGR); liquid-cooling TAM to ~$30bn by 2028 (~51% CAGR)** — CDU/sidecar (~$21bn) the largest liquid-cooling chunk — implying ~$450mn PSU / ~$360mn liquid-cooling TAM per incremental 1GW by 2028, with Vertiv named a key power and thermal beneficiary (JPM AI Datacenters & Power, 2025-11-04). VRT itself guides the DC market to **10-13% blended unit growth** (15-17% hyperscale/colo, 4-6% enterprise) and ~100GW added globally 2023-29; JPM's own model (~25% GW CAGR, installed base doubling to ~160GW) calls that "conservative" (JPM AI Capex & Financing, 2025-11-10).

  - **Portfolio breadth is the moat.** Vertiv spans the whole taxonomy — **SmartMod** (subsystem modules), **SmartRun** (overhead busway/containment), **MegaMod** (all-in-one prefab block; the **1 MW reference design runs ~26.5 m long × 24 m wide × 4 m high, MegaMod Plus extending to ~31 m wide**, shipped as transportable sections on heavy-haul and re-connected on site), and **OneCore** (the OEM-led datacenter-in-a-box, packaging power + cooling into **standardized 12.5 MW pods** that combine into larger AI-factory deployments). SemiAnalysis: "a vendor like Vertiv, Schneider, or Eaton sells a power module, a CDU, a white-space pod, and a whole block all at once."
  - **OneCore is inside NVIDIA's reference design.** **OneCore became the building block of NVIDIA's Vera Rubin DSX reference design as of March 2026**; Vertiv also sits in the DSX ecosystem alongside Cadence, Dassault Systèmes, Eaton, Jacobs, Nscale, Phaidra, Procore, PTC, Schneider Electric, Siemens, Switch and Trane.
  - **Capacity — not demand — is the binding constraint, and it is being bought.** **Vertiv's modular solutions run lead times of over 12 months today**, with some modular units sold out for 12+ months; big OEMs (Vertiv, Schneider, Siemens) are therefore **selective on capacity-slot allocation, requiring capacity minimums and favouring bigger projects** — pushing smaller developers toward system integrators. The fix: the **April 2026 BMarko acquisition is expected to increase regional modular manufacturing capacity by roughly 7x**. SemiAnalysis restates **$15.0bn of YE-2025 backlog and a 2.9x Q4 book-to-bill** (both already on this page — confirmation, not new).
  - **Field proof point — Hut 8 Beacon Point.** Hut 8 runs **Vertiv OneCore at its Corpus Christi Beacon Point campus to commercialise a 704 MW IT lease**, designed around NVIDIA's DSX reference architecture, with **American Electric Power on the utility relationship, Jacobs Solutions owning the EPCM scope and Vertiv supplying the critical power and cooling infrastructure**. Vertiv carries it from digital twin → factory assembly → site deployment → lifecycle support.
  - **The vendor-claim audit is a mild negative for the marketing, not for the model.** SemiAnalysis rebuilt the case bottom-up on a 50 MW liquid-cooled US hall and lands at **~36% shorter construction window (7-9 months) and ~8% lower all-in cost ($13.5mn vs $14.6mn per MW)** — well below headline vendor claims, and it explicitly narrows the scope of Vertiv's own numbers: **MegaMod's "up to 50% faster" measures module deployment vs on-site build, and SmartRun's "up to 85%" applies only to overhead busway and containment** (Schneider's 60% is power-and-cooling modules). It also flags the structural offset to OEM-led modular: a **double margin** — a module-vendor margin stacked on the site integrator's — which "compresses when the OEM also integrates on site, as Vertiv and Schneider do on their turnkey programs." Balancing bear datapoint: operators and MEP contractors in recent conversations claim **modular solutions have proven reliability issues and haven't lived up to their claims**, which would forfeit the time savings and put hardware at risk. (SemiAnalysis "The Wild Wild West Of LEGO Datacenters", 2026-07-29)
