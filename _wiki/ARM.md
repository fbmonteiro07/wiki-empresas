## Snapshot
Arm is the upstream IP-licensing layer of the compute industry: it designs CPU/GPU/NPU architectures and processor IP and collects (i) up-front license fees and (ii) a per-unit royalty on substantially every chip shipped using its designs — typically a % of chip ASP or a fixed fee per unit, rising as more Arm IP is included (FY26 20-F, Item 4). FY26 revenue $4.92B, +23% YoY, split License & other $2.31B (47%) / Royalty $2.61B (53%); operating margin ~18% GAAP, ~49% non-GAAP in Q4 (FY26 20-F; Q4 FY26 call, 2026-05-06). The franchise is in two transitions that define the thesis: (1) royalty mix-up from ARMv9 + Compute Subsystems (CSS) lifting per-chip rates, and (2) a move *downstream* into its own silicon — the **Arm AGI CPU** launched at Q4 FY26 with Meta as lead partner. ~57% of revenue comes from top-5 customers (incl. Arm China and SoftBank); SoftBank controls Arm (~controlled-company / FPI status). Mobile app processors are ~43% of royalty (FY26 20-F).

## Position in the value chain
Arm sits **upstream of nearly every SoC** in the world — virtually all smartphones, a majority of tablets/digital TVs, and a rising share of data-center CPUs run an Arm-architected core. It ships no volume silicon historically (AGI CPU is the first, production end-CY26); revenue is a **license + recurring per-unit royalty** model with a long tail (chips ship for years). The swing variable is the **server / data-center CPU TAM** — hyperscaler custom CPUs (AWS Graviton, Google Axion, Microsoft Cobalt, NVIDIA Vera/Grace) are Arm-architected, and agentic AI is lifting CPU:GPU ratios toward ~1:1.

- **🆕 CSS licence economics, sized — and the second-order effect on where EDA spend goes (SemiAnalysis "EDA Market Primer", 2026-05-21):** the page has carried CSS as a *royalty-rate* story (>10% of ASP, potentially >50% of royalties within a couple of years). The primer adds the *licence-fee* side, which is the bigger multiple on the upfront line: **CSS bundles CPU cores + interconnect (CMN) + memory controllers + system IP into a pre-integrated, pre-verified subsystem, licensed at $10-30M versus $1-5M for individual cores** — a 3-10x step-up in per-deal ASP — and it deepens lock-in beyond the ISA, because **"customers using CSS are locked into Arm's interconnect topology, memory architecture, and verification methodology,"** not just its cores. Business-model contrast worth keeping explicit: Arm's **FY2025 (ending March 2025) revenue of ~$4.0B split roughly 55% royalties ($2.2B) / 45% licensing ($1.8B)**, with royalties "typically 1-3% of chip ASP," versus Synopsys and Cadence, which "almost never collect royalties" and capture value upfront per design — i.e. Arm is the only large IP vendor whose economics compound with *volume* rather than with *design starts* (the primer also notes **70%+ of Arm's new licence agreements since 2019 run through Flexible Access**, all-you-can-evaluate for an annual fee with per-chip royalties only at production). The second-order effect is genuinely two-sided and is the part not on this page: **CSS reduces the custom RTL a customer writes, shrinking the implementation-tool TAM per design, while verification TAM holds or grows** because system-level integration testing still needs full emulation — so CSS **shifts customer EDA spend from synthesis/P&R toward emulation** (benefiting Cadence Palladium and Synopsys ZeBu). Read for Arm: CSS is doing to the EDA stack what it is doing to the customer's own design team — absorbing the front-end work — which is the mechanism behind the ASP step-up, and also the reason CSS adoption is a *substitution* of customer engineering spend rather than pure incremental cost. Arm is grouped with **Rambus and Alphawave** as IP companies whose per-engineer EDA spend is structurally lower because they "design once and license repeatedly." ## Risks
- **Own-silicon pivot (AGI CPU):** new competitive/brand/operational/financial risk; channel conflict with licensees; ~30% GM dilutive to IP model (FY26 20-F). Note the SoftBank licensing line (~$800M/yr) may not survive the chiplet transition (BofA, 2026-02-23).
- **Customer concentration:** top-5 ~57%, Arm China ~16–21%, mobile app processors ~43% of royalty (FY26 20-F).

- **Cyclicality / smartphone units:** royalty tied to end-market chip shipments; memory-driven handset unit softness (Q3 FY26 call); mobile ~50% of revenue exposed to tariff-related demand destruction (UBS, 2025-12-22).
- **Governance:** SoftBank-controlled company + FPI exemptions limit minority influence (FY26 20-F).
- **Litigation outcome — Arm lost the Qualcomm suit:** Arm's 2022 lawsuit against Qualcomm (over the Nuvia-derived architecture license), which had kept Qualcomm's SD1 datacenter CPU from launching, ended with **Qualcomm winning fully and preserving its Arm architectural license** — removing the legal barrier that had been protecting Arm's datacenter-CPU licensing base from Qualcomm competition; Qualcomm's SD2 now ships for revenue in 2027 (SemiAnalysis, Wong & Patel, "CPUs are Back," 2026-02-09).
- **Qualcomm Nuvia-team departures — competitive door reopening, but contained (SemiAnalysis):** Gerard Williams III and other original Nuvia team members left Qualcomm in January 2026, "casting a shadow over the future of their CPU program" — a potential (if delayed) opening for Arm's own licensing base to face less-credible Qualcomm competition; SemiAnalysis's own view is that "the impact will be limited until beyond 2028," i.e. not a near-term relief valve (SemiAnalysis, 2026-02-09).
