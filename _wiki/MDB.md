## Snapshot
MongoDB is a document (JSON) operational database sold two ways: **Atlas** (cloud DBaaS, consumption-billed, ~74–75% of revenue) and **Enterprise Advanced / "EA & other"** (self-managed licence, term-revenue under ASC 606, now the "run anywhere" leg). Search, vector search and — since the Feb-2025 Voyage AI acquisition ($220M, $20M cash; Q4 FY25 call, 2025-03-05) — embedding/re-ranking models are built into the platform. FY25 revenue crossed $2B (+19%) (Q4 FY25 call, 2025-03-05); FY27 is guided to $2.99–3.03B (+21–23%) (Q2 FY27 call, 2026-09-01). Positioning in the AI stack: the operational layer ("OLTP is the high ground") — agent memory, session state and retrieval — which is also why it is the public name that wears the **Postgres-for-new-AI-apps** bear (theme §2.4/§2.5). Leadership turned over completely inside the window: CFO Michael Gordon left (announced 2024-12-09), Mike Berry joined ~late May 2025 (Q1 FY26 call, 2025-06-04); **CEO Dev Ittycheria → CJ Desai** (ex-ServiceNow) — Desai's first call was Q3 FY26 on 2025-12-01, "day 28 on the job", i.e. he started ~2025-11-03; Ittycheria moved to the board.

## Current state (Q2 FY27, call 2026-09-01)
Beat-and-raise, the strongest print in the archive. **Revenue $772M, +30% y/y** (vs +24% a year ago), "the highest level of quarterly growth since fiscal 2024" (Desai/Berry). **Atlas ~+29% y/y for the fifth straight quarter**, ~300bp above guide; **record $127M year-over-year dollar add** (sixth straight quarter of rising y/y dollar growth); run rate "almost $2.3 billion", ~74% of revenue (Berry). **EA & other**: Desai said **+36% y/y**; Berry's section reads "approximately 30%… our strongest quarter in three years" — a transcript inconsistency, treat the exact figure as unverified; **EA & other ARR +11%** (third straight double-digit quarter), no large bundled deals this quarter (Berry). **Net ARR expansion 122%** (121% Q1, 119% a year ago), driven by both Atlas and EA. **RPO $1.52B, +91%**, current portion +73%. Customers 70,600 (record +2,900 net adds, "many of them AI natives"); Atlas 69,300; **~3,000 customers >$100K ARR, +17%**; **48% of $100K+ Atlas customers use two or more features (42% a year ago), "driven largely by vector and text search adoption"**; Voyage customer count roughly doubled q/q for the second consecutive quarter.

- **Margins / cash:** non-GAAP GM 75.9% (+210bp y/y), subscription GM 78.3% (+70bp, EA mix); **non-GAAP op margin 24%** (15% a year ago), op income $186M; EPS $1.90 on 85.8M shares (vs $1.00); third consecutive GAAP-profitable quarter. OCF $142M / **FCF $138M** (vs $72M/$70M); 1H FY27 FCF $335M. Cash $2.4B; $100M buyback + $59M RSU tax settlement in the quarter.
- **Guidance:** Q3 revenue **$756–761M (+20–21%)**, op income $152–156M (~20.5% margin at high end), EPS $1.57–1.61; **Atlas Q3 ~26%** ("toughest compare of the year"). **FY27 revenue $2.99–3.03B (+21–23%)** — raised from $2.92–2.96B; **Atlas FY27 ~27%** (+300bp vs prior midpoint of 23–25%); **EA & other FY27 ~+11%** (was mid-single-digit; 2H ~flat, Q3 MSD, on tough FY26 compares); op income $616–636M (~21% margin), **op-margin expansion now ~250bp** (was 100–150bp); EPS $6.39–6.58; FCF conversion at the upper end of 80–100%; "rule of 44" at the high end. - **AI evidence, as the company gave it:** "We are seeing AI workloads land on MongoDB across all of them" — labs, banks, public sector, startups (Desai). Enterprise pull-through is in **customer-facing, high-scale agents** (bank wealth-management chatbots over vectorised knowledge bases; fraud/identity), explicitly **not** the "co-pilot for a couple of hundred employees" (Desai to Keirstead). **The Postgres head-to-head:** one frontier lab "uses us for inference and chat workloads after moving away from PostgreSQL due to performance lags and outages… migrated their chat memory system onto Atlas in just four weeks and now run at 10x faster reads than PostgreSQL"; it started with one inference workload in Nov/Dec 2025 and added "another couple of workloads" in Jun/Jul 2026. Management's own caveats: "these relationships are still early, and engagement varies lab by lab"; AI benefit is "small" (Berry); labs' other use is storing experimental results, evals and training artifacts. Anthropic's CCO Paul Smith was quoted endorsing Voyage embeddings and the new managed MCP server. **Renewal pressure from over-consumption is "onesies and twosies, maximum single digits"** (Desai).
- **Organisation:** Ben Cefalo (CPO core), Pablo Stern (CPO AI/emerging); **John McMahon named as "our new CRO"** — note the Q1 FY27 call (2026-05-28) had introduced **Ryan Mac Ban** as CRO; a second CRO change inside one quarter is not explained on the call (watch item). ## Debate / thesis
The central question is **whether new AI applications default to Postgres (Supabase/Neon/pgvector) or to Atlas, and when the AI tailwind shows up in Atlas growth**. Two quarters of "early tailwinds" and one named lab migration have not yet moved Atlas off ~29%.

- **Bull:** (1) **Cleanest usage meter in the AI stack** — "agents repeatedly read/write session state, tool outputs, permissions… Atlas monetizes the hot working set" (Citrini Semis, "All Along the AI Watchtower," 2026-07-20). (2) **Operational data is the token path** — "the token path is data" (Jefferies · Thill, 2026-06-01); RAG "driving storage and database revenue" (MS · Nowak, 2026-08-12); agents run on CPUs and vector databases (UBS · Arcuri, Micron call, 2026-05-15). (3) **The company's own Postgres evidence is now specific**: frontier lab chat memory migrated in four weeks, 10x faster reads (Q2 FY27 call, 2026-09-01); Emergent Labs chose Atlas over PostgreSQL (Q4 FY26, 2026-03-02); Zepto, an automaker, TealBook (pgvector) migrated off Postgres (Q1/Q2 FY26, Q3 FY25 calls); LangGraph checkpointer collapses "a dedicated Postgres instance per agent" into one Atlas cluster (Q1 FY27). (4) **Attach is measurable**: 48% of $100K+ Atlas customers on 2+ features, vector/text search the driver (Q2 FY27); Voyage customers doubling q/q twice. (5) **Two growth engines**: EA & other ARR at double digits for three quarters with search/vector search now self-managed (Q2 FY27). (6) Street framing: "Winner Circle still pending, matter of time" (MS · Singh/Weiss, OW $335→$380, 2026-05-29); Bernstein Outperform PT $449 (10.4x/8.5x/7.1x EV/Sales 2026–28, priced 2026-06-16); "buy-rated MongoDB… will the AI tailwinds come a year from now, or potentially sooner?" (BofA · Ikeda, 2026-06-08); databases ranked among software categories least at threat from AI (Jefferies · Beavington client survey, 2026-05-20); GS desk: trades at a "5–8x discount to DDOG/SNOW" (Callahan, 2026-05-29).
- **Bear:** (1) **"Almost always Postgres"** — UBS: "I'm not hearing similar AI pull-through anecdotes that would point to real growth acceleration. The operational database layer overall is not seeing as material a pull-through as elsewhere. And when I do hear of database capacity needs rising, it's almost always for Postgres-based databases" (UBS · Karl Keirstead, State of AI Software call, 2026-08-31, the night before the print). Hyperscaler checks named "Redis and Postgres" at the database layer = "MongoDB facing material competition for AI workloads" (UBS · Keirstead/Arcuri, 2026-07-22). Microsoft's Azure PostgreSQL grew +55%, third straight quarter of acceleration (MSFT 4QFY26 call, 2026-07-29, via theme §2.5). (2) **Citrini's own bear:** "new AI apps may default to Postgres ecosystems such as Supabase/Neon/pgvector instead of MongoDB; hyperscalers and labs may internalize agent memory" (Citrini, 2026-07-20). (3) **Atlas has not accelerated**: ~29% for five quarters while total revenue accelerated on EA; Q3 Atlas guided to ~26% and Q4 implies further decel (Zukin's question, Q2 FY27 call) — the Q4 FY26 print fell -22% on a conservative guide when MDB was "Most Crowded Name in Software" (JPM · Schilsky, 2026-05-26). (4) **Management's own caveats** — "small," "still early," "varies lab by lab" — and the lab anecdote is one lab, company-sourced. (5) **Crowding / multiples**: "richer multiples, more crowded positioning, and bogeys that have inched up" (UBS · Keirstead, 2026-08-31); MDB "monster move off the April lows." (6) **Execution risk**: second CRO in one quarter (Mac Ban → McMahon, Q1→Q2 FY27 calls).
- **Where the sell-side & buy-side stand (all marks pre-print, 2026-05-29 unless noted):** **MS OW $380** (Singh/Weiss, from $335) · **Barclays OW $387** (Lenschow, from $370; "Solid Atlas; no AI inflection yet") · **UBS Neutral $350** (Keirstead, from $275; "No major AI pull-through yet") · **GS Buy** (briefing 2026-05-29) · **Bernstein Outperform $449** (2026-06-16) · BofA buy-rated (Ikeda, 2026-06-08) · JPM Schilsky's preview pecking order **DDOG >>> MDB > SNOW** and "The Anthropic Quarter — perhaps Dario can save MongoDB this quarter" (2026-05-26). UBS's stance is the useful one: **patient, not negative** — "Atlas growth of 29, maybe 30% is impressive. And the stock at around 10 times forward revenues is not outrageous… I'd rather stay patient on this one" (Keirstead, 2026-08-31). Barclays had called the Postgres bear "dormant" in June (Rand, 2026-06-09) — UBS's August checks said it is not. Jefferies lists MDB among the remaining public infra-software assets after IBM/Confluent but "that would probably take $40 billion… highly unlikely" (Thill, 2026-06-01). ⚠️ **Sell-side reactions to the 2026-09-01 print are not yet in the corpus.**

## Risks
- **Postgres share of new AI apps** (Supabase/Neon/pgvector; MSFT Postgres +55%) — the operational layer's AI pull-through accruing to hyperscaler-hosted Postgres rather than Atlas (UBS 2026-08-31; Citrini 2026-07-20).
- **Atlas consumption volatility** — a consumption business with limited back-half visibility; Q4 holiday seasonality flagged every year; April-2025 macro softness disclosed (Q1 FY26 call).
- **EA lumpiness** — ASC 606 recognises multi-year licence up front; FY26 saw a $50M→$40M headwind narrative, then a >$100M TCV deal and bundling that shaved ~1pt off Atlas growth (Q4 FY26); management "will always guide EA prudently."
- **Frontier-lab concentration and internalisation** — labs "may internalize agent memory" (Citrini); engagement "varies lab by lab" (Q2 FY27).
- **Gross-margin mix** — Atlas GM below company average; PS-heavy app modernisation and Voyage dilute GM (Q3 FY25, Q1 FY26 calls).
- **Leadership churn** — CFO (2024-12 → 2025-06), CEO (2025-11), CRO/field-ops (2026-03), CRO again (2026-05 → 2026-09); comp-plan and territory changes deferred to FY28.
- **Crowded positioning / multiple** — ~10x forward revenue (UBS, 2026-08-31); the -22% reaction to the Q4 FY26 guide (JPM, 2026-05-26) shows the downside on a soft Atlas guide.
- **Hyperscaler first-party competition** (DocumentDB to the Linux Foundation, Cosmos DB, Aurora/RDS) — management says clones are less visible and partnerships are strong (Q4 FY25, Q2 FY26), but the hyperscalers "have been bundling their database offerings with every offering" (Q3 FY25).
- **Enterprise AI timing** — Desai himself: 2025 "was supposed to be that year" and delivered coding + vertical AI only (Q4 FY26); production customer-facing agents in regulated industries still gated by governance/auditability (Q3 FY26).
