## Snapshot
MongoDB is a document (JSON) operational database sold two ways: **Atlas** (cloud DBaaS, consumption-billed, ~74–75% of revenue) and **Enterprise Advanced / "EA & other"** (self-managed licence, term-revenue under ASC 606, now the "run anywhere" leg). Search, vector search and — since the Feb-2025 Voyage AI acquisition ($220M, $20M cash; Q4 FY25 call, 2025-03-05) — embedding/re-ranking models are built into the platform. FY25 revenue crossed $2B (+19%) (Q4 FY25 call, 2025-03-05); FY27 is guided to $2.99–3.03B (+21–23%) (Q2 FY27 call, 2026-09-01). Positioning in the AI stack: the operational layer ("OLTP is the high ground") — agent memory, session state and retrieval — which is also why it is the public name that wears the **Postgres-for-new-AI-apps** bear (theme §2.4/§2.5). Leadership turned over completely inside the window: CFO Michael Gordon left (announced 2024-12-09), Mike Berry joined ~late May 2025 (Q1 FY26 call, 2025-06-04); **CEO Dev Ittycheria → CJ Desai** (ex-ServiceNow) — Desai's first call was Q3 FY26 on 2025-12-01, "day 28 on the job", i.e. he started ~2025-11-03; Ittycheria moved to the board.

## Debate / thesis
The central question is **whether new AI applications default to Postgres (Supabase/Neon/pgvector) or to Atlas, and when the AI tailwind shows up in Atlas growth**. Two quarters of "early tailwinds" and one named lab migration have not yet moved Atlas off ~29%.

- **Bull:** (1) **Cleanest usage meter in the AI stack** — "agents repeatedly read/write session state, tool outputs, permissions… Atlas monetizes the hot working set" (Citrini Semis, "All Along the AI Watchtower," 2026-07-20). (2) **Operational data is the token path** — "the token path is data" (Jefferies · Thill, 2026-06-01); RAG "driving storage and database revenue" (MS · Nowak, 2026-08-12); agents run on CPUs and vector databases (UBS · Arcuri, Micron call, 2026-05-15). (3) **The company's own Postgres evidence is now specific**: frontier lab chat memory migrated in four weeks, 10x faster reads (Q2 FY27 call, 2026-09-01); Emergent Labs chose Atlas over PostgreSQL (Q4 FY26, 2026-03-02); Zepto, an automaker, TealBook (pgvector) migrated off Postgres (Q1/Q2 FY26, Q3 FY25 calls); LangGraph checkpointer collapses "a dedicated Postgres instance per agent" into one Atlas cluster (Q1 FY27). (4) **Attach is measurable**: 48% of $100K+ Atlas customers on 2+ features, vector/text search the driver (Q2 FY27); Voyage customers doubling q/q twice. (5) **Two growth engines**: EA & other ARR at double digits for three quarters with search/vector search now self-managed (Q2 FY27). (6) Street framing: "Winner Circle still pending, matter of time" (MS · Singh/Weiss, OW $335→$380, 2026-05-29); Bernstein Outperform PT $449 (10.4x/8.5x/7.1x EV/Sales 2026–28, priced 2026-06-16); "buy-rated MongoDB… will the AI tailwinds come a year from now, or potentially sooner?" (BofA · Ikeda, 2026-06-08); databases ranked among software categories least at threat from AI (Jefferies · Beavington client survey, 2026-05-20); GS desk: trades at a "5–8x discount to DDOG/SNOW" (Callahan, 2026-05-29).
- **Bear:** (1) **"Almost always Postgres"** — UBS: "I'm not hearing similar AI pull-through anecdotes that would point to real growth acceleration. The operational database layer overall is not seeing as material a pull-through as elsewhere. And when I do hear of database capacity needs rising, it's almost always for Postgres-based databases" (UBS · Karl Keirstead, State of AI Software call, 2026-08-31, the night before the print). Hyperscaler checks named "Redis and Postgres" at the database layer = "MongoDB facing material competition for AI workloads" (UBS · Keirstead/Arcuri, 2026-07-22). Microsoft's Azure PostgreSQL grew +55%, third straight quarter of acceleration (MSFT 4QFY26 call, 2026-07-29, via theme §2.5). (2) **Citrini's own bear:** "new AI apps may default to Postgres ecosystems such as Supabase/Neon/pgvector instead of MongoDB; hyperscalers and labs may internalize agent memory" (Citrini, 2026-07-20). (3) **Atlas has not accelerated**: ~29% for five quarters — 29.2% → 29.4% → **28.9%** (Barclays · Lenschow, 2026-09-02) — while total revenue accelerated on EA; the market called Q2 the "wrong type of beat" and sold the stock −10%+ after hours (Barclays, 2026-09-01); Barclays' Similarweb Atlas login traffic turned negative q/q (−9.6% total, −8.9% unique, May–Jul 2026; unique +10.4% y/y vs +31.8% the prior quarter) (2026-08-26); Q3 Atlas guided to ~26% and Q4 implies further decel (Zukin's question, Q2 FY27 call) — the Q4 FY26 print fell -22% on a conservative guide when MDB was "Most Crowded Name in Software" (JPM · Schilsky, 2026-05-26). (4) **Management's own caveats** — "small," "still early," "varies lab by lab" — and the lab anecdote is one lab, company-sourced. (5) **Crowding / multiples**: "richer multiples, more crowded positioning, and bogeys that have inched up" (UBS · Keirstead, 2026-08-31); MDB "monster move off the April lows." ## Risks
- **Postgres share of new AI apps** (Supabase/Neon/pgvector; MSFT Postgres +55%) — the operational layer's AI pull-through accruing to hyperscaler-hosted Postgres rather than Atlas (UBS 2026-08-31; Citrini 2026-07-20).
- **Atlas consumption volatility** — a consumption business with limited back-half visibility; Q4 holiday seasonality flagged every year; April-2025 macro softness disclosed (Q1 FY26 call).
- **EA lumpiness** — ASC 606 recognises multi-year licence up front; FY26 saw a $50M→$40M headwind narrative, then a >$100M TCV deal and bundling that shaved ~1pt off Atlas growth (Q4 FY26); management "will always guide EA prudently."
- **Frontier-lab concentration and internalisation** — labs "may internalize agent memory" (Citrini); engagement "varies lab by lab" (Q2 FY27).
- **Gross-margin mix** — Atlas GM below company average; PS-heavy app modernisation and Voyage dilute GM (Q3 FY25, Q1 FY26 calls).
- **Leadership churn** — CFO (2024-12 → 2025-06), CEO (2025-11), CRO/field-ops (2026-03), CRO again (2026-05 → 2026-09); comp-plan and territory changes deferred to FY28.
- **Crowded positioning / multiple** — ~10x forward revenue (UBS, 2026-08-31); the -22% reaction to the Q4 FY26 guide (JPM, 2026-05-26) shows the downside on a soft Atlas guide.
- **Hyperscaler first-party competition** (DocumentDB to the Linux Foundation, Cosmos DB, Aurora/RDS) — management says clones are less visible and partnerships are strong (Q4 FY25, Q2 FY26), but the hyperscalers "have been bundling their database offerings with every offering" (Q3 FY25).
- **Enterprise AI timing** — Desai himself: 2025 "was supposed to be that year" and delivered coding + vertical AI only (Q4 FY26); production customer-facing agents in regulated industries still gated by governance/auditability (Q3 FY26); Barclays' callback names the bottleneck as "security and governance concerns (observability, audit, data regulatory compliance)" (Barclays · Lenschow, 2026-09-02).
