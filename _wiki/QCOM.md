## Snapshot
Qualcomm is the leading merchant supplier of premium mobile SoCs and cellular modem/RF, plus the dominant 3G/4G/5G patent licensor. Two reporting engines: **QCT** (chips — Handsets, Automotive, IoT) and **QTL** (licensing). FY25 revenue $44.3B, +14% YoY (10-K, 2025-11-05). QCT $38.4B (Handsets $27.8B / Auto $4.0B / IoT $6.6B); QTL ~flat YoY but ~70%+ EBT margin and the bulk of pre-tax profit. The structural debate: Handsets (≈72% of QCT) is a mature, China-heavy, Apple-eroding base, so the equity story rests on **diversification** (auto Snapdragon Digital Chassis, IoT/edge-AI, Snapdragon X PCs) and a nascent **data-center CPU / AI-inference** push offsetting Apple's modem in-sourcing.

## Position in the value chain
QCOM is fabless: it designs Snapdragon SoCs, modem/RF and auto/IoT silicon (built at TSMC/Samsung foundries) and sells to smartphone, auto and PC OEMs, while QTL monetizes its cellular IP across the industry regardless of who makes the chip. Key tension: **Apple is in-sourcing its baseband modem** ("we expect that Apple will increasingly use its own modem products… which will have a significant negative impact on our QCT revenues" — 10-K FY25), pushing QCOM to diversify non-handset revenue (auto exiting FY26 at a >$6B run-rate; data-center shipments to a hyperscaler starting Dec-2026). On the DC side, QCOM is now positioned as an **ARM-based merchant data-center vendor**: it launched the **AI200/AI250 inference-optimized accelerator cards and rack solutions on 2026-10-27** (PCIe for scale-up; AI200 commercial 2026, AI250 in 2027) (TD Cowen, 2025-10-27), and is one of two named CPU-side partners (with Fujitsu) on NVIDIA's **NVLink Fusion**, which extends NVLink to third-party CPUs/accelerators (MS Scale-up primer). First DC design win surfaced: **HUMAIN targeting 200MW from 2026 using QCOM AI200/AI250 rack solutions** (JPM, 2025-11/Oct-25).

## Debate / thesis
- **Citrini — HBC turns the memory constraint into an inference architecture (2026-07-20):** Qualcomm places the accelerator directly beneath an LPDDR stack and connects it vertically with TSVs, avoiding the conventional accelerator→interposer→HBM path. Qualcomm claims **6× more bandwidth/W than HBM, 200× more capacity/W than SRAM, and 4–8× better decode performance/W and TCO**; the headline **~133 TB/s** is best treated as aggregate effective bandwidth in memory-bound operations, not sustained application bandwidth. Roadmap: **AI200 (2026, LPDDR) → AI250 (2027, HBC Gen1) → AI300 (2028, HBC Gen2 + C1000 server CPU)**. Citrini’s differentiated read is that higher HBM prices help memory vendors near term but also accelerate architectures that use fewer HBM bytes/token (Citrini Semis, “All Along the AI Watchtower,” 2026-07-20).
- **Arete — HBC = near-memory computing, "promising but unproven"; Buy PT $314 (highest on page) (Arete "Real-Time Views #68", 2026-07-21):** Arete (Janco Venter) frames **HBC as near-memory computing rather than a new DRAM architecture** — it stacks compute logic (HBC accelerators) on top of conventional **LPDDR5** via 3D integration, architecturally closer to Samsung's earlier **Processing-Near-Memory (PNM)** than Processing-in-Memory (PiM); "the concept itself is not new," QCOM's contribution is integrating it into a **complete AI rack (CPU/MPU/networking)**. Mechanism: HBM "widens the highway" (raw bandwidth) while **HBC "reduces traffic"** by processing near memory — addressing the KV-cache/decode "memory wall." QCOM's **AI250 = 768GB HBC / 133TB/s effective bandwidth (18× over AI200)**, but Arete cautions this is **effective, not raw physical, bandwidth** (no HBC modules-per-rack or methodology disclosed), so **direct HBM comparisons are inappropriate**; QCOM claims ~6× bandwidth/W vs HBM (internal measurements, no third-party benchmark). Contrast: **NVDA's LPX ≈ ~40PB/s SRAM/rack, likely much faster** — QCOM deliberately chose LPDDR for capacity/power efficiency over absolute bandwidth. Ecosystem gaps: LPDDR supplier, 3D-stacking/packaging partner all undisclosed (likely needs HBM-like TSV packaging → could add AP-capacity demand). Timeline: **AI250 (Gen1 HBC) samples mid-2027, AI300 (Gen2) late-'28** — by volume, NVDA/AMD/Intel will have shipped several more generations, so "compare against contemporaneous products." QCOM also announced **UALink support in AI300** (vs NVLink), the **Modular acquisition** + Hugging Face partnership (software), and named **Meta as a CPU customer + Microsoft intending to deploy HBC in Azure**. Net: "compelling roadmap but HBC remains unproven — execution should determine success" (HBM took ~10yrs to go mainstream). Arete rating **Buy, PT $314** — the highest on the page. (Arete Research "Real-Time Views #68", 2026-07-21)
