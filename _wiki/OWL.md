## Snapshot

Blue Owl is an alternative asset manager with $329.9bn of AUM across three platforms: Credit (49% of AUM), Real Assets (29%) and GP Strategic Capital (22%). About 85% of its FRE management fees come from permanent capital, and its earnings are almost entirely fee-related. The thesis is that a permanent-capital fee stream can compound through net lease and digital infrastructure, institutional and insurance mandates, and a recovery in private wealth. The 2026 debate is whether the February private-credit shock in its wealth-channel BDCs has run its course, whether Credit fees can grow again, and whether the data-center franchise is a durable fee engine or a concentrated financing and reputation risk. The stock was $10.60 on 2026-11-02 ([Bloomberg via `_data/estimates.json`](_data/estimates.json)), against the $24 and then $18 price objectives BofA's Craig Siegenthaler carried in February and June (sales relays below). [Company Q3 release/deck, 2026-10-29](../OWL/earnings/2026-10-29_blueowlearningsdeck93026.htm); [Q3 10-Q, filed 2026-10-30](../OWL/OWL_10-Q_2026-10-30_0001823945-26-000055.html); Capstone synthesis, 2026-11-02.

**Real Assets is carrying growth; Credit fees are shrinking.** Credit FRE management fees fell 2% to $395.8m as Part I fees in Credit declined to $140.7m from $149.2m, while Real Assets FRE management fees rose 30% to $119.1m and GP Strategic Capital rose 7% to $175.0m. About 12 capital-markets transactions lifted Real Assets transaction fees to $48.6m; CFO Alan Kirshenbaum said he "would not run rate" Q3 and expects Q4 to fall back toward Q1–Q2 levels. [Q3 deck, slide 29](../OWL/earnings/2026-10-29_blueowlearningsdeck93026.htm); [Kirshenbaum, Q3 call, 2026-10-29](../OWL/transcripts/OWL_Q3-2026-earnings_2026-10-29.md).

**Institutions replaced wealth as the marginal source of capital.** Q3 was a record institutional quarter, with $5.6bn of Credit equity (including a $1bn Voya insurance mandate) and an $8.2bn seventh net-lease drawdown vintage above its $7.5bn hard cap. Private wealth raised $11.4bn over the last twelve months, against more than $17bn in FY2025 ([Q4 2025 call, 2026-02-05](../OWL/transcripts/OWL_Q4-2025-earnings_2026-02-05.md)). The 10-Q attributes the decline to lower industry subscriptions to non-traded BDCs and direct-lending products "amid heightened market and media attention on direct lending", and says private-wealth inflows "have, however, recovered from May 2026 levels". Management describes a bifurcation: OCIC redemption requests fell for a second quarter, with over 90% of its 90,000 shareholders fully invested, while ORENT requests hit a nine-quarter low, ODIT reached $4.6bn in under a year and OWLCX is approaching $3bn. [Q3 10-Q](../OWL/OWL_10-Q_2026-10-30_0001823945-26-000055.html); [Kirshenbaum, Q3 call](../OWL/transcripts/OWL_Q3-2026-earnings_2026-10-29.md). LTM new commitments of $48.8bn compare with $56.6bn at Q1 and $50.5bn at Q2 ([Q1 deck](../OWL/earnings/2026-04-30_blueowlearningsdeck33126.htm); [Q2 call, 2026-07-30](../OWL/transcripts/OWL_Q2-2026-earnings_2026-07-30.md)).

**Outlook: management expects FY2026 to beat the consensus anchor it chose and fee growth to accelerate in 2027.** Kirshenbaum reiterated that Blue Owl can beat $1.02 of FRE and $0.89 of DE per share for 2026. These are the Visible Alpha consensus figures cited at the Q1 call, not a company guide. He expects management-fee growth to be higher in 2027 than in 2026, a modest Q4 margin spike, margin expansion above 58.5% as the "new base case", the end of business-combination stock grants from Q4, and total 2027 stock compensation below 2026. Q3 DE of $0.24 covered the $0.23 dividend, and he expects coverage to continue through 2027. Year-to-date FRE is $0.77 and DE $0.65 per share, against $0.69 of dividends declared (Capstone arithmetic from the [Q1](../OWL/earnings/2026-04-30_blueowlearningsdeck33126.htm), [Q2](../OWL/earnings/2026-07-30_blueowlearningsdeck63026.htm) and Q3 decks). **Bull case — a permanent-capital fee stream that is diversifying away from its weakest channel.** Credit, net lease, digital infrastructure and GP stakes are mostly locked-up capital. The $36.3bn of AUM not yet paying fees is about 16% embedded growth on 2025 management fees (management). Institutions, insurers and real assets offset weaker wealth flows, and higher rates help floating-rate credit and Part I fees. Management guides to faster fee growth and margin expansion in 2027, and DE now covers the dividend. BofA's Siegenthaler argued in February that misinformation had created an entry point, that all profits are high-quality FRE, and that short interest near 20% of float created squeeze risk (BofA Short Analytics). [Lipschultz / Kirshenbaum, Q3 call, 2026-10-29](../OWL/transcripts/OWL_Q3-2026-earnings_2026-10-29.md); [BofA sales relaying Siegenthaler, 2026-02-24](../OWL/commentary/2026-02-24_late-edition-NVDA-Tomorrow-REAL-ASSET-Demand-Concerning-Moves-CPU-Renaissance-BDC-Cross-Asset-call-B_6f66b85d.md); Capstone interpretation, 2026-11-02.

**Bear case — the growth engine moved to the riskiest-perceived corners.** Direct lending fees are shrinking, the wealth franchise that drove 2024–25 is smaller, and the 2026 beat leans on transaction fees that management itself will not run-rate. Real Assets growth is concentrated in a handful of very large hyperscale projects, where financing headlines (Lancaster, Jupiter) hit the stock even when the funds, not OWL, carry the asset risk. Panzer framed February's episode as "a confidence game" with a potentially mismatched asset/liability schedule. LTM commitments have slid from $56.6bn to $48.8bn, and management told analysts in February it was "behind our Investor Day goals". [Jefferies / Panzer, sales, 2026-02-23](../OWL/commentary/2026-02-23_JEF-AM-FIG-PICKS-PANZ-2-23-26-----OWL-Part-3-FCNCA-Bank-M-A-AMG-Follow-Up-Into-This-Week-Into-JPM-In_1896e4dc.md); [Kirshenbaum, Q4 2025 call, 2026-02-05](../OWL/transcripts/OWL_Q4-2025-earnings_2026-02-05.md); Capstone interpretation, 2026-11-02.

**What would change the view:** two more quarters of falling OCIC/OTIC requests with rising gross wealth sales, Credit management fees growing again and a BODI IV first close on track for $10bn. On the downside: a renewed tender spike, non-accrual increases in the affiliated BDCs, or a data-center project where Blue Owl funds must carry construction funding they could not syndicate. Compare wealth-channel liquidity with BCRED at [BX](BX.md) and credit/insurance origination with [APO](APO.md) and [KKR](KKR.md), on matched FRE definitions only.

Track tender and redemption requests in continuously offered products (the 10-Q notes that a growing wealth share puts more FPAUM under quarterly liquidity and that requests "may be influenced by investor sentiment, market conditions and media attention"); credit losses, PIK and software exposure in the affiliated BDCs; concentration in a few hyperscale tenants and very large projects; construction, permitting, power and syndication risk in development funds; reputational and litigation risk tied to affiliated-vehicle transactions and related parties (Kuvare); stock-based compensation and the TRA liability ($1.66bn at 9/30); dividend coverage; and holding-company leverage ($3.69bn of debt).
