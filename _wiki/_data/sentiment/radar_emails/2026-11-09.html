Themes and quality-source notes

Capitulation, or not yet? Desks agree on the flush, disagree on the bottom. GS (Armbrust, 9 Nov) reports the largest weekly drop in US fundamental L/S gross leverage since Covid and net leverage at the 0th 5-year percentile; JPM (See, 8 Nov) calls this a positioning unwind near capitulation (biggest multi-strat de-gross since Covid, momentum L/S -40%); Jefferies (Favuzza) counts six weeks and >$20B of HF and LO net selling of tech. Against that, JPM Positioning Intelligence says the TPM buy signal has not triggered, GS's risk-appetite index is not yet at buy levels, and SIG (9 Nov) notes momentum crowding still in the 88th percentile with $60-135bn of possible vol-control selling. UBS's Arcuri (9 Nov) adds that Thursday's drop was macro, systematic and retail AI beta, not HF single-stock unwinds. Weighting: we give the flow data (GS prime, JPM PI) more weight than the narrative, since they are measured; they say 'cleaner, not clean'.

Midterms and Texas permits: 2027 protected, 2028 is the debate. UBS's Keirstead (8 Nov) ran 15+ data-center checks at DCD Connect: political messaging targets new permits (2028+), not projects mid-build, and the squeeze falls on speculative long-tail projects. JPM (See) says 41% of planned US data-center developments sit in states whose new governor favors a pause, but expects delays or relocation, not denial. BofA (Haas Liu, 9 Nov) models 40 GW of AI compute added in 2027 and 53 GW in 2028 (base), with 45 GW or a flat 40 GW as downside cases (BofA's own GW basis, not facility GW). UBS Taiwan strategy (Chen) sees ~30% of planned 2028 US additions potentially affected; Jefferies' digital-infra analyst cut 2026-30 to 112.8 GW from 122.4 GW. UBS's Dwyer frames the live semis debate as whether the cycle peak is pulled into 2027 or extended.

NVDA: the export-control channel matters more than the scandal. Redburn (9 Nov) separates a personal affair from a governance failure or an intelligence compromise; 22V (Hirson) argues the bigger investor risk is policy: tighter China export controls (MATCH Act, AI OVERWATCH, remote-access rules) and lost Nvidia influence in Washington ahead of the Trump-Xi meeting on 18-19 Nov. Vital Knowledge recalls Culper's claim that Chinese users could be up to ~20% of compute revenue. Stratechery (Thompson, forwarded by Grozdea) notes Nvidia's role as a financing source for AI infrastructure is also at stake. These are independent and research voices, not desk flows.

FundaAI: medium-term demand gap and slower Asian deliveries. FundaAI (independent author, 9 Nov weekly) separates horizons: long-term AGI optimism is higher than before, but through 2027 lab revenue leans on VC-funded startups and big tech already optimizing token use, enterprise surveys point to slower adoption, and the Asian supply chain reports slower deliveries and procurement schedules (not yet order cuts). It sees ~0.5-1 GW of on-orbit compute by end-2028 (SPCX). No DAMNANG or SemiAnalysis mail arrived in this window.
