Themes and quality-source notes

Long liquidation, partially done. Three PB books agree the AI drawdown is long selling, not short building: MS (15 Oct) long sales ~2:1 vs short adds with memory nets at ~4.7%; UBS (16 Oct) concentrated in semis and hyperscalers and now in European semis; SIG relaying GS (16 Oct) AI allocation 26.1% but still 83rd percentile. They disagree on how much is left: UBS says gross remains elevated with scope for more de-grossing, JPM sales trading (15 Oct) saw only 4-5/10 urgency, and Jefferies' Japan quant (15 Oct) warns against extrapolating with AI at 17.2x vs 25.6x in July. Weighting in words: PB data first, then desk anecdote.

Credit desks diagnose, equity research defends. On CoreWeave the split is by function: credit voices (Jefferies HY Haber 15 Oct; GS credit strategy and MS credit 16 Oct) describe a closing unsecured/convert channel and equity as the residual funder, while equity research relayed by desks (BofA Liani, Jefferies Thill, JPM Chatterjee) argues contract economics (~$40M/MW short-duration deals, prepayments, take-or-pay) outweigh funding costs, and Bernstein's Rezai stays Underperform. Jensen Huang's defence is a supplier and shareholder claim (Rothschild notes the ~10% stake). The contract-price $/MW figures quoted are rental contract bases, not the wiki's revenue-per-GW canon.

TSMC: capex raises versus record outflows. Research relayed through desks raised the capacity path (MS Chan 2027-28 capex $83bn/$92bn; BofA Haas Liu $80-85bn 2027; JPM Hariharan $90bn/$109bn vs buy-side $86bn/$99.5bn) while cutting or holding margins, but Taiwan saw record foreign selling (Jefferies, 16 Oct) and Chris Wood (Jefferies, 16 Oct) says the easy money in semis is made. Capex figures are each broker's own TSMC capex basis and are not reconciled here.

SemiAnalysis: ASML into hybrid bonding. SemiAnalysis (15 Oct) argues ASML is building a die-to-wafer hybrid-bonding tool on a track architecture, an independent-author view built from patents and a news report, not a company announcement; if right it is a new competitive vector for the hybrid-bonding incumbents.
