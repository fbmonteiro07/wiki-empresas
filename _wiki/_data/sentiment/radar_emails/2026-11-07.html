Desk read

Day three: the AI tape bounced (NDX +1.35%, momentum +3.4%) as desks sized the data-center hit at a few percent and called the hedge-fund unwind mostly done, but retail and systematic supply keep a second low on the table.

The narrative moved from 'how bad' to 'how much is really at risk': Bernstein (Rezaei, 6 Nov) puts only 2-4% of the credible US pipeline at real risk and MS (McVeigh via Wigg/Risom, 6 Nov) 4.9 GW of IT load in 2028 (~4% of cumulative capacity), against SIG's (Hosseini, 6 Nov) 60 GW 'delay' bucket and the WSJ-cited 25-45% claims. Positioning is cleaner: MS PB (via Wigg, 6 Nov) has L/S net leverage at 42%, bottom decile, cut entirely by long selling, and QDS (Metli) thinks the HF unwind is nearly complete. But retail and systematics are the swing: QDS still sees $30-40bn of systematic supply and expects a bounce then a new low, and UBS's Cheong (via Dwyer, 6 Nov) eyes a Monday-morning washout. The first upside optionality showed up (UBS Ruple, 6 Nov: NVDA call spreads, MSFT upside, a SOXX put spread sold), while LOs and HFs both kept selling semis and industrials into the close (BofA Villani/Fenske, 6 Nov).

Themes and quality-source notes

Sizing the data-center hit: the numbers differ by base. Bernstein (Rezaei, 6 Nov) sees the credible at-risk pipeline rising from 78 to 105 GW, of which ~17 GW could face tighter rules, so 2-4% of the credible pipeline (pipeline GW) is realistically lost. MS (McVeigh via Wigg and Risom, 6 Nov) puts 4.9 GW (IT load) at risk in 2028, 4% of cumulative capacity, and 12.4 GW through 2030, with no 2027 risk. SIG (Hosseini, 6 Nov) has 60 GW of 148 GW of training-focused AI DC projects at risk of delay and 12 GW canceled in a worst case. Tech Fund (7 Nov) relays a GS figure of ~45% of planned capacity facing delays or oversight, and Brad Gerstner's 25-35% of next year's build. These bases (credible pipeline GW, IT-load GW, project GW, share of plans) are not comparable; the desk consensus is that the sell-off priced more than the sell-side sizings.

Is the unwind done? Hedge funds yes; retail and systematics are the swing. MS PB (via Wigg, 6 Nov) has L/S net leverage at 42% after a top-5 one-day drop since 2005, driven entirely by long selling; QDS (Metli) thinks the HF unwind is nearly complete but expects a bounce then a new low, with $30-40bn of systematic supply left. UBS (Dwyer, 6 Nov) puts risk-control selling at $200-270bn over two days, and Cheong sees a possible Monday washout. UBS (Ruple, 6 Nov) sees the first call buying (NVDA, MSFT) and a SOXX put spread sold. BofA (Villani/Fenske, 6 Nov) saw the 5 Nov close flip to net buying with record retail ETF purchases, but LOs and HFs both kept selling semis and industrials. MS's Wilson (via Amerian/Risom, 6 Nov) sounded outright bullish on an entry point but wants a marginal new low first; one UBS client called it a regime change.

Inside the AI trade: networking/optical, memory and app software are treated differently. Desks are separating the AI complex. Fundamentals in networking and optics are called intact: BofA's desk (Villani, 6 Nov) cites ANET, ALAB and COHR (2027 fully booked, a new NPO LTA into 2028), and Jefferies' MRVL/LITE NDR (Curtis, 6 Nov) pulls scale-in into 2028-29. Memory is cheap but still being sold (Jefferies, UBS). In software, infrastructure (NET, FROG) is gaining while applications (TEAM, NOW) are the pain trade (BofA Fenske, 6 Nov).

Contrarian voices: peak capex and a Huang headline. Jefferies' Christopher Wood (GREED & fear, 6 Nov) argues that markets are signalling an unwillingness to fund AI and revives his call that AI capex could peak this year, pointing to AI credit spreads/CDS and to neocloud rentals that are not locked beyond one year. Separately, Tim Culpan (Culpium, 7 Nov, independent author) relays NYT/FT reports on alleged Chinese intelligence targeting of Jensen Huang; Nvidia calls the interactions normal customer meetings. It is a headline risk for NVDA's standing in Washington rather than a fundamental claim.
