Desk read

Friday's tech de-gross turned systematic: desks keep defending AMZN and the hyperscalers on fundamentals, but crowded semis longs (INTC, AMD) and memory (Kioxia, SNDK) are where conviction is fading.

Thursday's single-name earnings damage became a mechanical sell-off on Friday. UBS (Ruple/Cheon, 30 Oct) estimates $165-220bn of risk-control selling, the first meaningful one since Nov 2025, with $110-150bn still due Monday. Jefferies (Favuzza, 30 Oct) calls it its 3rd-largest tech sell week in 5 years and its 5th straight week as a net seller, with SOXX below its 200-day for the first time since May 2025. BofA (Villani, 30 Oct) sees hedge funds net sellers for 11 straight days and long-only funds on track for their biggest monthly selling of 2026. The fundamental read is unchanged: MS (Wigg, Risom), BofA (Fenske) and Bernstein (Patafio), all 30 Oct, call AMZN an expectations miss and GOOGL the only structural issue, while Jefferies (Wong, 30 Oct) now hears profit-taking and new shorts in AMD and research TP cuts pile onto INTC.

Themes and quality-source notes

The de-gross turned systematic. The selling is no longer only discretionary. UBS (Ruple, relaying macro specialist Rebecca Cheon, 30 Oct) estimates $70-120bn of systematic selling on Friday (risk-control, leveraged ETFs, CTAs) and up to $110-150bn more from risk-control funds on Monday, partly offset by ~$46bn of month-end pension buying. Its retail clients bought tech ETFs while selling single tech stocks, partly for tax-loss harvesting. Jefferies (Favuzza, 30 Oct) sees 'virtually zero upticks' in QQQ and its 3rd-largest tech sell week in 5 years. BofA (Villani, 30 Oct) says hedge funds have net sold for 11 days and semis had their 2nd-largest sell day of the month. Vanda (Nordvig, 30 Oct, forwarded by Rodrigo Nascimento) notes credit barely moved (CDX IG +1bp), so it is not yet a Fed-panic risk-off. These are flow statistics without tickers, so they carry no observation ids.

Defensive capex vs better financing: the macro and TMT desks disagree. 22V (DeBusschere, 30 Oct) says the macro community's narrative has swung back to hyperscalers spending defensively to protect core profits, and that this does not point to a strong 4Q for AI names. Vanda (Nordvig, 30 Oct) argues capital markets are now putting a bound on capex expectations. The TMT desks push the other way: MS (Wigg, 30 Oct) says this week's META, SPCX, AVGO and MSFT raises make financing look more constructive than the recent neocloud worries, and UBS Research (Abrams, 30 Oct) says the outlook is still better for suppliers but sentiment may cap the trade. The macro/TMT gap is the main divergence today.

MediaTek's TPU ramp vs the Broadcom read. Jefferies sales (O'Mara, 30 Oct) reads MediaTek's ~$16bn 2027 ASIC target (~20% of the AI ASIC market, per the company) and its second customer as negative for AVGO, which still says it does not see MediaTek in the supply chain with a chip. BofA Research (Liu, 30 Oct) confirms the 4Q TPUv8t ramp tracking $2.5bn. Substrates are the shared constraint: INTC (via Jefferies Wong) and MediaTek both name them, and Ibiden's new capacity does not contribute until FY30. These are management claims and one desk's interpretation, not a change in AVGO's own guidance.

Memory: Kioxia's caution vs the Hynix bull case. Kioxia's weak Dec-Q guide and CFO caution on next-FY NAND pricing (Jefferies Wong, 30 Oct) hit SNDK and MU in sympathy (GS Kamshad, 30 Oct). Research stays constructive elsewhere: BofA Research (Woo, 31 Oct) prefers SK Hynix's strategy and lifts global DRAM forecasts 5%, and Bernstein relays mostly positive SNDK feedback. No DAMNANG or SemiAnalysis piece arrived to provide an independent check.
