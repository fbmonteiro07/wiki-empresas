Themes and quality-source notes

Hyperscaler capex: 'no incremental help' vs 'capex raised'. Jefferies (Beavington, 29 Oct) says MSFT's CY26 capex trim to $170bn (third-party delays) and GOOGL's unchanged $195-205bn add nothing to near-term semis capex expectations, because the rate of increase matters. BofA research (Arya, relayed by Franco, 29 Oct) instead lifts CY26/27 AI capex growth to 99%/54% (from 98%/37%) and cites record GPU-hour prices implying under one-year paybacks. The desks frame it as a timing issue (third-party capacity shifting into 2H), not reduced appetite (GS Arruda, Bernstein Seidman).

Optics consolidation: is MRVL-LITE real, and who benefits?. JPM (Meyers) calls the Bloomberg report credible and frames it as MRVL buying scarce laser capacity, sold out into 2029. Bernstein (Seidman) hears it as widely viewed as unlikely. Both sides of the deal are unhappy: MRVL holders say expensive, LITE holders say it caps upside. COHR is flagged as the last independent laser player. Gaetano Research (independent Substack, 29 Oct, 'Marvell's Optical Ambition') arrived but was not read in this run.

AI-theme rotation vs semis-bounce case. JPM's market-intelligence desk (Tyler, 29 Oct) sees the AI theme being sold with a defensive bias, while discretionary and financials bid as the ex-AI 'broadening' trade, and Jefferies quant (Peramunetilleke, via Beavington) markets 'Diversifying away from A.I.' (AI earnings growth slowing to ~30% in 2027). JPM APAC (See, 29 Oct) argues HF de-grossing was large enough that semis can keep bouncing on 2027 capex upside and ROI clarity. The disagreement is open, and GS (Arruda) expects dips to get bought while credit stabilises.

ORCL as the credit linchpin. GS (Arruda) calls ORCL's funding cap and FCF path more consequential for the levered AI-infrastructure complex than either hyperscaler print, but says spreads still need to confirm. MS (Wigg) notes semis rose on ORCL's lower FY29 capex because BYOH shifts capex to others rather than removing it.
