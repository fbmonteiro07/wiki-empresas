Desk read

CRWV's print and OpenAI's revenue leak lifted the AI tape on 11 Nov, but desks call it a squeeze from record-low positioning; NVDA is the one name nobody is buying back.

The hot 10 Nov CPI (core +0.30% m/m) pushed yields up and SPX ex-AI outperformed (JPM's Tyler, 10 Nov), but positioning, not macro, is now driving the tape: GS's Arruda (11 Nov) says nets are historically low and the vol premium keeps decaying, MS PB (10 Nov) saw hedge funds flip back to large buyers, split evenly between long adds and short covers, and JPM's Meyers (10 Nov) warns the rebound was hedge removal and retail, with AI momentum faded nine sessions running. Software gets no rotation back (Barclays, 11 Nov), and desks are trading around the Anthropic IPO as the next tech catalyst.

Themes and quality-source notes

Positioning is the tailwind, rates the headwind. GS (Arruda, 11 Nov), MS PB (10 Nov) and SIG (10 Nov) agree that nets and tech positioning are at multi-year lows (SIG: mega-cap growth and tech at the 7th percentile; gross leverage saw its largest weekly cut since COVID). After the hot CPI, JPM's Tyler (11 Nov) is tactically bullish and sees the washout at or near completion. JPM's Meyers (10 Nov) disagrees on quality: the bounce was hedge removal and retail, AI momentum has been faded nine sessions running, and capitulation has not been signalled. Desks expect choppy moves, with dealers short gamma on rallies.

CRWV eases neocloud funding fears. CRWV's commentary went straight at the funding worry: <$5bn of parent capital through 2027, a 7-7.5% DDTL, about $9bn of prepayments, and GPU repricing (+30-50% q/q on older SKUs, >$40m/MW short-term). UBS, Jefferies and Rothschild read it as relief for the neocloud complex and its credit. Jefferies' Favuzza cautions that AI-levered names may not lead a skittish tape.

Where NVDA money goes: AMD/AVGO vs ASICs. Jefferies' US desk says AMD and AVGO; Jefferies' Asia desk points to ASIC designers (Alchip, GUC, MediaTek) on a Commercial Times report of slower NVDA orders; Rothschild thinks accelerator buyers do not care about CEO conduct, but the Claude-on-MI355X anecdote from the Anthropic roadshow weakens the CUDA-moat argument. These are diverging reads of one rotation, with no order evidence yet.

DAMNANG: next-generation DRAM economics. DAMNANG (an independent author, not a broker; 10 Nov, also forwarded by Rafael Verissimo) interviews a memory engineer at an equipment company. A structure's name (4F2, 3D DRAM) says little about the real gap: cell dimensions, yield, cost and scale decide it. 3D DRAM needs an economical way to build the stack, the HBM premium justifies process costs commodity DRAM cannot absorb, and in a shortage, equipment that cuts steps is worth more because it adds output. It takes no stance on any company; it is context for the memory rebound debate.
