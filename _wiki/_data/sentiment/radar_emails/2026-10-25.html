Desk read

Weekend desk mail calls October a sector rotation and HF de-gross, not capitulation: UBS PB sees semis de-risking bottoming and memory bought, but nobody wants to add before the mega-cap prints, the Fed and the midterms.

UBS's Ruple (24 Oct) says AI is the centre of the unwind: shorts rose mainly in AI segments, PB saw long-selling in semis/AI and hyperscalers de-risked by -1.3 std, but semis de-risking looks to be bottoming and memory/hardware saw +0.5 std net buying from longs and covers, while retail bought the dip. Bernstein's Seidman (25 Oct) and JPM's Silver (25 Oct) both describe risk appetite rotating from memory/storage/semicap into consumer internet and software, with good prints (WDC, SMCI, Nokia, BESI) faded and software used as a hiding place rather than a conviction long. Into the week, AMZN is the consensus long and MSFT the consensus worry (Bernstein); CNN Fear & Greed was 21.0 (extreme fear) at the 23 Oct close.

Themes and quality-source notes

Rotation, not capitulation; disagreement on whether the de-gross is done. JPM's Schlegel (via See, 25 Oct) and Vanda's Eric Liu (CIO Risk Report, 24 Oct, forwarded by Rodrigo Nascimento) both say aggregate equity positioning barely moved while AI/semis positioning stays elevated (94th percentile per JPM), which makes this a sector rotation. Kevin Muir (MacroTourist, 24 Oct, forwarded by David Leite) notes the GS AI Winners-vs-Losers basket is down ~55% since 22 Jun while the S&P is up. Views diverge on what comes next. 22V's Jordi Visser (25 Oct) calls it one of the largest HF deleveraging events of his career and thinks it is done or in the final innings. Jefferies/Vanda (24 Oct) say the washout is not complete, and JPM's Nishihara does not expect the AI/semis rebound until year-end.

Good prints faded: the earnings bar and where risk is hiding. Bernstein (Seidman, 25 Oct) and JPM (Silver, 25 Oct) both say beats are being sold in memory/storage/semicap and optical (WDC, SMCI, BESI, Nokia), while software and consumer internet attract flows used as a hiding place rather than out of conviction. UBS (Ruple, 24 Oct) adds that AAPL and HCM software screen overbought on defensive flows. Muir (MacroTourist) argues the ~30% y/y earnings growth priced in is nearly impossible to clear.

Financing cost versus compute pricing (Rasgon / Patel panel). At the CASPA panel moderated by Chipstrat's Austin Lyons (an independent author; transcript 24 Oct), Bernstein's Stacy Rasgon put financing near the top of buildout constraints (hyperscalers burning cash, CoreWeave unsecured paper near 15%) but sees the next 1–2 years as okay, citing SpaceX renting short-term capacity at ~$50bn/GW. Vanda (Flows & Facts, 24 Oct) frames the opposite risk: a 'token supply glut' as prices collapse and AI spend stays concentrated among VC-backed users. The Information (24 Oct) reports Rippling moving coding work from Anthropic (57%→18%) to OpenAI (16%→52%) on price, a datapoint on lab pricing pressure. These are themes only, with no ticker call.
