Desk read

A low-volume relief rally left NVDA behind: desks see clean positioning but little conviction, money rotating to hyperscalers, AMD and networking, and the scandal turning into a governance and export-control story.

The 9 Nov bounce (SPX +2.3%, SOX +6.9%) was broad but thin: JPM's desk (9 Nov) reports year-to-date low high-touch volumes with a lot of the demand short covering, UBS's desk saw semis skewed for sale into strength, and JPM's Euro specialist (10 Nov) says investors want a steadier tape before re-risking, even as UBS, Vanda and JPM flow data show systematic and hedge-fund positioning washed out. NVDA decoupled, underperforming SOX by 11% (GS, 10 Nov) as the story moved from the affair to governance and export controls (Stratechery, The Information via GS, 10 Nov) and BofA took NVDA, MU and LRCX off its US 1 list; desks still relay that most investors expect it to blow over. The rotation is toward hyperscalers (UBS prime book +2.3 std long buying; GS and JPM, 10 Nov), AMD as the NVDA alternative, and networking/optical (UBS desk better to buy ANET and COHR). Infineon went from funding short to constructive after its FY27 guide (JPM, 10 Nov); CRWV reports tonight with sentiment described as overly bearish (UBS).

Themes and quality-source notes

Positioning is clean, conviction is not. Desks agree on the flush and disagree on the bounce. UBS (Cheong, 9-10 Nov) has risk-control exposure at 55% and CTAs net short (-5%), calling the downside feedback loop removed; Vanda (9 Nov, via Rodrigo Nascimento) has aggregate positioning at -0.93 SD, the lowest since May 2025, but notes most similar unwinds retested the low first. Against that, JPM's Creager (9 Nov) says high-touch volumes hit year-to-date lows on the +2.3% day, JPM's Heavey says a lot of the demand was short covering, UBS's desk saw semis skewed for sale into strength with long-only engagement, and JPM's Silver (10 Nov) describes investors 'peeking out of the bunkers'. MS's QDS still expects $30-40B of systematic selling this week. Wells Fargo's strategist (Kwon, 10 Nov) moved AI infrastructure to neutral, saying a deceleration is priced but an EPS downturn is not.

The 2028 air pocket is the fundamental disagreement. MS is arguing with itself in public: Wilson and Moore say the correction is near its end and '2028' is the usual months-13-to-24 worry, while Shawn Kim says memory and the supply chain add capacity into late 2027-2028 just as MS's own model now has US data-center additions flat in 2028 (5 GW pushed to 2029). UBS's Arcuri (9 Nov) thinks SOX already implies about a 35% cut to CY28 EPS; MS's McVeigh sees only ~12 GW of Texas/Ohio pipeline at risk through 2030. GW figures here are each broker's own basis and are not comparable across notes.

NVDA: governance replaces 'affair' as the frame. Stratechery (Thompson, 10 Nov, independent author, forwarded by Daniel Grozdea) ranks Monday's reports by how they play in Washington: the general counsel's contact with Commerce during the Megaspeed probe and an under-count of chips sold matter more than the relationship itself. Sell-side and desks are mostly on the other side: BofA's Arya 'not yet an earnings event', MS's Moore Top Pick, UBS and Rothschild relaying that investors expect it to blow over. The gap between the independent read and the desk consensus is the thing to watch into 17-18 Nov.

Software: infrastructure and security bought, apps ignored. Barclays' Rand (10 Nov) sees no broad rotation back to software despite the semis volatility: long-onlys buy infrastructure, security and vertical SaaS (SNOW, NET, PANW, CRWD; hedge funds prefer OKTA), with no buying in apps after NOW. UBS's prime book did show hedge-fund buying of US software on 9 Nov (+2.1 std). Bernstein's Seidman is more cautious on cyber sentiment because of funding-flow events.
