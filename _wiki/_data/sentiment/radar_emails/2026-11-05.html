Desk read

The Democratic sweep turned the AI unwind into a rotation: desks buy hyperscalers that own capacity and sell semis, memory, neoclouds and AI power, while AMD/ANET beats fade and the MRVL-LITE spread widens.

Mail from 3-5 Nov is dominated by the midterms: Democrats took both chambers and Texas, and GS Econ (4 Nov) puts states covering ~45% of planned US data-center capacity at risk of short-term moratoria. Desks describe one rotation: GS's TMT trading desk (5 Nov) saw heavy buying of hyperscalers against supply in names that build or power data centers, UBS PB (5 Nov) had hedge funds buying hyperscalers and software and selling hardware and memory, and BofA Short Analytics (4 Nov) measured Electric Grid Enablers -7.7σ and AI Infrastructure -5.0σ. Positioning was already light: GS (3 Nov) has fundamental and systematic L/S both down more than 3% in October, BofA (4 Nov) says clients sold US equities for a 3rd week, and JPM Market Intel (4-5 Nov) thinks HF de-risking is likely behind us and stays Tactically Bullish. Desks disagree on whether this pulls the semis peak into 2027 (UBS) or is just a funding trade, and earnings did not help: AMD gave back a +9% after-hours move (GS, 4 Nov) and ANET's +38-40% FY27 guide is one of the few beats that held.

Themes and quality-source notes

Compute owners vs compute builders (desks agree on the trade, disagree on why). Every desk we read after the Democratic sweep describes the same rotation: hyperscalers bought, and semis, memory, neoclouds and AI power sold as the funding source (GS trading 5 Nov, UBS PB 5 Nov, Jefferies 5 Nov, BofA Egan 4 Nov). The disagreement is about mechanism. GS's desk relay and UBS's Dwyer argue that scarce energised capacity becomes more valuable and capex falls, so FCF improves. Barclays' Rand says it is about capacity owned today, not a capex cut. UBS frames the semis debate as whether the 2028/29 peak is pulled into 2027 or the cycle is stretched. JPM Market Intel stays Tactically Bullish and says HF de-risking is likely behind us; UBS PB found hedge-fund flows muted in the selloff, so the supply came from elsewhere.

MRVL-LITE: the arb market and the independents read it differently. UBS special sits (3-4 Nov) shows a 12.7% spread and 58% breakeven probability, far wider than China-approval precedents, and the offer is now at a discount for LITE holders. Bernstein's analyst, relayed 3 Nov, still thinks ~76% close odds are too high. DAMNANG (independent author, 3 Nov, preview only) argues the opposite reading, that NVIDIA arranged the deal and the 'optics war' is starting. LITE reports 5 Nov after the close, and BofA's LightCounting expert call (5 Nov) covers the deal.

SemiAnalysis on SpaceX 13GW vs the election tape. SemiAnalysis (3 Nov) says SpaceX lifted its 2027 datacenter target to ~13GW with >9GW identified and 3.6GW under construction (their GW basis, not reconciled to the wiki's facility/IT-load bases). A day later SPCX was among the biggest election losers (Jefferies Titu and pre-market screen, 4 Nov; JPM 4 Nov). The build-speed bull case and the moratorium risk now point in opposite directions for the same name.

DAMNANG on substrates, and the same names in the Asia selloff. DAMNANG (4 Nov, independent author) argues the substrate shortage lasts until 2031 and that larger substrates yield fewer good units. On 5 Nov Jefferies' Asia desk had Ibiden -16%, all the Taiwanese ABF names -10% and MLCCs -10-17%, so the bottleneck names were sold as AI hardware. That is a sentiment and positioning move, not a fundamental change in the article's thesis.
