### How to Win Deals & Grow a $250B Business | Nikesh Arora, Palo Alto Networks

_Picked up from exec-scan: Nikesh Arora (Palo Alto Networks)_

**TL;DR**

- **AI has been good for PANW's business, he says outright (Arora [00:57:10]):** *"Good news is our business has gone up since Mythos [transcribed "Mitosis"] came in."* The concrete product: a **virtual-patching** service — *"We write signatures, we put bandage around the entire perimeter"* while customers patch the underlying systems. Qualitative only; no number.
- **"Our business is not at risk" — the endpoint unit-cost argument (Arora [00:57:46]–[00:58:33]):** PANW charges *"25 bucks a year"* per laptop and watches *"150 megabytes a day, give or take"*; its marginal inspection cost *"is zero"*. If token-priced AI did the endpoint job it would cost *"$5,000 a day. Or pick a number… $500 a day. It'll become $50 a day. They can't do what I do at $25 an endpoint."* (The 10-21 All-In version was "$20 a year" on a "20, 30, 40 megabyte" payload — see What was different.)
- **Cyber in one breakdown (Arora [00:55:10]–[00:56:29]):** ~half of cyber is stopping bad actors at the perimeter, *"10 20%"* is hygiene/analytics inside, *"20 30%"* is detect-and-eject after a breach. AI makes the perimeter *"more porous"* because it finds holes faster; *"the new models that OpenAI has, the likes of GLM 5.3"* can be rented *"cheap"* by bad actors. AI does not patch — so *"the call of the hour"* is swift patching, which is PANW's virtual-patching pitch.
- **The M&A playbook, in more detail than any prior appearance (Arora [00:11:44]–[00:22:51]):** buy only the #1 or #2 in a category (they take *"disproportionate share of the profits"*); negotiate the deal directly with the founder, *"I don't involve bankers"*; then a **30-day joint product roadmap before signing the definitive agreement** — *"I don't sign the definite term sheet until I get the founder to sign on a joint product roadmap."* No founder has walked away at that stage; **PANW backed out twice**. Founders are reinvested for *"3 years, ideally four"*, and the acquired founder becomes the boss of PANW's internal team in that area.
- **Admitted misses (Arora [00:13:00], [00:30:35]):** Aporeto [transcribed "Aprimo"] — *"We thought the world was going to go to IP-based segmentation, but it never went there… We cut our losses quickly."* And Evident.io (bought by his predecessor, AWS-only) was *"too early"* vs RedLock (all three clouds), bought later in the same space. Also: *"we bought what we thought was number one and number two went around us and became number one because we screwed up the execution"* [00:17:19] (target unnamed).
- **Private-market valuations 3-4x two years ago — and he pays anyway (Arora [00:31:58]–[00:32:32]):** the same target *"would be 1/3 or 1/4 the price 2 years ago"*; *"You buy them. If it makes sense on your strategy… M&A is not a strategy."* Asked whether PANW could stop acquiring (Senra's Teledyne/Singleton analogy): *"Possibly… There's no hard and fast rule that we have to keep acquiring"* [00:37:46].
- **The original margin thesis (Arora [00:09:51]–[00:10:26]):** enterprise software spends 15-18% on R&D and 6-10% on G&A; *"If you can find a way of taking 60% of your cost, which typically live in sales, marketing, and customer support, take them down to 20, you can be a 40% operating margin business."* Cross-sell to the top 10,000 customers is how S&M gets amortized. Goal in 2018: from *"$18 billion market cap… to north of 100"*. PANW has *"6,000 people in the field"* [00:24:52] and *"70 plus products"* [00:07:20].
