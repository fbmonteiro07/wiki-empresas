### Why AI Could Turn Small Law Firms Into Billion-Dollar Businesses
_00:53:14 · [episode](https://a16z.simplecast.com/episodes/why-ai-could-turn-small-law-firms-into-billion-dollar-businesses-e8YNZDux) · transcript: YouTube auto-generated (ASR) · relevance: medium_

**TL;DR**

- **AI in plaintiff law is pitched as revenue expansion, not cost cutting.** Rampell: *"if I use Eve I make a lot more money for my firm and it's not because I'm cutting costs. It's like I can take on more cases"* [00:09:59]. Plaintiff firms are *"supply of labor"* constrained, not demand constrained (Madheswaran) [00:11:49].
- **The headline datapoint.** *"one firm in LA went from managing about a hundred clients per attorney to 500"* (Madheswaran) [00:14:36]. This is one customer anecdote. Neither the period nor the case mix is given.
- **Why cases go untaken today.** Lawyers do not take a case unless it makes *"at least 5,000"* [read: ~$5,000 in fees] [00:06:31]. An employment intake takes *"30 to 45 minutes"* plus *"10 hours"* of research [00:07:10]. *"96% of cases on the labor and employment side … gets rejected"* [00:08:15]. All three are Madheswaran's numbers, with no source given.
- **The "shadow labor market" sizing (Rampell, host).** Plaintiff-law software spend looks like *"tens of billions of dollars a year"*, but the *"outcomes that they deliver is in the hundreds of billions of dollars a year"*. So *"the software market is kind of like this shadow labor market"* [00:15:54]–[00:16:58]. The VC framing is not a sized TAM.
- **The labor budget gets redirected into tokens.** Madheswaran says roughly half of the attorney's third goes to labor. With that labor no longer needed, *"the best way to get more useful work is actually to spend it weirdly on more tokens"* [00:44:35]. This is a direct statement of the labor → token substitution.
- **Seat pricing is moving to outcome pricing.** Rampell: SaaS is the *"tall, grande, venti"* model (*"$79 a month, $99 a month, $129 a month"*, per seat), and *"Claude can clone them all so readily"*. Zendesk (*"$2 billion of ARR when they were last a public company"*) now faces *"how many seats do you need? Like maybe zero"* [00:38:59]–[00:39:40]. Eve started with seat pricing and went outcome-based *"like a year and a half ago"* [00:43:51]–[00:44:35]. The billable hour is *"on the way of the seat-based model"* [00:45:09].
- **Lab relationships and the limit on growth.** Madheswaran says legal benchmarks from *"Anthropic, OpenAI, many of whom we're working very closely with"* measure *"almost single outcome"* tasks, while Eve's work is agentic and long-horizon [00:46:40]. Eve is *"largely limited by engineers"* [00:47:18]. There has been no fully autonomous end-to-end case yet (*"Not yet, but we're close"*) [00:47:57].
- **Agents already negotiate with agents.** Eve's outbound voice AI calls insurers and providers for medical records, *"where we're already seeing agents talking to other agents"* [00:48:35]. Madheswaran also says *"a single case has probably 10,000 back and forths"* [00:47:57].

**What was different**

- **Labor spend becomes token spend at the vertical-app layer** → the tokenmaxxing theme holds the Jevons/labor-price framing from Levie (a16z, 2026-10-25: legal review *"$1,000"-"$2,000"* an hour vs an agent at *"$5 or $10 an hour"*) and Collison/Altman on token spend as a share of OpEx (Cheeky Pint, 2026-10-29). Eve adds the mechanism inside one P&L: about half of the contingency fee is labor, and that budget gets re-spent *"on more tokens"* [00:44:35]. **🆕 NOT ON THE WIKI** as a stated reallocation, though the direction ✅ CONFIRMS the page.
- **Outcome-based pricing in a vertical** → tokenmaxxing logs Altman (2026-10-29) saying outcomes are *"too different across different use cases"* to be the pricing unit for a frontier model, so per-token billing stays. Eve prices on outcomes because plaintiff law *"was always outcome-oriented"* (Rampell [00:39:40]). This **🔭 DIVERGES at the app layer** from the lab-layer view. It does not contradict it: the app charges for outcomes and pays the lab in tokens, which is the vertical carve-out the Altman bullet left open.
- **Seat erosion, named on a public-era SaaS comp** → ai-builder-toolkit's bear case is "fewer human seats" (Citrini, 2026-07-20), and tokenmaxxing carries JPM's Debate 4 (*"consumption upsides have to overpower seat-based headwinds"*). Rampell's *"maybe zero"* seats for Zendesk and *"Claude can clone them all"* ✅ CONFIRM the bear framing. It is a VC's opinion with no data.
- **Agents vs agents in an adversarial market** → the prior a16z episode (Cuban, 2026-10-28) argued payer/provider "bot wars" *"net out"* because *"AI is a great equalizer"*. Madheswaran's case is the opposite asymmetry: defendants still bill hourly and *"haven't quite caught up"*, so stalling tactics stop working [00:49:56]–[00:50:44]. **🔭 DIVERGES** from Cuban's net-out view, for the window before the defense side adopts AI.
