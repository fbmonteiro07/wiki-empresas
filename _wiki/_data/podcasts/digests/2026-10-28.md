### Cliff Sosin - Owning the Complements to Intelligence - [Invest Like the Best, EP.497]
_01:00:49 · [episode](https://colossus.com/episode/the-limits-of-intelligence/) · transcript: podscripts.co (machine transcript, no speaker labels) · relevance: high_

**TL;DR**

- **Intelligence has hard limits** (Sosin [00:01:54]–[00:06:22]). Most interesting questions sit in complex dynamic systems such as weather or groups of people, where simulation error compounds. Beyond a point, progress needs experiments that *"touch reality"*, and those take time. So "good enough" intelligence becomes near-free, but it does not "zoom to God".
- **Own the complements to intelligence** (Sosin [00:08:24]): data, attention, emotional attachment, physical proximity, scale and regulatory capture. Businesses whose value depends on customer *"lethargy"* lose once agents optimize for the customer.
- **META is his model "complement" name** (Sosin [00:09:25]–[00:12:06]). *"They have 250 billion of revenue and they spend $250 billion a year"*, almost all of it on intelligence: data centres, inference, engineers, code. If *"the price of intelligence is falling by a factor of 100 or something over the next five years"*, that cost base deflates. Meanwhile data and attention stay in place, and creators, ranking and ads all improve.
- **Frontier labs: commoditization behind the frontier, plus a "diamond-shaped" demand curve** (Sosin [00:13:53]–[00:16:19]). Each new frontier opens a larger market, and the one it leaves behind commoditizes (*"you would never use a frontier model for chatbot in a customer service application"*). He doubts there are many uses for *"super elite intelligence"*. Good-enough intelligence is *"highly competitive and commoditized"*, with *"rampant deflation"*.
- **Jevons is one point on a U-shaped demand curve** (Sosin [00:17:25]). His analogy is telecom: falling costs grew the market for a long time, then it shrank. Cogent *"routes 30 or 40 percent of all IP traffic"* and is *"a few billion dollar market cap company"* (his claim, unchecked).
- **He stays out of the AI build-out** (Sosin [00:24:12]): *"I can't forecast supply or demand within several orders of magnitude."* On overbuild he says *"I can tell both stories with equal certainty"* [00:52:20]. Consumer AI demand is *"really sensitive to the price"* [00:25:20].
- **NVDA vs TSMC** (Sosin [00:28:35]–[00:29:43]). NVIDIA *"quite literally makes flat files that they send to Taiwan"*, intelligence-heavy work that AI could in theory make cheaper to copy. TSMC's process know-how is physical and *"much less amenable to an AI"*, but its margins invite *"every one in the world to seek to copy their moat"*.
- **Agents break aggregation economics and push margins down** (Sosin [00:30:34]–[00:32:41], [00:48:01]–[00:48:37]). Agents switch customers to the lowest-cost provider. Ranking first on search matters less when *"your agent looks at everything"*. Aggregate corporate margins argue down and consumer surplus goes up, though he notes 1990s internet research made the same call and was wrong.

**What was different**

- **A buy-side "AI is deflationary for intelligence sellers" frame, from a META holder.** It puts META on the *demand/complement* side and reads Meta's ~$250bn of spend as a cost that deflates. The wiki's META bear case is the opposite: capital intensity and negative incremental ROIC (META thesis line). [[META]] has FY27 capex direction *"we expect that investment to grow in 2027"* (Meta investor video, 2026-10-26) and WFC's $250bn 2027 capex (Wells Fargo recap call, 2026-10-22). 🔭 **DIVERGES** in framing. Sosin does not engage with the FY27 capex step-up or the equity-raise plan on the page. He argues cost per unit of intelligence collapses, but never says Meta's dollar spend falls.
- **"250 billion revenue / 250 billion spend"** → [[META]] has FY26 revenue implied at **$254.7-257.7bn** (9M plus the 4Q guide; Meta Q3 release, 2026-10-21), FY26 capex at **$135-145bn**, total expenses at **$175-179bn**, and 3Q FCF at **−$2.12bn**. ✅ **CONFIRMS** the order of magnitude and the "they spend it all" (FCF ≈ 0) point. His $250bn is a rhetorical figure that mixes capex and opex, not a reconciled number.
- **AI capex at "3% of GDP"** (Sosin [00:38:05], *"Maybe it should be two. Maybe it should be six"*) → [themes/hyperscaler-capex](../../../_wiki/themes/hyperscaler-capex.md) has GS Briggs/Dong (2026-08-02) at **1.8% of US GDP in 2026, 2.5% in 2027, 2.8% in 2028**. 🔭 Slightly **ABOVE** the GS 2026 level. This is an off-the-cuff guest number, so don't cite it as a datapoint.
- **"Price of intelligence falling ~100x over five years"** (Sosin [00:10:26]) → [themes/tokenmaxxing](../../../_wiki/themes/tokenmaxxing.md) has Lambert on a16z (2026-10-14): 2x every eight months, roughly 1/22 to 1/64 of the cost over three to four years for a given tier. Mizuho's token-price index is down >50% since mid-2026 (10-05). ✅ Same direction, a faster rate stated loosely. Different basis again: a "price of intelligence" is not a per-tier token price or an index.
- **Jevons as a "U-shaped" curve, with telecom/Cogent as the end state.** 🆕 **NOT ON THE WIKI** in this form. Tokenmaxxing records Jevons framings from Jassy (10-23), Horowitz (10-24) and Levie (10-25), all bullish. This is the first explicit bear version from a podcast guest in the lane. It sits close to the Chris Wood "tokens-per-task falling" theory (Jefferies at Capstone, 2026-10-21, on tokenmaxxing).
- **NVDA "flat files" commoditization risk** → not a new debate. The page's ASIC-erosion debate (NVDA thesis) is the closer anchor. Sosin offers it as a hypothetical (*"in theory, you could make them"*), not a call. ✅ Restates a known bear argument.
- **"SpaceX's Investor Day yesterday"** (O'Shaughnessy [00:25:20]), with the build-out of data-centre supply as its theme. 🆕 **NOT ON THE WIKI.** [[SPCX]] has the 10-26 8-K (Apollo-managed GPU finance lease, ~$40bn) but no investor-day event. The recording date is not stated, so "yesterday" can't be dated. Worth checking whether an SPCX investor day transcript or deck exists.
- Vs the prior ILTB summary (Cacioppo, 2026-10-20, software durability): consistent on "AI erodes crystallized-intelligence software". Sosin adds the agent-driven price-competition mechanism.

### Reinventing School for the AI Era | Joe Liemandt, Gagan Biyani & Erik Torenberg
_00:56:52 · [episode](https://a16z.simplecast.com/episodes/reinventing-school-for-the-ai-era-joe-liemandt-gagan-biyani-erik-torenberg-qpcIrpfu) · transcript: podscripts.co (machine transcript, no speaker labels) · relevance: low_

**TL;DR**

- Liemandt says cognitive and learning science plus LLMs give *"a learning engine that teaches kids 10 times faster"* [00:02:32]. At Alpha, kids *"learn twice as much in two hours a day as sitting in class for six and doing homework"* [00:03:08].
- The engine keeps each student in an *"80 to 85 percent correct zone"*, which a teacher with 30 kids can't do [00:11:40]–[00:12:20].
- The rest of the day goes to life skills: leadership and teamwork, entrepreneurship and financial literacy, relationships, storytelling, and grit [00:04:21]. *"90-some percent"* of kids say they love school, and 50% (65% on one campus) would rather be at school than on vacation [00:03:08].
- Biyani on HAA, the college-age version: you can't copy K-12 over, and the design centres on building, relationships and frontier operators [00:13:58]. Students fear that entry-level jobs *"aren't there"* because of AI [00:16:41].
- Liemandt: *"big Fortune 500 [companies] are going to be smaller … because of AI"* but there will be *"an explosion of entrepreneurship"* [00:49:02]. Alpha's high-school track *"guarantee[s] that you'll start a business that will earn a million dollars of profit while you're in high school or we give you all your tuition back"* [00:49:48].

**What was different**

Nothing market-moving. The "AI shrinks large-company headcount, expands entrepreneurship" line echoes earlier a16z episodes (Horowitz 10-24, Levie 10-25) and is not new. No covered tickers.
