Can Gemini 4 Argon Recharge Google’s Mojo? Read full disclosures Overweight GOOG, GOOG US Price ( 30 Sep 26 ): $340.74 Price Target ( Dec-27 ): $420.00 Overweight GOOGL, GOOGL US Price ( 30 Sep 26 ): $344.08 Price Target ( Dec-27 ): $420.00 On Wednesday, Google announced its new frontier model, Gemini 4 Argon. The model shows frontier performance in complex workflows, cyber defense, and software engineering. According to DeepMind-provided benchmarks , Argon ranks favorably compared to GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1, &Claude Opus 5.5 across a wide range of use cases and workflows. While the model is being used extensively inside of Google, the company is only currently rolling it out to a set of trusted cyber defenders & the U.S. government through the voluntary process for pre-release model access. Importantly, Argon's output token limit per response is an industry-leading 1M tokens, compared to what we believe is 128K for other leading models. And Arena AI suggests that Argon's price per task is significantly lower than GPT-6.1 Sol & Claude Opus 5.5, placing Argon on the Pareto frontier. We recognize that Bloomberg yesterday reported on some internal employee skepticism around Argon's real-world performance, but we expect rapid model iteration, including model releases, which Sundar has previously indicated would come at an almost monthly cadence. While we believe it is critical for Google to re-establish itself at the frontier and as an AI leader for both the industry and investors, Gemini 4 must also strengthen core Google products such as Search, Cloud, Gemini, & many others, and serve as the foundation for new products, including agents. Google has been a source of funds over the past few months, with shares down -15% from their May highs, compared to the SPX +3%, AMZN -8%, & META +18%. The primary factors include Google slipping off the frontier, iterating on product more slowly & quietly than competitors, & facing tougher 2H Search comps & margin headwinds. Heavy AI infrastructure buildout remains in focus w/the 2026 capex guide at $195B-$205B, & we project an 85% increase in 2027 to $378B in capex, likely shifting Google to significant negative FCF next year—we model a loss of ($115B). However, we believe Google will continue to show the returns on AI spending, coming off 17% Search growth & 82% Cloud growth in 2Q. While it's very early, we’re encouraged by Gemini 4 Argon’s initial benchmarks and price/performance, and we expect the model to further strengthen ahead of the broader release & with more frequent iterations. We continue to believe that Google has all the critical pieces to lead in AI through its full stack offering including 1P silicon/chips, a return to frontier models, strong Cloud momentum, data, & distribution across 5 products with 3B+ users and 14 products with 1B+ users. We reiterate our Overweight rating & our $420 December 2027 PT is based on ~25x our 2028E GAAP EPS of $16.66. What’s Ailing Google? Frontier Sustainability. We believe investors have been concerned about Google’s ability to compete at the frontier, with both the pace & perceived quality of new model releases lagging that of peers in recent months. These concerns have been amplified by reported AI talent departures & leadership changes across Google DeepMind, as well as debates on whether the breadth of Google’s compute demands—including Cloud, internal products, & ongoing model training—creates added complexity around compute allocation & prioritization versus more AI-singular competitors. We believe the Gemini 4 Argon announcement should help address that concern, with the model at the frontier across several benchmarks, and at the Pareto frontier of intelligence vs cost. The said, Argon is still only available to an initial cohort of testers, & investors will likely remain focused on the cadence of model releases & the quality of real-world model performance. Quieter Product Launches. Google continues to ship a high volume of AI products, features, & enhancements across its ecosystem. Notable examples this year include the rollout of Personal Intelligence across Gemini & Search, multimodal model releases across image, video, & music, the launch of Gemini Spark, & a steady cadence of AI feature expansion across Search, Gmail & Workspace, Maps, YouTube, & other core products. However, Google has struggled to translate ongoing product velocity into splashy releases that resonate with consumers, capture sustained mindshare, & meaningfully drive sentiment around AI leadership. Challenging 2H Setup Alongside Growing Agentic Disruption Concerns. Coming out of 2Q, investors have been focused on a tougher setup for Search growth as comps get 5pts tougher through 2H26. At the same time, 3P compute capacity rentals (w/SPCX), depreciation, data center operations costs, AI & Cloud hires, AI product marketing, & the Wiz acquisition could pressure the P&L, raising concerns around 2H operating margins. More fundamentally, the recent traction of consumer agents (i.e. Muse, Grok Bot, Instinct) has reignited the debate around Search disruption risk. While AI has proven to be expansionary to Search thus far, consumer adoption of agents is still very early, and has the potential to pose a more meaningful risk if agents increasingly handle transactions for consumers, including across high-value verticals like retail & travel. What Gets It Going? Gemini 4. Initial benchmarks for Gemini 4 Argon are strong & likely position Google back to the frontier. Importantly, Argon is likely still an early Gemini 4 model, w/Google DeepMind’s new chief Koray Kavukcuoglu recently suggesting ( 9/24 ) that the company would release an early version as soon as possible, and we would expect continued improvement as the broader Gemini 4 family rolls out. Argon is also not yet broadly integrated across Google’s core surfaces, and deeper integration into Search, Cloud, Gemini, & agentic products should strengthen fundamentals. Monthly Model Cadence. Google has moved to a roughly monthly model update cadence, but this is for Flash — its lighter-weight tier — not the Pro model that we believe is more relevant for coding & agentic work. Recent releases include Gemini 3.8 Flash & Flash Cyber ( 9/2 ), Gemini 3.8 Live & Live Extended Thinking ( 9/15 ) for real-time dialogue, Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS / Flash-Lite TTS ( 9/23 ) for expressive & high-throughput speech, & Gemini Live with Live Avatar ( 9/24 ). We believe sustained updates across Flash & its variants have helped maintain product progression between frontier releases, but the launch of Gemini 4 Argon should mark the first step toward faster iteration on larger, more intelligent models, a key focal point for investors. We note that at 2Q earnings management said that releasing models at almost a monthly cadence is part of the roadmap as Google builds Gemini 4 and iterates on top of it. Consumer + Enterprise Agentic Products. Agent competition has heated up as Muse, Grok Bot, & Instinct set a high bar for end-to-end “do the work” experiences. Google’s competing surfaces include Gemini Spark, Antigravity, AI Mode / Search agents, & Gemini Enterprise on Cloud. Usage proof points are already there — Gemini app 1B+ MAU, Antigravity WAU ~2.4M in 2Q, Gemini Enterprise paid MAUs +40% Q/Q in 1Q. But the gap is still the “wow” factor vs. peers. We believe Google has the talent, capabilities, & compute, but needs a bigger step-up in agentic products that feels like must-use rather than just available. Search Growth. Search & other ads remains the largest revenue stream & the primary profit contributor—1Q +19% reported Y/Y, 2Q +17% Y/Y to $63.3B — with AI Mode >1B MAU. Comps get tougher in 2H, as management noted during the 2Q call. Even so, we think 3Q needs solid mid-teens+ growth to push back on the declining Search narrative—we project 14% reported in 3Q and 13% in 4Q. We continue to think AI is expansionary to Search, but Search also needs to evolve to be competitive with agentic platforms. Cloud Growth & Backlog. This is the cleanest fundamental support, w/2Q Cloud accelerating to +82% Y/Y to $24.8B and 2Q backlog +$52B Q/Q to $514B, w/just over half expected as revenue in the next 24 months. We look for 3Q demand still outrunning supply, backlog converting (not just stacking), & third-party bridge capacity costs staying “modest” as flagged. We model continued acceleration in Cloud growth to +88% reported Y/Y in 3Q & +90% in 4Q. TPU / Accelerator Hardware. Revenue recognition began in 2Q with the first customer DC deliveries. Existing deals are a small portion in 2026, but ramp exiting the year, w/the vast majority coming in 2027. Incremental hardware dollars that read as a real growth driver—even without a discrete disclosed TPU segment—would validate the full-stack silicon foundation.
