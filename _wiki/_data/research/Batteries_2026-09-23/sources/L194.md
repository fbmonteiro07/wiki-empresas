MODEL PROLIFERATION. Edison notes <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  that In AA's top 14 LLM list, four new models appeared in Sep, although ranked in the lower half. They are from Singapore, Korea, UAE, and China. All four had only limited capital raise. It shows 1) competition in LLM and the number of players continue to rise, 2) many countries may prefer local models (limiting the TAM of US and Chinese models), and 3) capex is low, likely due to distillation. High capex but uncertain return still a big risk to the AI supply chain.  The rising number of LLM players and narrowing model performance gap would, in our view, continue to put pressure on API pricing. One of the newly ranked models, Apodex 1.1, listed a blended API price of US$0.3 per token, the fifth-cheapest model among the top 15 on the AA list. The average API price discount of Chinese vs US models has expanded from 60% in Aug to 80% in Sep, reversing the narrowing trend in the previous three months, since Anthropic raised its API price by 86% for Fable 5.1 (vs Opus 5) while GPT6 pricing is up 77% (vs GPT5.6).

DEEPSEEK CUT HBM BY ¼ and SSD BY 1/8TH. In his note Edison write that DS released its V4.1 Flash model on Sep 10, at a blended API price of US$0.2/token, 74% lower than the previous version. By using Casual Encoder-Decoder design, Engram conditional memory (storing recurring tokens and retrieve them only when needed) and Compressed Sparse Attention 2, it cuts HBM demand by 1/4 and SSD by 1/8, resulting in lower inference cost. We believe DS' focus is more on compute and memory efficiency than model intelligence, which is likely a strong differentiation for it to support China's skyrocketing inference demand.

DEEPSEEK V4.1 FLASH IS NOT A BETTER MODEL; IT IS A BETTER INFERENCE ENGINE. Here is some more information on this from Deepseeks technical paper here <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  It focuses on reducing the memory, storage, and bandwidth costs of serving long-context AI agents. It KV-cache efficiency, allowing DeepSeek to lower HBM and SSD while improving benchmark performance versus the prior generation. The implications of this are negative for HBM and NAND usage if more models were to adopt this approach. SWA KV is no longer cached in the persistent KV cache and instead stored in a distributed memory pool provisioned from 10% of the host DRAM on each machine. However even Deepseek itself says this is not a performance enhancing model. It says “ Our internal evaluations cover a diverse range of test cases and boundary conditions, and we have not observed any systematic degradation in model capabilities in the evaluated settings. We will also monitor real-world workloads, systematically characterize potential failure modes and robustness boundaries, and further improve model robustness under extreme conditions”

Key points from the paper (attached)

*	Prefill remains computationally expensive, and large KV caches continue to strain HBM and SSD capacity and data-transfer bandwidth. These compute, storage, and bandwidth demands constitute the primary bottleneck to further lowering deployment costs
*	We introduce DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, a multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model with 552B backbone parameters and support for contexts of up to 1m tokens. 
*	With its Causal Encoder-Decoder (CED) architecture, the model activates 16B parameters per token during decode but only 8B parameters during prefill, substantially improving cost efficiency for agentic workloads. 
*	To push the limits of KV cache compression, DeepSeek-V4.1Flash combines cross-layer KV cache reuse in Compressed Sparse Attention 2 (CSA2) with FP4 KV caching. 
*	These designs reduce its global KV cache footprint (always in HBM) to 890 bytes per token, roughly 1/4 of the corresponding footprint of DeepSeek-V4-Flash. 
*	Through a dedicated deployment optimization known as SWA Bounded Replay, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash reduces its persistent KV cache footprint (always on SSD or in host memory) to roughly 1/8 of that of DeepSeek-V4-Flash. 
*	Despite its much smaller KV cache footprint, the model delivers substantially better performance than the baseline.
*	We design Compressed Sparse Attention 2 (CSA2), which applies cross-layer reuse to global KV (including main KV and indexer K) and Top-K indices to substantially reduce KV cache storage. 

	*	CSA2 has three statically assigned modes: Full, Reindex, and Reuse. Full Mode generates global KV and performs indexing. 
	*	Reindex Mode reuses the global KV from a preceding layer, and uses its own indexer Q to rescore the shared indexer K and select fresh Top-K indices. 
	*	Reuse Mode reuses both global KV and the Top-K indices in a preceding layer, and directly performs sparse attention. 
	*	In all three modes, each layer retains its own global Q and SWA KV. Sharing global KV and indexer K reduces duplicated cache storage

*	Together, CSA2 and FP4 KV caching reduce global KV cache storage to approximately 1/4 of that of DeepSeek-V4-Flash, as shown in Figure 1(b)

*	In DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, we introduce SWA Bounded Replay, which approximately reconstructs the required SWA KV states by replaying only the most recent 𝑛win tokens. 

	*	Our experiments show that this incurs only negligible performance degradation. This finding establishes a new storage–computation trade-off, allowing us to avoid persisting SWA KV cache to SSD while incurring a small amount of prefill recomputation. 
	*	With SWA Bounded Replay, the persistent KV cache footprint is further reduced to approximately 1/8 of that of DeepSeek-V4-Flash.
	*	Under identical workloads, V4.1 reduces the persistent KV cache footprint to 1/8 of that of V4. Two multiplicative factors account for this reduction: the persistent KV cache no longer stores SWA KV, which almost halves its size, and the global KV retained in it is further compressed to 1/4 of V4’s footprint through architectural and precision optimizations.
	*	SWA KV is no longer cached in the persistent KV cache and instead stored in a distributed memory pool provisioned from 10% of the host DRAM on each machine. Although this pool is far smaller in aggregate capacity, its short TTL (only minutes) allows expired entries to be recycled immediately for new sessions; under real-world workloads, this high turnover suffices to serve the vast majority of concurrent active sessions. Global KV remains in the persistent KV cache with a guaranteed lifetime of at least 72 hours.

*	To fully realize the KV cache compression benefits of these architectural designs and further improve training and inference efficiency, we systematically co-optimize the training infrastructure and inference system for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, ensuring efficient and scalable large-scale multimodal training and long-context deployment.
