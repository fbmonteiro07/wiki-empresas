Good Evening, 

GS China Basic Materials team runs a monthly survey tracking orderbooks of downstream and basic materials producers. 

Below our main takeaways: 

*	* End-user orderbooks picked up as of mid-Sep, inline with past seasonality. The sequential pickups in demand were led by export machinery, EV, battery, construction and metal fabrication, but were absent in auto-ICE, appliance, and paper packaging related to domestic consumer sectors. Infrastructure construction was contained to ongoing projects. 

*	* On the other hand, end user responses to a higher copper price appeared more modest than in the past. On supply: 1) both thermal and met coal supply are impacted by safety enforcement and disruption in Mongolia imports, 2) domestic copper scrap remains tight and led to further delays in return of copper smelter from maintenance, 3) lithium supply is easing as Zimbabwe spodumene lands in China, and 4) NBS output of primary aluminum annualized at 46.9mnt, but we see mild delay in aluminum projects in both China and Indonesia. 

	*	* High-frequency data suggest that in the first half of Sep Chinese demand was 2-6% lower yoy for cement and construction steel, 1-2% higher yoy for copper, aluminium and flat steel. Margin/pricing for aluminium, copper and coal improved, lithium softened, while steel and cement were mostly stable. 

*	* Steel: The forward orderbook trend of most steel mills picked up MoM in September, and saw weaker than normal seasonality. High-frequency weekly data suggest steel demand declined by 6.0% yoy for construction steel, but increased by 1.4% yoy for flat steel as of the second week of September. Most steel mills plans to maintain production stable in September. 

	*	* Unit gross profit was Rmb20-70/t for domestic sales, mostly stable vs. Rmb20-60/t in August. Profit for exports was Rmb80-100/t, vs Rmb80-160/t in August, per mills’ comments. Industry pricing and margin trend further softened MoM, as lower steel price met with higher costs in coke. 

*	* Iron Ore: Steel mills commented on the balanced or small surplus market for IO, while coke and met coal supply were tight. Both IO and met coal inventory remained at low levels in most steel mills, or normal from the mills' perspective. 

	*	* Selected steel mills commented on lower IO shipment from non-mainstream countries and Brazil under rising transportation cost while Simandou production is on track. Price expectation remains at US$90-105/t per traders' comments. 

*	* Copper: Our channel checks with major fabricators suggest that the orderbook was mixed MoM, weaker than normal seasonality. Fabricators commented on MoM demand improvement as of mid-Sep, but weaker than past season pattern. Demand improvement is mostly absent in grid, appliance, and auto/EV, while it remains strong in semiconductor and datacenter. End user responses to higher copper price appeared more modest than in the past, with higher reservation price at Rmb105k/t. Domestic scrap supply remains tight - and the return of copper smelter from maintenance has been further delayed to Oct-26, per industry feedback. 

	*	* By Sep 11th, the physical premium for domestic refined copper turned positive at Rmb65/t. TCRC remains negative at US$-210/t with US$34/t MoM decline, and remained negative at US$-0.18/lb if adjusted by income from sulfuric acid price in our estimate. 

*	* Lithium: Domestic demand in the form of cathode continued to grow in August and is expected to further edge up in September - cathode output will increase by 4% MoM (or up by 62% yoy) in September, per SMM. YTD cathode output in China is 16% ahead of the implied demand based on downstream demand, in our estimates. Feedback suggested decelerating growth in both domestic ESS amid declining project returns and EV. EV OEMs also commented EV sales pick up by mid-Sep is milder than past peak season pattern. 

	*	* By Sep 11th, China spot lithium carbonate price was down 6% MoM and Guangzhou futures price decreased by 9% MoM. China lithium carbonate spread was mostly flat MoM, while Asia CIF hydroxide spread much declined, due to lower Asia CIF hydroxide price. 

Forward orderbook trends: Feedback from end-user forward orderbooks as of mid-Sep 2026 suggests that 61% of respondents expect a MoM improvement, versus 42% in Aug-26. 

Inventory levels at producers and end-users: Our inventory survey as of mid-September suggests above normal for coal/steel/Al/paper/finished products, normal levels for iron ore/met coal/alumina/copper/building materials, and below normal for OCC.
