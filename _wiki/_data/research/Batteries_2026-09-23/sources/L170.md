BESS in Brazil: first auction sets the stage for a broader storage market 

We have engaged in discussions on the development of Brazil’s battery energy storage system (BESS) market, covering the first storage auction, regulatory priorities, demand growth and emerging applications across the power sector. Discussions suggested that the December auction should act as a catalyst for market development, helping establish storage as a new stand-alone segment within Brazil’s electricity sector. Key themes included the need for regulatory evolution, more efficient price signals and a broader set of storage use cases beyond centralized auctions. This report is part of UBS’ ongoing series on BESS and builds on our note: BESS: From renewable expansion to system flexibility <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive] .

Expected next steps for the first BESS auction 
Brazil’s first BESS auction is scheduled for December 2 and 4 of this year, with a workshop on the qualification process set for October 14 and publication of the final auction documents expected later in October. Discussions suggested the auction was designed primarily to address Brazil’s growing capacity needs, as rising solar penetration increases the gap between periods of abundant daytime generation and tighter supply conditions during evening peak demand. Following the auction, authorization grants are expected by May 2027, with project commissioning targeted for August 2028. See the full auction schedule here <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive] .

First auction aims to address capacity needs 
While the EPE registration process attracted a record of 6,091 projects, representing 296,8 GW (link <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive] ), participants do not view the volume of projects as surprising. As seen in other Brazilian energy auctions, project duplication and qualification requirements are expected to materially reduce the number of bidders by the final stage. Meanwhile, cost allocation of the BESS projects appeared as one of the key open issues, with a public consultation on the methodology for the division of storage related costs among generators open until September 14, following the framework established by Law 15.269. Beyond the auction, participants highlighted storage’s recognition as a stand-alone market participant, a structural change that could support new business models over time. Combined with the transition toward hourly pricing, BESS could increasingly monetize intraday price spreads while helping narrow peak and off-peak price differentials.

Main investor questions 
Investor questions focused on how ancillary services will ultimately be compensated, both in the December auction and in future storage projects. Discussions indicated that the auction will remunerate BESS through power availability, with ancillary services incorporated into the contract structure as broader regulations continue to evolve. As a result, near-term revenues should be driven largely by capacity payments, while future regulatory advancements could create additional monetization opportunities for storage assets. Participants also emphasized that the growth of the BESS market will ultimately depend on enabling a broader set of applications beyond centralized auctions, allowing storage to capture value across multiple segments of the power system. In this sense, this week ANEEL (Brazilian Electricity Regulatory Agency) announced a public consultation for Brazil’s first transmission auction involving battery storage systems, currently scheduled for April 30, 2027 (link <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive] ). Interested parties may submit contributions until October 26. 

Data centers as a possible important source of future power demand 
Data centers were highlighted as a significant emerging source of electricity demand, although the associated infrastructure investments will depend on greater visibility around project execution. Discussions suggested that the scale of potential load growth could require substantial upgrades across the electricity network, but planners need confidence that the expected demand will materialize before committing capital. As a result, the quality of demand forecasts and commitments from data center operators may become a key factor determining the timing and scale of future grid investments.

Applications beyond auctions could expand the addressable BESS market 
Discussions suggested that Brazil should accelerate storage deployment through mechanisms beyond centralized auctions, allowing BESS to address a broader set of network and operational needs. A good example of different storage solutions being implemented was the recent approval of ISA Energia <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  to offer BESS solutions to private-sector clients. Another notable example is the country's first storage procurement associated with a transmission substation, scheduled for April 30th 2027 (link <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive] ). More broadly, participants highlighted storage’s potential role across transmission, distribution and generation.
