GNRC disclosed in a Filing an agreement of up to $8 B with AMZN for backup generators with initial deliveries of $2.4 B in 2027 and 2028.  AMZN is also buying up to 1.694 mm warrants with a 200.9266 strike with 307,954 vesting immediately and the balance vesting in tranches contingent on payments received by GNRC for Generators.  The filing really didn’t have a huge amount of details, but this is obviously positive for the stock.  

FLNC guided fiscal 2026 Revenue to about $2.4 B vs. the prior midpoint of guidance of $3.0 B.  They guided EBITDA to a loss of $200 mm vs. previous guidance of $10 mm.   They continue to experience delays in the ramp up of their Houston contract management facility which is the primary reason for the lower guidance.  (80% of the guidance change is due to U.S. production issues.)   They said in the presentation that they have had $450 mm in production delays and incurred $65 mm in penalties for late deliveries.  The remaining $85 mm reflects logistical issues. The company indicated that the contract manufacturer has taken corrective actions that have already yielded an increase in daily production levels.  The company also said that they are taking additional steps to restructure their operational organization and streamline across the supply chain, manufacturing, and delivery functions.  They said demand continues to be strong.

Recall that Christine Cho downgraded the stock to UW on 9/3 after the previous warning because she thought that fiscal 2027 estimates were too high.  [original URL omitted; available in raw archive] <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive] 

Christine had a note out after the conference call.  She continues to think the key debate around the stock Is no longer demand generation, but rather the time of conversion from backlog into revenue, earnings and cash flow.  She notes that management disclosed on the call that the Houston facility averaged less than one unit of production in August vs,. a target of 11 units per day, with September improving to about 3 units a day.  Automated welding continues to be a source of delays, so the company is no using manual welding temporarily.  She notes that company will provide a quarterly update on progress in November. She notes that the company expects to fulfill its $2.9 B backlog, but may have to turn down incremental orders who want U.S. manufactured products while it implements a right sizing plan.  This could limit revenue from being much higher than the current backlog in Fiscal 2027.  Christine thinks that last nights announcement underscores the risk to 2027 consensus with respect to backlog conversion, earnings recovery and cash flow that she wrote about in her recent downgrade.
