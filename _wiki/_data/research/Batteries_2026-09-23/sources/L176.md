What a hyperscaler uses batteries for 

A hyperscaler cannot let chips go dark. There are two backups: First, a short backup in or next to the rack that covers a power dip from a fraction of a second to a few minutes so the rack stays up while building power supply recovers. The battery back up unit (BBU) and the faster capacitor back up unit (CBU) play this role. Second, the building (data-centre hall) covers a longer outage until generators start, and can run for hours. This is an uninterruptible power supply (UPS), or hall battery system. Architecture and dollar forecasts below follow UBS APAC Focus - AI Power: From watts to gigawatts <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive] . 

Why the rack changes this 

Older Hopper racks pulled high current at low voltage. The 800-volt Rubin design cuts current and lets voltage step down closer to the chips. Backup systems can now sit in a power cabinet (sidecar) beside the IT rack instead of inside every Hopper style server tray. Some sites will still put a BBU in the sidecar. Others will lean on the building UPS and keep only small, fast buffers at the rack. That is the operator's choice. Our working case for Rubin-class systems is about half still take a rack BBU. 

BBU market $2.5bn in 2026E, to $13bn by 2030E 

The BBU market is the battery next to / in the rack, not the building UPS. We expect BBU sales should rise from $0.7bn in 2025 to $2.5bn in 2026 to $12.8bn by 2030. Nvidia is about $1.2bn of the 2026 total. Assuming a 5 min duration and a BBU system cost of $0.2/W, we estimate battery cells represent 10-15% of the BBU system cost. If we include the capacitor shelf, the combined market (BBU + CBU) is $3.1bn in 2026E, reaching $15.9bn in 2030E. Growth in 2026-27E is more racks and more power per rack. Some investors treat Rubin as the end of BBU. In our view, Rubin gradually moves the battery out of the tray, not off the floor. The 800-volt sidecar still has an optional BBU and model a 40-50% attach rate on Rubin class systems. Dollars of BBU per rack rise versus Hopper because the rack draws much more power. What can fall is cylindrical cells in the tray, not backup as a product. If a site leans on hall UPS, attach in sidecar drops. Total batteries on the floor can still rise. 

Valuation: Panasonic vs Samsung SDI 

Panasonic is the compact rack product: cylindrical cells and full backup modules for the sidecar. Customers should keep buying that short duration backup. If reserve minutes move into the hall, cylindrical form factor in the tray could decline. Samsung SDI sells both rack cells and larger building batteries (UPS). In our view, SDI could begin LFP for UPS at one of its two US expansion sites. This could be margin accretive with onshore battery production: US cell production can receive production tax credit; overseas cylindrical lines do not.
