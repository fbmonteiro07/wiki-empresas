MONC.IM <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  – Luca Solca has upgraded Moncler to Outperform as the stock trades near its post-IPO low, driven by a combination of the seasonal trading pattern and the broader luxury sector de-rating. Valuation has de-rated 29% YTD to 17.4x NTM, in line with historical seasonal drawdowns. Moncler brand has strong traction in Asia, catering well to high end demand, and the opening of Moncler's Fifth Avenue flagship could create a halo effect across the broader business in coming quarters. “Moncler to Measure” service is a step forward to serve the higher-end demand for more personalisation. Weakness in Europe has been reflected in expectations with consensus EMEA 3Q26E revenue estimates cut by 11pts to -6.6% (vs. -8% for 2Q26) from the +4.5% prior to 2Q26 results. Stone Island (15% group revenue) is doing much better with DD% OSG over the last 3 quarters. It has taken a sweet spot in terms of pricing structure, with most of its price architecture positioned at the lower end of outerwear peer. For 4Q we model 7% Moncler DTC (vs. consensus 6.9%) and 13% SI DTC (vs. consensus 10.2%). On total group revenue we are 1% above consensus for FY26 and FY27 and if weather is more helpful (ie cold), then 4Q could be at LSD% higher than our 7%.

DOL <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  - Zhihan Ma likes the risk/reward after beat raise... see ‘26 as peak investment year with plenty of catalysts in ’27. Q2 same-store sales grew 5.4% versus 4.2% consensus, driven by strong traffic growth (+3.7%), while EPS of C$1.29 beat by C$0.03. Management raised FY27 Canadian same-store sales guidance to 4.0%-4.5% from 3.0%-4.0%, with Zhihan noting the implied H2 outlook still appears conservative despite a supportive macro backdrop. Dollar city remained a key growth engine, delivering 30% sales growth and continued store expansion, while contributions from the company's LATAM investments grew 30% YoY. Looking ahead, Zhihan sees FY27 as a peak investment year and highlights the Calgary distribution center opening, improving profitability in Australia and Mexico, the potential consolidation of Dollar city, and a future $6 price point as catalysts that support an attractive risk/reward following the recent selloff.

CATL <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  – Neil is a buyer of recent weakness as industry and company fundamentals remain strong and valuation is attractive. Reasons that investors have cited for recent weakness include expectations that Q3 earnings will disappoint, market share loss to competitors, slowing demand growth, and concerns over capacity expansion. Local brokers are estimating that CATL will report may report 3Q sales volume of RMB260-265GWh and net income of RMB24-25bn. 1H26 net income was RMB43bn and our full year net income estimate is RMB96bn. This implies 2H26 of RMB53bn or RMB26.5bn per quarter, although the NI split for 2025 was split 44%/56%. This implies RMB23.3bn for 3Q, which seems in line with full year forecasts. On competition concerns, Neil highlights that these concerns are not new and that CATL maintains competitive advantages in battery performance, quality, safety, scale, and manufacturing. Additionally, industry demand indicators and channel checks continue to point to robust EV and ESS growth into 2027. Finally, while new Chinese policies tighten scrutiny of greenfield capacity additions, we view CATL’s recent acquisition of Chongqing Yaoning as an indicator that while CATL’s expansion route may change, capacity growth overall is unlikely to be constrained.
