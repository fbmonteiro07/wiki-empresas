From Barclays Research

Christine Cho downgrades FLNC to UW as she thinks that consensus estimates for fiscal 2027 overestimate the timing and magnitude of earnings realization of the backlog.  She’s forecasting EBITDA of $63 mm in fiscal 2027 vs. consensus $113.4 mm.  With last quarter’s earnings the company reduced fiscal 2026 guidance due to multiple execution related issues – at the same time that orders have been robust and the company has achieved a record backlog. Christine writes that the issues aren’t full resolved as the manufacturing facilities in Houston still require grid connection and are currently producing limited amounts.  She thinks that the recent surge in orders does little to alleviate her concerns about fiscal 2027 estimates as many of the recent awards, particularly those associated with large scale data center deployments , face multi-year development and construction schedules before meaningful revenue can be recognized.  She believes that the full financial benefit is more likely to materialize in in F28 than F27.  Her downgrade is driven less by the long-term opportunity and more by her declining confidence in the timing of the earnings recovery.  Her target price moves from $16 to $10 based on a 50/50 split between 16 X (unchanged)  EV/EBITDA on 2027 and a DCF analysis.

Ajay Rajadhyaksha, Harry Mateer, Christian Keller, Dave Anderson, Betty Jiang, and team view the global energy ecosystem as an underappreciated structural growth opportunity.  They argue that the transition increasingly requires adding rather than replacing supply, as oil, gas, electricity, and renewable demand rise concurrently.  Population growth, economic development, electrification, AI, and digital infrastructure are lifting demand, while geopolitical fragmentation raises the premium on secure and reliable supply. After a decade of underinvestment, constrained supply and infrastructure are creating a more capital-intensive, strategically important, and potentially inflationary system; annual investment could reach approximately $3.6 trillion in 2027, while demand is forecast to grow at a 1.9% CAGR from 2025 to 2050.  They identify opportunities across integrated energy, LNG, pipelines, power infrastructure, utilities, energy services, and companies enabling system expansion.

Will Thompson highlights that data center opposition is broad and bipartisan, reflecting concerns over electricity costs, water use, emissions, and local disruption even in traditionally pro-growth U.S. states.  He argues that permitting and political decisions made today will determine capacity later in the decade, intensifying the tension between national AI leadership and local priorities. The industry is emphasizing jobs, infrastructure funding, tax revenue, transparency, and sustainability, although many community concerns appear structural rather than reputational. He expects electricity costs and water-use debates to remain constraints, while speed-to-power increasingly takes precedence over emissions considerations.

Bring-your-own-power models, compute efficiencies, advanced energy technologies, and distributed architectures can ease bottlenecks, but long-term growth still requires grid expansion; even space-based data centers are drawing interest.
