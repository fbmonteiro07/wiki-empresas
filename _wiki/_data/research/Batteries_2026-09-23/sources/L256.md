KR EVB volumes lag global growth; weak US, EU m/s loss

Korea's EVB installations fell 22% YoY in July, lagging global EVB growth of +22% YoY. Regionally, Europe EVB market remained solid (+35% YoY), while US demand stayed weak (-39% YoY). Among K-battery makers, LGES (-18% YoY) outperformed SK/SDI (-25%/-32%), supported by stronger EU shipments (LGES/SK/SDI: +10%/-5%/+7% YoY), while US shipments remained weak across the board (-64%/-59%/-67% YoY). YTD, Korean EVB shipments are down 5% YoY vs 20% growth in global EVB shipments, reflecting weak US demand (-24% YoY) and continued share loss in Europe YTD (EU EVB demand +29% YoY vs LGES/SK/SDI +12%/-1%/flat YoY).

US ESS in focus; m/s gain continues; order upside remains

K-batteries remain focused on ESS. According to media reports on Aug 31, SDI and SK On secured new US ESS orders worth W3tn/W1.5tn, respectively. As of 1H26, K-battery makers have expanded their share of the N. American ESS market (e.g., LGES/SDI: 14%/6% vs 2%/2% in 2025), and we expect further gains, supported by growing preference for non-PFE suppliers amid a favorable regulatory backdrop (e.g., Trump's executive order restricting PFE's power equipment/ESS, alongside AMPC and ITC benefits for non-PFE ESS alongside c.40% tariffs on Chinese imports). Given the strong US ESS outlook (20% CAGR; c.100GWh demand in 2026; 700GWh+ cumulative TAM over the next five years), combined with K-batteries' current c.250GWh backlog (roughly one-third of the TAM) and further share-gain opportunities, we see meaningful room for additional order growth. We continue to view ESS as the key earnings driver for the sector, particularly for LGES <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  (150GWh+ backlog; c.3x capacity; additional ESS orders and ex-AMPC turnaround likely in 2H26) and SDI <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  (c.90GWh backlog; c.3x capacity, supported by BBU/UPS demand), followed by SKI <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  (c.15GWh backlog; c.1x capacity). 

Prefer US ESS leverage with meaningful utilization gain

Among Korean battery upstream suppliers, we continue to prefer names with (1) ESS leverage, (2) meaningful earnings recovery potential from UTR gains, and (3) attractive valuations. We reiterate our Buy ratings on L&F <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  (a leading Korean LFP cathode supplier, with 60ktpa+ capacity equivalent to c.30GWh of ESS demand; trading at 20x 27E EV/EBITDA) and WCP <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  (SDI's key separator supplier; trading at 14x). In contrast, we maintain Underperform ratings on PFM <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  (potential 2nd supplier for SDI's LFP ESS with 15-25ktpa capacity, but with anode UTR still below break-even; trading at 58x), EBM <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  (primarily exposed to SDI's slower EU EV ramp; trading at 40x), Solus/SKC (ongoing copper foil ASP pressure, with utilization still below break-even levels despite gradual improvement; trading at 18x/20x), and SKIET <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  (largely reliant on SK On and lagging peers in the LFP ESS opportunity). We remain Neutral on PNT <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive] , as Korean battery makers' EV-to-ESS line conversions limit the need for new battery equipment orders.
