CATL: Gravity Kicks In — Is China ESS Peaking?

CATL is down 7% today and the question flooding into our inbox is simple: why now?

The sell-off is being driven by a combination of sector concerns and positioning rather than any sudden deterioration in CATL's fundamentals. 

The biggest debate currently gripping the market centres on whether China's energy storage system (ESS) demand is approaching a cyclical peak. Importantly, this is not a CATL-specific issue. 

The weakness is broad-based, with Chinese battery makers and ESS suppliers selling off in tandem, confirming this is a sector-wide de-rating rather than a company-specific event. CATL is among the worst performers on the CSI 300 today, while its Hong Kong-listed shares have fallen as much as 8%.

There is also a clear liquidity rotation at work. Investors are trimming exposure to crowded, high-multiple winners—including battery stocks—to raise cash ahead of the highly anticipated CXMT IPO. 

CATL happens to be one of the most widely owned names across EM long-only portfolios, making it a natural source of funds. This looks far more like a pre-IPO positioning trade than a reassessment of CATL's long-term earnings power.

So what about the ESS demand concerns?

Some analysts have recently cut 2028 China ESS shipment forecasts, citing channel checks that suggest demand growth could be approaching its peak. That narrative is gaining traction, but it is hardly new. 

Alan Lau, our lead China Solar analyst, has been cautious on the sustainability of China's ESS buildout since late last year. The core argument remains unchanged: 2026 and potentially 2027 should still be strong years, but they may ultimately represent the high-water mark of a policy-driven installation cycle – Alan Lau’s note here <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive] .

China is on track to install roughly 300GWh of ESS capacity this year. However, much of the current demand is tied to grid infrastructure programmes that are inherently finite. We expect the bulk of these grid-related deployments to be completed over the next one to two years. 

Beyond that, incremental demand will increasingly depend on integrated data-centre, renewable energy and storage projects—a structurally attractive opportunity, but one with a slower and less predictable growth profile.

Europe is booming ( chart below), but it is still a side show in the global ESS story. China is the main event. Europe represents a 36–50GWh market versus 187–300GWh in China, which accounts for around 60% of global demand. 

That's why any signs of a China ESS slowdown matter far more for sentiment, earnings expectations and battery valuations globally.

My view on CATL remains unchanged. 

I continue to be a long-term bull on the company and see CATL as one of the highest-quality ways to play global electrification and energy storage- see :  A Convincing Beat Reinforces Its “TSMC of Electrification” Status. <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive] 

That said, the stock has become a crowded positioning trade and today is a reminder that even the market's favourite names are not immune when the narrative starts to wobble. 

Fundamentals remain intact, but near-term volatility is likely to stay elevated as investors digest the implications of a maturing China ESS cycle.
