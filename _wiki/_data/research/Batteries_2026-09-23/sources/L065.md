It’s essentially the combination of Battery Cells and Capacitors. As we get even higher voltage servers, CBUs would be needed for peak cutting during power spikes. Panasonic pitches it’s advantage as one of only player with expertise in both Battery cells and Capacitors (maybe TDK bulls would beg to differ). The market has (until very recently) been bullish on Musashi Seimitsu as a supplier of super capacitors that go into CBUs/CESS. MS Head of Greater China Hardware Sharon Shih along with US Industrials analyst Chris Snyder recently wrote a great Data Center Power & Cooling deep-dive <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive] Safety Catch#e=ZmVsaXBlLm1vbnRlaXJvQGNhcHN0b25lLmNvbS5icg==> . The report’s focus is on the positive implications for Delta and VRT. But I previously wrote I think there are parts of the report that read positively for Panasonic:

*	The next step in the development roadmap is adopting STANDALONE POWER RACKS which Sharon sees as well suited for Rubin GPU platform. These will enhance power conversion efficiency and free up space in the AI server rack. These power racks will continue PSU, BBU, and capacitor shelf.

*	Panasonic has recently shown impressive growth in its Data Center Batteries business, where it specifically provides BBU (Backup Battery Units). These are NOT UPS for those familiar (so don’t compete directly vs LGES/CATL), and they currently are placed in the server rack. Sharon’s work shows that 70% of the BBU design comes from battery technology, but Delta doesn’t do much BBU because Delta does not produce the battery pack internally. Therefore, Delta’s value-add is lower and that’s why BBU makes up just 1-2% of Delta rev vs PHU >20%.

*	Sharon thinks BBU adoption will increase starting w Rubin. Currently there is limited room to add BBU tray within GB200/300 racks. But once there is a stand alone power rack, it will allow room for more PHU and BBU use. She thinks BBU in certain cases will replace some UPS. I personally think this is interesting because Panasonic has explained that BBU adoption is still not across all the major US hyperscalers, so it could speak to some of the bottlenecks to adoptions that could wane over time.

*	The power rack is still in the design stage, but Sharon & Chris conducted a rough estimate of its potential content value. They expect the value per power rack to reach US$233,645, including integration work and testing service charges, based on the Vera Rubin CPX system that features 380kW per rack. This is up from US$42,400 for GB200 racks, assuming only power shelf and BBU shelf are included.
