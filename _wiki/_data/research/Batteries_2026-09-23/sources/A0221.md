PLS GROUP LIMITED <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  PLS: reinstate at Buy. Volume growth, asset quality and track record.
Pureplay lithium exposure with growth optionality: PLS is the largest lithium producer on the ASX and holds the second largest resource base in LCE terms behind RIO. PLS has more than four consecutive years of guidance delivery, a tier one lithium portfolio with a strong track record of balance sheet discipline through the cycle plus a flexible strategy i.e. pioneering spot 'BMX' auctions. We set our rounded price objective at A$5.90/sh based on 1.4x P/NAV and 11x forward EV/EBITDA.
 
Pilgangoora: operating tenure supports premium to peers: We see PLS' operating performance as a key differentiator vs peers. Pilgangoora has 'best in class' metrics such as lithium recovery after years of iterations of geological understanding and testwork. The management team has extensive tenure at Pilgangoora overseeing expansions, offtake relationships and several price cycles. 
 
Growth optionality with a net cash balance sheet: Having weathered two lithium downturns PLS has A$2.3bn cash in the bank (net cash +A$1bn). Pilgangoora is a tier-one deposit which creates valuable brownfields optionality. Having expanded with 'P1000', the next iteration is the P2000 (in BofA base case from FY30e) which could add ~1000kt of spodumene concentrate. A FID is due this CY. The deposit also remains open at depth with an underground option being evaluated. In Brazil, PLS owns the prospective Colina project. While PLS hasn't updated metrics post-acquisition it could comfortably add +300ktpa of concentrate. An updated feasibility study is due this CY; PLS has been acquiring adjacent tenement packages. 
 
Lithium: energy storage strong…but so is supply: Key risks: lithium is one of the most volatile major commodities given the ~2Mt market size i.e. between Nov25 to Jan26 Asian prices moved by ~120% with PLS moving ~165% over this period. While BofA expects pricing to move lower next year we expect PLS to generate cash through the cycle. We reinstate at Buy with PLS our lithium preference.
 
LIONTOWN LIMITED <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  Execution, scale and growth but fully priced into ramp up.
Kathleen Valley: resilience and growth but value stretched: Liontown operates the Kathleen Valley underground lithium mine in Western Australia. While the team has executed well and shown agility in navigating challenging market conditions, on our below consensus lithium deck our valuation is stretched. Coupled with a lack of conviction in medium term costs and BofA's proprietary quant data, we reinstate coverage of Liontown (LTR) at Underperform. Kathleen Valley sits higher on the cost curve than PLS and IGO. Our PO is A$1.10/sh based on 1.2x NAV and 8x EV/EBITDA. 
 
Back to 4Mtpa? First underground lithium mine of scale: LTR has one of the top-five lithium resources in Australia with size and grade attracting an infamous A$6.6bn bid from Albemarle in 2023. Large orebodies support brownfields optionality. LTR paused the initial 4Mtpa (700ktpa SC from two orebodies) plan opting for a higher margin one-orebody-only 2Mtpa (500ktpa SC) plan when pricing dropped to <US$800/t in late 2024 from +US$4000/t the prior year. The 4Mtpa case is now back on the agenda with a FID expected before DecQ. We include 4Mtpa in our base case. 
 
Medium term: a top 3 producer but costs key question: LTR intends to hit 2.8Mtpa by FY27 end. LTR has a track record of delivery so we have comfort with production i.e. LTR should become the second biggest producer on the ASX. With FY27 the first year of exclusive underground mining, the $/tonne costs step up materially yoy and we lack visibility on medium-term costs. The FID for the 4Mtpa is a key disclosure catalyst for us to become more positive. Our medium-term production, capex and FOB opex is broadly similar to consensus but our spodumene assumption is ~30%/20% below consensus in FY27-28e driving below street earnings and negative FCF in FY27e. LTR ended FY26 with $561m cash for A$192m net cash. We model Kathleen Valley in the lower third quartile of the global cost curve on a LCE basis. 
 
Lithium: energy storage strong…but so is supply: Key risks: lithium is one of the most volatile major commodities i.e. between Nov25 to Jan26 prices moved by ~120%. BofA Metal Strategists expect pricing to move lower next year. LTR's contract book has both chemicals and spodumene price linkages with LTR receiving the weakest realised pricing on a SC6 eqv. basis in our coverage as chemicals pricing lagged. 
 
IGO LIMITED <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive]  Tier 1 lithium investment vehicle with a Kwinana overhang. Reinstate at Neutral.
Exposure to Tier 1 lithium…with a sub-par structure: We reinstate coverage of IGO with a Neutral rating and a PO A$8.9/sh balancing asset quality and capital returns against our bearish view on lithium and lack of visibility on a Kwinana resolution. IGO has exposure to the largest and highest-grade hard rock lithium mine Greenbushes via their interest in TLEA (see through 25% to IGO). While we like the asset quality, the JV structure is challenging. IGO has impaired Kwinana hydroxide to zero with an exit catalyst elusive. We set our A$8.90/sh PO on 1.2x P/NAV and 8x forward Greenbushes EV/EBITDA.
 
Did someone say dividends? From CY27 when Nova nickel mine ends, IGO is essentially a vehicle to collect dividends from TLEA. Given the JV structure, holding IGO requires being comfortable with the corporate strategies of Tianqi and Albemarle. Windfield resumed paying dividends in JunQ and we expect a FY27+ IGO dividend yield of ~2%. We expect dividends from Windfield (Greenbushes) to sweep to IGO shareholders with a customary lag. In practice, cash goes to sustaining Kwinana making forecasting dividends to IGO somewhat challenging. 
 
An exit out of Kwinana not obvious here. Key catalyst: Since FY21 i) impairments have totalled ~A$2.2bn (Western Areas/Kwinana) ii) corporate costs have consumed A$270m; and spending on exploration/evaluation totalled A$360m. Encouragingly, corporate costs and exploration expenditure in FY26 were 20% and 66% lower, respectively, than in FY24. The board comprises six down from seven. As TLEA (IGO 49%) holds shares in Windfield, there's no catalyst to us for IGO to exit Kwinana without the support of Tianqi. This would be a key catalyst for us to become more positive.
 
What's next? Exploration, cost out, Kwinana resolution…: IGO's strategy is to "discover, develop and deliver battery minerals lithium, copper and nickel". IGO has spent cA$420m on exploration over the past 6yrs with limited tangible benefits to show. IGO's outlines the case for copper -we're on board with the bullish pitch-but we struggle to see how IGO can compete for copper assets here even if they're "staged and de-risked". Lithium prices are a key IGO driver; and a key risk.
