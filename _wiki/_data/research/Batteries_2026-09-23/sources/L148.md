CTG Brazil: capacity and flexibility are becoming key value drivers 

We hosted a discussion with CTG Brazil to discuss Brazil’s power market outlook. The main takeaway was that value in the power sector is shifting from energy volumes toward capacity, flexibility and reliability. The meeting highlighted storage, renewable capacity and pumped hydro as the most attractive opportunities to address tightening reserve margins and growing peak demand, while viewing distributed generation subsidies as the largest source of market distortion. The speaker expects future regulatory discussions to increasingly focus on mechanisms that remunerate capacity and flexibility, creating potential upside for dispatchable and flexible assets. This report is part of UBS’ ongoing series on Energy Transition and builds on our note: BESS: From renewable expansion to system flexibility <[original URL omitted; available in raw archive] .

Flexibility is becoming the key value driver in Brazil’s power market 
A key insight from the meeting was that as renewable penetration rises, hydropower is gradually being compensated not only for energy production, but also for the flexibility and capacity they provide to the system, a characteristic that has historically been under remunerated. In this context, the discussion reinforced pumped hydro, storage and capacity additions at existing hydro plants as some of the most attractive medium-term opportunities. The speaker estimates more than 10GW of potential pumped storage opportunities in Brazil, with roughly 2-3GW already approaching viability studies.

BESS economics remain challenging despite supportive long-term fundamentals 
While batteries appear as an important part of the solution, the meeting highlighted caution on the economics of co-located BESS projects. Studies conducted at the company’s solar complex in Northern Minas Gerais suggest that revenues from curtailment mitigation and merchant price arbitrage alone are insufficient to justify investment, reinforcing the need for dedicated capacity remuneration mechanisms. Beyond storage, data centers were discussed as a potentially important source of future demand growth.

Distributed generation as the sector’s biggest structural distortion 
The discussion highlighted that distributed generation (DG) subsidies in Brazil have become a larger structural issue than curtailment itself. Under the current framework, residential solar generators are credited based on the volume of energy injected into the grid rather than the value of that energy at the time of injection. As a result, consumers export power during periods of oversupply, when energy values may fall to R$0-10/MWh, and later consume during peak-demand periods, when energy values can reach approximately R$1,600/MWh. This compensation mechanism weakens price signals, incentivizes faster DG expansion and contributes to market distortions. The meeting highlighted hourly metering and financial settlement based on hourly prices as a key measure to better align compensation.

Capacity adequacy is becoming the sector’s top priority 
The meeting pointed to a recent increase in peak hourly prices from ~R$300/MWh to ~R$1,600/MWh alongside a decline in reserve margins from roughly 1.3GW to zero, highlighting tightening system conditions. In this context, the upcoming capacity auction was described as an important milestone, although questions remain regarding technology-specific remuneration and the overall market design. The speaker argued that a durable solution will require a combination of batteries, pumped hydro, hydro capacity upgrades and transmission expansion, particularly as demand growth from data centers, electrification and higher temperatures continues to accelerate. 

El Niño reinforces long-term demand growth and capacity needs 
Regarding El Niño, the speaker’s main concern is electricity demand rather than hydro generation. Extreme temperatures are increasingly influencing electricity consumption patterns, driving higher cooling demand and placing additional pressure on evening peak loads, when solar generation is unavailable. As a result, this moment reinforces the growing importance of flexibility, storage and dispatchable capacity.
