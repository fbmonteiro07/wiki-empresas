Battery costs + curtailment relief: netting out the impact

On Aug 25, regulators proposed an impact analysis on allocating new Battery (BESS) storage costs across Brazil's power generators. We think this discussion could unlock the awaited BESS auction debut and we take the opportunity to assess the impact of a hypothetical addition of ~5GW BESS (even unlikely in the very short-term) across our coverage under its 3 cost-allocation alternatives - netting the new charges against the curtailment-relief benefit (~3-4pp lower curtailment). Within coverage, AURE (+0.9% to +2.7% NPV vs. base) and EQTL (+0.3% to +0.6%) would be the main beneficiaries, as curtailment savings would outweigh the added costs; while AXIA (-2.9% to 0%) and CPLE (-1.1% to +0.3%) would be modest laggards. Despite the exercise, we think it's too early to draw firm conclusions as 1) BESS volumes/rules remain unclear + ~5GW scenario is unlikely in the ST and 2) litigation risks could emerge. We thus don't view this as a sector game-changer and keep our preference for flexible-hydro generators.

What was proposed: ANEEL's 3 cost-allocation design 

ANEEL compared three alternatives, under a hypothetical 5GW/R$900k-MW·fixed revenue year BESS scenario: #1) flat uniform charge (R$7.4/MWh) to all generators, regardless of flexibility; #2) binary exemption: flexible sources (e.g. hydro) pay zero, inflexible ones (e.g. renewables) pay 100%, pushing the charge to ~R$13/MWh (+76%) and creating an unlimited "shrinking payer base" risk; #3) the preferred option - a graded 4-tier flexibility scale, ranging from ~R$11/MWh (least flexible) to ~R$3/MWh (most flexible). Alternative #3 scored the best as it avoids the payer-base erosion risk while still rewards flexibility a lower charge, in ANEEL's point of view - see pages 1 and 2 for more details. In addition, according to ANEEL's proposal, the impact on regulated contracts will be a pass-through to customers tariffs with no impact on the generators' gross margin. Next steps: 30-day public consultation aiming to conclude the rules before the Dec/26 BESS auction. Charging framework development through 2027. First billing in Aug/2028.

Sizing the proposal impact 

We ran an analysis for the companies assuming a curtailment-relief + BESS cost charge in 2028+ against each company's generation mix. With a 5GW BESS implementation at a conservative 4h/cycle and 85% efficiency, renewables curtailment could fall ~3-4pp (~20% to ~17%), an offset available only to renewables. Also important, we still do not balance potential impacts in the sector LCOE or modulation premium.

*	(=) neutral: Engie and Eneva on high % of regulated contract
