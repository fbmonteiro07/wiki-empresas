# Batteries: saved Twitter feed and email review

As of September 23, 2026. Search window: June 25–September 23, through retrieval this morning.

**The main conclusion is that batteries are becoming a power-infrastructure investment theme with several distinct profit pools. Stationary storage, AI backup and grid flexibility have stronger support in this material than a broad recovery in EV batteries. The investment debate is increasingly about where storage sits, which suppliers capture the system value, and whether projects and factories produce cash on schedule.** This is my synthesis of the sources below, rather than a new industry forecast.

The review searched a dated universe of **113,728 saved posts** and **22,323 broker emails with archived bodies**, and supplemented it with a live Outlook subject search across received-mail folders and all senders. The broad first pass returned **309 tweet keyword hits** and **1,017 email keyword hits**; the live search returned **258 raw matches**, including duplicates and irrelevant substring matches. These are search counts, not counts of independent substantive reports. The readable evidence pack contains **29 selected email copies and 57 selected posts**. [Coverage and limitations](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/coverage.json>) · [Source register](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/source_register.md>).

The saved feed ends September 22. Archived broker bodies can be truncated at 9,000 characters; the selected live Outlook messages provide complete bodies. This was a body search of the broker archive plus a broader live subject search, not an exhaustive live body search of every non-broker email. Linked reports, attachments and paywalled articles were not all opened. Event invitations, repeated headlines, consumer-device battery-life posts and unrelated uses of “battery” were excluded from the synthesis.

**1. The strongest change in the conversation: storage has acquired its own demand drivers.**

The social feed repeatedly frames batteries as infrastructure that can unlock existing generation and accommodate AI demand: ARK on June 25, TrendForce on July 10, Coatue on August 14 and Gavin Baker on August 30. These are distinct framings, but their posts do not independently establish market size or project returns. Reuters’ August 20 post on LG Energy Solution’s pivot from EVs to storage gives a particularly clear description of the business-model shift. [ARK](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/T306.md>), [TrendForce](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/T268.md>), [Coatue](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/T135.md>), [Baker](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/T077.md>), [Reuters](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/T111.md>).

The emails provide the more useful economic detail. **BofA’s Taekyoung Ha, September 1**, sees US stationary storage as the principal growth driver for Korean battery makers despite weak EV exposure. The same day's global battery note, by **Ming Hsun Lee and colleagues**, reports July global EV battery installations **up 22% year over year**, while Korean suppliers’ installations were **down 22%**. This is regional and supplier divergence, not a global collapse of EV battery demand. [Korea](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L256.md>) · [Global EV/ESS](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L255.md>).

Keep the applications separate:

| Application | Customer buys | Relevant exposures in the reviewed sources | Investment question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grid / renewable storage | Energy shifting, dispatchable capacity and flexibility | CATL, LGES, Samsung SDI, SK On, Sungrow, Fluence | Who earns attractive returns after procurement, interconnection, financing and degradation? |
| AI rack / near-rack backup | Short backup and rapid power buffering | Panasonic, Samsung SDI, Dynapack, AES-KY | Does the battery move to a sidecar or building, and who owns the module design? |
| AI campus storage | Load management, ride-through and coordination with generation | Tesla Megapack, integrated BESS suppliers and power-conversion vendors | Can the complete system meet the site’s operating requirements? |
| EV / specialty cells | Range, weight, charging and qualification | CATL, Korean suppliers, QuantumScape, Enovix | Does technical progress translate into qualified volume and acceptable manufacturing economics? |

The company mapping follows **UBS Bush, September 14; Citi Wong et al., July 17; BofA Ha, September 1; GS Delaney, July 24; and BofA Bhattacharya/Mohan, August 17**. “AES” in Citi’s Asian BBU supplier list refers to the battery-module context; it must not be conflated with the US utility AES Corporation. [UBS](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L176.md>) · [Citi](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/A0754.md>) · [QS](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L048.md>) · [ENVX](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L251.md>).

**Coatue follow-up — added September 23 after the initial review.**

Two saved Coatue posts make the infrastructure argument more explicit. The **August 14** post was already captured above; the **August 3** post was recovered by searching the account rather than battery keywords. Both original charts were visually checked on Coatue's website.

- **August 14, Coatue, citing BloombergNEF:** annual capacity additions increased from **10GW to 100GW in four years for energy storage**, compared with **eight years for solar and fifteen for wind**. Storage excludes pumped hydro. This compares the time to reach an annual deployment scale, measured in GW; it does not compare cumulative installed capacity, GWh, electricity generation or supplier profits. [Original chart and post](https://www.coatue.com/c/takes/chart-of-the-day-2026-08-14).
- **August 3, Coatue, citing the US EIA:** transmission and distribution account for a growing share of US utility electricity-infrastructure spending. Coatue links that shift to its backing of **Base Power** and the **Base Core** launch. The chart shows a rising delivery share, not that delivery already exceeds production in the latest period. [Original chart and post](https://www.coatue.com/c/takes/chart-of-the-day-2026-08-03).

**My interpretation:** Coatue adds a distributed-storage business model to this review. Base describes earning revenue from operating home batteries collectively for grid balancing, while also providing household backup. This directs attention to dispatch, software, customer access and deployment economics alongside cell manufacturing. It reinforces storage as power infrastructure, but these posts do not establish attractive returns for every battery producer, or disclose Coatue positions in the listed companies discussed here. [Base's business-model explanation, April 6; background outside the 90-day window](https://help.basepowercompany.com/en/articles/10194881). [Follow-up evidence and chart details](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/coatue_followup.md>).

**2. AI batteries: the placement changes, and the system value matters more than the cell count.**

The most useful document is **UBS Tim Bush et al., “Rubin moves batteries out of the tray,” published September 14**; it arrived in Outlook on September 13. UBS models the **near-rack BBU market at $2.5bn in 2026 and $12.8bn in 2030**, or **$3.1bn and $15.9bn including capacitor backup units**. Those forecasts explicitly exclude building UPS. UBS assumes only **40–50% BBU attachment for Rubin-class systems**, because operators can put reserve capacity elsewhere. These are broker assumptions, not NVIDIA commitments. [Source](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L176.md>).

An especially valuable detail is that UBS estimates **battery cells at 10–15% of BBU system cost**, using its assumed five-minute duration and system cost. That supports an investment distinction between selling cells and supplying qualified modules, power conversion and integration. **My inference:** more battery-system spending need not generate the same revenue growth or margins for every cell producer. [UBS Bush, September 14](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L176.md>).

**Citi Kyna Wong and colleagues, July 17**, presents a more aggressive architectural adoption case, forecasting BBU content per rack rising from **$4–5k in 2022 to $33–34k in 2029** and identifying Panasonic, Dynapack and AES as direct BBU exposures. Its combined BESS/BBU growth framing has a different scope from UBS’s near-rack revenue model; the forecasts should not be added together. [Source](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/A0754.md>).

Panasonic deserves attention, with a qualification that the wiki’s shorter relay obscures. **Morgan Stanley sales’ Amir Amerian, July 30**, described an overall beat and raise but said **data-center batteries were approximately in line or slightly below expectations**; much of the positive surprise came from other AI-related businesses, industrial automation, vehicle batteries and Blue Yonder. His personal long view also differs from research analyst Kazuo Yoshikawa’s **Equal-weight** rating. Treat this as sales commentary relaying results, not a research upgrade or evidence of a BBU earnings beat. [Source](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L065.md>).

For technical corroboration outside the review window, **NVIDIA’s Sean James, Harry Petty and Sai Somayajula, June 10**, describes BESS as a coordinated system of cells, inverters, controls and telemetry. NVIDIA also says GPU- and rack-level smoothing is improving, leaving BESS to manage residual fluctuations and wider site duties. **My inference:** avoid sizing campus storage from an assumption that all GPU volatility reaches the grid unchanged. [NVIDIA technical explanation](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/designing-production-ready-battery-energy-storage-systems-for-ai-factories/).

Tesla’s July 22 earnings transcript provides an in-window management example: Musk says Megapacks at SpaceX data centers are used principally to smooth training-load fluctuations and can also help negotiate flexible grid access. This is Tesla management’s account of a related-party application, not proof of equivalent adoption across all hyperscalers. [Tesla Q2 call](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/TSLA/transcripts/TSLA_Q2-2026-earnings_2026-07-22.md:402>).

**3. China and CATL: structural leadership coexists with a real cycle debate.**

The social feed moves between strong storage demand and mounting competitive concerns. Reuters reported CATL’s storage-supported quarterly profit beat on **July 24**; YuanTalks flagged storage price increases on **August 13**, then slower capacity expansion on **September 8**. Bloomberg’s saved posts on **September 16–18** focus on production expectations, the equity drawdown and customers seeking alternatives to CATL. These are headline relays, not a homogeneous stream of bullish evidence. [Reuters](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/T219.md>) · [August prices](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/T143.md>) · [Capacity](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/T043.md>) · [September concerns](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/T018.md>) · [Customer diversification](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/T006.md>).

The early warning is **Jefferies sales’ Cristina Titu, July 10**, relaying analyst **Alan Lau’s** concern that Chinese grid-related installations could produce a strong 2026–27 cycle followed by slower growth. Titu remained bullish on CATL’s long-term position. Her **September 16** update distinguishes unverified production/profit rumors from management’s assertion of full utilization and strong orders. **Jefferies Conor O’Mara, September 16**, separately relays **Xiaoyi’s** view that CATL’s global share gains and resilience make the selloff excessive. These are the competing arguments, not evidence that one has already won. [July debate](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L027.md>) · [September sales view](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L190.md>) · [Research relay](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L194.md>).

The latest substantive counterweight is **Goldman Sachs Trina Chen / Joy Zhang’s September materials monitor, relayed September 17 by Marcio Farid**. It reports softer lithium pricing, easing supply as Zimbabwe spodumene reaches China, slowing domestic ESS growth amid declining project returns, and Chinese cathode output running ahead of implied downstream demand. **My reading:** the market can support leading suppliers over time while still experiencing inventory corrections, weak project economics and near-term estimate cuts. [Source](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/A0042.md>).

**4. Korea and US localization: a potential utilization recovery, with policy and factory risk.**

**BofA Ha, September 1**, reports North American ESS shares of **14% for LGES and 6% for Samsung SDI in 1H26**, versus **2% each in 2025**. The note attributes opportunity to local capacity, preference for qualifying non-Chinese supply, order growth and conversion of EV lines. Its stated preference is LGES/SDI and selected upstream names; it is cautious on suppliers whose utilization remains poor. Its Neutral stance on equipment maker PNT reflects a useful offset: **converting existing lines can support cells without requiring an equally strong new-equipment cycle**. [Source](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L256.md>).

**BofA Sun Jung Lee, September 1**, relays a **9GWh SK On–NeoVolta LFP supply agreement for 2027–31**, involving a Georgia plant currently making EV batteries. The additional potential agreement is explicitly prospective. [Source](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/A0231.md>).

**Morgan Stanley Andrew Percoco, September 9**, articulates the policy tension through Ford: the US wants domestic storage manufacturing while much of the technology and operating know-how sits in China. He states that the criticism of Ford’s CATL licensing arrangement does not itself prohibit the technology or cancel credits. Do not convert a policy headline into an enacted blanket prohibition. This memo summarizes the broker’s dated assessment rather than offering a legal eligibility determination. [Source](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/A0151.md>).

**5. Fluence is the clearest demonstration that strong demand is insufficient.**

There is a useful sequence of changing expectations. A saved **August 25 @wallstengine post relays UBS Jon Windham’s upgrade to Neutral**, premised partly on an apparent near-term earnings trough. **Barclays Christine Cho, relayed by Stan Solomon on September 3**, then downgraded to Underweight because backlog realization and Houston execution made the next fiscal year too optimistic. **Jefferies Julien Dumoulin-Smith’s September 18 recap** reports its downgrade to Hold, with concern that execution failures could damage customer relationships and share. The August social relay should not be treated as the latest broker conclusion. [UBS relay](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/T089.md>) · [Barclays](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L147.md>) · [Jefferies](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L207.md>).

The company itself confirmed the issue on **September 16**: FY26 revenue guidance fell from a prior **$3.0bn midpoint to approximately $2.4bn**, while adjusted EBITDA moved from a **$10m loss midpoint to approximately a $200m loss**. Fluence attributed the revision chiefly to the Houston manufacturing ramp while maintaining that demand was strong. **My inference:** supplier selection needs evidence of throughput, delivery, working capital and cash conversion as well as orders. [Fluence release](https://ir.fluenceenergy.com/news-releases/news-release-details/fluence-energy-announces-revised-guidance-fiscal-year-2026).

**6. Lithium: the short-term squeeze thesis has support, but the latest checks complicate it.**

**GS Hugo Nicolaci et al., report dated August 17, received August 16**, relays Albemarle IR’s view that rapidly growing, predominantly LFP-based ESS demand was tightening lithium carbonate. That is management’s market assessment transmitted through a broker. [Source](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L096.md>).

**MS Rahul Anand, September 1**, highlights upside risks from delayed mine restarts, low inventories and possible shipment pull-forward. The team’s reported **2026 ESS shipment forecast is 887GWh**, with shipments tracking ahead; MS nevertheless expects additional supply and a looser market over the longer term. This is not an unconditional lithium supercycle forecast. [Source](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L134.md>).

The chronology matters: **GS’s September 17 checks** report weaker prices and easing supply after those earlier tightness arguments. **BofA Kate McCutcheon, relayed September 2 by Felicity Robson**, also combines strong storage demand with expected lithium supply growth, distinguishing PLS’s operating quality from higher-cost or more expensive alternatives. **My reading:** storage demand improves the demand base, but the commodity trade still depends on restarts, inventory and realized pricing. [Latest GS checks](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/A0042.md>) · [BofA](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/A0221.md>).

Do not reconcile MS’s **shipment** forecast mechanically with **BofA Sun Jung Lee’s September 1 forecast of 867GWh of annual additional ESS installations in 2030**. They differ in year, metric and potentially coverage; the emails do not provide the bridge. Likewise, GW of discharge power, GWh of stored energy, annual generation and cumulative backlog are different quantities. The **August 9 Cathie Wood post** does not establish its generation-share conclusion from the power-capacity figure alone. [BofA installation forecast](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/A0231.md>) · [Saved post](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/T166.md>).

**7. Brazil: a market-design and capacity-payment story.**

**UBS Luciana Carvalho / Shneur Gershuni and colleagues, September 3**, summarize CTG Brasil’s assessment that avoided curtailment and merchant arbitrage alone did not justify investment in the co-located projects studied. Storage economics depended on remuneration for capacity and flexibility. This is company-specific project evidence, not a claim that every Brazilian battery project is uneconomic. [Source](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L148.md>).

Their **September 10** note identifies the December auctions as an important catalyst, while cautioning that the large registered project pipeline includes duplication and will shrink through qualification. **BofA Gustavo Faria / Andre Silveira, August 27**, finds that cost allocation can offset curtailment benefits and does not view the proposed framework as a wholesale sector revaluation. Its **5GW scenario is illustrative**, explicitly considered unlikely in the very short term. [UBS](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L170.md>) · [BofA](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L254.md>).

The official **ANEEL July 28 consultation announcement** scheduled auctions for **December 2 and 4**, with proposed **15-year contracts starting August 2028**. These are prospective auction terms, not awarded capacity. **My inference:** the next useful evidence is final rules, qualified bids, contract economics and the cost-allocation outcome. [ANEEL](https://www.gov.br/aneel/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/2026-defeso-eleitoral/primeiros-leiloes-de-armazenamento-de-energia-do-brasil-entram-em-consulta-publica).

**8. New chemistry and new end markets: distinguish agreements, qualification and production.**

The feed highlights sodium-ion, solid-state, drones, defense and robotics. **The Information’s August 17–20 posts** describe startups finding customers outside EVs, and its **September 3 Nyobolt post** emphasizes warehouse-robot charging uptime. **TrendForce, August 12**, frames solid-state as an engineering and qualification story. [New markets](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/T109.md>) · [Nyobolt](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/T062.md>) · [Solid-state](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/T146.md>).

Sodium-ion has concrete commercial evidence within the window: **CATL and Solarpro announced a 2GWh cooperation agreement on July 21**. This is an agreement, not evidence that the contracted volume has been commissioned. **My inference:** sodium adoption can support storage growth while changing its lithium intensity, so storage and lithium are related but distinct exposures. [CATL announcement](https://www.catl.com/en/news/6916.html).

Execution risk remains visible. **GS Mark Delaney, July 24**, acknowledged QuantumScape’s Honda agreement and defense-cell shipment but retained Sell, including concern about VW/PowerCo opportunity. **BofA Ruplu Bhattacharya / Wamsi Mohan, August 17**, retained Neutral on Enovix amid remaining qualification work, manufacturing economics and leadership uncertainty. These are development-stage investment cases, with different evidence requirements from established ESS demand. [QS](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L048.md>) · [ENVX](<E:/Wiki Felipe empresas/_wiki/_data/research/Batteries_2026-09-23/sources/L251.md>).

**Where I would focus the next research effort, based on this review:**

- **Panasonic versus Samsung SDI:** customer-specific rack/sidecar/building placement, module versus cell revenue, and realized margins. UBS’s architecture assumptions and the July Panasonic result make this the clearest technical and economic debate.
- **LGES / SDI / SK On:** actual ESS conversion throughput, customer qualification and earnings excluding production credits. Booked orders and installed capacity must be distinguished from profitable utilization.
- **CATL / Sungrow:** global competitiveness versus Chinese project returns, order quality and the policy exposure of each geography. September checks should carry more weight than earlier generic enthusiasm.
- **Fluence:** Houston production, deliveries, customer retention and cash conversion. An order announcement by itself does not resolve the current debate.
- **Lithium:** carbonate inventories, restart timing and spot realizations. Avoid treating storage growth as a sufficient price forecast.
- **Brazil:** capacity-contract economics and final cost allocation. Avoid treating registered projects or illustrative auction scenarios as committed deployment.

These are research priorities, not new ratings or portfolio recommendations. No company thesis page or canonical industry assumption was changed.
